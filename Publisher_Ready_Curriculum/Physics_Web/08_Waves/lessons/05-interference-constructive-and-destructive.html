Interference: Constructive and Destructive

How can adding a second wave make a spot quieter ?

BTC
Download teacher guide (DOCX)

Phenomenon

Two speakers play the exact same steady tone. As you walk across the room, the volume rises and falls — some spots are loud, some are nearly silent — and nobody touched the volume knob. Noise-canceling headphones do something even stranger: they add sound to create silence.

Driving Question

How can adding a second wave make a spot quieter?

Notice & Wonder

Walk the room between two speakers. Write two things you notice and two things you wonder about the loud and quiet spots.

Share with your partner: what would have to be true about two waves arriving at a quiet spot for them to cancel?

Initial Model

Draw two waves arriving at the same point. Draw one version that makes a LOUD spot and one that makes a QUIET spot. What is different about how the crests line up?

Investigate

Two source waves (purple and blue) have the same amplitude and wavelength. Drag the phase slider to shift the second wave. The thick orange wave is their sum (superposition). Find the phase for the loudest sum (in phase) and the silent sum (out of phase).

Result: fully constructive (amplitude doubles)

When the two waves cancel at a quiet spot, where did the energy go? Is it gone everywhere, or moved to the loud spots? Convince your partner.

Make It Make Sense

  1. State the principle of superposition in your own words.
  2. Two waves of amplitude 3 cm meet exactly in phase. What is the resulting amplitude? What if they meet exactly out of phase?
  3. Explain how noise-canceling headphones make a hum disappear. Use the term destructive interference.
Stuck? Sentence frame

"When crest meets ___ the waves add (constructive); when crest meets ___ the waves cancel (destructive)."

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • superposition — the principle that when waves overlap, the total displacement at each point is the sum of the individual displacements
  • constructive interference — when waves combine in phase (crest on crest) to make a larger amplitude
  • destructive interference — when waves combine out of phase (crest on trough) to make a smaller amplitude, or cancel completely

Revise Your Model

Return to your loud-spot and quiet-spot drawings. Label one constructive and one destructive. Add a sentence using the word superposition.

Return to the Phenomenon

Back to the two speakers: in one sentence, explain why some spots in the room are loud and some nearly silent, using constructive and destructive interference.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. Two speakers play the same tone. At one point in the room a listener hears almost nothing.
    (a) Is this constructive or destructive interference? (b) Describe how the two waves are arriving at that point (in phase or out of phase?). (c) Two waves of amplitude 4 cm meet exactly in phase. What is the resulting amplitude?
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
