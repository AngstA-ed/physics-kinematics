Digital Technologies and Information

Why does a digital copy survive perfectly while an analog copy degrades?

BTC
Download teacher guide (DOCX)

Phenomenon

A vinyl record gets scratchier every time it's played, and a copy of a copy of a photocopy turns to mush. But you can stream the same song a million times, or copy a PDF endlessly, and the last copy is identical to the first. Numbers survive copying; smooth waves do not.

Driving Question

Why does a digital copy survive perfectly while an analog copy degrades?

Notice & Wonder

Compare a worn record to a streamed song. Write two things you notice and two things you wonder about why one degrades and the other does not.

Share with your partner: what is it about storing something as numbers (digital) that lets it survive copying perfectly?

Initial Model

Draw a smooth wave (analog). Now show how you would store it using only a few measured points (samples). What information do you lose? What do you gain?

Investigate

The smooth purple curve is the analog signal. The orange stair-step is its digital version — measured (sampled) at regular intervals and rounded to discrete levels. Drag the sampling rate slider: more samples means a stair-step that hugs the curve more closely.

Levels (quantization): 8 (= 3 bits) · more samples → digital hugs analog · fewer samples → blocky stair-step

After you add noise to both signals, which one can you clean back to the original exactly — analog or digital? Tell your partner why rounding to the nearest level rescues the digital copy.

Make It Make Sense

  1. Define analog and digital signals in your own words.
  2. Why does a digital photo copied 100 times look identical, but a photocopied photo degrades?
  3. Name one disadvantage of digital — what gets lost when you sample a smooth wave?
Stuck? Sentence frame

"A digital copy survives because each value is rounded to a clean ___, so noise can be ___."

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • analog signal — a smooth, continuous signal that can take any value (e.g., a vinyl groove, a sound wave in air)
  • digital signal — a signal stored as discrete numbers, sampled at intervals and rounded to set levels, so it can be copied without loss
  • bit — a single binary digit (0 or 1); the basic unit of digital information; 3 bits encode 2³ = 8 levels

Revise Your Model

Return to your sampled wave. Label the smooth curve analog and the stair-step digital. Add a one-sentence evaluation: why is digital better for sending a song across the world?

Return to the Phenomenon

Back to the worn record versus the streamed song: in one sentence, explain why the digital copy never degrades, using sample and bit.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. A friend says, "Records sound warmer, so analog must be better in every way."
    (a) Define analog and digital signals. (b) Give two advantages of digital for transmitting and storing music. (c) Evaluate the friend's claim: name one thing analog might do well and one clear advantage of digital they're overlooking.
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
