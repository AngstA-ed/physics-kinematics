The Doppler Effect

If the siren makes the same pitch the whole time, why do you hear it change?

Hochman
Download teacher guide (DOCX)

Phenomenon

An ambulance races past you. The driver hears one steady siren the whole time — but you, on the curb, hear the pitch drop the instant it passes. Out in space, astronomers see the light from distant galaxies "shifted" toward red. The source never changed its note.

Driving Question

If the siren makes the same pitch the whole time, why do you hear it change?

Notice & Wonder

Listen to a siren pass by. Write two things you notice and two things you wonder about the pitch you hear before and after it passes.

Share with your partner: if the siren's pitch is constant, why does the listener on the curb hear it change?

Initial Model

Draw a source sitting still, making circular wavefronts. Then draw the same source moving to the right. Show what happens to the spacing of the wavefronts ahead of it and behind it.

Investigate

The source (orange) emits circular wavefronts at a steady rate. Drag the source speed slider. Watch the wavefronts bunch up ahead (shorter wavelength → higher observed frequency) and stretch out behind (longer wavelength → lower observed frequency). The listeners' frequency read-outs update live.

Observer ahead hears f ≈ — · Observer behind hears f ≈ — (source emits 1.00)

When the source moves toward an observer, what happens to the wavelength they receive — and therefore to the frequency they hear? Use the slider to show your partner.

Make It Make Sense

  1. Why does an approaching source sound higher in pitch? Use the word wavelength.
  2. A star's light is redshifted. Is it moving toward us or away from us? How do you know?
  3. Does the Doppler effect change the speed of the wave in the medium? Explain.
Stuck? Sentence frame

"As the source approaches, the wavefronts ahead ___, so the wavelength is ___ and the frequency I hear is ___."

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • Doppler effect — the change in observed frequency/wavelength when a wave source and observer move relative to each other
  • frequency shift — the difference between the frequency a source emits and the frequency an observer receives because of relative motion
  • redshift — a shift of light toward longer wavelengths (lower frequency), indicating the source is moving away; a blueshift is the opposite

Revise Your Model

Return to your moving-source drawing. Label the bunched side "higher frequency (blueshift)" and the stretched side "lower frequency (redshift)." Then write a Because / But / So sentence.

Return to the Phenomenon

Back to the ambulance: in one sentence, explain why you hear the pitch drop the instant it passes, even though the driver hears a steady siren.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. A race car drives past a spectator.
    (a) Does the engine sound higher or lower as it approaches? Explain using wavelength. (b) After it passes, what happens to the pitch and why? (c) Astronomers see the light from a distant galaxy redshifted. What does this tell us about the galaxy's motion, and what does that suggest about the universe?
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
