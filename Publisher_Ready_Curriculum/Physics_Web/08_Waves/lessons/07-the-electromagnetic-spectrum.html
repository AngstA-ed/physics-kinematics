Lesson 07 · Unit: Waves & Sound

The Electromagnetic Spectrum

If all these waves travel at the same speed, what makes a radio wave different from an X-ray?

Active Learning
Download teacher guide (DOCX)

Phenomenon

Look around the room. The speaker plays music (radio waves), the microwave heats lunch (microwaves), the remote changes the channel (infrared), your eyes read the board (visible light), sunscreen blocks sunburn (ultraviolet), and a nurse can take an X-ray. Every one of these is the same kind of wave, all traveling at the same speed.

Driving Question

If all these waves travel at the same speed, what makes a radio wave different from an X-ray?

Notice & Wonder

Look at all the everyday EM waves around you. Write two things you notice and two things you wonder about how they differ.

Share with your partner: if all these waves travel at the same speed, what must be different between a radio wave and a gamma ray?

Initial Model

Make your best guess at the order of the spectrum from longest wavelength to shortest. Where does visible light fall? Where would the highest-energy waves go?

Investigate

Drag the slider across the spectrum. Every band travels at the speed of light, c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. Watch how the wavelength and frequency trade off (since c = f·λ): radio has long wavelength / low frequency; gamma has short wavelength / high frequency.

Band: visible light · Wavelength ≈ — · Frequency ≈ — · Speed = c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s (always)

Since v = c is the same for every band, what must be true about frequency and wavelength as you move from radio toward gamma? Use the slider to convince your partner.

Make It Make Sense

  1. Which has the longer wavelength: a radio wave or an X-ray? Use v = f·λ to justify your answer.
  2. Do microwaves travel faster than visible light in a vacuum? Explain.
  3. An FM radio station broadcasts at 100 MHz (1.0 × 10⁸ Hz). Use λ = c/f to find its wavelength. (c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s)
Stuck? Sentence frame

"Because c is fixed, a band with a longer wavelength must have a ___ frequency."

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • electromagnetic wave — a transverse wave of oscillating electric and magnetic fields that needs no medium and travels at the speed of light in a vacuum
  • spectrum — the full range of electromagnetic waves ordered by wavelength/frequency, from radio to gamma rays
  • speed of light — c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s, the speed of every electromagnetic wave in a vacuum

Revise Your Model

Return to your guessed order. Correct it using the spectrum. Add an arrow for "increasing frequency" and one for "increasing wavelength," pointing opposite ways.

Return to the Phenomenon

Back to the room full of EM waves: in one sentence, explain what makes a radio wave different from an X-ray, even though both travel at c.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. Use the EM spectrum.
    (a) List these in order of increasing frequency: visible light, radio, X-ray, infrared. (b) A microwave and a gamma ray both travel through a vacuum. Which is faster? Explain. (c) Ultraviolet light has a higher frequency than visible light. Compare their wavelengths.
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
