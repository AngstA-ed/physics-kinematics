Lesson 02 · Unit: Kinematics

Distance and Displacement

When you describe how far something moved, which number is "right" — the path length, or the straight-line gap?

Download teacher guide (DOCX)

Phenomenon

One classmate is going to walk from their seat to the door — but they are going to take the long way around (behind chairs, around tables). We will measure two things: the length of the path they actually walked, and the straight-line gap from their seat to the door. Are those the same number?

Driving Question

When you describe how far something moved, which number is "right" — the path length, or the straight-line gap?

Notice & Wonder

Watch the classmate's walk and write what you notice and what you wonder.

Share with your partner: which number do you think is "the answer" — the path length or the straight-line gap? Why?

Initial Model

Sketch the path on a grid. Label the path length. Then draw a straight arrow from start to end and label its length too.

Investigate

Use the buttons below to add steps to a path on the grid. Then toggle "show distance" and "show displacement" to compare.

Distance (path length): 0 units · Displacement: 0.0 units, —

When are distance and displacement equal? When is displacement zero but distance is not? Build at least one example of each on the grid above to convince your partner.

Make It Make Sense

  1. A jogger runs around a 400 m oval track exactly once. What is the distance? What is the displacement?
  2. Sketch a path on the grid that has distance = 12 units and displacement = 4 units. Then sketch one with distance = 8 units and displacement = 0 units.
  3. Can displacement ever be larger than distance? Explain.
Stuck? Sentence frame

"The distance is ___ but the displacement is ___ because ___."

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • distance — total length of the path traveled (a scalar; always ≥ 0)
  • displacement — straight-line change in position from start to end, with direction (a vector)
  • scalar — a quantity with magnitude only (no direction)

Revise Your Model

Re-sketch the classroom walk path. Label the distance and the displacement using today's vocabulary. Add an arrow that shows the displacement.

Return to the Phenomenon

If we re-do the walk in a perfectly straight line from seat to door, would distance and displacement still differ? Predict in one sentence, then test if there is time.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. A student walks 4 m east, then 3 m north, then 4 m west.
    (a) Total distance? (b) Displacement (magnitude and direction)? (c) One sentence on why your two answers differ.
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
