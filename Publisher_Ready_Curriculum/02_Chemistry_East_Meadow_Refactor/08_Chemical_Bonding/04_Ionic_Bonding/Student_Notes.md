By the end of today's 42 minutes I can:

## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Structure and Function:** The way the ions are __________ at the particle scale (structure) decides how the whole material __________ (function). The cube you see is a scaled-up picture of the repeating cube of ions you cannot see.

**Step 1 — Electron transfer (how the ions form):**

- A **metal** atom has few valence electrons; it __________ them.
- A **nonmetal** atom needs a few more to fill its shell; it __________ them.
- The metal loses negative electrons, so it becomes __________ (a **cation**).
- The nonmetal gains negative electrons, so it becomes __________ (an **anion**).
- Each ion now has a stable, full __________ shell (octet rule).

**Step 2 — The ionic bond (what holds them together):**

> An ionic bond is the __________ attraction between a __________ ion and a __________ ion. Opposite charges __________; like charges __________.

**Step 3 — The ionic lattice (the structure):**

- The ions arrange in a repeating 3-D pattern where each ion is surrounded by __________ of the opposite charge.
- An interior ion in NaCl touches __________ opposite-charged neighbors (up, down, left, right, front, back).
- This balanced, alternating pattern forces the whole crystal into a __________ shape.
- There is **no single molecule** — the formula NaCl gives the __________ of ions (1 Na⁺ : 1 Cl⁻), repeated billions of times.

**Step 4 — Properties explained by the lattice:**

| Property | Cause (in terms of the lattice) |
|---|---|
| High melting point | Every ion is held by ____ strong attractions reaching across the whole crystal, so breaking them apart needs lots of __________. |
| Conducts only when dissolved or melted | In the solid, ions are __________ (cannot move). Dissolved or melted, ions are __________ to move and carry current. |
| Brittle (shatters along flat faces) | A strike slides __________ charges next to each other; they __________ and the crystal splits along a flat plane. |

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** Show how an ionic bond forms in **magnesium oxide, MgO**.

**Step 1 — Identify the metal and the nonmetal:**

Metal: ___________   Nonmetal: ___________

**Step 2 — Count valence electrons (from the Periodic Table group number):**

Mg (Group 2) has ____ valence electrons.   O (Group 16) has ____ valence electrons and needs ____ more to fill its shell.

**Step 3 — Transfer the electrons:**

Magnesium transfers ____ electrons to oxygen.

**Step 4 — Write the ions formed:**

Magnesium loses 2 electrons → Mg____   Oxygen gains 2 electrons → O____

**Step 5 — Form the lattice:**

The Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ ions attract and arrange into an ionic __________, just like NaCl.

**Step 6 — Because / But / So**

MgO has a higher melting point than NaCl
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name the larger charges (2+ and 2− vs. 1+ and 1−); "but" should note that the lattice structure is otherwise the same; "so" should state that stronger attractions need more energy to break, raising the melting point.

**Reminder:** Ionic bonding is *transfer*, not sharing. A metal gives electrons away; a nonmetal takes them. The opposite charges that result are held together by electrostatic attraction in a repeating lattice — and that lattice is the source of every property you observed today.
