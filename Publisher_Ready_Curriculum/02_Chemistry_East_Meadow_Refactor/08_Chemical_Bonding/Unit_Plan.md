## Unit Scope (from East Meadow Scope & Sequence)

This unit moves students from the *language* of compounds to the *structure* and *behavior* that bonding produces. They open with the rule sets that translate any formula into a name and any name into a formula, then learn to read electronegativity to decide whether atoms transfer, share, or pool their electrons. The three bonding models — ionic, covalent (with a first look at organic structures), and metallic — each connect a particle-scale mechanism to observable bulk properties (crystal shape, melting point, conductivity, malleability). The unit then builds the representational toolkit that the new NYSSLS Regents leans on: Lewis dot diagrams, VSEPR molecular geometry, intermolecular forces, and finally bond and molecular polarity — the synthesis that explains why like dissolves like. Throughout, students *explain* properties from structure rather than merely labeling a bond type.

| # | Lesson | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Naming Compounds | One naming system, four rule sets (ionic, covalent prefixes, acids, polyatomic ions) keep substances precise — and safe |
| 02 | Identifying Bond Type & Number | Electronegativity difference (ΔEN) predicts ionic vs. covalent; valence states set how many bonds an atom forms |
| 03 | Writing Formulas from Names | Crossing charges forces the lowest whole-number ratio of ions that makes a neutral compound |
| 04 | Ionic Bonding | Electron transfer builds a repeating lattice of cations and anions — the cube you can see |
| 05 | Covalent Bonding (incl. Organic Structures) | Nonmetals share one, two, or three electron pairs; structural formulas draw bonds as lines |
| 06 | Metallic Bonding | A "sea" of delocalized electrons around fixed cations explains conductivity, malleability, and luster |
| 07 | Lewis Dot Diagrams | Valence electrons as dots make an atom's bonding capacity (and the octet rule) visible |
| 08 | Molecular Geometry (VSEPR) | Electron groups repel and spread apart, setting a molecule's 3-D shape |
| 09 | Intermolecular Forces | Attractions *between* molecules set boiling point, viscosity, and surface tension |
| 10 | Polar & Nonpolar Bonds and Molecules | Unequal sharing makes polar bonds; shape decides whether a molecule has a net dipole — like dissolves like |

## Pacing Calendar

| Day | Lesson | Phenomenon anchor | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 01 Naming Compounds | "Sodium" vs. "sodium chloride"; CO vs. CO₂ vs. CaCO₃ — one letter flips safe to deadly | Restorative Circle (unit opener) + Active Learning |
| 2 | 02 Identifying Bond Type & Number | Salt dissolves in water; oil refuses — same solvent, opposite outcome | Hochman |
| 3 | 03 Writing Formulas from Names | "Aluminum oxide" is always Al₂O₃, never Al₃O₂ — a hidden rule fixes the ratio | BTC |
| 4 | 04 Ionic Bonding | Every grain of salt grows as a cube no machine cut | Active Learning |
| 5 | 05 Covalent Bonding (incl. Organic Structures) | Water bulges over the rim, ice floats, water boils 262° higher than methane | Hochman |
| 6 | 06 Metallic Bonding | Copper wire bends and holds its shape; a salt crystal shatters under the same tap | BTC |
| 7 | 07 Lewis Dot Diagrams | Sodium (one dot) reacts violently; neon (eight dots) does nothing | Active Learning |
| 8 | 08 Molecular Geometry (VSEPR) | CO₂ is straight, H₂O is bent — same three-atom pattern, different shape | Hochman |
| 9 | 09 Intermolecular Forces | A water strider stands on the pond; one drop of soap sinks the paper clip | BTC |
| 10 | 10 Polar & Nonpolar Bonds and Molecules | An M&M's "m" floats free in water but nothing happens in oil | Active Learning |

Double-period days (every other day) let the ionic-crystal build, the metallic-properties stations, and the polarity dissolve/no-dissolve lab run long.

## Strategy Rotation

- **RESTORATIVE CIRCLE**: Lesson 01 (unit-opening circle that surfaces where students already meet chemical names — labels, medicines, road salt).
- **ACTIVE LEARNING**: Lessons 01, 04, 07, 10 (compound-naming card game; build-the-lattice-before-you-name-it; every-student-draws Lewis whiteboards; data-before-vocabulary dissolve stations).
- **HOCHMAN (literacy)**: Lessons 02, 05, 08 (appositive defining ionic/covalent bonds; appositive + Because/But/So on water's anomalies; appositive packing the VSEPR idea into a working sentence).
- **BTC (Building Thinking Classrooms)**: Lessons 03, 06, 09 (visibly random groups at vertical surfaces for crossing-charges thin slices; the metals-vs-salt puzzle; the surface-tension/viscosity thin-slice sequence).

SEL (opening connection + closing reflection), ELL/SPED supports, and OPTIC/CER literacy appear in **every** lesson.
