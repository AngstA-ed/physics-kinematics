Unit: Chemical Bonding — Lesson 05: Covalent Bonding (incl. Organic Structures)

## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. **Water (H₂O).** **Bonds total:** 2 (one single bond from oxygen to each hydrogen). **Structural formula:** H–O–H, drawn **bent** (the two O–H bonds form an angle of about 105°, not a straight line). · *Rubric:* full credit for 2 single bonds AND a bent drawing; partial credit if drawn straight (H–O–H in a line) but with the correct two single bonds. · NYSSLS: HS-PS1-1 (supporting).

2. **Methane (CH₄).** **Bonds on carbon:** 4 (one single bond to each of the four hydrogens). **Structural formula:** carbon in the center with four single lines, one to each H (drawn spread out, roughly in four directions). · *Rubric:* full credit for exactly 4 single bonds on carbon; common error is drawing only 3 or 5 — carbon must have exactly 4. · NYSSLS: HS-PS1-1 (supporting).

3. **Carbon dioxide (CO₂).** **Shared pairs between carbon and EACH oxygen:** 2 (a double bond = two shared pairs = 4 electrons). **Structural formula:** O=C=O, with a double line (two parallel lines) between the carbon and each oxygen. Carbon's bonds total 2 + 2 = 4. · *Rubric:* full credit for double bonds (two lines) to each oxygen AND recognition that carbon still has 4 bonds total; partial credit if single bonds are drawn (the most common error — students forget CO₂ needs double bonds). · NYSSLS: HS-PS1-1 (supporting).

**Investigation reference (for grading Parts 1–3):**

- Part 1: H₂O — central O, 1 stick to each H, 2 bonds on O. CH₄ — central C, 1 stick to each H, 4 bonds on C. NH₃ — central N, 1 stick to each H, 3 bonds on N. CO₂ — central C, 2 sticks to each O, 4 bonds on C. (a) CO₂ needed more than one stick. (b) 2 sticks. (c) Yes — carbon has 4 bonds in both CH₄ (4 singles) and CO₂ (2 doubles = 4).
- Part 2: (a) 6 hydrogens. (b) C₂H₆ (ethane). (c) Carbon forms four bonds and can bond to another carbon, so carbons link into chains.
- Part 3: Single = 1 line, 1 pair, 2 electrons. Double = 2 lines, 2 pairs, 4 electrons. Triple = 3 lines, 3 pairs, 6 electrons.

## Exit Ticket Answer

**(a) CCl₄ (carbon tetrachloride).** Carbon is the central atom with **four single bonds**, one to each chlorine. Structural formula: C in the center, four single lines, one Cl on the end of each line (drawn spread out). Carbon's bonds total 4. · *Rubric:* full credit for exactly 4 single bonds, central carbon, 4 chlorines. Note: this molecule was NOT practiced on the worksheet — it tests transfer of the "carbon makes 4 bonds" rule to a new molecule. Partial credit for correct central atom and bond type but wrong count.

**(b) HCN (hydrogen cyanide, H–C≡N).** Between carbon and nitrogen there are **3 shared pairs** (6 electrons); this is a **triple bond**. (The H–C bond is a single bond, but the question asks only about the C–N connection.) · *Rubric:* full credit for "3 pairs" AND "triple"; partial credit for naming "triple" without the pair count, or vice versa.

**(c) Because/But/So — structure to function.** Sample full-credit responses (any ONE property is acceptable):

- *Surface tension:* "Water has high surface tension **because** its bent structure makes each molecule cling tightly to its neighbors, **but** a flat steel paperclip cannot push through that clinging surface, **so** the denser paperclip floats on top."
- *Floating ice:* "Ice floats **because** water's bent structure forces frozen molecules into an open, spread-out arrangement, **but** the same mass then takes up more space than liquid water, **so** solid ice is less dense and floats."
- *High boiling point:* "Water boils at a high temperature **because** its bent, lopsided structure makes molecules cling strongly to one another, **but** methane molecules do not cling nearly as strongly, **so** it takes far more energy (a higher temperature) to boil water into a gas."

· *Rubric:* full credit requires the "because" to name the molecule's **structure** (bent shape / shared-electron arrangement / clinging molecules), and the "so" to name the resulting **property** (function). Any equivalent, logically connected response earns full credit. · NYSSLS: HS-PS1-1 (supporting).

*Tolerance/rubric summary:* (a) 1 point — correct CCl₄ structure with 4 single bonds. (b) 1 point — "3 pairs" and "triple" (½ for one of the two). (c) 1 point — Because/But/So that links structure to a property; full credit only when "because" names structure and "so" names the property. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-1 (supporting).

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine response. Look for:

- A specific everyday example the student now believes they could explain through bonding — "I think soap bubbles hold their shape because of how the molecules connect" — rather than a generic "chemistry is cool."
- Naming a peer or a moment from the model-kit build as the thing that helped — this signals the collaborative Explore structure landed.
- Any response that explicitly links *structure* to *behavior* (Structure and Function) shows the strongest transfer of the day's CCC.

If a student writes "nothing — I still don't get it," have a brief, low-stakes one-on-one before the next lesson: re-run the CO₂ build (two sticks per oxygen) by hand, since the single-vs-double distinction is the most common sticking point and the foundation for everything in the organic unit ahead.
