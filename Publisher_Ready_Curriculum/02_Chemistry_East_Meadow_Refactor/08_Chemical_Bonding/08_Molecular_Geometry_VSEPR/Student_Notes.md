## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Structure and Function:** The arrangement of __________ groups around the central atom (the structure) determines the molecule's __________ (the function-defining feature). Two molecules with the same number of __________ can have different shapes if their central atoms have different numbers of __________ pairs.

**The VSEPR rule:**

Electron groups around a central atom __________ each other and spread as far __________ as possible. To find the shape:

1. Draw the Lewis structure and find the __________ atom.
2. Count the atoms __________ to the central atom.
3. Count the __________ pairs on the central atom.
4. Add them together to get the total number of __________ groups.
5. Match the count (and lone-pair situation) to the shape.

**Important rule about double and triple bonds:** A double or triple bond counts as __________ electron group, not two or three — all its electrons point in one direction.

**The shape chart:**

| Electron groups | Lone pairs on center | Shape name | Bond angle | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0 | linear | ______° | CO₂ |
| 3 | 0 | trigonal planar | ______° | BF₃ |
| 4 | 0 | tetrahedral | ______° | CH₄ |
| 4 | 1 | trigonal pyramidal | ≈107° | NH₃ |
| 4 | 2 | bent | ≈______° | H₂O |

**Why lone pairs matter:** A lone pair takes up space and __________ on the bonded atoms, often pushing them closer together and __________ the molecule. Water and ammonia are bent and pyramidal *because of* their lone pairs.

**The Hochman appositive definition:**

> VSEPR — _________________________ — predicts a molecule's shape from the number of electron groups around its central atom.

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A student needs to predict the molecular geometry of water, H₂O.

**Step 1 — Identify the central atom:**

The central atom in H₂O is ___________.

**Step 2 — Count the bonded atoms:**

Oxygen is bonded to ___ hydrogen atoms.

**Step 3 — Count the lone pairs on the central atom:**

Oxygen has ___ lone pairs.

**Step 4 — Total the electron groups:**

___ bonded atoms + ___ lone pairs = ___ electron groups.

**Step 5 — Name the shape:**

4 electron groups would be tetrahedral if all were bonds — but ___ of them are lone pairs, so the shape we *name* (based on where the atoms are) is ___________, with a bond angle of about ___°.

**Step 6 — Appositive sentence:**

The molecular geometry of water — ___________ — is determined by two bonds and two lone pairs on the oxygen.

**Step 7 — Because / But / So**

Water is bent instead of linear
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name the lone pairs on oxygen; "but" should compare to CO₂'s carbon (no lone pairs); "so" should state the resulting shapes (bent vs. linear).

## Summary

Complete the appositive sentence:

Complete the Because / But / So sentence:

> Carbon dioxide is linear but water is bent

**Reminder:** Always count BOTH bonded atoms AND lone pairs on the central atom. Lone pairs are invisible in the shape name, but they still push the bonded atoms aside — so a molecule with lone pairs is almost never a straight line or a flat triangle.
