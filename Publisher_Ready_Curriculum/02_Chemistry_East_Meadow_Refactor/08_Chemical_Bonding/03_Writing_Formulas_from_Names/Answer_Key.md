# Writing Formulas from Names — Answer Key

## Cover

Unit: Chemical Bonding — Lesson 03: Writing Formulas from Names
East Meadow Schools × Valley Stream Central High School District
NYSSLS tag: HS-PS1-2 (skill bridge — formula as the neutral ratio of ions; no standalone content PE)

## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. Magnesium chloride (Mg²⁺ and Cl⁻):**

- Cation charge: +2  Anion charge: −1
- Crossing: magnesium's 2 → chloride's subscript; chloride's 1 → magnesium's subscript → MgCl₂
- **Formula: MgCl₂**

Charge check: total positive = 1 × (+2) = +2; total negative = 2 × (−1) = −2; they cancel. The 2 and 1 share no common factor, so no reduction is needed.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct formula (MgCl₂); 1 point for showing the charges and the crossed subscripts (the second chloride is the key — a student who writes MgCl missed that one chloride only cancels +1 of magnesium's +2). NYSSLS: HS-PS1-2.

**2. Aluminum oxide (Al³⁺ and O²⁻):**

- Cation charge: +3  Anion charge: −2
- Crossing: aluminum's 3 → oxygen's subscript; oxygen's 2 → aluminum's subscript → Al₂O₃
- **Formula: Al₂O₃**

Charge check: total positive = 2 × (+3) = +6; total negative = 3 × (−2) = −6; they cancel. The 2 and 3 share no common factor, so the formula is already at its lowest ratio.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct formula (Al₂O₃); 1 point for showing the crossed charges and verifying the +6/−6 balance. A student who writes AlO has not balanced the charges (net +1 left over) — note the error and redirect to the charge total. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-2.

**3. Calcium nitrate (Ca²⁺ and NO₃⁻):**

- Cation charge: +2  Anion charge: −1
- Crossing: calcium's 2 → nitrate's subscript; nitrate's 1 → calcium's subscript. Because the nitrate subscript (2) is greater than 1, the polyatomic ion is wrapped in parentheses.
- **Formula: Ca(NO₃)₂**

Charge check: total positive = 1 × (+2) = +2; total negative = 2 × (−1) = −2; they cancel.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct formula including parentheses (Ca(NO₃)₂); 1 point for keeping the nitrate ion intact and showing the charge balance. A student who writes CaNO₃₂ or CaN₂O₆ has broken the polyatomic ion apart — flag the parentheses rule. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-2.

## Exit Ticket Answer

**(a) Magnesium chloride:**

- Mg charge: +2  Cl charge: −1
- **Formula: MgCl₂**

Crossing magnesium's 2 onto chloride and chloride's 1 onto magnesium gives MgCl₂. Charge check: 1 × (+2) = +2 and 2 × (−1) = −2 cancel. (Note: this compound appears on the worksheet investigation as well, so use it here as the accessible first item; the discriminating item is part b.)

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct formula (MgCl₂); 1 point for showing both charges and the crossed subscripts. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-2.

**(b) Aluminum sulfate (Al³⁺ and SO₄²⁻):**

- Al charge: +3  Sulfate charge: −2
- Crossing: aluminum's 3 → sulfate's subscript (3); sulfate's 2 → aluminum's subscript (2). The sulfate subscript is greater than 1, so parentheses are required.
- **Formula: Al₂(SO₄)₃**

Charge check: total positive = 2 × (+3) = +6; total negative = 3 × (−2) = −6; they cancel. The 2 and 3 share no common factor, so no reduction. This compound is **not** on the worksheet practice tables, so students must apply the cross-and-parentheses procedure to a fresh case rather than recall a memorized formula.

*Rubric:* 2 points — 1 point for the correct crossed subscripts (Al₂ and three sulfates) and the charge balance; 1 point for correctly placing parentheses around SO₄ so the subscript multiplies the whole ion. Common errors: Al₂SO₄₃ or Al₂S₃O₁₂ (parentheses omitted/ion broken apart) → award the first point only and flag the parentheses rule; AlSO₄ (charges not crossed) → no points, redirect to the crossing step. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-2.

**(c) Why the subscripts are not arbitrary:**

*Full-credit model answer:* The subscripts are the lowest whole-number ratio of ions that makes the total positive charge exactly cancel the total negative charge, so the compound is electrically neutral — change the ratio and the charges would no longer balance.

*Acceptable variations:* Any response stating that the subscripts are set so the positive and negative charges cancel (neutral compound) earns full credit. Responses that mention "the ratio of atoms is fixed" without connecting it to charge balance receive partial credit.

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for identifying that the subscripts make the charges cancel / the compound neutral; 1 point for noting that this is the *lowest whole-number* ratio (or equivalently, that the formula is forced, not chosen). NYSSLS: HS-PS1-2.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any mention of a specific moment on a group's vertical surface — catching a missing parenthesis, realizing Ca₂O₂ had to reduce to CaO — that signals the BTC board work made the thinking visible and shareable.
- Responses that name the charge-balance idea ("I finally got that the charges have to cancel") show the conceptual core landed, not just the mechanical trick.
- Students who name a partner who helped them cross the charges or catch an error are signaling that the visibly-random-group structure supported their learning — note this as evidence the BTC routine is working.

If a student's Exit Ticket shows the charges crossed but the formula **not reduced** (e.g., leaving Ca₂O₂ on a different item) or **parentheses omitted** on a polyatomic subscript > 1, those are the two dominant procedural errors — a brief check-in at the start of the next lesson on "reduce" and "parentheses" should be sufficient. If the ion charges themselves are wrong, the student needs practice locating charges on the Periodic Table and Table E before the next lesson builds on formula-writing.
