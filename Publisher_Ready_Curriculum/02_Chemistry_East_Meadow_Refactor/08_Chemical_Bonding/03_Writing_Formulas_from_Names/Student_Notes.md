## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Scale, Proportion, and Quantity:** An ionic compound must be electrically __________. That means the total __________ charge has to exactly cancel the total __________ charge. The subscripts in a formula are whatever it takes to make those charges balance — so a formula is really a statement of the __________ of ions.

**Crossing charges — how it works:**

1. Write the cation with its __________ and the anion with its __________.
2. Take the *number* of the cation's charge and write it as the __________ of the anion.
3. Take the *number* of the anion's charge and write it as the __________ of the cation.
4. __________ the subscripts to the lowest whole-number ratio (divide out any common factor).

> Each ion's charge becomes the **other** ion's subscript. That is why it is called *crossing*.

**Checking neutrality** (always do this last):

> total positive charge = (cation charge) × (cation subscript)
> total negative charge = (anion charge) × (anion subscript)
> The formula is correct when these two totals are __________.

**Polyatomic ions and parentheses:**

A polyatomic ion is a __________ of atoms that carries one overall charge and stays together as a unit (for example, nitrate NO₃⁻ and sulfate SO₄²⁻). When a polyatomic ion needs a subscript __________ than 1, wrap it in __________ so the subscript multiplies the whole group: Ca(NO₃)₂ means ___ calcium, ___ nitrogen, and ___ oxygen atoms.

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** Write the formula for **aluminum oxide** from the ions Al³⁺ and O²⁻.

**Step 1 — Write each ion with its charge:**

Cation: Al __________   Anion: O __________

**Step 2 — Cross the charges (charge number → other ion's subscript):**

Aluminum's 3 → oxygen's subscript = ___   Oxygen's 2 → aluminum's subscript = ___

Crossed formula: __________

**Step 3 — Reduce if possible:**

Do 2 and 3 share a common factor? __________  →  Final formula: __________

**Step 4 — Check neutrality:**

total positive = ___ × (+3) = ___   total negative = ___ × (−2) = ___   Equal? ___

**Step 5 — Because / But / So**

Aluminum oxide must be Al₂O₃, not AlO,
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should state each ion's charge; "but" should explain what one of each would leave unbalanced; "so" should give the correct lowest-ratio formula.
