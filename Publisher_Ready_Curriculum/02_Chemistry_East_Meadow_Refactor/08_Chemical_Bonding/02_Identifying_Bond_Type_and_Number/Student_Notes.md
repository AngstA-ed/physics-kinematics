## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Patterns:** Bond type is not random. It follows a predictable pattern set by the __________ of the two elements (metal or nonmetal) and the __________ in their electronegativities. The same rule predicts the bond for any element __________ you are given.

**The three bond types:**

| Bond type | Element types | What the valence electrons do | ΔEN clue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ionic | metal + nonmetal | one atom __________ electrons to the other | large (≥ 1.7) |
| Covalent | nonmetal + nonmetal | the atoms __________ electrons | small (< 1.7) |
| Metallic | metal + metal | electrons pool into a shared __________ | (check element type, not ΔEN) |

**How to predict a bond — the two clues:**

1. Identify each element as a __________ or a __________ on the Periodic Table.
2. Look up the __________ of each element from the Reference Tables.
3. Subtract the smaller from the larger to get __________ (the electronegativity difference).
4. Place ΔEN on the number line **and** check the element types to choose the bond.

**The number line (fill from the figure):**

- ΔEN from 0 to 0.4 → __________ covalent (even sharing)
- ΔEN from 0.4 to 1.7 → __________ covalent (uneven sharing)
- ΔEN of 1.7 or more (metal + nonmetal) → __________
- Metal + metal (any ΔEN) → __________

**The Hochman appositive definition:**

> Electronegativity — _________________________ — determines whether a bond is __________ or __________.

**The careful case — why ΔEN = 0 is not always the same bond:**

O₂ has ΔEN = 0 and Cu–Cu has ΔEN = 0. They are different bonds because __________. O₂ is two __________ (covalent); Cu–Cu is two __________ (metallic).

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A student needs to predict the bond in **calcium chloride, CaCl₂.** Electronegativity values: Ca = 1.0, Cl = 3.0.

**Step 1 — Identify the element types:**

Ca is a ___________ ; Cl is a ___________ .

**Step 2 — Look up each electronegativity from the Reference Tables:**

Ca = _______  Cl = _______

**Step 3 — Calculate the electronegativity difference (larger − smaller):**

ΔEN = _______ − _______ = _______

**Step 4 — Place ΔEN on the number line and check element types:**

ΔEN = _______ falls in the __________ band. The elements are a __________ + a __________, which confirms the call.

**Step 5 — State the predicted bond type:**

The bond in CaCl₂ is __________ .

**Step 6 — Appositive sentence:**

The bond in calcium chloride — an __________ bond formed because ΔEN = ___ — holds the compound together as charged ions.

**Step 7 — Because / But / So:**

CaCl₂ is held together by ionic bonds
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name the large ΔEN and the metal + nonmetal pair; "but" should contrast with a covalent case; "so" should state the bond type and one consequence (e.g., it dissolves in water).

Complete the appositive sentence:

Complete the Because / But / So sentence:

> Salt dissolves in water but oil does not

**Reminder:** Predicting a bond takes **two** clues working together — the **element types** (metal vs. nonmetal) and the **electronegativity difference (ΔEN)**. A ΔEN of 0 is covalent between two nonmetals but metallic between two metals — always check the element type.

**Key reference (from the 2025 NYS Reference Tables):**
Electronegativity values: Periodic Table (each element's value is listed; fluorine is highest at 4.0).
Bond-type cutoffs: ΔEN < 0.4 nonpolar covalent · 0.4–1.7 polar covalent · ≥ 1.7 ionic · metal + metal → metallic.
