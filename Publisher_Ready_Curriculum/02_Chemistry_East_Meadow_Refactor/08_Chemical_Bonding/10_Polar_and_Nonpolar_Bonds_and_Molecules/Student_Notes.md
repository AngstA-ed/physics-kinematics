## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Structure and Function:** A molecule's __________ (which atoms, how electronegative, and its shape) determines its __________ — what it mixes with and dissolves in. The rule that links them is **"__________ dissolves __________"**: polar substances dissolve in polar solvents, nonpolar in nonpolar.

**Step 1 — Bond polarity (is the BOND polar?):**

A covalent bond is **polar** when the two atoms share the bonding electrons __________ (unequally / equally). This happens when one atom has a higher __________, so it pulls the shared electrons closer.

- Look up each atom's electronegativity (Reference Tables).
- Find the __________ (bigger value − smaller value).
- Difference about **0.5 or more** → __________ bond. The more electronegative atom gets a partial **negative** charge (δ⁻); the other gets a partial **positive** charge (δ⁺).
- Difference about **zero** (identical atoms, like O₂) → __________ bond. Electrons shared evenly.

> The δ (delta) means **partial** — no electron is transferred. The shared electrons just spend more __________ near one atom.

**Step 2 — Molecular polarity (is the whole MOLECULE polar?):**

A molecule can have polar bonds and *still* be nonpolar — it depends on the __________.

| Molecule | Bonds | Shape | Bond dipoles… | Whole molecule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CO₂ | polar (C=O) | linear | __________ (cancel?) | __________ |
| H₂O | polar (O–H) | bent | __________ (cancel?) | __________ |

- **Symmetric** shape (linear CO₂, tetrahedral CCl₄): equal bond dipoles point in __________ directions and __________. Net dipole = 0 → **nonpolar molecule**.
- **Asymmetric** shape (bent H₂O): bond dipoles __________ up to one net dipole → **polar molecule** (it has a + end and a − end).

- H₂O is a __________ molecule because its __________ shape lets the dipoles add to a net dipole.

**Reminder — two questions, in order:**
1. Is the **bond** polar? → check the **electronegativity difference**.
2. Is the **molecule** polar? → check the **shape**. Symmetric shapes can cancel polar bonds.

**Key reference (from the 2025 NYS Reference Tables):**
Electronegativity values: Periodic Table / Reference Table S.
Rule of thumb: difference ≥ 0.5 → polar bond; ≈ 0 → nonpolar bond. "Like dissolves like."
