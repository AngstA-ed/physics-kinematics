## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Energy and Matter:** Energy is __________, not destroyed. When a hot object meets a cooler one in a closed container, energy flows from __________ to __________ until they reach the same temperature. The heat lost by the hot object __________ the heat gained by the cool one: q_lost = −q_gained.

**The three heat equations:**

| Situation | Equation | What stays the same |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature is **changing** (no phase change) | q = m × c × ΔT | the phase (still solid, liquid, or gas) |
| **Melting or freezing** at the melting point | q = m × H_f | the __________ (stays at the melting point) |
| **Boiling or condensing** at the boiling point | q = m × H_v | the __________ (stays at the boiling point) |

**How to choose the equation:** First ask — *is the temperature changing?*
- If **yes** → use q = __________  (you will have a ΔT).
- If **no** (a phase change at constant temperature) → melting? use q = __________ · boiling? use q = __________ (no ΔT, no *c*).

**Computing ΔT:**  ΔT = T___________ − T___________   (always **final minus initial**)

m = ______ g    c = ______ J/g·°C    ΔT = ______ °C

**Step 5 — Sign:** The water got __________ (warmer / cooler), so energy flowed __________ (in / out), so q is __________ (positive / negative).

**Step 6 — Appositive sentence:**

The specific heat capacity of water — ___ J/g·°C — is the value of c that made q = ___________ J for this sample.
