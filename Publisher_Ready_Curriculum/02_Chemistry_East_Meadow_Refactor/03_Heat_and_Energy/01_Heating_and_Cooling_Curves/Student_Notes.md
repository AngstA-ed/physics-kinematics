## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Energy and Matter:** Energy that flows into a sample is never lost — it is always __________ for. It either makes the particles move faster (raising __________ energy, which we read as a rising __________) or it pulls the particles apart during a phase change (raising __________ energy, with no change in temperature).

**Reading a heating curve:**

- A **sloped** segment (line going up or down) → the temperature is __________ → __________ energy is changing.
- A **flat** segment (plateau) → the temperature is __________ → __________ energy is changing → a __________ change is happening.

**The five segments of the heating curve of water (ice → steam):**

|---|---|---|---|

| C (0–100 °C, sloped) | rising | liquid | |

| E (above 100 °C, sloped) | rising | gas | |

**The key rule:**

> Heat added on a **slope** → faster particles → **temperature** changes (kinetic energy).
> Heat added on a **plateau** → particles pulled apart → **phase** changes (potential energy).

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A heating curve of water shows a flat plateau at 100 °C (segment D). The burner adds energy at a constant rate the entire time the plateau lasts. Explain what is happening to the energy and to the water.

**Step 1 — Is the temperature changing?**

The plateau is __________ (flat / sloped), so the temperature is __________ (changing / constant).

**Step 2 — What does constant temperature tell you about kinetic energy?**

Temperature measures the average __________ energy. Since the temperature is constant, the average kinetic energy is __________.

**Step 3 — If the kinetic energy is not changing, where is the added energy going?**

The added energy is raising the __________ energy — it is pulling the liquid molecules apart to form __________ (a gas).

**Step 4 — Name the phase change:**

Segment D is __________ (the phase change from __________ to __________).

**Step 5 — Because / But / So**

The thermometer stays at 100 °C while water boils
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should say the energy goes into potential energy / the phase change; "but" should say temperature only measures kinetic energy; "so" should say the temperature stays constant even though heat keeps flowing in.

**Reminder:**

- **Sloped** segment = temperature changing = **kinetic** energy changing (no phase change).
- **Flat** plateau = temperature constant = **potential** energy changing = **phase change**.
- Energy is always conserved: every joule added either speeds particles up (kinetic) or pulls them apart (potential).

**Key reference:**
- Pure water: melting/freezing point = 0 °C; boiling/condensation point = 100 °C.
- Temperature is a measure of the average **kinetic** energy of particles.
