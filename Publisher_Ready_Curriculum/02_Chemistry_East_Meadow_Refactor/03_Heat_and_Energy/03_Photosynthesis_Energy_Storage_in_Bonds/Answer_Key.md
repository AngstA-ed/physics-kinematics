# Photosynthesis — Energy Storage in Bonds — Answer Key

## Cover

Unit: Heat and Energy — Lesson 03: Photosynthesis — Energy Storage in Bonds
East Meadow Schools × Valley Stream Central High School District

## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. The dried leaf bursts into flame and releases heat and light.**

- **Where the energy came from originally:** the Sun. During photosynthesis, the plant used the Sun's light energy to store chemical potential energy in the bonds of the sugar that built the leaf.
- **Created or released:** *released* from storage, not created. The energy was already in the bonds; burning lets it back out as heat and light.
- **Up or down on the diagram:** *down* — burning runs the reaction in reverse (products → reactants), moving from the high energy level to the low energy level and releasing the stored energy.

*Rubric:* 1 point for identifying the Sun (via photosynthesis) as the original source; 1 point for "released, not created" AND "down" on the diagram. A student who answers "the fire makes the energy" has the core misconception — provide written feedback: energy is never created; it was stored by the Sun and is only being released. NYSSLS: HS-LS1-5.

**2. You eat a salad and later have energy to walk to class.**

- **What the plant stored:** chemical potential energy — sugar (and other high-energy molecules) built using sunlight while the lettuce sat in the field.
- **Original source of that energy:** the Sun (light energy captured by photosynthesis).
- **Forward or reverse:** in *reverse* — your body runs cellular respiration, which is photosynthesis backwards (C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O), releasing the stored energy for you to use.

*Rubric:* 1 point for naming sunlight/photosynthesis as the source of the stored energy; 1 point for recognizing that the body releases it by running the reaction in reverse (respiration). Accept "burns the sugar" as an informal description of respiration. NYSSLS: HS-LS1-5.

**3. Same atoms, two sides of the equation.**

- **Which side stores more chemical potential energy:** the **products** (C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂). The sugar is the fuel; it holds more energy than CO₂ + H₂O.
- **What makes one side store more if the atoms are identical:** the atoms are arranged into *different bonds*. Different bonds store different amounts of energy; the product bonds (in sugar and O₂) hold more chemical potential energy than the reactant bonds.
- **Where the extra energy came from:** the Sun's light energy, added during photosynthesis and stored in the new product bonds.

*Rubric:* 1 point for correctly choosing the products as the higher-energy side; 1 point for explaining that the *bonds* differ (not the atoms) AND naming light/the Sun as the source of the extra energy. A student who says "the products have more atoms" has the count-vs-energy confusion — note that the atom counts are equal (the equation balances); it is the bonds that differ. NYSSLS: HS-LS1-5.

## Exit Ticket Answer

**(a) Which side stores more chemical potential energy?**

The **products** (C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂) store more chemical potential energy than the reactants (CO₂ + H₂O). *How we know:* the sugar is a fuel — it releases energy when burned or respired — so it must have held that energy beforehand; on the energy diagram the products sit on the higher level.

*Rubric:* 1 point for choosing the products; 1 point for a correct one-sentence justification (the products are the fuel / they release energy when burned / they sit higher on the diagram because light energy was added and stored). Choosing the reactants is incorrect — the energy-flow logic is reversed. NYSSLS: HS-LS1-5.

**(b) What supplies the energy that photosynthesis stores?**

**Light energy** supplies it, and it ultimately comes from the **Sun**. The plant absorbs sunlight and stores that energy in the chemical bonds of the sugar it builds.

*Rubric:* 1 point for "light energy / sunlight," with full credit for naming the Sun as the ultimate source. A response of "the soil" or "nutrients" is incorrect for the *energy* source (soil supplies atoms, not the energy) — provide feedback distinguishing matter source from energy source. NYSSLS: HS-LS1-5.

**(c) Trace the marshmallow's heat back to its source.**

*Full-credit model answer:* The campfire's heat is chemical potential energy released from the bonds of the wood/marshmallow → that energy was stored in the bonds when the plant (sugarcane for the marshmallow's sugar, or the tree for the wood) ran photosynthesis → photosynthesis used light energy → that light energy came from the **Sun**. So the marshmallow's heat is stored sunlight being released, with energy conserved at every step.

*Acceptable variations:* Any chain that ends at the Sun and correctly identifies the middle steps — energy stored in bonds by photosynthesis, released by burning — earns full credit. The minimum acceptable chain is: heat ← stored chemical energy in bonds ← photosynthesis ← light ← Sun.

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for tracing the heat to stored chemical (bond) energy from photosynthesis; 1 point for reaching the Sun/light as the original source. Partial credit (1 point) for stopping at "the wood" or "the plant" without reaching sunlight. NYSSLS: HS-LS1-5.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any statement connecting their own energy (food, calories, fuel) back to sunlight — students who say "all my energy is basically stored sunlight" are showing the central transfer of the lesson landed.
- Responses that name a partner or a moment at the vertical-surface boards as the thing that shifted their thinking — this signals the BTC group structure supported their reasoning; note these students as evidence the random-groups + vertical-surfaces routine is working.
- Surprise specifically about *conservation* — "I thought fire made energy, but it was already there" — is the strongest evidence the conservation-of-energy concept took hold.

If a student's Exit Ticket places the reactants (CO₂ + H₂O) as the higher-energy side, the energy-flow logic is reversed — a 30-second check at the start of the next lesson (ask: "which one burns and releases heat? that's the one with more stored energy") should resolve it before the next lesson builds on stored chemical energy.
