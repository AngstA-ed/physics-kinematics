## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

The shared solution: **40.0 g of NaOH** dissolved to make **0.500 L of solution**; total solution mass **520 g**; GFM of NaOH = 40.0 g/mol.

**1. Molarity:**

> moles of NaOH = 40.0 g ÷ 40.0 g/mol = 1.00 mol
> Molarity = 1.00 mol ÷ 0.500 L = **2.00 mol/L (2.00 M)**

Step-by-step: convert the mass of solute to moles using the gram formula mass (40.0 g ÷ 40.0 g/mol = 1.00 mol), then divide moles by liters of solution (1.00 ÷ 0.500 = 2.00). The unit is mol/L.

*Rubric:* 1 point for converting 40.0 g to 1.00 mol with GFM; 1 point for the correct molarity (2.00 M) with the mol/L unit. A student who divides 40.0 g ÷ 0.500 L and writes "80 M" skipped the grams-to-moles step — provide written feedback: "molarity needs *moles* on top; 40.0 g of NaOH is 1.00 mol." NYSSLS: HS-PS1-10.

**2. Percent by mass:**

> % by mass = (40.0 g ÷ 520 g) × 100 = **7.69 %**

Step-by-step: divide the mass of solute by the mass of the whole solution (40.0 ÷ 520 = 0.0769), then multiply by 100. Accept 7.7 % or 7.69 %.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct fraction (40.0 ÷ 520); 1 point for multiplying by 100 to get 7.69 %. A common error is dividing by the volume (0.500 L) or by the solvent mass instead of the total solution mass — note the denominator error if it appears. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-10.

**3. Parts per million:**

> ppm = (40.0 g ÷ 520 g) × 1,000,000 = **76,900 ppm**

Step-by-step: same fraction as percent by mass (40.0 ÷ 520 = 0.0769), but multiplied by 1,000,000 instead of 100. 0.0769 × 1,000,000 = 76,900 ppm (accept 76,923 or 76,900). Note this equals 7.69 % × 10,000 — confirming percent and ppm describe the same solution.

*Rubric:* 1 point for using the same solute/solution mass fraction; 1 point for multiplying by 1,000,000 to get ≈ 76,900 ppm. A student who writes the same number as the percent answer forgot to change the scale factor from 100 to 1,000,000. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-10.

**4. Dilution:**

> M₁V₁ = M₂V₂ → (2.00 M)(50.0 mL) = M₂(200. mL)
> M₂ = (2.00 × 50.0) ÷ 200. = 100 ÷ 200. = **0.500 M**

Step-by-step: multiply the starting molarity by the starting volume (2.00 × 50.0 = 100), then divide by the final volume (100 ÷ 200. = 0.500). The volume quadrupled, so the concentration dropped to one-fourth — adding water spreads the same moles of solute through more solution. Units of volume need only match on both sides (mL with mL); no conversion to liters is required.

*Rubric:* 1 point for correct setup of M₁V₁ = M₂V₂ with values in the right slots; 1 point for the correct answer (0.500 M). A student who gets 8.00 M (multiplying instead of dividing, or inverting the volumes) inverted the equation — redirect to "diluting must *lower* the concentration." NYSSLS: HS-PS1-10.

## Exit Ticket Answer

**(a) Molarity of the KCl solution:**

> Molarity = 0.25 mol ÷ 0.50 L = **0.50 mol/L (0.50 M)**

The KCl is already given in moles (0.25 mol), so no GFM conversion is needed here — divide moles by liters directly: 0.25 ÷ 0.50 = 0.50. The unit is mol/L. This differs from the Make-It-Make-Sense problem, where the solute was given in grams and required a GFM step first.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct division (0.25 ÷ 0.50); 1 point for the answer 0.50 M with the mol/L unit. A student who writes 2.0 M divided liters by moles (inverted the ratio) — redirect to "moles of solute on top." NYSSLS: HS-PS1-10.

**(b) Percent by mass of sugar:**

> % by mass = (15 g ÷ 300 g) × 100 = **5.0 %**

Divide the mass of solute (15 g) by the mass of the whole solution (300 g) to get 0.050, then multiply by 100 to get 5.0 %.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct fraction (15 ÷ 300); 1 point for multiplying by 100 to get 5.0 %. A student who multiplies by 1,000,000 computed ppm (50,000 ppm) instead of percent — note the scale-factor confusion. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-10.

**(c) Dilution of HCl:**

> M₁V₁ = M₂V₂ → (6.0 M)(100. mL) = M₂(300. mL)
> M₂ = (6.0 × 100.) ÷ 300. = 600 ÷ 300. = **2.0 M**

Multiply the starting molarity by the starting volume (6.0 × 100. = 600), then divide by the final volume (600 ÷ 300. = 2.0). The volume tripled, so the concentration dropped to one-third.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct M₁V₁ = M₂V₂ setup; 1 point for the answer 2.0 M. A student who gets 18 M multiplied by the volume ratio instead of dividing — redirect to "dilution lowers concentration." NYSSLS: HS-PS1-10.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any mention of the dye-cup demo or the "5 ppm of lead" phenomenon and a connection to concentration being a ratio — students who tie "how much depends on what it's mixed into" to the solute-over-solution idea are showing the Scale, Proportion, and Quantity CCC has landed.
- Responses that use the appositive structure ("molarity — moles of solute per liter — is found by...") are demonstrating the Hochman writing move from Phase 3 and Phase 4. Note these students as having integrated the vocabulary into their active sentence structure.
- A real-life example of "how much depends on what it's in" (hot sauce, medicine dose, perfume, salt in food) signals transfer of the concentration concept beyond the chemistry problem set.

If a student's Exit Ticket shows the percent-by-mass fraction correct but multiplied by the wrong scale factor (100 vs. 1,000,000), the error is conceptual — they have not separated percent from ppm — and a quick reteach of "× 100 for percent, × 1,000,000 for ppm" should fix it. If a student divides grams by liters for molarity without converting to moles, reteach the grams-to-moles (GFM) step from Lesson 06 before the molarity lab, since that conversion is the foundation of the entire lab.
