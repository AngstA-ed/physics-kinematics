## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A solution is made by dissolving **40.0 g of NaOH** in water to make **0.500 L of solution**. The total mass of the solution is **520 g**. (GFM of NaOH = 40.0 g/mol.)

**Step 1 — Convert grams of solute to moles (needed for molarity):**

moles of NaOH = 40.0 g ÷ 40.0 g/mol = ___________ mol

**Step 2 — Molarity = moles of solute ÷ liters of solution:**

Molarity = ___ mol ÷ 0.500 L = ___________ mol/L = ___________ M

**Step 3 — Percent by mass = (mass of solute ÷ mass of solution) × 100:**

% by mass = (40.0 g ÷ 520 g) × 100 = ___________ %

**Step 4 — Parts per million = (mass of solute ÷ mass of solution) × 1,000,000:**

ppm = (40.0 g ÷ 520 g) × 1,000,000 = ___________ ppm

**Step 5 — Dilution.** Take 50.0 mL of this 2.00 M solution and dilute to 200. mL:

M₁V₁ = M₂V₂ → (2.00 M)(50.0 mL) = M₂(200. mL) → M₂ = ___________ M

**Step 6 — Appositive sentence:**

The molarity of the sodium hydroxide solution — ___ M — is found by dividing ___ mol of NaOH by ___ L of solution.

**Step 7 — Because / But / So**

Adding water to a solution lowers its molarity
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should explain what dilution does to the solute; "but" should note what does (or does not) change; "so" should state what happens to the concentration.

**Reminder:** Concentration is always **solute over solution**. Molarity puts moles on top and liters on the bottom; percent by mass and ppm put mass of solute on top and mass of solution on the bottom. To get molarity you must convert grams to moles first — always.

Concentration formulas: Reference Table T (molarity, percent by mass, parts per million)
Conversions: 1 ppm = 1,000 ppb; 1 ppm = 1 mg/L in dilute water solutions; ppm = percent × 10,000
