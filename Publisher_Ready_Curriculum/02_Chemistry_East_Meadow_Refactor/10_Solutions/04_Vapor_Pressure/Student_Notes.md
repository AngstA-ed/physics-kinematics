## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Cause and Effect:** A liquid boils when its __________ pressure equals the surrounding __________ pressure. So if you change the pressure pushing down on the surface, you change the __________ at which the liquid boils.

**Equilibrium vapor pressure — how it forms:**

In a closed container, particles leave the liquid surface to become __________, and vapor particles fall back into the liquid (__________). When these two rates are __________, the pressure of the vapor stops changing. That steady pressure is the **equilibrium vapor pressure** — and it is exactly what every curve on Reference Table H plots.

Equilibrium vapor pressure depends only on the __________ and the __________. It does **not** depend on how much liquid you have.

**Boiling point and normal boiling point:**

- A liquid **boils** at the temperature where its vapor pressure equals the __________ pressure.
- The **normal boiling point** is this temperature when the external pressure is exactly __________ kPa.
- On Reference Table H, the normal boiling point is where each curve crosses the __________ kPa line.

**Intermolecular forces — the cause behind the curve:**

- **Strong** IMF (like hydrogen bonding in water) → particles escape __________ → __________ vapor pressure → __________ boiling point.
- **Weak** IMF (like in propanone) → particles escape __________ → __________ vapor pressure → __________ boiling point.

**How to read a normal boiling point off the curve (use this every time):**

1. Start at __________ kPa on the pressure (y) axis.
2. Slide __________ until you reach the liquid's curve.
3. Drop straight __________ to the temperature (x) axis.
4. Read the __________ — that is the normal boiling point.

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A backpacker wants to know what temperature water will boil at on a mountain where the air pressure is about **70 kPa**, and whether the same water will ever boil at a deep-sea vent where the pressure is enormous.

**Step 1 — State the boiling rule:**

A liquid boils when its __________ pressure equals the __________ pressure.

**Step 2 — Read the mountaintop boiling point from the curve:**

Start at ___ kPa on the pressure axis → slide right to the **water** curve → drop down → read ___ °C.

So on the mountain, water boils at about ___ °C (lower / higher) than the sea-level value of 100 °C.

**Step 3 — Reason about the deep-sea vent (very high external pressure):**

At the vent the external pressure is far above 101.3 kPa. For the water to boil, its vapor pressure would have to climb to match that huge pressure — which only happens at a very __________ temperature. So the vent water stays __________ even at 350–400 °C.

**Step 4 — Because / But / So**

Water boils at a lower temperature on a mountaintop
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name the lower external pressure; "but" should restate the boiling rule (vapor pressure must equal external pressure); "so" should state the lower boiling temperature.

## Summary

Complete the cause-and-effect sentence:

> A liquid with strong intermolecular forces has a __________ (low / high) vapor pressure and a __________ (low / high) boiling point, because its particles escape the liquid __________ (easily / with difficulty).

Complete the Because / But / So sentence:

> Water boils at a lower temperature on a mountaintop than at sea level
> because __________________________________________,
> but __________________________________________,
> so __________________________________________.

**Reminder:** Boiling point is **not** a fixed number. Water's 100 °C is true only at 101.3 kPa. Raise the external pressure (pressure cooker, ocean depth) → boiling point goes **up**. Lower it (mountaintop, vacuum) → boiling point goes **down**.
