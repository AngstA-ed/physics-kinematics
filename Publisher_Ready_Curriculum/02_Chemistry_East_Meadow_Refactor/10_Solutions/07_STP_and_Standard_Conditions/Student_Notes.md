## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A student needs the volume of **3.00 mol of nitrogen gas (N₂) at STP**, and then needs to convert **44.8 L of oxygen gas (O₂) at STP** back to moles.

**Step 1 — Identify the conversion factor.**

At STP, molar volume = __________ L/mol (from Reference Table A).

**Step 2 — moles → liters (for N₂).** You have moles and want liters, so __________ by the molar volume:

3.00 mol × ______ L/mol = ______ L

**Step 3 — Check the units.** mol × (L / mol): the "mol" cancels, leaving __________. ✓

**Step 4 — liters → moles (for O₂).** You have liters and want moles, so __________ by the molar volume:

44.8 L ÷ ______ L/mol = ______ mol

**Step 5 — Check the units.** L ÷ (L / mol): the "L" cancels, leaving __________. ✓

**Step 6 — Because / But / So**

You can use 22.4 L/mol for both N₂ and O₂
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should explain why one mole of any gas occupies the same volume at STP; "but" should note what would change this (different conditions); "so" should state that the factor only works at STP.
