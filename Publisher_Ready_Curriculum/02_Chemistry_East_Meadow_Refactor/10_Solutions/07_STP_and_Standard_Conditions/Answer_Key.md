## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. Volume of 2.00 mol of O₂ at STP:**

> 2.00 mol × 22.4 L/mol = **44.8 L**

Step-by-step: the gas is at STP, so molar volume is 22.4 L/mol (Reference Table A). You have moles and want liters, so multiply: 2.00 mol × 22.4 L/mol = 44.8 L. The "mol" units cancel, leaving liters.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct volume (44.8 L); 1 point for showing the setup with 22.4 L/mol and the unit "L" on the answer. A student who writes 0.089 divided instead of multiplied — note the direction error (moles → liters multiplies).

**2. Volume of 0.500 mol of N₂ at STP:**

> 0.500 mol × 22.4 L/mol = **11.2 L**

Step-by-step: 0.500 mol × 22.4 L/mol = 11.2 L. A fractional number of moles gives a fractional multiple of the molar volume; the method is identical to Problem 1.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct volume (11.2 L); 1 point for showing the multiplication by 22.4 L/mol. Accept 11.2 L or 11 L. A student who reports 22.4 L likely ignored the 0.500 coefficient — redirect to multiply the moles given.

**3. Moles of He in 67.2 L at STP:**

> 67.2 L ÷ 22.4 L/mol = **3.00 mol**

Step-by-step: this conversion goes the other way — liters back to moles — so divide by the molar volume: 67.2 L ÷ 22.4 L/mol = 3.00 mol. The "L" units cancel, leaving moles. (Equivalently, multiply by 1 mol / 22.4 L.)

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct answer (3.00 mol); 1 point for dividing by 22.4 L/mol (or multiplying by its reciprocal) with the unit "mol" on the answer. A student who multiplies and reports 1505 L made the direction error — liters → moles divides.

**4. Standard temperature and standard pressure (both unit systems):**

> Standard temperature: **273 K = 0 °C**
> Standard pressure: **101.3 kPa = 1 atm**

Both values are read directly from Reference Table A. Standard temperature is the freezing point of water (0 °C = 273 K). Standard pressure is one atmosphere (1 atm = 101.3 kPa).

*Rubric:* 1 point for temperature in both units (273 K and 0 °C); 1 point for pressure in both units (101.3 kPa and 1 atm). Accept 273.15 K. Deduct nothing for omitting only one of the paired units if the other is correct and labeled; full credit requires both pairs.

## Exit Ticket Answer

*(The Exit Ticket uses values distinct from the worksheet practice: CH₄ at 4.00 mol, not the O₂/N₂/He set.)*

**(a) Standard temperature and standard pressure:**

> Standard temperature: **273 K (0 °C)**
> Standard pressure: **101.3 kPa (1 atm)**

*Rubric:* 1 point total — full credit for stating 273 K and 101.3 kPa with correct units; award full credit if the student gives one unit system correctly for each (e.g., 273 K and 101.3 kPa). Bonus acknowledgment if both unit systems are given. No credit for "room temperature" or "25 °C" — standard temperature is 0 °C / 273 K.

**(b) Volume of 4.00 mol of CH₄ at STP:**

> 4.00 mol × 22.4 L/mol = **89.6 L**

Step-by-step: CH₄ is a gas at STP, so molar volume 22.4 L/mol applies regardless of the identity of the gas. You have moles and want liters, so multiply: 4.00 mol × 22.4 L/mol = 89.6 L.

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for the correct answer (89.6 L); 1 point for showing the setup (4.00 mol × 22.4 L/mol) with the unit "L." A student who uses the molar mass of CH₄ (16 g/mol) instead of the molar volume has confused mass and volume conversions — note this; molar volume (22.4 L/mol), not molar mass, converts moles of gas to liters.

**(c) Why a reported gas volume is meaningless without stated conditions:**

*Full-credit model answer:* The volume of a gas changes with temperature and pressure, so a volume reported without those conditions cannot be reproduced or compared by another scientist; stating the conditions (or reporting at STP) is what makes the number meaningful.

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that identifies gas volume as dependent on temperature and/or pressure, and connects that to the need for stated conditions (or to comparability / reproducibility), earns full credit. Responses that only say "you need the conditions" without explaining *why* (because volume depends on them) earn partial credit.

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for identifying that gas volume depends on temperature and pressure; 1 point for connecting that to comparability or reproducibility across labs (e.g., "so another scientist can repeat or compare it").

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any mention of the two lab groups and the realization that a correct measurement can still be non-comparable until conditions are fixed — students who connect the standard to fairness/comparability are demonstrating the central insight of the lesson, not just recalling 22.4 L/mol.
- Responses that name a real-world standard (a shared unit, a rule everyone agrees on, a common measurement) show transfer of the abstract idea of a "standard" beyond chemistry — note these as strong conceptual grasp.
- Students who name a partner who helped them catch a direction error (multiply vs. divide) signal that the group conversion structure supported their learning — note this as evidence the collaborative format is working.

If a student's Exit Ticket shows the correct molar-volume factor but the wrong operation (dividing instead of multiplying for moles → liters, or vice versa), the error is in choosing the direction, not in recalling the value — a brief unit-cancellation check-in at the start of the next lesson should be sufficient, and it matters because the combined-gas-law work builds directly on this setup. If a student states standard temperature as room temperature (25 °C) or uses molar mass instead of molar volume, target a quick Reference Table A review before the next lesson.
