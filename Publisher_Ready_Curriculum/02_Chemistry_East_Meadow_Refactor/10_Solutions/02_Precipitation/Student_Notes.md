## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Patterns:** Whether two solutions form a solid is not random. It follows a __________ you read from the solubility table. The same rules apply to every cation–anion pairing, so one __________ replaces memorizing dozens of separate reactions.

**What "dissolved" really means:** When an ionic compound dissolves, it does not disappear — it breaks apart into free __________ that spread through the water. A clear solution is full of invisible, dissolved ions. KI(aq) is full of __________ and __________ ions; Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) is full of __________ and __________ ions.

**Why a solid forms:** When two solutions are mixed, all the ions share one beaker. If one *new* cation–anion pairing is __________, those ions lock into a solid that drops out of the liquid. That solid is the __________. The ions that start and end dissolved are called __________ ions.

1. Identify the __________ ions present.
2. __________ partners to get two possible new compounds.
3. Check each new compound on the solubility __________.
4. If one is insoluble → that is the __________. If both are soluble → __________ forms.

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A student mixes silver nitrate solution, AgNO₃(aq), with sodium chloride solution, NaCl(aq). Does a precipitate form?

**Step 1 — Identify the four ions:**

From AgNO₃: __________ and __________  From NaCl: __________ and __________

**Step 2 — Swap partners (two new possible compounds):**

__________ (cation from AgNO₃ + anion from NaCl) and __________ (cation from NaCl + anion from AgNO₃)

**Step 3 — Check each on the solubility table:**

| New compound | Rule that applies | Soluble or insoluble? |
|---|---|---|
| AgCl | chlorides soluble except Ag⁺, Pb²⁺, Hg₂²⁺ | __________ |
| NaNO₃ | Group 1 metals soluble; nitrates soluble | __________ |

**Step 4 — Conclusion:**

The precipitate is __________ because it is the insoluble pairing. The spectator ions __________ and __________ stay dissolved.

**Step 5 — Appositive sentence:**

Silver chloride — _________________________________________ — forms when Ag⁺ and Cl⁻ ions meet in solution.

**Step 6 — Because / But / So**

Mixing AgNO₃ with NaCl forms a precipitate
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name the insoluble pairing; "but" should note that the other new compound is soluble; "so" should name the precipitate.

## Summary

Complete the appositive sentence:

> A precipitate — _________________________________________ — appears only when _________________________.

Complete the Because / But / So sentence:

> Mixing two clear solutions can form a solid
> because __________________________________________,
> but __________________________________________,
> so __________________________________________.

**Reminder:** Always **swap partners** and check **both** new compounds on the table. A precipitate forms only when at least one new pairing is **insoluble**. If both are soluble, the solution stays clear.
