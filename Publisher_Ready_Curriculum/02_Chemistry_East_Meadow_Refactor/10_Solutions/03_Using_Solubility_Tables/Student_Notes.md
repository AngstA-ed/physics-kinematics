## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A student needs to find the solubility of **KNO₃ (a solid)** at **60 °C**, and then decide whether **120 g of KNO₃ in 100 g of water at 60 °C** is saturated.

**Step 1 — Find the temperature on the x-axis:**

Temperature = ___ °C

**Step 2 — Trace up to the KNO₃ curve, then across to the y-axis:**

Solubility of KNO₃ at 60 °C ≈ ___ g per 100 g water

**Step 3 — Compare the stated amount to the curve value:**

Stated amount = ___ g.  Curve value ≈ ___ g.

The stated amount is (above / on / below) the curve: __________

**Step 4 — Decide saturated / unsaturated / excess:**

Because the amount is __________ the curve value, the solution is __________, and about ___ g of KNO₃ will stay undissolved.

**Step 5 — Because / But / So**

Heating the water would dissolve the leftover KNO₃
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name how a solid's curve changes with temperature; "but" should note what is true at the original temperature; "so" should state what heating does to the excess.

## Summary

Complete the Because / But / So sentence:

> A warm soda goes flat faster than a cold one
> because __________________________________________,
> but __________________________________________,
> so __________________________________________.

**Reminder:** The height of a solubility curve is the *maximum* mass of solute that dissolves at that temperature. The *direction* of the curve tells you the substance type: rising = solid (dissolves more when hot), falling = gas (dissolves less when hot).
