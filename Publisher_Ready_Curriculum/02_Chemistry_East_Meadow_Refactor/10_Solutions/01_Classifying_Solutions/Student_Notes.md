## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A beaker holds 100 g of water at **40 °C**. A student stirs in **64 g of KNO₃** and it all dissolves. Classify the solution and decide what happens if the beaker is cooled to 20 °C.

**Step 1 — Read the saturation limit at 40 °C from the curve:**

At 40 °C, the KNO₃ curve sits at about ___ g per 100 g water.

**Step 2 — Compare the amount dissolved to the limit:**

The student dissolved ___ g. The limit at 40 °C is ___ g. Since 64 g ____________ (equals / is below / is above) the limit, the solution is ____________ (saturated / unsaturated / supersaturated).

**Step 3 — Cool the beaker to 20 °C and re-read the curve:**

At 20 °C, the KNO₃ curve sits at about ___ g per 100 g water. The beaker still holds 64 g dissolved, which is now ____________ the new limit.

**Step 4 — Predict what happens:**

Because the dissolved amount is now above the 20 °C limit, about ___ g of KNO₃ will ____________ (dissolve / crystallize out) as the beaker cools.

> Calculation: 64 g − (20 °C limit) = ___ g of solid expected to come out of solution.

**Step 5 — Because / But / So**

KNO₃ that was fully dissolved at 40 °C comes out of solution when cooled to 20 °C
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name the lower limit at the cold temperature; "but" should note the solution is still holding the original amount; "so" should state that the excess crystallizes out.

**Reminder:** *Saturated* means **full** (holding the maximum), not empty and not "extra strong." *Supersaturated* means **overfull** (holding more than the limit) and is unstable — the excess solute crashes out the moment the balance is disturbed.
