By the end of today's 42 minutes I can:

- State that dissolving a solute **lowers** a solution's freezing point and **raises** its boiling point compared with the pure solvent.
- Explain, at the particle level, **why** dissolved particles get in the way of freezing and make boiling require more energy.

- Explain, using Cause and Effect, why the **number** of dissolved particles — not their identity — drives the size of the effect, **without calculating** the amount.

## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Cause and Effect:** When you __________ a solute in a solvent, the solution's freezing point goes __________ and its boiling point goes __________ compared with the pure solvent. The *cause* is the dissolved __________; the *effect* is the shift in the freezing and boiling points.

**Two directions, one mechanism:**

| Property | Direction the temperature moves | Everyday example |
|---|---|---|
| Freezing-point depression | freezing point goes ______ (up / down) | __________________________ |
| Boiling-point elevation | boiling point goes ______ (up / down) | __________________________ |

**Why freezing point goes DOWN:** Ice is water particles locked into a neat, repeating pattern. To freeze, the water particles must line up. Dissolved particles are scattered __________ the water particles, getting in the __________, so the water must be cooled __________ than 0 °C before it can lock into ice.

**Why boiling point goes UP:** To boil, water particles must gather enough __________ to escape from the surface into the gas. Dissolved particles take up room at the surface, so the solution must be heated __________ than 100 °C before it can boil.

**What "colligative" means:** A colligative property depends on the __________ of dissolved particles, not on __________ they are. More dissolved particles → a __________ shift (qualitatively). *(We do NOT calculate the amount — direction and cause only.)*

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A homeowner spreads rock salt on an icy front step on a 0 °C morning. By noon the step is wet and clear, while the unsalted neighbor's step is still ice.

**Work through the reasoning (qualitative — no numbers):**

1. **Name the colligative property at work:** _______________________________________________

2. **State the direction:** Dissolving salt makes the water on the step freeze at a __________ temperature than 0 °C.

3. **Particle-level cause:** The dissolved salt particles are scattered __________ the water particles, so the water cannot easily __________ into ice; it would have to get __________ than the air to freeze.

4. **The effect on the step:** Because the air is at 0 °C but the salty water now freezes *below* 0 °C, the water on the step stays __________ (solid / liquid).

5. **Because / But / So:** The salted step stays clear
   **because** _______________________________________________,
   **but** _______________________________________________,
   **so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name the dissolved particles getting in the way; "but" should note that the air is still freezing; "so" should state that the freezing point dropped below the air temperature, keeping the step liquid.

Complete the sentence:

> Dissolving a solute in water is a colligative effect: the freezing point goes __________ and the boiling point goes __________, and the size of the shift depends on the __________ of dissolved particles, not on what they are.

Complete the Because / But / So sentence:

> A car's radiator uses an antifreeze-and-water mixture instead of plain water
> because __________________________________________,
> but __________________________________________,
> so __________________________________________.

**Reminder:** Colligative properties are **qualitative** in this course. You explain the *direction* (freezing ↓, boiling ↑) and the *cause* (dissolved particles in the way). You are **not** asked to calculate how many degrees.

**Two-arrow memory cue:**
↓ Freezing point goes DOWN (depression)  ·  ↑ Boiling point goes UP (elevation)
