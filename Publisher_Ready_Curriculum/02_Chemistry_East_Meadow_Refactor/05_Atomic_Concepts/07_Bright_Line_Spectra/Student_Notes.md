## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Patterns:** Each element gives off a fixed, repeatable set of light wavelengths. The unique pattern of lines is a __________ that lets us identify the element — even in a star we can never __________.

**Two kinds of spectra:**

| Type | What it looks like | What produces it |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous spectrum | a full __________ , every color present | a hot, dense glowing source (filament, Sun's surface) |
| Bright-line (emission) spectrum | a few __________ colored lines on a black background | a thin, excited gas of a single element |

**Why discrete lines? — the energy-level model:**

1. Inside an atom, an electron can only have certain allowed __________ levels (the shells from the Bohr model). It can never have an energy *in between* two levels.
2. When an electron drops from a __________ level to a __________ level, the atom releases the leftover energy as light of one specific __________ .
3. Because only certain levels exist, only certain drops are possible — so only certain colors appear. That is why a spectrum is separate __________, not a smooth rainbow.

**Color and energy (no calculation needed):**

- A **bigger** energy drop releases light toward the __________ end of the spectrum (higher energy).
- A **smaller** energy drop releases light toward the __________ end of the spectrum (lower energy).

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** You view a mystery gas tube through a spectroscope. It shows **four** lines: a red line far to one side, a blue-green line near the middle, and two lines close together at the violet end. You compare it to your reference spectra.

**Step 1 — Count the lines:** The mystery tube has ___________ visible lines.

**Step 2 — Compare to references.** Hydrogen has exactly 4 lines (656 nm red, 486 nm blue-green, 434 and 410 nm violet). Helium has more lines, including a strong yellow line.

Does the mystery tube have a strong yellow line? ___________  So it is **not** ___________.

**Step 3 — Match line by line:**

| Mystery line (color/position) | Matches hydrogen line? |
|---|---|
| red, far side | 656 nm — yes / no |
| blue-green, middle | 486 nm — yes / no |
| two violet lines, close together | 434 + 410 nm — yes / no |

**Step 4 — Identify:** The mystery tube is ___________.

**Step 5 — Explain the lines:** Each of those four lines is produced by an ___________ ___________ — an electron dropping from a higher ___________ level to a lower one, releasing light of one specific color.

**Step 6 — Because / But / So**

The mystery tube must be hydrogen
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name the matching lines; "but" should name a line a competitor (like helium) has that the mystery tube lacks; "so" should state the identification.

**Reminder:** Light comes *out* (a bright line appears) when an electron drops *down* from a higher energy level to a lower one. Energy going *in* lifts the electron up; the bright line is the energy coming back out as light.

**Key reference (Atomic Concepts unit):** Electron energy levels = the shells of the Bohr model. Only certain levels are allowed, so only certain transitions — and therefore only certain colors — are possible for each element.
