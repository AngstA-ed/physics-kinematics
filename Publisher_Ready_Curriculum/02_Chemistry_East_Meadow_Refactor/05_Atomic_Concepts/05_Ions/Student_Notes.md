## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Patterns:** Ion charge is not random. It repeats predictably down each __________ of the periodic table. The group number sets the number of __________ electrons, and that sets the stable ion an atom forms.

**What is an ion?**

- The nucleus (protons and neutrons) __________ change when an ion forms. Only __________ are lost or gained.
- If protons and electrons balance → the atom is __________ (no charge).
- If an atom **loses** electrons → more protons than electrons → overall charge is __________ → called a __________.
- If an atom **gains** electrons → more electrons than protons → overall charge is __________ → called an __________.

**Why do atoms form ions?** Atoms gain or lose electrons to reach a full, __________ outer shell (8 electrons, or 2 for the smallest atoms). Atoms take the __________ path: lose a few, or gain a few — whichever is fewer.

|---|---|---|---|---|

**Pattern rule:** Metals on the left __________ electrons → __________ ions (cations). Nonmetals on the right __________ electrons → __________ ions (anions). Same group → same __________.

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** Predict the ion that chlorine (Cl) forms, and explain why.

**Step 1 — Find the group and valence electrons:**

Chlorine is in Group ___. Valence electrons = ___.

**Step 2 — Decide the shortest path to a full outer shell (8):**

Chlorine has 7 valence electrons. To reach 8, it is easier to __________ 1 electron than to lose 7.

**Step 3 — Count protons and electrons after the change:**

| | Protons | Electrons | Overall charge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cl atom | 17 | 17 | 0 |
| Cl after gaining 1 | 17 | ___ | ___ |

**Step 4 — Write the ion:**

Chlorine gains 1 electron and forms the ion ___ — a (cation / anion): __________.

**Step 5 — Appositive sentence:**

The chloride ion (Cl⁻) — _________________________ — has one more electron than proton.

**Step 6 — Because / But / So**

Chlorine gains an electron rather than losing seven
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name the shortest path to a full shell; "but" should name what gaining a negative electron does to the charge; "so" should state the final ion (Cl⁻, an anion).

**Reminder — the sign rule:** *Lose* electrons → **positive** (cation). *Gain* electrons → **negative** (anion). The charge is opposite to the direction you might first guess, because electrons themselves are negative.

**Key reference (from the 2025 NYS Reference Tables):**
Periodic Table — group number gives the valence-electron count, which predicts the ion charge. Group 1 → 1+; Group 2 → 2+; Group 16 → 2−; Group 17 → 1−; Group 18 → no ion.
