## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. Solid iodine warmed → purple gas appears, no liquid:**

- **Phase change:** sublimation (solid → gas directly).
- **Energy:** absorbed (energy is added by the warming).
- **What is happening to the particles:** the iodine particles gain kinetic energy, move faster, and break free from the packed solid arrangement straight into the fast, far-apart gas arrangement — skipping the liquid state.

*Rubric:* 1 point for naming sublimation; 1 point for "energy absorbed"; 1 point for a particle-level explanation that mentions particles gaining energy / moving faster and going directly to gas. A student who answers "evaporation" has the right energy direction but the wrong start state — evaporation is liquid → gas, while this starts as a solid; mark sublimation as the correct term. NYSSLS: SEP-2 / Energy and Matter.

**2. Liquid water in a freezer → solid ice cubes:**

- **Phase change:** freezing (liquid → solid).
- **Energy:** released (energy is removed by the freezer; the water gives up energy to its surroundings).
- **What is happening to the particles:** the water particles lose kinetic energy, slow down, and settle into the fixed, packed positions of the solid; they can no longer slide past one another.

*Rubric:* 1 point for naming freezing; 1 point for "energy released/removed"; 1 point for a particle-level explanation (particles slow down and lock into fixed positions). A common error is to say energy is "absorbed" because the freezer is doing work — clarify that *the water* loses energy; the change toward solid always releases energy. NYSSLS: SEP-2 / Energy and Matter.

**3. Dry ice (solid CO₂) shrinks and gives off gas, no puddle:**

- **Phase change:** sublimation (solid → gas directly).
- **Energy:** absorbed (energy is added from the warm room).
- **Why there is no puddle:** carbon dioxide has no stable *liquid* state at normal room pressure, so the solid converts directly to gas. There is no liquid step, so no puddle ever forms. (Bonus: the visible white fog is *not* the CO₂ gas — CO₂ gas is invisible — it is water vapor from the air that condensed/deposited because the escaping gas is so cold.)

*Rubric:* 1 point for naming sublimation; 1 point for "energy absorbed"; 1 point for explaining the missing puddle as "no stable liquid state at room pressure / solid goes straight to gas." Full bonus credit if the student also explains the fog is condensed air moisture, not CO₂. NYSSLS: SEP-2 / Energy and Matter.

## Exit Ticket Answer

Note: the Exit Ticket uses substances and scenarios deliberately *different* from the worksheet practice, so students must transfer the reasoning rather than recall an answer.

**(a) Naphthalene vapor → solid crystals on a cold lid, no liquid:**

- **Phase change:** deposition (gas → solid directly).
- **Energy:** released (the gas gives up energy to the cold lid as it becomes a solid).

*Rubric:* 1 point for naming deposition; 1 point for "energy released." A student who answers "condensation" identified the correct direction (gas losing energy) but the wrong end state — condensation ends in a liquid, while here the gas becomes a *solid* with no liquid step, so deposition is correct. Award partial credit (1 of 2) and note the distinction. This is the reverse of the sublimation in the worksheet. NYSSLS: SEP-2 / Energy and Matter.

**(b) Steam → liquid droplets fogging a cold mirror:**

- **Phase change:** condensation (gas → liquid).
- **Energy:** released (the water vapor gives up energy to the cold mirror as it becomes liquid).

*Rubric:* 1 point for naming condensation; 1 point for "energy released." A student who writes "evaporation" reversed the direction — evaporation is liquid → gas; here a gas becomes liquid, so condensation is correct. NYSSLS: SEP-2 / Energy and Matter.

**(c) Why dry ice sublimes instead of melting (energy + particle motion):**

*Full-credit model answer:* As the solid CO₂ absorbs energy from the warm room, its particles gain kinetic energy and move faster until they break free from the packed solid arrangement; because carbon dioxide has no stable liquid state at normal room pressure, the particles go directly into the far-apart gas state, so the solid sublimes instead of melting.

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that (1) describes the particles gaining energy / speeding up and (2) explains that CO₂ goes directly from solid to gas (no stable liquid step / skips the liquid) earns full credit. A response that only says "it turns to gas" without the energy/particle reasoning earns partial credit.

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for the energy/particle-motion mechanism (particles absorb energy, move faster, break free); 1 point for stating it goes directly solid → gas (sublimation) with no liquid step. NYSSLS: SEP-2 / Energy and Matter.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- A specific everyday example of a change in form where the substance stayed the same — "my breath fogging the window," "a popsicle melting" — rather than a generic "things change." Students who connect the atomic-scale idea to a concrete personal observation are showing transfer.
- Any mention of the dry-ice demonstration and the dry tray as the thing that shifted their thinking — this signals the phenomenon landed.
- Naming a peer or a moment from the particle-model comparison as what helped them — this signals the collaborative structure and the model-building activity supported learning.

If a student's particle drawings still show gas particles as *larger* (rather than the same size but farther apart), or shows particle motion fully stopping in the solid, flag it for a brief one-on-one at the start of the next lesson — those are the two most common particle-model misconceptions and they undermine the gas-law and heating-curve lessons that follow.
