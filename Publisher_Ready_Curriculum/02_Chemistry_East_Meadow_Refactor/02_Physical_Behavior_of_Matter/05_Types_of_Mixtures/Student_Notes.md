## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A student is given an unknown cloudy liquid and must decide whether it is a solution, a colloid, or a suspension. She runs the three tests in order.

**Step 1 — The laser test:** She shines a laser through the liquid. The beam is **visible** (it glows).

→ What does a visible beam tell her? The particles are large enough to scatter light, so the liquid is **not** a __________. It must be a colloid or a suspension.

**Step 2 — The standing test:** She lets the cup sit for 5 minutes. **Nothing settles** to the bottom; the liquid stays uniform.

→ What does "nothing settles" tell her? The particles are not heavy enough to settle, so it is **not** a __________. That points to a colloid.

**Step 3 — The filter test:** She pours a sample through a coffee filter. **Nothing is caught**; the liquid passes straight through.

→ What does "nothing caught" confirm? The particles are too small to be trapped by the filter — consistent with a __________.

**Conclusion:** Beam visible + does not settle + passes the filter → the liquid is a __________.

**Appositive sentence:**

> A colloid — _________________________ — scatters light (the Tyndall effect) but never settles out.

**Because / But / So**

The beam glows in this liquid but the particles never settle
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name why the beam is visible (particles scatter light); "but" should note why the particles do not settle (they are small/light); "so" should name the classification (colloid).

## Summary

Complete the appositive sentence:

> The Tyndall effect — _________________________ — is why a flashlight beam glows in fog but vanishes in clear water.

Complete the Because / But / So sentence:

> A flashlight beam shows up in fog but not in clear air
> because __________________________________________,
> but __________________________________________,
> so __________________________________________.

**Reminder — three tests, one variable:** The laser test, the standing test, and the filter test are all indirect ways of measuring **particle size**. Tiny particles (solution) hide the beam, never settle, and pass any filter. Medium particles (colloid) scatter light but stay suspended and pass a filter. Large particles (suspension) scatter light, settle out, and are caught by a filter.

**Key idea (the trap to avoid):** Color is *not* the same as cloudiness. A solution can be brightly colored and still let a beam pass invisibly — only the *scattering* of light, not the color, signals a colloid or suspension.
