## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. Copper wire (Cu) — ELEMENT.**
*Reason:* It is made of only one kind of atom (copper). It cannot be broken into a simpler substance by a chemical reaction. Its particle diagram is all identical circles. · *Rubric:* full credit for "element" AND a reason that says *one kind of atom*; partial for "element" with a vague reason ("it's pure"). NYSSLS: foundation for HS-PS1-1.

**2. Carbon dioxide (CO₂) — COMPOUND.**
*Reason:* The formula contains two *different* elements (carbon and oxygen) chemically bonded in a fixed ratio (1 C : 2 O). Bonded different atoms in a fixed ratio = compound. · *Rubric:* full credit for "compound" AND a reason citing two different elements bonded; partial for "compound" alone. Watch for students who say "mixture because it has carbon and oxygen in it" — the atoms are *bonded*, not mixed. NYSSLS: foundation for HS-PS1-1.

**3. Trail mix — MIXTURE (heterogeneous).**
*Reason:* Several substances are physically combined, not bonded; each part (peanut, raisin, chocolate) keeps its own properties and can be picked out by physical means. There is no fixed ratio. · *Rubric:* full credit for "mixture" AND a reason about parts keeping properties / being separable; accept "heterogeneous mixture" as a bonus. NYSSLS: foundation for HS-PS1-1.

**4. Oxygen gas (O₂) — ELEMENT.** *(diagnostic item)*
*Reason:* Although the two atoms are bonded, they are the *same* kind of atom (both oxygen). A compound requires two or more *different* elements; O₂ has only one element, so it is an element (a diatomic molecule of an element). · *Rubric:* full credit requires "element" AND the *same-kind-of-atom* reason. A student who writes "compound because two atoms are bonded" holds the central misconception — give targeted feedback: *bonding is necessary for a compound but not sufficient; the atoms must be different elements.* NYSSLS: foundation for HS-PS1-1.

**5. Table salt (NaCl) — COMPOUND.**
*Reason:* Two *different* elements (sodium and chlorine) are chemically bonded in a fixed 1-to-1 ratio. *Phenomenon explanation (full-credit model):* Salt is safe to eat even though sodium and chlorine are dangerous because the atoms are *bonded* into the compound sodium chloride, which is a brand-new substance with its own properties — not a blend of the reactive metal and the toxic gas. The structure (bonded atoms) gives it a completely new function (a safe, edible solid). · *Rubric:* 1 point for "compound" with the two-different-elements reason; 1 point for the phenomenon explanation that names *bonding → new substance → new properties* (not just "they cancel out"). NYSSLS: foundation for HS-PS1-1.

## Exit Ticket Answer

*(Note: these items are intentionally different from the worksheet practice set — sugar water, an H₂O-type picture, CO₂, and Ne were not classified in the Investigation or Make-It-Make-Sense sections.)*

**(a) Sugar water — MIXTURE (homogeneous).**
*How they should know:* The sweetness is spread evenly (homogeneous), but when the water boils away the sugar is recovered unchanged — the parts were never bonded and can be separated by a physical method (evaporation), so it is a mixture, not a compound. There is no fixed ratio (it could be more or less sweet). · *Rubric:* 1 point for "mixture"; 1 point for a reason citing physical separation OR no fixed ratio OR parts keep their properties. Accept "homogeneous mixture" as full credit. A student who answers "compound because sugar dissolved in water" should get partial credit and feedback that *dissolving is physical mixing, not bonding.* NYSSLS: foundation for HS-PS1-1.

**(b) Picture of identical bonded three-atom units (one red bonded to two white) — COMPOUND.**
*How they should know:* Two *different* kinds of atom (red and white) are bonded together into identical units in a fixed ratio (1 red : 2 white). Different bonded atoms = compound. (This is the particle picture of a substance like water, H₂O.) · *Rubric:* 1 point for "compound"; 1 point for citing *two different atoms bonded in a fixed ratio*. A student who says "element because all the units are the same" has confused *identical units* with *one kind of atom* — note that each unit itself contains two different atoms. NYSSLS: foundation for HS-PS1-1.

**(c) CO₂ and Ne.**
- **CO₂ — COMPOUND** because the formula has two *different* element symbols (C and O) bonded in a fixed ratio.
- **Ne (neon) — ELEMENT** because the formula is a single element symbol — one kind of atom only.
*Rubric:* 1 point per correct classification with a formula-based reason (2 points total). Full credit requires the reason to reference *how many different elements the formula shows.* NYSSLS: foundation for HS-PS1-2 (reading formulas).

**(d) Structure-and-Function sentence (full-credit model):**
*"A compound's atoms are chemically bonded into a new arrangement (structure), so the substance behaves in completely new ways (function) — its properties are not a blend of the separate elements' properties."*
*Acceptable variations:* Any sentence that links *bonded particle structure* to *new/different properties* and contrasts with the un-bonded elements earns full credit. Responses that only restate "compounds are different" without the structure→function link earn partial credit. · *Rubric:* 2 points — 1 for naming the structural cause (atoms bonded / new arrangement), 1 for naming the functional result (new properties, unlike the elements). NYSSLS: CCC Structure and Function.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any analogy that correctly captures *combination creating something new* — "my parents are strict and my coach is laid-back, but together they keep me balanced" — shows transfer of the bonded-compound idea beyond chemistry.
- Responses that name a *person* who helped them see the value of combining signal that the opening circle and partner talk landed; note these as evidence the collaborative structure is working.
- Students who reference the salt phenomenon directly ("dangerous things made something good, like salt") are showing the phenomenon anchored the abstract classification — the strongest transfer.

If a student's Exit Ticket still classifies O₂ (or, on item b, the bonded units) as the wrong category, the misconception is *bonded-means-compound* — a brief one-on-one at the start of the next lesson with one element molecule (O₂) and one compound (CO) side by side will resolve it quickly. If a student classifies sugar water as a compound, revisit *dissolving is physical mixing* before the separation-techniques lesson.
