## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Structure and Function:** How the particles are __________ and __________ (the structure) decides how the substance __________ (the function). Bonding *different* atoms makes a brand-new substance; physically mixing does __________ make a new substance.

**The three categories — sort by the particles:**

| Category | How many *kinds* of atom? | Are the atoms bonded? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Element | __________ kind | (may be bonded as a molecule, but all the same kind) | Cu, O₂ |
| Compound | two or more __________ kinds | __________ — in a fixed ratio | H₂O, NaCl |
| Mixture | two or more substances | __________ — just physically combined | salt water, air |

**Reading symbols and formulas:**

- A single symbol or a formula with only *one kind* of element (Cu, O₂) → __________.
- A formula with two or more *different* element symbols (H₂O, NaCl, CO₂) → __________.
- A substance with no single fixed formula and no fixed ratio → __________.

**The watch-out (O₂):** O₂ has two atoms __________ together — but they are the *same* element, so O₂ is still an __________, not a compound. A compound needs two or more __________ elements.

**Separating them:**

- A **mixture** can be separated by __________ means (magnet, filtering, evaporating) because the parts kept their own properties.
- A **compound** cannot be separated this way — it would take a __________ reaction, because the atoms are bonded into one new substance.

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** Classify each of these four substances — **He, CO, brass (copper + zinc), N₂** — as element, compound, or mixture, using the formula or description.

**Step 1 — He (helium):**

Number of *different* elements in the formula: ___  →  Classification: __________

**Step 2 — CO (carbon monoxide):**

Number of *different* elements in the formula: ___  (which two? ___ and ___)  →  Are they bonded in a fixed ratio? ___  →  Classification: __________

**Step 3 — Brass (copper mixed with zinc):**

Are copper and zinc *bonded into a new substance*, or just *physically combined*? __________  →  Classification: __________

**Step 4 — N₂ (nitrogen gas):**

The two atoms are bonded — but are they the *same* kind or *different* kinds? __________  →  Classification: __________

**Step 5 — The tricky pair (CO vs. N₂):** Both CO and N₂ have two bonded atoms. What single question separates them into different categories?

> _______________________________________________

**Step 6 — Because / But / So:**

N₂ has two atoms bonded together, but it is classified as an element
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name *how many kinds* of atom; "but" should contrast with a compound like CO; "so" should state N₂'s correct classification.

## Summary

Complete each sentence.

> An **element** is a pure substance made of __________ kind of atom.
> A **compound** is two or more __________ elements __________ in a fixed ratio, with new properties.
> A **mixture** is two or more substances __________ combined, where each part keeps its own properties.

Complete the Because / But / So sentence (the phenomenon):

> Table salt is safe to eat even though it is made of dangerous sodium and chlorine
> because __________________________________________,
> but __________________________________________,
> so __________________________________________.

**Remember:** Bonding alone does not make a compound — you need two or more *different* elements bonded. O₂ and N₂ are bonded *elements*. CO₂ and NaCl are bonded *compounds*.
