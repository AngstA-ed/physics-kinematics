## Unit Scope (from East Meadow Scope & Sequence)

This unit builds the particle-level classification scheme that the rest of HS-PS1 depends on. Students begin by sorting everyday samples into pure substances and mixtures, then explain states of matter and phase changes as energy-driven rearrangements of particles that conserve a substance's identity. From there they learn to tell a physical change from a chemical one by reading the signs of new-substance formation, sharpen the element / compound / mixture distinction at the particle level, classify mixtures by particle size (solution, colloid, suspension), and finally choose a separation technique by matching a method to the physical-property difference it exploits. The through-line is *structure determines behavior*: how matter is composed at the atomic scale governs how it is classified, how it changes, and how it can be pulled apart.

| # | Lesson | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Classification of Matter | Matter sorts into pure substances (elements, compounds) and mixtures (homogeneous, heterogeneous) — the eye is not enough; you must ask what the particles are |
| 02 | States of Matter & Phase Changes | Adding or removing energy changes particle spacing and motion, driving phase changes while the substance's identity is conserved |
| 03 | Physical vs. Chemical Properties / Changes | A physical change re-spaces particles (same substance); a chemical change breaks and remakes bonds to form new substances |
| 04 | Distinguishing Elements, Compounds, Mixtures | One kind of atom vs. atoms chemically bonded in a fixed ratio vs. substances physically combined in any ratio |
| 05 | Types of Mixtures | Particle size sorts mixtures into solutions, colloids, and suspensions — inferred from bulk evidence like the Tyndall effect |
| 06 | Separating Mixtures | Read the property difference (size, boiling point, solubility), then pick the tool — filtration, distillation, chromatography |

## Pacing Calendar

| Day | Lesson | Phenomenon anchor | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 01 Classification of Matter | Same "clear liquid" — distilled water is a compound, saltwater is a mixture | Restorative Circle (unit opener) |
| 2 | 02 States of Matter & Phase Changes | Dry ice sublimes — no puddle, ever; water ice melts | Hochman |
| 3 | 03 Physical vs. Chemical Properties / Changes | Cut paper is still paper; burned paper is ash you cannot un-burn | BTC |
| 4 | 04 Distinguishing Elements, Compounds, Mixtures | Reactive sodium + poisonous chlorine → edible table salt | Active Learning |
| 5 | 05 Types of Mixtures | A flashlight beam vanishes in clear water but glows in milk or fog | Hochman |
| 6 | 06 Separating Mixtures | Surrounded by undrinkable seawater — how do you make it fresh? | BTC |

Double-period days (every other day) let the classification sort and the separation challenge run long.

## Strategy Rotation

- **RESTORATIVE CIRCLE**: Lesson 01 (unit-opening circle: a time something looked one way but turned out to be another).
- **ACTIVE LEARNING**: Lessons 01, 04 (sample-card sorting with particle-level justification; classify-before-naming sort).
- **BTC (Building Thinking Classrooms)**: Lessons 03, 06 (physical-vs-chemical thin-slices and "which separation method?" design tasks at vertical non-permanent surfaces, visibly random groups).
- **HOCHMAN (literacy)**: Lessons 02, 05 (appositive sentences defining *sublimation* / *phase change* and the *Tyndall effect*; Because/But/So in the Elaborate phase).

SEL (opening connection + closing reflection), ELL/SPED supports, and OPTIC/CER literacy appear in every lesson, alongside a Curated Resources block.
