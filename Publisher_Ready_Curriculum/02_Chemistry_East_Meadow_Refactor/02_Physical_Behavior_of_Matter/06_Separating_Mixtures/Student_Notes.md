## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Structure and Function:** A separation method only works when the parts of a mixture differ in some physical __________. The *structure* of the tool (the filter's holes, the still's heated flask and cooled collector, the chromatography paper) is built to grab onto that one difference. No property difference → __________ separation.

**Five separation methods — match each to its property:**

| Method | What you do | Physical property it exploits | What you keep |
|---|---|---|---|
| Filtration | pour mixture through filter paper | | undissolved solid (and/or the liquid) |
| Distillation | boil, then catch and cool the vapor | | the purified liquid |
| Evaporation | let the liquid dry away | boiling point | |
| Decanting | let the solid settle, pour the liquid off | | the liquid (solid stays behind) |
| Chromatography | let a solvent move through paper | | the separated components |

**Distillation vs. evaporation — same property, opposite goal:**

- Both rely on a difference in __________ __________.
- Distillation *captures* the vapor → you keep the __________ (fresh water from seawater).
- Evaporation *lets the vapor escape* → you keep the __________ (sea salt from seawater).

**Why filtration fails on dissolved substances:**

> Filtration separates by particle __________. Dissolved salt or sugar particles are far __________ than the holes in the filter paper, so they pass straight through with the liquid. Filtration only catches __________ solids like sand or wood.

**Reminder — read the mixture first, then pick the tool.** There is no single "best" separation method. You identify the **property difference** between the components — particle size, boiling point, density, magnetism, or solubility — and then choose the method whose structure exploits that difference.

**Key idea (from this unit):** Separating a mixture is a *physical* change. The salt, water, and sand you recover are the **same substances** you started with — mixtures keep the individual identities of their components, which is exactly why they can be separated and recovered unchanged.
