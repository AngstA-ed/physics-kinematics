## Unit Scope (from East Meadow Scope & Sequence)

This unit answers two questions about every chemical reaction: *how fast* does it go, and *how far*? Students begin with the collision model of reaction rate — temperature, concentration, surface area, and catalysts all act by changing how often and how hard particles collide — then turn to the energy of reactions, distinguishing exothermic from endothermic changes and reading the potential-energy diagrams that map the activation-energy barrier and ΔH. From there the unit develops *spontaneity* (the enthalpy-and-entropy reasoning behind which direction a change runs on its own) and then *equilibrium* (the dynamic, condition-dependent balance a reversible reaction settles into). The capstone, Le Châtelier's principle, lets students engineer that balance — predicting and justifying how a stress shifts a reaction toward the products they want, anchored in the Haber process that feeds half the world. The unit closes the kinetics-and-equilibrium strand of the NYS Chemistry course and is built on the energy and reasoning foundations laid in earlier units.

| # | Lesson | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Collision Theory & Rates of Reaction | Reactions occur when particles collide with enough energy and correct orientation; temperature, concentration, surface area, and catalysts change the rate |
| 02 | Energy in Reactions | Exothermic releases / endothermic absorbs energy; net change is set by which bond-energy total is larger |
| 03 | Potential Energy Diagrams | A PE diagram maps reactant and product levels, the activation-energy barrier, the activated complex, and ΔH on a relative energy scale |
| 04 | Spontaneous Reactions | A change runs on its own when favored by lower energy (ΔH < 0) and/or greater disorder (ΔS > 0); spontaneity is direction, not speed |
| 05 | Chemical Equilibrium | A reversible reaction reaches a dynamic balance — equal forward/reverse rates, constant concentrations — not a stopped reaction |
| 06 | Le Châtelier's Principle | A stressed equilibrium shifts to partially relieve the stress; engineer conditions to maximize product (Haber process) |

## Pacing Calendar

| Day | Lesson | Phenomenon anchor | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 01 Collision Theory & Rates | Two identical glow sticks — hot one bright now, cold one glows for hours | Restorative Circle (unit opener) + Active Learning |
| 2 | 02 Energy in Reactions | A hand warmer heats your hand; an instant cold pack chills it — nothing plugged in | Hochman |
| 3 | 03 Potential Energy Diagrams | Gasoline releases huge energy yet won't ignite until a spark gives it a push | BTC |
| 4 | 04 Spontaneous Reactions | A tidy room gets messy on its own; the reverse never happens for free | Active Learning |
| 5 | 05 Chemical Equilibrium | Sealed soda keeps its fizz for months, then goes flat once opened | Hochman |
| 6 | 06 Le Châtelier's Principle | Same N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ reaction yields more ammonia at higher pressure | BTC |

Double-period days (every other day) let the Alka-Seltzer rate investigation and the entropy demonstration stations run long.

## Strategy Rotation

- **RESTORATIVE CIRCLE**: Lesson 01 (unit-opening circle).
- **ACTIVE LEARNING**: Lessons 01, 04 (Alka-Seltzer manipulate-a-variable rate trials; entropy demonstration stations and spontaneity card sort).
- **BTC (Building Thinking Classrooms)**: Lessons 03, 06 (PE-diagram thin-slice at vertical surfaces; Le Châtelier shift-prediction thin-slices before the rule is named).
- **HOCHMAN (literacy)**: Lessons 02, 05 (appositive sentences defining exothermic/endothermic; appositive packing the full dynamic-equilibrium definition).

SEL (opening connection + closing reflection), ELL/SPED supports, OPTIC/CER literacy, and a Curated Resources block appear in every lesson.
