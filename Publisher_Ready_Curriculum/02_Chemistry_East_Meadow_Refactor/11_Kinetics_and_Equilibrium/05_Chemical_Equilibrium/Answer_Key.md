## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. Sealed soda bottle (CO₂ escaping and re-dissolving at the same rate; dissolved amount constant):**

- **At equilibrium?** Yes.
- **Which two rates are equal?** The rate at which CO₂ leaves the liquid (forward) equals the rate at which CO₂ re-dissolves into the liquid (reverse): CO₂(aq) ⇌ CO₂(g).
- **Is the reaction stopped?** No. CO₂ is continuously escaping *and* re-dissolving — the system is dynamic. The dissolved amount is constant only because the two opposite processes run at equal rates, not because anything has stopped.

*Rubric:* 1 point for identifying the system as at equilibrium; 1 point for naming the two equal rates (escaping = re-dissolving); 1 point for explaining that "constant amount" comes from equal rates, not from a stopped reaction. Full credit (3) requires the dynamic explanation, not just "yes." NYSSLS: HS-PS1-B.

**2. Open soda bottle (CO₂ escapes into the room, almost none re-dissolves, dissolved CO₂ drops to zero):**

- **At equilibrium?** No.
- **Why not?** Opening the bottle removes the escaped CO₂ from the system, so there is no reverse process to balance the forward (escaping) process. The two rates can never become equal, so the system cannot reach equilibrium.
- **What happens to the fizz?** The forward (escaping) process runs unopposed, so the dissolved CO₂ steadily decreases until the soda goes flat.

*Rubric:* 1 point for "not at equilibrium"; 1 point for explaining the missing/unbalanced reverse process (escaped gas can't return); 1 point for connecting that to the soda going flat. A student who only says "the gas leaves" earns partial credit; full credit requires identifying that the *reverse* process is absent so the rates can never balance. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-B.

**3. Cup simulation at round 7 (≈7 Reactants, ≈33 Products; counts steady, counters still moving):**

- **At equilibrium?** Yes.
- **Does 7 ≠ 33 mean NOT at equilibrium?** No. Equilibrium does not require the two cups (amounts) to be equal. It requires the forward and reverse *rates* to be equal. The unequal amounts (7 vs. 33) are exactly what an equilibrium often looks like.
- **What IS equal?** The number of counters moving forward (R→P) each round equals the number moving back (P→R) each round — the rates are equal. That is why each cup's total holds constant even though counters keep moving.

*Rubric:* 1 point for "at equilibrium"; 1 point for correcting the equal-amounts misconception (equal *rates*, not equal amounts); 1 point for naming the equal forward/back transfers as the reason the counts hold steady. This item directly targets the dominant misconception — flag any student who says the unequal cups mean "not at equilibrium" for a quick re-teach. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-B.

## Exit Ticket Answer

*Context: a sealed flask half-filled with liquid water; the water level and humidity stop changing; the process is H₂O(l) ⇌ H₂O(g). This is a closed evaporation/condensation system — distinct from the soda and cup contexts on the worksheet, so students must transfer the concept rather than recall a practiced answer.*

**(a) The two characteristics of equilibrium:**

1. The rate of **evaporation** (forward, H₂O(l) → H₂O(g)) equals the rate of **condensation** (reverse, H₂O(g) → H₂O(l)) — the forward and reverse rates are equal.
2. The amounts/concentrations are **constant** — the liquid water level and the humidity (gaseous water) in the flask stay unchanged.

*Rubric:* 1 point for "equal forward and reverse rates" (accept "evaporation rate = condensation rate"); 1 point for "constant concentrations / amounts" (accept "water level and humidity stop changing"). Both characteristics required for full credit (2 points). A student who lists only one earns 1 point. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-B.

**(b) Why "evaporation must have stopped" is wrong:**

*Full-credit model answer:* The water level stops changing not because evaporation stopped, but because evaporation and condensation are now happening at equal rates — every water molecule that evaporates is balanced by one that condenses back, so the level holds steady while both processes keep running. The system is at dynamic equilibrium, not stopped.

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that states evaporation is still occurring but is now balanced by an equal rate of condensation earns full credit. A response that only says "it's still evaporating" without mentioning the balancing condensation earns partial credit.

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for stating evaporation is still occurring (the reaction is dynamic/ongoing); 1 point for explaining that condensation occurs at an equal rate so the net level is constant. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-B.

**(c) Appositive sentence defining dynamic equilibrium:**

*Full-credit model answer:* "Dynamic equilibrium — the condition where the forward and reverse reactions occur at equal rates so the concentrations stay constant — is not a stopped reaction."

*Acceptable variations:* Any sentence that (i) uses dashes (or commas) to set off an appositive phrase renaming "dynamic equilibrium," (ii) the appositive states both *equal rates* AND *constant concentrations*, and (iii) is a complete subject-verb sentence. Example: "Dynamic equilibrium — equal forward and reverse rates keeping concentrations constant — describes a reaction that is balanced, not finished."

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for correct appositive structure (a dash- or comma-set phrase renaming the term inside one complete sentence); 1 point for the appositive containing BOTH equal rates and constant concentrations. A definition that mentions only one of the two characteristics earns 1 point. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-B.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any mention of the cup simulation, the soda bottles, or the rate graph as the thing that made "staying the same while still changing" click — students who anchor the abstract idea to a concrete observation are showing the dynamic model is real to them, not memorized.
- Responses that use the appositive structure ("dynamic equilibrium — ... — is not a stopped reaction") are demonstrating the Hochman writing move from Phase 3 and Phase 4. Note these students as having integrated the vocabulary into their active sentence structure.
- Students who name a partner who helped them see that the cups don't have to be equal are signaling that the collaborative simulation supported their learning — note this as evidence the partner structure is working.

If a student's Exit Ticket states that equilibrium means "the concentrations are equal" or "the reaction stopped," the two dominant misconceptions are still in place. Have a brief one-on-one at the start of the next lesson before Le Châtelier's principle is introduced — the shift reasoning in that lesson depends entirely on understanding that equilibrium is dynamic and that rates (not amounts) are what equalize.
