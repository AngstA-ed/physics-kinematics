## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Cause and Effect at the particle level:** A reaction happens when reactant particles __________ with each other. A reaction goes *faster* when those collisions happen more __________ and/or with more __________. Each factor we can control is a *cause* that changes the collisions; the *effect* is a change in the __________ rate.

**Collision theory — two conditions for a successful collision:**

For a collision to actually cause a reaction, the colliding particles must have:

1. Enough __________ (at least the activation energy), AND
2. The correct __________ (the right way of lining up).

If a collision is too gentle or poorly aimed, the particles just __________ apart and no reaction happens.

**The four factors that change reaction rate:**

| Factor (cause) | What it does to collisions | Effect on rate |
|---|---|---|
| Raise temperature | particles move ______; collide more ______ and with more ______ | rate goes ______ |
| Raise concentration | particles are more crowded, so they collide more ______ | rate goes ______ |
| Increase surface area | more of the solid is ______, so more collisions can happen at once | rate goes ______ |
| Add a catalyst | lowers the ______ energy barrier so more collisions succeed | rate goes ______ |

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** Two cups each get one effervescent (Alka-Seltzer–type) tablet in the same amount of water. Cup 1 uses **ice water (5 °C)**; Cup 2 uses **hot water (50 °C)**. Cup 2 finishes fizzing far sooner. Explain why, using collision theory.

**Step 1 — Identify the factor that changed:**

The only variable changed is the __________ of the water. (The tablet, the amount of water, and the tablet form are all the same.)

**Step 2 — Describe what that factor does to the particles:**

Hotter water means the particles have more __________ energy, so they move __________.

**Step 3 — Connect to collisions (two effects):**

Faster-moving particles collide **more __________** per second AND each collision carries **more __________**, so more collisions clear the __________ energy barrier.

**Step 4 — State the effect on rate:**

More successful collisions per second means the reaction __________ is higher, so Cup 2 finishes sooner.

**Step 5 — Because / But / So:**

The hot-water tablet reacts faster
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name what heat does to the particles; "but" should note that the amount/type of chemical did NOT change; "so" should state how the rate changed and why.

## Summary

Complete the Because / But / So sentence about the anchor phenomenon:

> A glow stick glows brighter but for a shorter time in hot water than in cold water
> because __________________________________________,
> but __________________________________________,
> so __________________________________________.

**Reminder:** Temperature, concentration, and surface area all speed up a reaction by sending **more** (or more energetic) collisions at the reactants. Only a **catalyst** changes the reaction itself — by **lowering the activation energy** so more everyday collisions succeed. A catalyst is not used up and does not appear in the overall equation.
