## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Energy and Matter:** In every chemical change, atoms are rearranged into new molecules, and energy is transferred __________ or __________ of the system. A potential energy diagram is the __________ of that energy as the reaction goes from reactants to products.

**Reading a potential energy diagram — the five parts:**

| Part | Where it is on the diagram |
|---|---|
| Reactants | the flat level on the __________ side |
| Products | the flat level on the __________ side |
| Activated complex | the point at the __________ of the curve |
| Activation energy (Ea) | the height from the __________ level up to the peak |
| ΔH | the change from the reactant level to the __________ level |

**Two key measurements (always read a *difference*, never an absolute height):**

- **Activation energy:**  Ea = (peak energy) − (__________ energy)
- **Heat of reaction:**  ΔH = (__________ energy) − (reactant energy)

**Exothermic vs. endothermic — read it off the picture:**

- If the products are **lower** than the reactants → energy was __________ → ΔH is __________ (negative) → **exothermic**.
- If the products are **higher** than the reactants → energy was __________ → ΔH is __________ (positive) → **endothermic**.

**The reference level (PS3-1):** The energy axis is a __________ scale. The reactants do **not** have to start at __________. What is physically real is the *differences* — the height of Ea and the size of ΔH.

A catalyst __________ the activation energy (the barrier) but does **not** change the reactant level, the product level, or __________. The reaction reaches the same finished state and releases the same total energy — just __________.

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A reaction has reactants at 40 kJ, an activated complex (peak) at 90 kJ, and products at 25 kJ.

**Step 1 — Identify the three energy levels from the diagram:**

Reactants = ____ kJ   Activated complex (peak) = ____ kJ   Products = ____ kJ

**Step 2 — Find the activation energy (peak minus reactants):**

Ea = ____ − ____ = ____ kJ

**Step 3 — Find ΔH (products minus reactants):**

ΔH = ____ − ____ = ____ kJ

**Step 4 — Classify the reaction:**

The products (____ kJ) are __________ than the reactants (____ kJ), so the reaction is __________ and ΔH is __________ (positive / negative).

**Step 5 — Add a catalyst:** A catalyst lowers Ea. If the new peak is 75 kJ, the new Ea = ____ − ____ = ____ kJ. Did ΔH change? __________

**Step 6 — Because / But / So:**

A spark is needed to start this reaction
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name the activation-energy barrier; "but" should note that the products end up lower (energy is released overall); "so" should explain that the spark gets the first molecules over the barrier and then the reaction sustains itself.

> A catalyst makes a reaction go faster

**Reminders:**

- Activation energy is measured from the **reactant level** up to the peak — *not* from the bottom of the axis.
- A catalyst lowers **Ea only**. Reactants, products, and ΔH are unchanged.
- "Exothermic" and "needs a spark to start" can both be true at once: ΔH < 0 overall, but Ea must still be supplied to begin.
