## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

All three items use the labeled exothermic diagram: reactants **30 kJ**, activated complex (peak) **85 kJ**, products **10 kJ**; the catalyzed path has a peak of **60 kJ**.

**1. Activation energy of the uncatalyzed reaction:**

Ea = peak − reactants = 85 − 30 = **55 kJ**.

Step-by-step: the activation energy is the height of the barrier measured from the *reactant* level (30 kJ) up to the peak (85 kJ), so Ea = 55 kJ.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct value (55 kJ); 1 point for showing the subtraction *from the reactant level* (peak − reactants), not from zero. A student who answers "85 kJ" read Ea as the peak height above the axis — provide feedback: Ea starts at the reactants, not at the bottom of the graph. NYSSLS: DC1.B.

**2. ΔH and classification:**

ΔH = products − reactants = 10 − 30 = **−20 kJ**. The reaction is **exothermic** because the products (10 kJ) are *lower* than the reactants (30 kJ) — the system released 20 kJ.

*Rubric:* 1 point for ΔH = −20 kJ (accept "−20 kJ" or "20 kJ released"); 1 point for "exothermic" with the reason that products are lower than reactants. A sign error ("+20") with correct reasoning earns partial credit; remind the student that ΔH = products − reactants, so a lower product level gives a negative value. NYSSLS: DC1.B.

**3. Catalyzed activation energy and the effect on ΔH:**

Catalyzed Ea = peak − reactants = 60 − 30 = **30 kJ**.
The catalyst lowered Ea by 55 − 30 = **25 kJ**.
ΔH did **not** change — it is still −20 kJ, because the catalyst did not move the reactant level (30 kJ) or the product level (10 kJ).

*Rubric:* 1 point for the catalyzed Ea (30 kJ); 1 point for stating ΔH is unchanged (−20 kJ) *with* the reason that the reactant and product levels did not move. A student who says the catalyst changed ΔH has the central misconception — flag it for a one-on-one: the catalyst lowers only the barrier. NYSSLS: DC1.B; PS3-1.

## Exit Ticket Answer

The Exit Ticket uses a **new endothermic** diagram — reactants **20 kJ**, activated complex (peak) **70 kJ**, products **50 kJ** — deliberately different from the exothermic diagram practiced during the lesson, so students must read a fresh diagram rather than recall a number.

**(a) Activation energy:**

Ea = peak − reactants = 70 − 20 = **50 kJ**.

The only operation is reading the peak (70 kJ) and the reactant level (20 kJ) and subtracting.

*Rubric:* 1 point for 50 kJ; 1 point for showing peak − reactants (not "70 kJ"). A student who writes 70 kJ measured from the bottom of the axis instead of from the reactant level — reteach the from-the-reactants rule. NYSSLS: DC1.B.

**(b) ΔH and classification:**

ΔH = products − reactants = 50 − 20 = **+30 kJ**. The reaction is **endothermic** because the products (50 kJ) are *higher* than the reactants (20 kJ) — the system absorbed 30 kJ of energy.

*Rubric:* 1 point for ΔH = +30 kJ (accept "+30 kJ" or "30 kJ absorbed"); 1 point for "endothermic" justified by the products sitting *higher* than the reactants. A student who says "exothermic" misread the relative heights — point to the diagram: products are above reactants here, the opposite of the practice diagram. NYSSLS: DC1.B.

**(c) Why a catalyst speeds the reaction without changing ΔH:**

*Full-credit model answer:* A catalyst lowers the activation energy — the height of the barrier the reactants must climb — so more collisions have enough energy to react and the reaction goes faster; but the catalyst does not move the reactant level (20 kJ) or the product level (50 kJ), so ΔH stays +30 kJ.

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that (1) says the catalyst lowers the activation energy / barrier and (2) notes that the reactant and product levels (and therefore ΔH) are unchanged earns full credit. "It just makes it faster" with no mention of the activation energy earns partial credit.

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for identifying that the catalyst lowers Ea (the barrier), 1 point for stating that ΔH is unchanged because the reactant and product levels do not move. NYSSLS: DC1.B; PS3-1.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- A specific concept that clicked — "I finally get that 'releases energy' and 'needs a spark' are two different parts of the same graph" — rather than a generic "I learned about diagrams." Specificity signals real transfer.
- Any mention of the whiteboard build (BTC) or a groupmate who helped — "my group caught that I drew the catalyst lowering the products instead of the peak" — signals that the visibly-random-groups structure supported learning. Note these students.
- Responses connecting the activation-energy "hump" back to the opening-circle story (a hard push to start something) show the strongest everyday-to-science transfer.

If a student's Exit Ticket reads Ea as the peak height above the axis (e.g., "70 kJ" instead of 50 kJ), the error is in the *from-the-reactant-level* reading, not the arithmetic — a 30-second check at the start of the next lesson, pointing to where the Ea arrow begins, should resolve it. If a student claims a catalyst changes ΔH, address the misconception directly before the equilibrium lessons build on it.
