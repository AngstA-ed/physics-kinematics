**The stoichiometry road map (use this for every problem):**

> **grams (given)**  →  ÷ molar mass  →  **moles**  →  × mole ratio  →  **moles**  →  × molar mass  →  **grams (wanted)**

- To cross *between* two substances (moles ↔ moles): use the __________ from the balanced equation.

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A ribbon of **4.86 g Mg** reacts completely with excess hydrochloric acid. What mass of H₂ gas is produced?
Equation: **Mg + 2 HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂.**  Molar masses: Mg = 24.3 g/mol, H₂ = 2.0 g/mol.

**Step 1 — Convert grams of Mg to moles of Mg (÷ molar mass):**

> 4.86 g Mg × ( 1 mol Mg / ___ g Mg ) = ______ mol Mg

**Step 2 — Cross from Mg to H₂ using the mole ratio (from the coefficients):**

The coefficient on Mg is ___ and on H₂ is ___, so the mole ratio Mg : H₂ is ___ : ___.

> ______ mol Mg × ( ___ mol H₂ / ___ mol Mg ) = ______ mol H₂

**Step 3 — Convert moles of H₂ to grams of H₂ (× molar mass):**

> ______ mol H₂ × ( ___ g H₂ / 1 mol H₂ ) = ______ g H₂

**Step 4 — One-line chain (all together):**

> 4.86 g Mg × (1 mol Mg / ___ g) × (___ mol H₂ / ___ mol Mg) × (___ g H₂ / 1 mol H₂) = ______ g H₂

**Step 5 — Because / But / So**

The mole ratio step (× mol H₂ / mol Mg) is the part that comes from the balanced equation
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should say where the ratio comes from (the coefficients); "but" should name what molar mass does instead (grams ↔ moles); "so" should state why you cannot skip the mole ratio when crossing substances.
