## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. Mg + CuSO₄ (magnesium into copper(II) sulfate):**

- **React or no reaction:** Reaction occurs.
- **Reason:** Magnesium is *above* copper on the activity series (Mg is more active than Cu), so magnesium replaces copper from its compound.
- **Balanced products:** Mg + CuSO₄ → MgSO₄ + Cu. Magnesium goes into solution as Mg²⁺ (pairing with SO₄²⁻ → MgSO₄); copper comes out as free metal. The equation is already balanced (1 Mg, 1 Cu, 1 SO₄ on each side). Observable evidence: a reddish-brown copper coating forms and the magnesium strip dissolves.

*Rubric:* 1 point for "reaction occurs" with the correct activity-series reason (Mg above Cu); 1 point for the correctly balanced products (MgSO₄ + Cu). Partial credit if the reason is correct but the products are unbalanced or formulas are wrong (e.g., MgSO₄ written as Mg₂SO₄). NYSSLS: PS1-2.

**2. Cu + ZnSO₄ (copper into zinc sulfate):**

- **React or no reaction:** No reaction.
- **Reason:** Copper is *below* zinc on the activity series (Cu is less active than Zn), so copper cannot replace zinc from its compound.
- **Balanced products:** none — write "no reaction." This is exactly the second beaker from the phenomenon.

*Rubric:* 1 point for "no reaction"; 1 point for the correct reason (Cu is below/less active than Zn). A student who writes products here has applied the rule backwards — note the one-way nature of single replacement. NYSSLS: PS1-2.

**3. Zn + MgSO₄ (zinc into magnesium sulfate):**

- **React or no reaction:** No reaction.
- **Reason:** Zinc is *below* magnesium on the activity series (Zn is less active than Mg), so zinc cannot replace magnesium from its compound.
- **Balanced products:** none — write "no reaction."

*Rubric:* 1 point for "no reaction"; 1 point for the correct reason (Zn is below/less active than Mg). NYSSLS: PS1-2.

**Note on the Revise Your Model prediction (Cu + AgNO₃):** Copper is *above* silver on the activity series, so a reaction occurs: Cu + 2 AgNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2 Ag. Copper goes into solution as Cu²⁺ (requiring two NO₃⁻ ions → Cu(NO₃)₂), so silver must be balanced with a coefficient of 2. This shows that copper, which *lost* to zinc and magnesium, still *beats* silver — activity is relative to the specific pair.

## Exit Ticket Answer

**(a) Will Fe react with CuSO₄?**

- **Reaction occurs.** Iron is *above* copper on the activity series (Fe is more active than Cu), so iron replaces copper.
- **Balanced products:** Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu. Iron goes into solution as Fe²⁺ (pairing with SO₄²⁻ → FeSO₄); copper comes out as free metal. The equation is balanced as written (1 Fe, 1 Cu, 1 SO₄ each side).

*Rubric:* 1 point for "reaction occurs" with the correct reason (Fe above Cu); 1 point for the correctly balanced products (FeSO₄ + Cu). Partial credit for the correct prediction but a wrong formula (e.g., Fe₂SO₄ or Fe₂(SO₄)₃ — Fe is Fe²⁺ here, so FeSO₄). NYSSLS: PS1-2.

**(b) Will Ag react with Cu(NO₃)₂?**

- **No reaction.** Silver is *below* copper on the activity series (Ag is less active than Cu), so silver cannot replace copper from its compound. Sample one-sentence reasoning: *"Silver sits below copper on the activity series, so silver is less active than copper and cannot push copper out of solution."*

*Rubric:* 1 point for "no reaction"; 1 point for the reasoning that correctly cites silver's lower position / lower activity relative to copper. NYSSLS: PS1-2.

**(c) Why does the activity series let you predict the outcome before you run the reaction?**

*Full-credit model answer:* Because the activity series ranks every metal from most active to least active, you can compare the two metals' positions: if the free metal is higher (more active) it will replace the metal in the compound, and if it is lower it will not — so the same one rule predicts the result of any single-replacement pairing without testing it.

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that says the activity series is a *ranking* (or pattern) and that you predict by *comparing positions* — more active replaces less active — earns full credit. Responses that only restate "more active wins" without connecting it to using the ranked list to compare two specific metals receive partial credit.

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for naming the activity series as a ranking/pattern of metals by activity; 1 point for stating that you predict by comparing the two metals' relative positions (more active replaces less active). NYSSLS: PS1-2.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- A student who names a *specific* prediction they got wrong at the start (most commonly "I predicted every metal reacts with every solution") and explains that the evidence — the no-reaction wells, or the "own salt" diagonal — changed their mind. This signals the active-learning arc landed: the rule came from their data, not from being told.
- Responses that name a partner who helped catch a wrong prediction or spot the diagonal pattern — this is evidence that the micro-lab's collaborative structure supported learning.
- Students who connect the one-way reaction to relative *position* on the ladder (rather than to weight, shine, or "strength") show the strongest conceptual transfer.

If a student writes that "nothing changed" or that "every metal reacts," that is a content flag, not just a reflection gap: their prediction grid likely still shows all-R. Plan a brief one-on-one at the start of the next lesson to re-walk one no-reaction case (e.g., copper in zinc sulfate) against the ladder before the predict-the-product assessment.
