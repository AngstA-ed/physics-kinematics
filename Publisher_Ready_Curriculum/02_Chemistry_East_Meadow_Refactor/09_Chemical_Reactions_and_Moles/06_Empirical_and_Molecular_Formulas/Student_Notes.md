## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Scale, Proportion, and Quantity:** A chemical formula is a statement of __________ (proportion), not amount. You cannot read it straight off the masses, because a heavier atom contributes more __________ per atom. To compare *atom counts* fairly, you must first convert each element's mass to __________.

**Why mass fools you:** Magnesium oxide is about 60% magnesium by mass — but the formula is **MgO**, a 1-to-1 atom ratio. The mass looks lopsided because one magnesium atom (24.3 g/mol) is __________ than one oxygen atom (16.0 g/mol). Equal __________ means equal numbers of atoms, even when the masses are unequal.

**The four-step routine for an empirical formula:**

1. Write the __________ (grams) of each element. *(For percent problems, assume a __________ g sample so each percent becomes grams.)*
2. Divide each mass by the element's __________ → moles of each element.
3. Divide every mole value by the __________ value → a ratio.
4. Round the ratio to whole numbers → these are the __________ in the empirical formula.

> **Watch out:** if a ratio comes out like 1 : 1.5, you may **not** round 1.5 away. Multiply *both* numbers by a small whole number (here ×2 → 2 : 3) to clear it.

**From empirical to molecular formula:**

- The empirical formula is the __________ whole-number ratio.
- The molecular formula is a whole-number __________ of the empirical formula.
- Multiplier = (molar mass) ÷ (__________ formula mass). Multiply every subscript by it.

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A compound is **40.0% carbon, 6.7% hydrogen, and 53.3% oxygen** by mass. Its molar mass is **180 g/mol**. Find the empirical formula and the molecular formula.

**Step 1 — Assume 100 g of sample, so each percent becomes grams:**

C = _____ g   H = _____ g   O = _____ g

**Step 2 — Divide each mass by its molar mass to get moles:**

| Element | Mass (g) | ÷ molar mass (g/mol) | = moles |
|---|---|---|---|
| C | 40.0 | 12.0 | ___ |
| H | 6.7 | 1.0 | ___ |
| O | 53.3 | 16.0 | ___ |

**Step 3 — Divide every mole value by the smallest:**

The smallest mole value is ___ . Dividing each by it:

C: ___ ÷ ___ = ___   H: ___ ÷ ___ = ___   O: ___ ÷ ___ = ___

**Step 4 — Write the empirical formula from the ratio:**

Ratio C : H : O = ___ : ___ : ___   →   Empirical formula: ___________

**Step 5 — Use the molar mass to find the molecular formula:**

Empirical formula mass = (1 × 12.0) + (2 × 1.0) + (1 × 16.0) = ___ g/mol

Multiplier = 180 ÷ ___ = ___

Molecular formula = (empirical) × multiplier = ___________

**Step 6 — Because / But / So**

The molecular formula is six times the empirical formula
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should explain what the molar mass tells you; "but" should note that CH₂O alone weighs only 30 g/mol; "so" should give the multiplier and the final formula.
