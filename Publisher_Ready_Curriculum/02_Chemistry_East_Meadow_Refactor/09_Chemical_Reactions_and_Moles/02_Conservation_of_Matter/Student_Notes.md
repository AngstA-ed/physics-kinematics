## Worked Example

**Scenario:** Confirm that mass is conserved when hydrogen burns in oxygen to form water.

> **2 H₂ + O₂ → 2 H₂O**   (atomic masses: H = 1, O = 16)

**Step 1 — Find the gram formula mass of each species:**

- H₂ = (___ × 2) = ___ g/mol
- O₂ = (___ × 2) = ___ g/mol
- H₂O = (___ × 2) + ___ = ___ g/mol

**Step 2 — Multiply each by its coefficient and total the reactant side:**

|---|---|---|---|

| **Reactant total** | | | ___ |

**Step 3 — Multiply each by its coefficient and total the product side:**

| Product | Coefficient | GFM (g/mol) | Mass contribution (g/mol) |
|---|---|---|---|
| H₂O | 2 | ___ | ___ |
| **Product total** | | | ___ |

**Step 4 — Compare:**

Reactant total = ___ g/mol.  Product total = ___ g/mol.  Are they equal? ___

**Step 5 — Appositive sentence:**

The Law of Conservation of Mass — _________________________ — means the ___ g/mol of reactants equals the ___ g/mol of products.

**Step 6 — Because / But / So**

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name atoms being rearranged (not created); "but" should acknowledge that a brand-new substance was made; "so" should land on equal total mass / conserved.

**Reminder:** A balanced equation is the *mathematical* statement of conservation of mass. Equal atom counts on both sides force equal total mass on both sides. If a balance reading changes during a reaction, matter crossed the system **boundary** — it was not created or destroyed.
