## Phenomenon

A pad of fine steel wool sits on a balance and reads **1.40 g**. Your teacher touches a 9-volt battery to it; the strands glow and "burn." After it cools, the same steel wool is re-massed: **1.52 g**. It got *heavier*. Burning a log leaves a pile of ash that weighs *less* than the log — but burning steel wool makes it *more*. Nothing was added by hand.

![Particle diagram for the reaction 4 Fe plus 3 O2 yields 2 Fe2O3. The left 'Before' panel shows four purple iron atoms and three bonded blue oxygen-pair molecules, tallied as 4 Fe atoms and 6 O atoms. An arrow points to the 'After' panel showing two iron(III) oxide units, each two purple iron atoms and three blue oxygen atoms, tallied again as 4 Fe atoms and 6 O atoms. The title reads 'Atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed.'](figures/steel_wool_particle_conservation.png)

**Driving question:** If atoms are never created or destroyed, why does burning steel wool make it heavier?
