## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. H₂ + Cl₂ → HCl**

Balanced: **H₂ + Cl₂ → 2 HCl**

| | H | Cl |
|---|---|---|
| Left | 2 | 2 |
| Right | 2 | 2 |

Step-by-step: As written, the left has 2 H (H₂) and 2 Cl (Cl₂), but the right has only 1 H and 1 Cl (one HCl). Placing a coefficient of 2 in front of HCl gives 2 H and 2 Cl on the right. Every element now matches. The coefficient of 1 in front of H₂ and Cl₂ is understood and not written.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct coefficient (2 HCl); 1 point for a completed atom-count check showing H: 2 = 2 and Cl: 2 = 2. A student who writes H₂ + Cl₂ → HCl₂ or H₂Cl₂ changed a subscript and invented a substance — provide feedback: balance only by adding coefficients in front, never by changing subscripts. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7 (1.a.iii), PS1-2.

**2. Mg + O₂ → MgO**

Balanced: **2 Mg + O₂ → 2 MgO**

| | Mg | O |
|---|---|---|
| Left | 2 | 2 |
| Right | 2 | 2 |

Step-by-step: O₂ supplies 2 oxygen atoms on the left, but one MgO holds only 1 oxygen, so place a coefficient of 2 in front of MgO → 2 O on the right (and 2 Mg on the right). Now magnesium is off (1 on the left, 2 on the right), so place a 2 in front of Mg. Final check: Mg 2 = 2, O 2 = 2. This is the magnesium-burning reaction from the demonstration — the oxygen atoms come from the air, which is why the sealed mass rises.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct coefficients (2 Mg and 2 MgO); 1 point for the completed atom-count check. A common error is 2 Mg + O₂ → 2 MgO written as Mg + O₂ → MgO₂ (a subscript change). If this appears, note the subscript-vs-coefficient error. Partial credit (1 of 2) for balancing oxygen but forgetting to re-count and fix magnesium. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7 (1.a.iii), PS1-2.

**3. CH₄ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O**

Balanced: **CH₄ + 2 O₂ → CO₂ + 2 H₂O**

| | C | H | O |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left | 1 | 4 | 4 |
| Right | 1 | 4 | 4 |

Step-by-step: Carbon is already balanced (1 = 1). Hydrogen: CH₄ has 4 H on the left, but one H₂O has only 2 H — place a 2 in front of H₂O so the right has 4 H. Now total the oxygen on the right: 2 (from CO₂) + 2 (from 2 H₂O) = 4 O. The left has only 2 O (one O₂), so place a 2 in front of O₂ → 4 O on the left. Final check: C 1 = 1, H 4 = 4, O 4 = 4.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct coefficients (2 O₂ and 2 H₂O); 1 point for the completed three-element atom-count check. A student who balances oxygen before hydrogen and gets stuck should be reminded to save oxygen for last because it appears in two products. A student who gets 4 on the right oxygen but leaves O₂ at 1 has not finished — indicate the missing coefficient on O₂. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7 (1.a.iii), PS1-2.

## Exit Ticket Answer

**(a) Balance N₂ + H₂ → NH₃:**

Balanced: **N₂ + 3 H₂ → 2 NH₃**

| | N | H |
|---|---|---|
| Left | 2 | 6 |
| Right | 2 | 6 |

Step-by-step: N₂ gives 2 nitrogen atoms on the left, but one NH₃ has only 1 N — place a 2 in front of NH₃ so the right has 2 N. With 2 NH₃, the right now has 2 × 3 = 6 H atoms. The left has only 2 H (one H₂), so place a 3 in front of H₂ → 6 H on the left. Final check: N 2 = 2, H 6 = 6. This equation (the synthesis of ammonia) does **not** appear anywhere on the worksheet, so students must apply the method to a new reaction rather than recall a practiced answer.

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for the correct coefficient set (N₂ + 3 H₂ → 2 NH₃; accept the equation written with an explicit 1 in front of N₂); 1 point for a completed atom-count check showing N: 2 = 2 and H: 6 = 6. Common errors: placing 2 in front of NH₃ but forgetting to update H₂ to 3 (yields N balanced, H unbalanced) — award 1 of 2 and note the re-count step; or changing the subscript on NH₃ (e.g., NH₂) — note the subscript-vs-coefficient rule. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7 (1.a.iii), PS1-2.

**(b) One-sentence conservation-of-mass explanation:**

*Full-credit model answer:* By the Law of Conservation of Mass, atoms are neither created nor destroyed in a reaction — they are only rearranged — so every atom present in the reactants must also appear in the products, which is why the count of each element must be the same on both sides of a balanced equation.

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that uses the term "conservation of mass" (or states that matter/atoms are not created or destroyed) and connects it to equal atom counts on both sides earns full credit. Responses that say only "both sides have to be equal" without naming conservation of mass or explaining *why* should receive partial credit.

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for correctly invoking conservation of mass (atoms/matter neither created nor destroyed, only rearranged); 1 point for linking it to the requirement that each element's atom count is equal on both sides. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7 (1.a.i), PS1-2.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any mention of the burning phenomenon (steel wool or magnesium gaining mass) connected to atoms being rearranged rather than destroyed — students who link the surprising balance reading to conservation of mass are showing the phenomenon landed as evidence, not just a demo.
- Responses that name the coefficient-vs-subscript distinction ("I learned you only change the big number out front") — these students have internalized the procedural rule that drives the rest of the unit.
- Students who name a partner who helped them catch a balancing error or build the counter model are signaling that the ACTIVE LEARNING group structure supported their learning — note this as evidence the manipulative model is working.

If a student's Exit Ticket shows correct atom counting but a missing or incomplete coefficient update (e.g., 2 NH₃ but H₂ left at 1), the error is in the re-count step, not the concept — a brief reminder to re-count every element after each change should suffice. If a student changed subscripts to balance, address the subscript-vs-coefficient distinction directly before the next lesson, since all later stoichiometry depends on it.
