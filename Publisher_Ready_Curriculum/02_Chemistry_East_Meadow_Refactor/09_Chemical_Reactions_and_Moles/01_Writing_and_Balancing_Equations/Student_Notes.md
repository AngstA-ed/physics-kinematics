## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Energy and Matter (conservation):** In a chemical reaction, atoms are never __________ or __________ — they are only __________. So the number of each kind of atom is the __________ before and after the reaction. That is the Law of Conservation of __________.

**Two kinds of numbers in a formula:**

- The **subscript** is the __________ number, written low. It tells how many atoms of an element are in __________ formula unit. We **never** change subscripts when balancing, because that changes the __________.
- The **coefficient** is the __________ number, written in front. It tells how many __________ of the whole molecule we have. A coefficient multiplies __________ atom in the formula behind it.

Example: In **2 H₂O**, the coefficient 2 means ___ water molecules, which is ___ H atoms and ___ O atoms.

**How to balance an equation:**

1. Write the unbalanced equation and **count the atoms** of each element on each side.
2. Adjust a __________ in front of a formula to fix the element that doesn't match.
3. **Re-count every element** — changing one coefficient can throw another off.
4. Repeat until every element has the __________ number of atoms on both sides.
5. Use the smallest __________ whole numbers.

> **Strategy tip:** Balance an element that appears in only one place on each side first. Save **oxygen** and **hydrogen** for last, since they often appear in more than one molecule.

**State-of-matter symbols** (written in parentheses after each formula):

- (s) = __________   (l) = __________   (g) = __________   (aq) = __________ (dissolved in water)

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** Balance the combustion of methane: **CH₄ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O**

**Step 1 — Count the atoms as written (unbalanced):**

| | C | H | O |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left | 1 | 4 | 2 |
| Right | 1 | 2 | ___ (2 from CO₂ + ___ from H₂O) |

**Step 2 — Balance hydrogen.** Left has 4 H, right has only 2 H. Put a coefficient of ___ in front of H₂O.

Now the right has ___ H atoms. Hydrogen is balanced.

**Step 3 — Re-count oxygen.** With 2 H₂O on the right, oxygen on the right = 2 (from CO₂) + ___ (from 2 H₂O) = ___ O atoms. Left has only 2 O atoms. Put a coefficient of ___ in front of O₂.

**Step 4 — Final atom-count check:**

| | C | H | O |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left | 1 | 4 | ___ |
| Right | 1 | ___ | ___ |

Balanced equation: **______ CH₄ + ______ O₂ → ______ CO₂ + ______ H₂O**

**Step 5 — Because / But / So:**

I adjusted coefficients and not subscripts
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should explain what a coefficient does; "but" should explain what changing a subscript would do instead; "so" should state why coefficients are the right tool.

## Summary

Complete the sentence:

> A chemical equation is **balanced** when _______________________________________________, which must be true because of the Law of Conservation of __________.

Complete the Because / But / So sentence:

> When something burns, the atoms seem to disappear
> because __________________________________________,
> but __________________________________________,
> so __________________________________________.

**Reminder:** Balance with **coefficients** (the big numbers in front), never **subscripts** (the small numbers inside). A coefficient multiplies *every* atom in the formula behind it.
