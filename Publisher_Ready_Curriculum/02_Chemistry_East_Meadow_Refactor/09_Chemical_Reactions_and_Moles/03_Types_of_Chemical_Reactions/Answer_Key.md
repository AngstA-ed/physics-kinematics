# Types of Chemical Reactions — Answer Key

## Cover

Unit: Chemical Reactions & Moles — Lesson 03: Types of Chemical Reactions
East Meadow Schools × Valley Stream Central High School District
NYSSLS tag: HS-PS1-2 (use patterns of attraction to predict reaction type; identify reactants/products and formulas)

## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. **2 Na + Cl₂ → 2 NaCl — Type: synthesis.** *Pattern:* two substances (an element and an element) combine into a single product, A + B → AB. The reactive metal sodium gives up its one valence electron to chlorine, forming the ionic compound sodium chloride. · *Rubric:* names synthesis AND cites "two combine into one" → full credit; names synthesis without the pattern reason → partial. · NYSSLS: HS-PS1-2.

2. **2 HgO → 2 Hg + O₂ — Type: decomposition.** *Pattern:* a single compound breaks apart into two simpler substances, AB → A + B. One reactant in, two products out, is the giveaway. · *Rubric:* names decomposition AND cites "one breaks into simpler substances" → full credit. · NYSSLS: HS-PS1-2.

3. **Mg + 2 AgNO₃ → Mg(NO₃)₂ + 2 Ag — Type: single replacement.** *Pattern:* a free element (Mg) trades places with one element in a compound — magnesium replaces silver, which is set free as a metal. A free element + a compound → one swap. · *Rubric:* names single replacement AND identifies the free element doing one swap → full credit; says "double replacement" → no credit (only one swap, and one reactant is a free element). · NYSSLS: HS-PS1-2.

4. **C₃H₈ + 5 O₂ → 3 CO₂ + 4 H₂O — Type: combustion.** *Pattern:* a hydrocarbon fuel (propane) burns in oxygen, producing carbon dioxide and water. The CO₂ + H₂O products are the signature of complete combustion. · *Rubric:* names combustion AND cites "fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + water" → full credit. · NYSSLS: HS-PS1-2.

5. **Predict the products — Pb(NO₃)₂ + 2 KI → PbI₂ + 2 KNO₃.** Swap partners: Pb²⁺ pairs with I⁻ to give **PbI₂**; K⁺ pairs with NO₃⁻ to give KNO₃. **The solid (precipitate) is PbI₂** — lead(II) iodide, a bright yellow solid that does not stay dissolved. (KNO₃ stays in solution.) · *Rubric:* correct partner-swap products → full credit; identifying PbI₂ as the precipitate → bonus point; if a student also balances it correctly, accept and praise. Accept the unbalanced product identities (PbI₂ + KNO₃) for full credit since balancing was not required. · NYSSLS: HS-PS1-2.

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) **2 Al + 3 CuCl₂ → 2 AlCl₃ + 3 Cu — single replacement.** A free element (aluminum) trades places with one element in a compound (it replaces copper, which is set free as a metal). The tell: one reactant is a *free element* and one is a *compound*, and only one swap occurs. *Patterns of attraction note (PS1-2):* aluminum is the more reactive metal and gives up its three valence electrons, displacing the less reactive copper.

(b) **2 KClO₃ → 2 KCl + 3 O₂ — decomposition.** A single compound (potassium chlorate) breaks apart into two simpler substances (potassium chloride and oxygen gas). One reactant in, two products out, AB → A + B, is the pattern.

(c) Sample full-credit response: *"Both pairs follow the same double-replacement pattern and swap partners, but silver chloride (from Cup 1) cannot stay dissolved so it appears as a white solid, while every product in Cup 2 stays dissolved — so the same pattern gives a visible reaction in one cup and no visible change in the other."* Any response that names the shared double-replacement *pattern* and explains that one cup's product is insoluble (a precipitate) earns full credit. The atoms are rearranged in both cups; only the solubility of the products differs.

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) 1 point for "single replacement," 1 point for citing the free-element + one-swap pattern — 2 points; partial credit for the correct type without the reason. (b) 1 point for "decomposition," 1 point for citing the one-reactant-breaks-into-two pattern — 2 points. (c) up to 2 points: 1 for naming the shared (double-replacement) pattern, 1 for explaining that only Cup 1's product is insoluble. Do not deduct for spelling. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-2.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine response. Look for:

- A specific, transferable pattern the student can now spot — "I can tell single replacement because one reactant is a free element and one is a compound" — rather than a memorized single example.
- Naming a specific groupmate, a station, or the gallery walk as the thing that helped them see a pattern — this signals the BTC collaborative structure landed.
- Any response that connects back to the opening-circle instant-change examples (bleach on a stain, vinegar and baking soda) shows the strongest transfer from everyday experience to formal classification.

If a student writes "I still can't tell the reaction types apart," flag them for the start of the next lesson — focus the one-on-one on the single-vs-double-replacement distinction (count the swaps), which is the most common stumbling block.
