## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A lab finds that a wooden artifact has **1/4** of the carbon-14 of a fresh sample. Carbon-14 has a half-life of 5,730 years. How old is the artifact?

**Step 1 — Match the fraction remaining to a number of half-lives:**

Fraction remaining = 1/4. Write 1/4 as a power of one-half: 1/4 = (1/2)^___

So the number of half-lives = ___

**Step 2 — Look up the half-life on Reference Table N:**

Half-life of carbon-14 = __________ years

**Step 3 — Find the total time elapsed:**

total time = number of half-lives × half-life

total time = ___ × __________ years = __________ years

**Step 4 — State the answer in a sentence:**

The artifact is about __________ years old, because its carbon-14 has been cut in half ___ times.

**Step 5 — Because / But / So**

The artifact has 1/4 of its original carbon-14
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should explain why 1/4 means 2 half-lives; "but" should note that we only see the fraction left, not the original amount; "so" should give the age in years.

> A big sample and a tiny sample of the same radioisotope both lose the same FRACTION in one half-life

**Reminder:** The number of half-lives is the **exponent** on 1/2 — NOT the denominator of the fraction. 1/4 = (1/2)² → **2** half-lives (not 4). 1/8 = (1/2)³ → **3** half-lives (not 8). Count how many times the sample was cut in half.
