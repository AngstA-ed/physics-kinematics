## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. Alpha decay of uranium-238:**

> ²³⁸₉₂U → ²³⁴₉₀Th + ⁴₂He

| | Mass number (top) | Atomic number (bottom) |
|---|---|---|
| Missing particle | 238 − 4 = **234** | 92 − 2 = **90** |

Missing nucleus: **²³⁴₉₀Th (thorium-234)**. Atomic number 90 is thorium on the Periodic Table. This is **natural** transmutation — uranium-238 decays spontaneously, with no incoming particle. Alpha decay is also a type of fission because a fragment (the ⁴₂He alpha particle) splits off the nucleus.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct nucleus (²³⁴₉₀Th, mass 234 and atomic number 90); 1 point for correctly labeling it natural transmutation. A student who writes atomic number 94 added instead of subtracting — the alpha particle *leaves*, so subtract. Partial credit for correct mass number but wrong element identification. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8.

**2. Beta decay of carbon-14:**

> ¹⁴₆C → ¹⁴₇N + ⁰₋₁e

| | Mass number (top) | Atomic number (bottom) |
|---|---|---|
| Missing particle | 14 − 0 = **14** | 6 − (−1) = **7** |

Missing nucleus: **¹⁴₇N (nitrogen-14)**. The atomic number went **up** by 1 (from 6 to 7) because a neutron inside the nucleus converted into a proton and emitted the beta particle (⁰₋₁e). The mass number stayed 14 because a proton and a neutron have nearly the same mass — only the charge balance changed.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct nucleus (¹⁴₇N); 1 point for stating the atomic number went up AND giving the neutron-to-proton reason. A student who writes ¹⁴₅B (boron) subtracted the −1 instead of subtracting a negative — watch the sign: 6 − (−1) = 7. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8.

**3. Fusion (D–T reaction):**

> ²₁H + ³₁H → ⁴₂He + ¹₀n

| | Mass number (top) | Atomic number (bottom) |
|---|---|---|
| Missing particle | (2 + 3) − 4 = **1** | (1 + 1) − 2 = **0** |

Missing particle: **¹₀n (one neutron)**. Two small nuclei (H-2 and H-3) **merged into one larger nucleus** (He-4), so this is fusion. The leftover particle is a single neutron — mass number 1, atomic number 0.

*Rubric:* 1 point for identifying the missing particle as ¹₀n (a neutron); 1 point for correctly stating that two nuclei merged into one (fusion). A student who writes the missing particle as a proton (¹₁H) missed the atomic-number balance — the bottom is already balanced by He (2 = 2), so the missing particle must have atomic number 0. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8.

## Exit Ticket Answer

**(a) Alpha decay of polonium-210:**

> ²¹⁰₈₄Po → ²⁰⁶₈₂Pb + ⁴₂He

| | Mass number (top) | Atomic number (bottom) |
|---|---|---|
| Missing particle | 210 − 4 = **206** | 84 − 2 = **82** |

Missing nucleus: **²⁰⁶₈₂Pb (lead-206)**. Atomic number 82 is lead on the Periodic Table. (Note: this Po-210 alpha decay is distinct from the worksheet's U-238 alpha decay, so students must balance a new equation rather than recall a practiced answer.)

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct nucleus (²⁰⁶₈₂Pb, mass 206 and atomic number 82); 1 point for correctly naming the element (lead). A student who writes atomic number 86 added instead of subtracting — the alpha particle leaves, so subtract 2. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8.

**(b) Classify fusion vs. fission:**

- **Equation 1: ⁶₃Li + ²₁H → 2 ⁴₂He is FUSION.** Two small nuclei (lithium-6 and hydrogen-2) combine and rearrange into helium nuclei — small nuclei merging into a more stable arrangement. (Check: mass 6 + 2 = 8 = 2 × 4 ✓; atomic 3 + 1 = 4 = 2 × 2 ✓.)
- **Equation 2: ²³⁹₉₄Pu + ¹₀n → ¹³⁴₅₄Xe + ¹⁰³₄₀Zr + 3 ¹₀n is FISSION.** One large nucleus (plutonium-239) is struck by a neutron and splits into two smaller fragments (Xe and Zr) plus extra neutrons — a large nucleus splitting into smaller pieces. (Check: mass 239 + 1 = 240 = 134 + 103 + 3 ✓; atomic 94 + 0 = 94 = 54 + 40 + 0 ✓.)

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for each correct label *with* a size-based justification. Full credit requires the reasoning ("two small nuclei merge" for fusion; "one large nucleus splits" for fission), not just the labels. Labeling correctly without justification earns 1 point of 2. A student who calls Equation 1 fission likely saw two product nuclei and assumed a split — redirect them to the small *reactant* nuclei merging. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8.

**(c) Why a nuclear reaction releases far more energy than a chemical reaction (appositive sentence):**

*Full-credit model answer:* "A nuclear reaction — a rearrangement of the protons and neutrons inside the nucleus — releases roughly 10⁵–10⁶ times more energy than a chemical reaction because it changes the powerful forces holding the nucleus together rather than the weaker bonds between atoms."

*Acceptable variations:* Any sentence that (1) uses appositive structure (a defining phrase set off by dashes or commas) and (2) attributes the larger energy release to changes *inside the nucleus* (strong nuclear forces) versus the chemical bonds *between atoms*, and/or cites the ~10⁵–10⁶× scale, earns full credit. A sentence that merely says "nuclear reactions release more energy" without the appositive structure OR without the inside-the-nucleus reasoning earns partial credit.

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for correct appositive structure (a renaming/defining phrase set off by dashes or commas); 1 point for the correct reason (energy comes from inside the nucleus / strong nuclear forces, ~10⁵–10⁶ times more than chemical bonds). NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any mention of the anchoring phenomenon (the Sun's fusion vs. the power plant's fission) and a connection to the balanced equations students worked — students who tie the abstract equation back to the real Sun or reactor are showing that the model is meaningful, not just arithmetic.
- Responses that use the appositive structure ("transmutation — the change of one element into another — …") are demonstrating the Hochman writing move from Phase 3 and Phase 4. Note these students as having integrated the vocabulary into their active sentence structure.
- Students who name a partner who helped them catch a balancing error (forgetting the incoming neutron, or subtracting a negative atomic number incorrectly) are signaling that the group investigation structure supported their learning — note this as evidence the collaborative ledger format is working.

If a student's Exit Ticket balances the mass numbers correctly but not the atomic numbers (or vice versa), the error is in checking only one of the two conserved totals — a brief one-on-one at the start of the next lesson should reinforce that *both* the top and the bottom must balance independently. If a student consistently adds when an emitted particle should be subtracted (e.g., gets atomic number 86 for Po-210 alpha decay), focus the check-in on the direction of change: an emitted particle *leaves* the nucleus, so its mass and charge are subtracted from the parent.
