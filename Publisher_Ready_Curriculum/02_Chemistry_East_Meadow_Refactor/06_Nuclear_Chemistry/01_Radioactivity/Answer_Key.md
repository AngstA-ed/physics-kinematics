## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. Alpha decay of uranium-238:**

> ²³⁸₉₂U → ²³⁴₉₀Th + ⁴₂He

- Emitted particle name: **alpha particle** (a helium-4 nucleus, ⁴₂He, mass 4, charge +2)
- Daughter element: **thorium (Th)** — atomic number 90

Step-by-step: the alpha particle carries away mass 4 and charge +2. Daughter mass = 238 − 4 = 234; daughter atomic number = 92 − 2 = 90 → thorium-234. Conservation check: top 234 + 4 = 238 ✓; bottom 90 + 2 = 92 ✓.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct daughter (²³⁴₉₀Th) with both A and Z right; 1 point for naming the emitted particle as an alpha particle (accept "alpha" or "helium nucleus" with the ⁴₂He symbol). Partial credit for a correct method with one transposed number. A student who keeps the element as uranium has the proton-number misconception — note that 90 protons is thorium, a different element. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8 (1.a.i–iii).

**2. Beta-minus decay of carbon-14:**

> ¹⁴₆C → ¹⁴₇N + ⁰₋₁e

*Why the atomic number rose:* Inside the nucleus a neutron converts into a proton plus the emitted electron (the beta particle). The new proton raises the atomic number from 6 to 7, while the emitted electron carries away the −1 charge so total charge is conserved (7 + (−1) = 6). The mass number stays 14 because the electron has essentially no mass. The daughter, with 7 protons, is **nitrogen-14**.

Conservation check: top 14 + 0 = 14 ✓; bottom 7 + (−1) = 6 ✓.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct daughter (¹⁴₇N); 1 point for an explanation that identifies the neutron → proton conversion (or simply that the daughter must gain a proton to conserve charge against the −1 electron). A student who writes carbon-13 or boron-13 moved Z the wrong direction — reinforce 6 − (−1) = 7. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8 (1.a.i–iii).

**3. Positron decay of sodium-22:**

> ²²₁₁Na → ²²₁₀Ne + ⁰₊₁e

*The atomic number went from 11 to 10*, because a proton inside the nucleus converted into a neutron plus the emitted positron (charge +1). Losing a proton drops the atomic number by 1, while the positron carries off the +1 charge so total charge is conserved (10 + 1 = 11). The mass number stays 22. The daughter, with 10 protons, is **neon-22**.

Conservation check: top 22 + 0 = 22 ✓; bottom 10 + 1 = 11 ✓.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct daughter (²²₁₀Ne); 1 point for stating that Z dropped to 10 with a correct reason (proton → neutron, or daughter loses a proton to conserve charge against the +1 positron). Common error: treating the positron like the electron and raising Z to 12 — reinforce that the positron's +1 charge moves Z in the opposite direction from beta-minus. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8 (1.a.i–iii).

## Exit Ticket Answer

**(a) Identify the emitted particle in ²²⁶₈₈Ra → ²²²₈₆Rn + ____:**

The missing particle has mass number 226 − 222 = 4 and charge 88 − 86 = +2, so it is an **alpha particle: ⁴₂He** (a helium-4 nucleus).

Conservation check: top 222 + 4 = 226 ✓; bottom 86 + 2 = 88 ✓.

*Rubric:* 1 point for naming it an alpha particle; 1 point for the correct symbol ⁴₂He with mass 4 and charge +2. Accept "helium nucleus / He-4." This nuclide (radium-226) is distinct from the worksheet practice, so the student must apply conservation, not recall. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8 (1.a.i–iii).

**(b) Balance ⁴⁰₁₉K → ____ + ⁰₋₁e and name the daughter:**

> ⁴⁰₁₉K → ⁴⁰₂₀Ca + ⁰₋₁e

Daughter mass = 40 − 0 = 40; daughter atomic number = 19 − (−1) = 20 → **calcium-40 (⁴⁰₂₀Ca)**. The daughter element is **calcium**.

Conservation check: top 40 + 0 = 40 ✓; bottom 20 + (−1) = 19 ✓.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct daughter nuclide ⁴⁰₂₀Ca (both A and Z); 1 point for naming the element calcium. A student who writes ⁴⁰₁₈Ar (argon) lowered Z instead of raising it — reinforce that beta-minus raises the atomic number by 1. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8 (1.a.i–iii).

**(c) Why paper stops an alpha particle but a gamma ray passes through:**

*Full-credit model answer:* An alpha particle is large and carries a +2 charge, so it collides strongly with the atoms in the paper and quickly loses its energy and stops; a gamma ray has no mass and no charge, so it interacts very little with matter and passes straight through the paper (only thick lead reduces it).

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that ties alpha's stopping power to its mass and/or charge (it ionizes/collides and stops), and gamma's penetration to its lack of mass and charge, earns full credit. Saying only "alpha is bigger" without connecting size/charge to why it stops earns partial credit.

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for explaining alpha is stopped because of its mass and +2 charge (collides with matter), 1 point for explaining gamma penetrates because it has no mass or charge. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8 (1.a.i–iii).

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any connection back to the phenomenon — Becquerel's fogged film or the cloud-chamber tracks — and the realization that the emission was *spontaneous* (no outside trigger). Students who name "I didn't know an atom could change on its own" are showing the core conceptual shift.
- Responses that name a partner who helped them catch a sign error on the bottom (charge) number, or who helped them see that the proton count sets the element. This signals the collaborative card-sort structure worked.
- Students who connect penetrating power to mass and charge (e.g., "I was surprised the heaviest particle was the easiest to block") show transfer from the shielding investigation to the particle model.

If a student's Exit Ticket shows the daughter nuclide with the atomic number moved the wrong direction (e.g., argon instead of calcium for K-40 beta decay), the error is in the *direction* of the charge shift, not the method — a 60-second check on "which way does beta-minus move Z?" at the start of the next lesson should be sufficient before half-life builds on this skill. If the mass numbers are wrong, revisit which particle carries mass (alpha = 4) versus essentially none (beta, positron, gamma).
