## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. **Sig-fig counts for the five values:**

   - **0.0040 → 2 sig figs.** The three leading zeros are place-holders; they locate the decimal but represent nothing the instrument measured. The 4 is a non-zero digit (1 sig fig). The trailing zero after the decimal is significant because it is a deliberate measurement claim — the person who wrote 0.0040 is saying the value is 4.0 thousandths, not just 4 thousandths. Total: 2. *Rubric:* 1 point for the correct count; 1 point for correctly distinguishing the leading zeros (not significant) from the trailing zero (significant). NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7 foundational.

   - **1500 → ambiguous; accept 2, 3, or 4 with appropriate justification.** Without a decimal point, there is no way to know whether the trailing zeros are significant or mere place-holders. The only fully credited answer is one that names the ambiguity: "1500 could have 2, 3, or 4 sig figs — we cannot tell from the notation." *Rubric:* 1 point for identifying the ambiguity; 0 points for a definite count without noting the ambiguity. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7 foundational.

   - **1500. → 4 sig figs.** The decimal point after the final zero is a convention that signals: "all four digits are intentional." The trailing zeros become significant. *Rubric:* 1 point for the correct count; 1 point for explaining the role of the decimal point. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7 foundational.

   - **0.03080 → 4 sig figs.** Leading zeros: 0 and 0 — not significant (place-holders). 3 — non-zero, significant. 0 — captive between 3 and 8, significant. 8 — non-zero, significant. 0 — trailing after the decimal, significant. Total: 3, 0 (captive), 8, 0 (trailing) = 4. *Rubric:* 1 point for the count; 1 point for correctly categorizing all four zero types encountered in the value. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7 foundational.

   - **102.0 → 4 sig figs.** 1 (non-zero), 0 (captive between 1 and 2), 2 (non-zero), 0 (trailing after decimal) = 4. *Rubric:* 1 point for the count; 1 point for identifying the captive zero and the trailing-decimal zero as distinct categories. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7 foundational.

2. **Rounding a multi-digit calculator result to the appropriate sig figs:**

   The two tools support 0.1 cm precision (3 sig figs for a typical reading like 8.47) and 1 cm precision (1 sig fig). The result must be limited to the *least* precise measurement — 1 sig fig — so 48.7263 rounds to **50 cm** (or 5 × 10¹ in scientific notation to make the sig-fig count unambiguous). *Common error:* students often round to "the one decimal place" rather than "one significant figure," giving 48.7 or 49 — neither is correct. *Rubric:* 1 point for the correctly rounded value; 1 point for identifying the limiting measurement and explaining why. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7 foundational.

3. **Scientific notation conversions:**

   - 0.000452 = **4.52 × 10⁻⁴** (decimal moved 4 places to the right → negative exponent; 3 sig figs from the three non-zero digits).
   - 92,500,000 = **9.25 × 10⁷** (decimal moved 7 places to the left → positive exponent; 3 sig figs if the trailing zeros are treated as ambiguous place-holders — accept 9.2500 × 10⁷ if a student argues the zeros are significant). *Rubric:* 1 point per correct conversion (coefficient correct, exponent correct, and exponent sign correct). NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7 foundational.

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) **0.03080 has 4 significant figures.** Walk through digit by digit: first 0 — leading, not significant; second 0 — leading, not significant; 3 — non-zero, significant; 0 — captive between 3 and 8, significant; 8 — non-zero, significant; final 0 — trailing after the decimal, significant. The four significant digits are: 3, 0, 8, 0.

(b) **48,500,000 = 4.85 × 10⁷.** Move the decimal seven places to the left. The coefficient (4.85) is between 1 and 10, and the exponent (7) is positive because the original number is greater than 1. Three significant figures.

(c) **7.86342 rounded to 3 significant figures = 7.86.** The first three significant figures are 7, 8, and 6. The next digit is 3, which is less than 5, so the 6 stays — no rounding up. Result: 7.86.

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) 1 point for the correct count (4); 1 bonus point for correctly categorizing all four digit-types. (b) 1 point for the correct coefficient (4.85); 1 point for the correct exponent including sign (10⁷). (c) 1 point for the correct rounded value; partial credit (0.5) if the student rounds to 7.9 (rounding correctly but misidentifying which digit is the third sig fig). NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7 foundational.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- A concrete connection to a context outside chemistry — cooking portions, woodworking measurements, financial rounding, health data — that shows the student grasps that sig figs are a claim-making practice, not just a chemistry rule.
- Any response that uses the word "claim" or "honest" in relation to digits shows the deepest transfer of the Scale, Proportion, and Quantity framing.
- Students who name a peer or a moment during the BTC board work as the thing that changed their thinking are showing that the collaborative structure landed — note these students as candidates for peer-teaching roles in subsequent lessons.

If a student leaves the reflection blank, check whether they engaged during the BTC sorting rounds. A student who was productive on the vertical surface but disengaged at the Exit Ticket may be showing fatigue with pencil-and-paper tasks, not a content gap.
