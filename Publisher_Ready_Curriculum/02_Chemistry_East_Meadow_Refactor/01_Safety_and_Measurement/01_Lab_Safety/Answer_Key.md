## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. **Hazard:** the test tube contains a heated liquid that can splash in any direction when tilted; pointing it at a partner puts another person in the splash zone. **Missing safety step:** point the test tube away from all people (including yourself) when heating. **Without it:** the boiling or superheated liquid could splash onto the partner's face, eyes, or skin, causing burns or eye injury. · *Rubric:* names the specific direction hazard AND states to redirect the tube away from people → full credit; names only goggles without addressing the direction problem → partial. · NYSSLS: SEP-3 / foundational.

2. **Hazard:** chemical splash to clothing and skin underneath. **Missing PPE:** apron (a chemical-resistant lab apron). **The apron would have prevented:** the chemical from soaking through clothing and contacting skin, reducing the risk of a chemical burn or irritation. · *Rubric:* names apron AND explains skin/clothing protection → full credit. · NYSSLS: SEP-3 / foundational.

3. **Hazard:** an acidic or reactive chemical that can dissolve or permeate standard latex gloves, contacting the skin underneath. **What the student should have done:** read the SDS before starting, specifically Section 8 (Exposure Controls / PPE), to identify the correct glove type. **Where that information lives:** the SDS — Section 8 specifies required glove material (e.g., nitrile, neoprene, or acid-resistant). · *Rubric:* names the SDS AND Section 8 → full credit; says "the SDS" without section → partial credit. · NYSSLS: SEP-3 / foundational.

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) **Goggles** — protect the eyes from the liquid splash; a splash that contacts an unprotected eye can cause serious eye irritation or damage. **Apron** — protects skin and clothing from the splash; if the liquid is corrosive or irritating, the apron prevents skin contact and chemical absorption through fabric.

(b) The **SDS (Safety Data Sheet)** — specifically Section 2 for the hazard statement and Section 4 for first-aid steps. Every chemical used in the lab has an SDS on file; looking it up before starting is standard practice.

(c) Sample full-credit response: *"Following the safety routine matters because it breaks the cause→effect chain — the hazard (the splash) still happens, but the PPE and procedures prevent the effect (an injury) from reaching the student."* Any equivalent response that uses cause/effect or hazard/prevent language and explains prevention (not just rule-following) earns full credit.

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) 1 point for each PPE item with a correct reason — 2 points total; partial credit for naming the item without the reason (1 point total). (b) 1 point for naming SDS; bonus for citing a specific section number. (c) 1 point for any honest, evidence-based explanation; full credit requires either cause/effect vocabulary or hazard/prevent vocabulary and a logical connection. NYSSLS: SEP-3 / foundational.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine response. Look for:

- A specific behavioral intention — "I will always put my goggles on before I open any chemical container" — rather than a generic statement like "I'll be safe."
- Naming a peer, a moment from the scavenger hunt, or the SDS reading as the thing that changed their thinking — this signals the lesson's collaborative structure landed.
- Any response that connects the hazard concept to their prior experience (the restorative circle story) shows the strongest transfer.

If a student writes "nothing changed," have a brief one-on-one conversation before the next lab — not punitive, but a low-stakes check on whether safety norms have been established.
