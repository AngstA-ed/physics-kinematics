## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Scale, Proportion, and Quantity:** A conversion factor is a __________ — a ratio equal to exactly __________ — that lets you cross between different scales. Multiplying by a conversion factor never changes the __________ quantity, only the __________ used to express it.

**The factor-label track — how it works:**

Each "car" in the track holds a quantity (number + unit). When a unit appears once in a __________ and once in a __________, the two cancel diagonally — they cross each other out. The unit that has not been canceled is the __________ unit.

**Setting up a conversion factor:**

A conversion factor can be written two ways:

| Option A (going mol → atoms) | Option B (going atoms → mol) |
|---|---|
| 6.02 × 10²³ atoms / 1 mol | 1 mol / 6.02 × 10²³ atoms |

Choose the orientation that puts the unit you want to __________ in the denominator.

**Avogadro's number (from the 2025 Reference Table):**

1 mol = 6.02 × 10²³ particles (atoms, molecules, or ions)

**Metric prefix conversion factors:**

| Prefix | Meaning | Conversion factor (going → base unit) |
|---|---|---|
| kilo- (k) | 10³ = 1000 | 1 km / 1000 m  (or 1000 m / 1 km) |
| centi- (c) | 10⁻² = 1/100 | 1 cm / 0.01 m  (or equivalently: 100 cm / 1 m) |
| milli- (m) | 10⁻³ = 1/1000 | 1 mL / 0.001 L  (or equivalently: 1000 mL / 1 L) |

For a metric conversion, the conversion factor you choose is the one that puts the __________ you want to cancel in the denominator.
