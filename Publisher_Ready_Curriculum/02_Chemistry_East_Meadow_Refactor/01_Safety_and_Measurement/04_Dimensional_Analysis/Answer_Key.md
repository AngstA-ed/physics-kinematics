## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. **Convert 2.5 km to centimeters — two-step factor-label track:**

   Track: 2.5 km × (1000 m / 1 km) × (100 cm / 1 m) = 250,000 cm

   Step-by-step: First conversion factor cancels km, leaving m. Second conversion factor cancels m, leaving cm. Numerically: 2.5 × 1000 = 2,500 m; 2,500 × 100 = 250,000 cm. Answer in scientific notation: 2.5 × 10⁵ cm.

   *Rubric:* 1 point for the correct numerical answer (250,000 cm or 2.5 × 10⁵ cm); 1 point for showing both conversion factors with units written at every step and the two cancellations marked. Full credit requires a visible unit check — km cancels in step 1, m cancels in step 2, cm survives. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7.

2. **Convert 0.75 mol to atoms — one-step factor-label track:**

   Track: 0.75 mol × (6.02 × 10²³ atoms / 1 mol) = 4.515 × 10²³ atoms

   The "mol" in the numerator of the starting quantity cancels with the "mol" in the denominator of the conversion factor. Surviving unit: atoms. Numerically: 0.75 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 4.515 × 10²³ atoms. Round to 2 significant figures (limited by 0.75): 4.5 × 10²³ atoms.

   *Rubric:* 1 point for the correct answer (accept 4.515 × 10²³ or 4.5 × 10²³; either is full credit with appropriate sig-fig notation); 1 point for writing the conversion factor with mol in the denominator and showing the cancellation explicitly. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7.

3. **Convert 250 mL to liters — one-step factor-label track:**

   Track: 250 mL × (1 L / 1000 mL) = 0.25 L

   The "mL" in the numerator of the starting quantity cancels with the "mL" in the denominator of the conversion factor. Surviving unit: L. Numerically: 250 / 1000 = 0.25 L.

   *Rubric:* 1 point for the correct numerical answer (0.25 L); 1 point for the complete track showing the conversion factor (1 L / 1000 mL) with the mL cancellation marked. A student who writes (1000 mL / 1 L) and gets 250,000 mL²/L should receive written feedback identifying the unit error — the surviving unit would not be liters. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7.

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) **Convert 3.0 mol of Cu to atoms.**

Track: 3.0 mol Cu × (6.02 × 10²³ atoms / 1 mol) = 1.806 × 10²⁴ atoms

Round to 2 significant figures (limited by 3.0): **1.8 × 10²⁴ atoms.**

The "mol" units cancel: mol appears in the numerator of the starting quantity and the denominator of the conversion factor. Only "atoms" survives.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct numerical answer (accept 1.806 × 10²⁴ or 1.8 × 10²⁴); 1 point for the complete factor-label track with mol shown canceling; 0 points if no unit track is shown, even if the number is correct. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7.

(b) **Convert 5,400 mg to grams.**

Track: 5,400 mg × (1 g / 1000 mg) = 5.4 g

The "mg" units cancel (mg in numerator of starting quantity, mg in denominator of conversion factor). Only "g" survives. Numerically: 5,400 / 1000 = 5.4 g.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct answer (5.4 g); 1 point for writing the conversion factor (1 g / 1000 mg) with the cancellation shown. A student who inverts the factor and gets 5,400,000 mg²/g should receive feedback identifying the unit error. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7.

(c) **How do you know your units are right?**

*Full-credit model answer:* The unwanted units cancel diagonally (they appear once in the numerator and once in the denominator), so only the target unit survives at the end of the track.

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that identifies the mechanism of diagonal cancellation (top and bottom, numerator and denominator) as the check earns full credit. Responses that say only "the units match" or "I checked my work" are insufficient — the answer must reference the cancellation structure.

*Rubric:* 1 point for mentioning that the unwanted unit must appear on both the top and the bottom (in the correct positions) to cancel; 0.5 partial credit for "the units cancel" without naming the mechanism. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-7.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any mention of a real-world conversion (money exchange, recipe scaling, speed conversions, sports statistics) that shows the student grasps dimensional analysis as a universal method, not a chemistry-only trick.
- Responses that name the conversion factor as a "ratio equal to 1" or say "multiplying doesn't change the amount, only the label" show the deepest transfer of the Scale, Proportion, and Quantity framing.
- Students who name a moment during the card-train activity or a partner who helped them "flip the card" are showing that the physical ABCs activity built conceptual understanding — note these students as having benefited from the tactile scaffold; they may need it again on the first multi-step stoichiometry problem.

If a student leaves the Exit Ticket unit track blank but wrote a correct number, follow up in the next lesson: they likely computed by mental decimal movement. On a mol → atoms → grams chain, that shortcut fails — the track is what keeps the method generalizable.
