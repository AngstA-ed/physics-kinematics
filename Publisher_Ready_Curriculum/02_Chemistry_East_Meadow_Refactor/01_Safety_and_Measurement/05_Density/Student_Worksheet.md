## Phenomenon

A glass of ice water sits on a lab bench. The ice cubes float. For almost every substance on Earth, the solid form is denser than the liquid — solid iron sinks in molten iron, solid copper sinks in molten copper. But water is the spectacular exception: ice floats on liquid water. Because of this, lakes freeze from the top down, and the liquid water beneath the ice protects aquatic life through winter.

**Driving question:** Why does ice float on water when most solids sink?
