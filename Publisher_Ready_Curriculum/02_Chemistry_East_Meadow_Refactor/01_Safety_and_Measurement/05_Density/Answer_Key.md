## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. **Find the density of a 54.0 g aluminum block with a volume of 20.0 mL:**

   D = m / V = 54.0 g / 20.0 mL = **2.70 g/mL**

   Float/sink: **sinks** — because 2.70 g/mL > 1.0 g/mL (water). An object sinks when its density exceeds that of the surrounding liquid.

   *Rubric:* 1 point for the correct numerical answer (2.70 g/mL); 1 point for the correct float/sink call with a comparison of the two densities stated. A student who writes "it floats because it is aluminum" (without the density comparison) earns partial credit — they must compare numbers. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-6.

2. **Find the mass of a copper piece with density 8.96 g/mL and volume 3.0 mL:**

   m = D × V = 8.96 g/mL × 3.0 mL = **26.88 g ≈ 26.9 g** (3 sig figs, limited by 3.0 mL)

   *Rubric:* 1 point for the correct setup (m = D × V, not V = m/D or another inversion); 1 point for the correct numerical answer (accept 26.88 g or 26.9 g with appropriate sig-fig notation). A student who writes D = m/V and then divides 8.96 by 3.0 (getting ≈ 2.99 g/mL, the wrong quantity) should receive feedback identifying the unit error — the result has units of g/mL, not grams. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-6.

3. **Find the volume of a mystery liquid with density 0.79 g/mL and mass 47.4 g:**

   V = m / D = 47.4 g / 0.79 g/mL = **60.0 mL** (3 sig figs)

   Float/sink context: the density 0.79 g/mL < 1.0 g/mL, so this liquid floats on water (it is likely ethanol or a similar organic solvent).

   *Rubric:* 1 point for the correct setup (V = m/D); 1 point for the correct numerical answer (60.0 mL or 60 mL). Students who invert and write m/V and arrive at the right number but wrong units (g/mL) should be marked down — the unit is the check. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-6.

4. **Explain why the slope of a mass-vs-volume graph equals density:**

   *Full-credit model answer:* The slope of a graph equals rise divided by run. On a mass-vs-volume graph, rise is mass (in grams) and run is volume (in mL), so slope = mass/volume = D (in g/mL). The slope equals density because density is defined as the ratio of mass to volume — which is exactly what slope measures on this graph.

   *Rubric:* 1 point for identifying that slope = Δmass/Δvolume; 1 point for connecting this ratio to the definition of density (D = m/V). Responses that say only "because the axes are mass and volume" without explicitly connecting slope to the ratio earn partial credit. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-6.

5. **Predict float/sink for an unknown object with density 0.72 g/mL:**

   *Full-credit model answer:* The object floats in water because its density (0.72 g/mL) is less than the density of water (1.0 g/mL). An object floats when it is less dense than the liquid it is placed in.

   *Rubric:* 1 point for the correct prediction (floats); 1 point for the correct reasoning — must include an explicit numerical comparison (0.72 g/mL < 1.0 g/mL). NYSSLS: HS-PS2-6.

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) **Density of a 13.5 g aluminum cube that displaces 5.0 mL of water:**

D = m / V = 13.5 g / 5.0 mL = **2.7 g/mL**

(2 significant figures, limited by 5.0 mL)

The displaced water volume (5.0 mL) is the volume of the cube — this is the water-displacement method for measuring volume of an irregular solid.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct numerical answer (2.7 g/mL); 1 point for the correct setup (m in numerator, V in denominator). A student who writes V/m = 5.0/13.5 = 0.37 mL/g should receive feedback that the units are inverted — the unit g/mL is the self-check. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-6.

(b) **Float or sink in water?**

**Sinks** — because 2.7 g/mL > 1.0 g/mL (the density of water). To float, the object's density must be less than the liquid's density.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct call (sinks); 1 point for citing both densities and the comparison (2.7 > 1.0). A student who says "it sinks because it is aluminum" without the density comparison earns 0.5 partial credit. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-6.

(c) **Why does ice float — particle-level explanation:**

*Full-credit model answer:* Ice forms an open hexagonal lattice that spaces water molecules farther apart than in liquid water, giving ice a lower density (≈ 0.92 g/mL) than liquid water (1.0 g/mL), so ice floats.

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that (i) names the structural reason (open lattice / spread-out particles in ice), (ii) connects this to density (ice less dense than liquid water), and (iii) states the consequence (floats) earns full credit. Responses that say only "ice is less dense" without any particle-level reasoning earn partial credit.

*Rubric:* 1 point for a structural reason (particle spacing, open lattice, or equivalent); 1 point for the density comparison; 0.5 partial credit for "ice is less dense" with no structural explanation. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-6.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Responses that name the specific structural feature (open hexagonal lattice, hydrogen bonds, spread-out particles) show that the Structure and Function CCC has taken root — the student can name the molecular-level mechanism rather than just the macroscopic outcome.
- Responses that mention the ecological importance (fish, lakes, aquatic life) show that the phenomenon anchored the concept in a real-world context larger than the lab bench — a sign that the phenomenon-based design worked.
- Students who name a partner who helped them during the measurement activity or the graph interpretation are demonstrating that collaborative sense-making happened — note these pairs for strategic grouping in the next lesson.
- If a student's Exit Ticket part (c) says "ice floats because it is less dense" without particle reasoning, the follow-up prompt is: "Why is ice less dense? What is different about the arrangement of the water molecules in ice vs. liquid water?" This probes whether the student understands the mechanism or has only memorized the outcome.
