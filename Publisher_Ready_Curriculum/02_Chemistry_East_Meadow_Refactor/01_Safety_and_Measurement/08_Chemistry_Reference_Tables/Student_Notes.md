## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A student on the Regents exam needs the mass of 250 mL of water at 4 °C. The problem does not give the density — it expects the student to look it up.

**Step 1 — Look up the density of water:**

Density of water at 4 °C (from the reference tables): _______________ g/mL

Table used: _______________________________________________

**Step 2 — Set up the calculation:**

mass = density × volume

mass = _______________ g/mL × _______________ mL

**Step 3 — Cancel units and solve:**

mass = _______________ g

Unit cancellation: (g/mL) × (mL) = g   ✓

**Step 4 — Check for reasonableness:**

250 mL of water has a mass of 250 g. Does that seem right? (1 mL of water weighs about 1 g, so 250 mL should weigh about 250 g.) ___

**Step 5 — Appositive sentence about this problem:**

The density of water — _______________ — was found in the _________________________ table of the reference tables and used to calculate a mass of _____________ g for _______________ mL of water.

**Step 6 — Because / But / So**

A student who memorizes the density of water as "about 1" instead of looking it up
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should describe why approximate memory might work; "but" should name the risk of approximation; "so" should connect to exam accuracy.
