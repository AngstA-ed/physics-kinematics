## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Scale, Proportion, and Quantity:** Mass scales with the __________ of atom and the __________ of atoms per formula unit. A mole always contains the same __________ of particles, but the __________ of one mole depends on what kind of particles they are.

**The gram formula mass — how it works:**

To find the gram formula mass of a compound:

1. Identify every __________ in the chemical formula.
2. Find the __________ of each element from the Periodic Table (unit: g/mol).
3. Multiply each atomic mass by the __________ for that element.
4. __________ all the products.

The result is the gram formula mass — the mass in grams of __________ of that substance.

**The unit of gram formula mass:** __________ (read as "grams per mole")

**The Hochman appositive definition:**

> Gram formula mass — _________________________ — is found by _________________________.

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A student needs to calculate the gram formula mass of CaCl₂.

**Step 1 — Identify the elements and their subscripts:**

Formula: CaCl₂  →  Elements: ___________  and ___________

Subscripts: Ca = ___  Cl = ___

**Step 2 — Look up each atomic mass from the Periodic Table:**

Ca = ___________ g/mol  Cl = ___________ g/mol

**Step 3 — Multiply each atomic mass by its subscript:**

| Element | Atomic mass (g/mol) | Subscript | Mass contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ca | ___ | 1 | ___ |
| Cl | ___ | 2 | ___ |

**Step 4 — Sum all mass contributions:**

GFM = ___ + ___ = ___________  g/mol

**Step 5 — Appositive sentence:**

The gram formula mass of calcium chloride — ___ g/mol — is found by adding ___ (for Ca) and ___ (for 2 × Cl).

**Step 6 — Because / But / So**

The subscript 2 on Cl is multiplied by the atomic mass of Cl
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should explain why subscript matters; "but" should acknowledge what you would get if you forgot it; "so" should state the correct GFM.

**Reminder:** The subscript in a formula tells you how many atoms of that element are in one formula unit. Each atom contributes its full atomic mass — so you always **multiply** atomic mass by subscript before adding.
