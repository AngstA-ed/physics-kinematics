## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Scale, Proportion, and Quantity:** A measurement has two parts: the __________ (how many) and the __________ (what kind). Without both parts, the measurement is meaningless. 36.5 is a number. 36.5 mL is a measurement.

**The Mars Climate Orbiter (1999):** Engineers reported thruster force in __________ instead of __________. The numbers were correct, but the units were wrong. The spacecraft missed its orbit and burned up. One unit error cost $125 million.

**SI units — the international agreement:**

| Quantity | SI Unit | Symbol | Instrument |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mass | kilogram | kg | balance |
| Volume | liter / milliliter | L / mL | graduated cylinder |
| Length | meter / centimeter | m / cm | ruler |
| Temperature | kelvin (scientific) | K | thermometer |

Note: In chemistry, temperature is often recorded in °C in the lab and converted to K for calculations. Conversion: K = °C + __________.

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A student measures the mass of a metal sample four times on an electronic balance: 12.3 g, 12.3 g, 12.4 g, 12.3 g. The accepted mass of the sample is 15.8 g.

**Step 1 — Is the data precise?**

The four readings are: 12.3, 12.3, 12.4, 12.3 g. They cluster __________ (tightly / loosely) around __________ g.
Conclusion: the data is __________ (precise / not precise).

**Step 2 — Is the data accurate?**

The average measured value is approximately __________ g. The true value is 15.8 g.
The readings are __________ from the true value.
Conclusion: the data is __________ (accurate / not accurate).

**Step 3 — What might explain this result?**

The balance reads the same wrong value repeatedly. This is called a __________ error — the instrument is __________ (calibrated correctly / miscalibrated).

**Step 4 — Because / But / So**

The balance gives a precise but inaccurate reading
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name what is wrong with the instrument; "but" should name why the readings are still precise; "so" should name the action to take (recalibrate, repeat, or check against a known standard).
