## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) **Yes — the data is precise.** The four readings (24.9, 25.0, 24.8, 25.1 mL) span a range of only 0.3 mL and cluster tightly around 24.95 mL. The repeated measurements are close to one another, which is the definition of precision.

(b) **No — the data is not accurate.** The cluster of readings (≈ 25 mL) is far from the true value of 30.0 mL — a difference of approximately 5 mL. Closeness to the true value is the definition of accuracy, and these measurements are not close.

(c) **Sample full-credit response:** *"Precision is agreement among repeated measurements — the darts cluster together — while accuracy is closeness to the true value — the cluster lands near the bullseye; data can be precise without being accurate, as when a miscalibrated instrument consistently gives the same wrong reading."* Any equivalent response that (i) defines precision as repeatability/consistency and (ii) defines accuracy as closeness to the true value, and (iii) notes that the two are independent, earns full credit.

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) 1 point for "yes" + correct reasoning citing the range or clustering; (b) 1 point for "no" + correct reasoning citing the distance from 30.0 mL; (c) 2 points for a response that correctly distinguishes both terms and notes independence; 1 point for defining only one term correctly. ## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- A concrete behavioral intention — "I will write the unit immediately after every number in my data table, not at the end" — rather than a generic statement like "I'll include units."
- Any response that connects the Mars orbiter story to their own measurement practice shows the strongest transfer — e.g., "if a spacecraft can be lost because of a unit mistake, I can lose points on a Regents question for the same reason."
- Responses that distinguish precision from accuracy in their own words — e.g., "I realized I thought precise and accurate were the same word" — signal that the central conceptual goal of the lesson landed.

If a student writes "nothing" or leaves the reflection blank, check in briefly before the next class — the measurement-station format requires students to record real data, and students who disengaged may have gaps in their data table that need to be addressed before Unit 2.
