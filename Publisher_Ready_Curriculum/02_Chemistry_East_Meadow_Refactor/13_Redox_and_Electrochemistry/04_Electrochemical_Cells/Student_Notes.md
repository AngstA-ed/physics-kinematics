## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A voltaic cell is built from a **zinc** strip in ZnSO₄(aq) and a **copper** strip in CuSO₄(aq), joined by a salt bridge. The voltmeter reads about 1.1 V, and the red (positive) lead is on the copper.

**Step 1 — Identify which metal is oxidized:**

Zinc gives up electrons more easily than copper, so __________ is oxidized.

**Step 2 — Assign the electrodes:**

The electrode where oxidation happens is the __________ → that is the __________ strip.
The electrode where reduction happens is the __________ → that is the __________ strip.

**Step 3 — Write the half-reactions:**

Anode (oxidation): Zn → __________ + __________
Cathode (reduction): Cu²⁺ + __________ → __________

**Step 4 — State the direction of electron flow:**

Electrons flow from the __________ (Zn) to the __________ (Cu) through the external wire.

**Step 5 — Identify the salt bridge's job:**

Without the salt bridge the voltage was about __________ V. The salt bridge lets __________ move between the beakers to keep each one electrically __________, so the electrons can keep flowing.

**Step 6 — Because / But / So**

The zinc electrode loses mass as the cell runs
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name the oxidation half-reaction; "but" should note that matter is conserved (the zinc becomes ions, not nothing); "so" should explain what stops when the zinc is gone.

**Reminders:**

- **AN OX** = ANode is OXidation. **RED CAT** = REDuction is CAThode.
- Electrons travel through the **wire** (anode → cathode); **ions** travel through the **salt bridge**.
- **Voltaic** cell = spontaneous, *makes* electricity (a battery). **Electrolytic** cell = non-spontaneous, *uses* electricity (electroplating, recharging).

**Key reference (from the 2025 NYS Reference Tables):**
Reduction potentials are listed on Reference Table J (Activity Series) and in the standard reduction potential table — the metal higher on the activity series is oxidized more easily and becomes the anode.
