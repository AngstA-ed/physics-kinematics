## Phenomenon

A clean strip of zinc metal is dropped into a clear blue copper(II) sulfate solution. After a few minutes, the zinc is coated with reddish copper metal and the blue color of the solution fades. Something moved from one substance to the other — but you can't see it. Now the same reaction is built as a cell: zinc in one beaker, copper in another, connected by a wire and a salt bridge, with a voltmeter clipped into the wire. The needle jumps. The meter is reading the electrons flowing through the wire — the invisible made visible.

**Driving question:** How can we "see" the invisible flow of electrons — and write it down — in a chemical reaction?
