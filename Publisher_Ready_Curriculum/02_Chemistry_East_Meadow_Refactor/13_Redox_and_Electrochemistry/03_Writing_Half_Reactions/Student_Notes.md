- Split a single redox reaction into an **oxidation half-reaction** and a **reduction half-reaction**, each showing the electrons (e⁻) explicitly.

## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Energy and Matter (conservation):** Electrons are __________. In a redox reaction they are not created or destroyed — they are __________ from one species to another. So the number of electrons __________ by the species that is oxidized must equal the number __________ by the species that is reduced.

**How to write and balance half-reactions (use this every time):**

1. Use the oxidation-number change to decide which species is __________ (loses e⁻) and which is __________ (gains e⁻).
2. Write the oxidation half-reaction with e⁻ on the __________ side.
3. Write the reduction half-reaction with e⁻ on the __________ side.
4. Count the electrons in each half. If they are equal, you are done. If not, multiply each half-reaction by the smallest whole number — the __________ — so the counts match.
5. Add the two balanced halves and cancel the electrons to get the __________ equation.

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** Write and balance the half-reactions for **Al + Cu²⁺ → Al³⁺ + Cu**.

**Step 1 — Decide who loses and who gains (oxidation numbers from Lesson 02):**

Al goes from 0 to +3, so Al __________ electrons → __________ (oxidation).
Cu goes from +2 to 0, so Cu²⁺ __________ electrons → __________ (reduction).

**Step 2 — Write each half-reaction with electrons on the correct side:**

Oxidation: Al → Al³⁺ + ___ e⁻
Reduction: Cu²⁺ + ___ e⁻ → Cu

**Step 3 — Count the electrons:**

Electrons lost = ___   Electrons gained = ___   Equal? ___

**Step 4 — Balance the electrons (least common multiple of 3 and 2 = ___):**

Multiply the oxidation half by ___:  ___ Al → ___ Al³⁺ + ___ e⁻
Multiply the reduction half by ___:  ___ Cu²⁺ + ___ e⁻ → ___ Cu

Now electrons lost = ___ and electrons gained = ___. ✓

**Step 5 — Net ionic equation (add the halves, cancel the e⁻):**

> ___ Al + ___ Cu²⁺ → ___ Al³⁺ + ___ Cu

**Step 6 — Because / But / So**

We had to scale the half-reactions
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should explain why the counts didn't match; "but" should name what conservation requires; "so" should state the least-common-multiple fix and the final electron count.
