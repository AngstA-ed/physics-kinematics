## Unit Scope (from East Meadow Scope & Sequence)

This unit takes students from the qualitative behavior of acids and bases to the quantitative tools chemists use to characterize them. Students begin with the Arrhenius model — the foundational pattern that explains why every acid shares the same properties (H⁺) and every base shares a different set (OH⁻) — then watch those models do real chemical work in neutralization, where an acid and a base react to make a salt and water while atoms are conserved. From there the unit becomes measurable: titration uses M_aV_a = M_bV_b to pin down an unknown concentration from two volumes and a color change, and pH puts a number on hydrogen-ion concentration that indicators make visible. The Brønsted-Lowry model closes the unit's central gap — how ammonia can be a base with no OH⁻ in its formula — by redefining acids and bases as proton donors and acceptors, the lens that ties every reaction in the unit together.

| # | Lesson | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Arrhenius Theory | An acid produces H⁺ in water and a base produces OH⁻; shared common ions explain shared properties |
| 02 | Neutralization | Acid + base → salt + water; the H⁺ and OH⁻ are consumed and atoms are conserved (balanced equation) |
| 03 | Titration | A known solution + a burette + a color change yields an unknown concentration via M_aV_a = M_bV_b |
| 04 | Brønsted-Lowry Theory | Acids donate protons, bases accept them; conjugate pairs differ by one H⁺ (explains NH₃) |
| 05 | pH & Indicators | pH measures [H⁺] on a 0–14 logarithmic scale; indicators read pH out as visible color |

## Pacing Calendar

| Day | Lesson | Phenomenon anchor | Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 01 Arrhenius Theory | Lemon juice, vinegar, battery acid — unrelated origins, identical acid properties | Active Learning |
| 2 | 02 Neutralization | An antacid (a base) makes the burning of stomach acid go away | Hochman |
| 3 | 03 Titration | An unlabeled acid bottle; one drop flashes phenolphthalein pink and the molarity drops out | BTC |
| 4 | 04 Brønsted-Lowry Theory | Ammonia (NH₃) turns red litmus blue despite having no OH⁻ in its formula | Active Learning |
| 5 | 05 pH & Indicators | Genetically identical hydrangeas bloom blue in one yard and pink in another | Hochman |

Double-period days (every other day) let the titration and indicator labs run long.

---

## Strategy Rotation

- **ACTIVE LEARNING**: Lessons 01, 04 (stations-lab classification of acids/bases before the vocabulary; proton-tracking sort that discovers donor/acceptor/conjugate pairs).
- **HOCHMAN (literacy)**: Lessons 02, 05 (appositive sentences defining *salt* and *neutralization*; appositive sentences defining *pH* and *indicator*).
- **BTC (Building Thinking Classrooms)**: Lesson 03 (random groups solve titration thin-slices at vertical non-permanent surfaces before consolidation).

SEL (opening connection + closing reflection), ELL/SPED supports, and OPTIC/CER literacy appear in every lesson.
