## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. Convert each pH to [H⁺] and classify:**

| pH | [H⁺] (mol/L) | Acidic / neutral / basic |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10⁻¹ = 0.1 | acidic |
| 3 | 10⁻³ = 0.001 | acidic |
| 7 | 10⁻⁷ = 0.0000001 | neutral |
| 11 | 10⁻¹¹ = 0.00000000001 | basic |

Step-by-step: the [H⁺] is just 10 raised to the negative pH. pH 1 → 10⁻¹; pH 3 → 10⁻³; pH 7 → 10⁻⁷ (neutral, where [H⁺] = [OH⁻]); pH 11 → 10⁻¹¹. Solutions below pH 7 are acidic, exactly 7 is neutral, above 7 is basic.

*Rubric:* 1 point for all four [H⁺] values written as 10⁻ᵖᴴ; 1 point for all four classifications correct. A student who writes the exponent as positive (10⁺³ instead of 10⁻³) has the sign error — provide feedback: a bigger pH means a *smaller* concentration, so the exponent must be negative. NYSSLS: PS1-11.

**2. How many times greater is [H⁺] at pH 3 than at pH 6?**

Steps from pH 3 to pH 6: 3 → 4 → 5 → 6 = **3 steps**. Each step is ×10, so the factor is 10 × 10 × 10 = 10³ = **1000 times more** H⁺ at pH 3.

*Rubric:* 1 point for identifying 3 steps; 1 point for the correct factor of 1000 (or 10³). The classic error is to subtract (6 − 3 = 3) and answer "3 times" — if this appears, note that 3 is the number of *steps* and each step multiplies by 10, giving 10³ = 1000. NYSSLS: PS1-11.

**3. Why vinegar (pH 3) is acidic and water (pH 7) is not:**

*Full-credit model answer:* The vinegar is acidic because its hydrogen-ion concentration is much higher than the water's — [H⁺] = 10⁻³ mol/L in the vinegar versus 10⁻⁷ mol/L in the neutral water (10,000 times more H⁺) — so the lower pH of the vinegar reflects its greater [H⁺].

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that states the vinegar has a higher hydrogen-ion concentration (more H⁺) and connects that to its lower pH / acidic classification earns full credit. A response that only says "vinegar is an acid and water isn't" without referencing [H⁺] receives partial credit.

*Rubric:* 1 point for correctly using *hydrogen-ion concentration* (more H⁺ in the vinegar); 1 point for connecting the higher [H⁺] to the lower pH / acidic label. NYSSLS: PS1-11.

## Exit Ticket Answer

**(a) Classify pH 3 and give [H⁺]:**

A pH of 3 is **acidic** (it is below 7). Its hydrogen-ion concentration is [H⁺] = 10⁻³ = **0.001 mol/L**.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the classification "acidic"; 1 point for [H⁺] = 10⁻³ mol/L (accept 0.001 mol/L). A student who writes "basic" has reversed the scale — reteach that low pH = acidic. This pH value (3) is reused from the worksheet *conversion table*, but the Exit Ticket asks for classification + concentration together, distinct from the practice rows. NYSSLS: PS1-11.

**(b) How many times greater is [H⁺] at pH 2 than at pH 5?**

Steps from pH 2 to pH 5: 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 = **3 steps**. Each step is ×10, so the factor is 10 × 10 × 10 = 10³ = **1000 times more** H⁺ at pH 2.

*Rubric:* 1 point for identifying 3 steps (or computing 5 − 2 = 3 steps); 1 point for the factor of 1000 (or 10³). A student who answers "3 times" made the additive error — note that each step multiplies by 10. This uses pH 2 and pH 5 (distinct from the worksheet's pH 3 vs. pH 6 comparison, which also equals 1000× — students must redo the step-count, not copy the number). NYSSLS: PS1-11.

**(c) Litmus color at pH 11:**

Litmus is **blue** at pH 11. *How I know:* pH 11 is basic (well above 7), and litmus is red in acid and blue in base — its color-change range tops out around pH 8.3, so by pH 11 it is fully blue.

*Rubric:* 1 point for "blue"; 1 point for the reasoning (pH 11 is basic / above litmus's ~8.3 transition, and litmus is blue in base). A student who answers "red" has the acid/base ends of litmus reversed — redirect to the figure's litmus band. NYSSLS: PS1-11.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any mention of a real-life pH context — a food (citrus, soda, coffee), a household cleaner, garden soil, pool water, or the body (stomach acid, blood) — paired with a reason it matters. Students who connect the lesson to a substance they actually encounter are showing that pH is real, not abstract.
- Responses that use the appositive structure ("pH — a measure of the hydrogen-ion concentration — …") are demonstrating the Hochman writing move from Phase 3 and Phase 4. Note these students as having integrated the vocabulary into their active sentence structure.
- Students who name a partner or the cabbage-juice demo as the thing that made it click are signaling that the collaborative investigation structure supported their learning — note this as evidence the lab format is working.

If a student's Exit Ticket part (b) shows "3 times" instead of 1000×, the error is the additive/multiplicative confusion, not a [H⁺] lookup problem — a brief "count the ×10 steps" check-in at the start of the next lesson should be sufficient. If part (a) shows a reversed classification (basic instead of acidic for pH 3), the student needs a quick reteach of the scale orientation (low = acidic) before the next lesson builds on pH.
