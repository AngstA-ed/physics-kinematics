## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. Solve for M_a — 25.0 mL HCl neutralized by 25.0 mL of 0.10 M NaOH:**

> M_a × 25.0 = 0.10 × 25.0
> M_a × 25.0 = 2.5
> M_a = 2.5 ÷ 25.0 = **0.10 M**

Step-by-step: the acid (M_a, V_a = 25.0 mL) is the unknown; the base is M_b = 0.10 M, V_b = 25.0 mL. Substitute and divide both sides by 25.0 mL. The volumes are equal, so the acid molarity equals the base molarity: 0.10 M. This is the unlabeled bottle from the phenomenon.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct setup (acid and base values placed correctly in M_a V_a = M_b V_b); 1 point for the correct answer 0.10 M. A student who writes M_a = 2.5 forgot to divide by V_a — note the missing algebra step. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-11.

**2. Solve for M_a — 25.0 mL HCl neutralized by 50.0 mL of 0.10 M NaOH:**

> M_a × 25.0 = 0.10 × 50.0
> M_a × 25.0 = 5.0
> M_a = 5.0 ÷ 25.0 = **0.20 M**

Step-by-step: twice the base volume was required compared with Problem 1, so the acid is twice as concentrated: 0.20 M. This is the proportional pattern the groups derived at the whiteboards (Trial 2).

*Rubric:* 1 point for correct setup; 1 point for the correct answer 0.20 M (accept 0.2 M). Watch for students who substitute 50.0 as V_a instead of V_b — redirect them to label the acid and base first. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-11.

**3. Solve for V_b — mL of 0.10 M NaOH needed to neutralize 25.0 mL of 0.05 M HCl:**

> 0.05 × 25.0 = 0.10 × V_b
> 1.25 = 0.10 × V_b
> V_b = 1.25 ÷ 0.10 = **12.5 mL**

Step-by-step: here the unknown is a *volume*, not a concentration. The acid side (0.05 × 25.0 = 1.25) is known; divide by the base molarity (0.10) to isolate V_b. The dilute acid (0.05 M, half of 0.10 M) needs only half the base volume — 12.5 mL (Trial 3).

*Rubric:* 1 point for correct setup with V_b as the unknown; 1 point for the correct answer 12.5 mL. A student who solves for a molarity instead of a volume mislabeled the unknown — note it. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-11.

## Exit Ticket Answer

**(a) M_a of HCl — 20.0 mL HCl neutralized by 40.0 mL of 0.25 M NaOH:**

> M_a × 20.0 = 0.25 × 40.0
> M_a × 20.0 = 10.0
> M_a = 10.0 ÷ 20.0 = **0.50 M**

The acid (M_a, V_a = 20.0 mL) is the unknown; the base is M_b = 0.25 M, V_b = 40.0 mL. Substitute, then divide both sides by 20.0 mL. These values are distinct from the worksheet practice, so a correct answer demonstrates transfer of the method rather than recall of a memorized result.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct setup (M_a × 20.0 = 0.25 × 40.0, acid and base values placed correctly); 1 point for the correct answer 0.50 M (accept 0.5 M). A student who answers 10.0 forgot to divide by V_a; a student who answers 2.0 M divided the wrong direction (20.0 ÷ 10.0) — both are method errors, give 1 of 2 and note the specific slip. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-11.

**(b) Define the equivalence point:**

*Full-credit model answer:* The equivalence point is the moment in a titration when the moles of added base exactly equal the moles of acid present, so neither acid nor base is in excess; for a strong acid and strong base it occurs at pH 7, where the titration curve makes its steep vertical jump.

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that states the moles of acid equal the moles of base (or that the acid is exactly neutralized / exactly used up) earns full credit. Mentioning pH 7 or the steep jump on the curve is a bonus, not required. A response that only says "when it turns pink" describes the *endpoint* (the indicator's signal) rather than the equivalence point — award partial credit and clarify the distinction.

*Rubric:* 2 points — 1 point for "moles of acid equal moles of base" (or equivalent: acid exactly neutralized, neither in excess); 1 point for a correct supporting detail (pH 7, steep jump, or no excess of either). NYSSLS: HS-PS1-11.

**(c) Why a more concentrated acid requires a larger volume of the same base:**

*Full-credit model answer:* A more concentrated acid contains more moles of H⁺ in the same volume, so it takes more moles of OH⁻ — and therefore a larger volume of the same base — to supply enough OH⁻ to reach the equivalence point where the moles balance.

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that connects higher concentration to more acid particles (more moles of H⁺ / more acid) and therefore more base needed to neutralize earns full credit. Responses that just say "because it's stronger" without explaining *more moles of acid need more moles of base* receive partial credit.

*Rubric:* 2 points — 1 point for identifying that a more concentrated acid has more moles of acid (H⁺) in the same volume; 1 point for connecting that to needing more (a larger volume of) base to reach the equivalence point. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-11.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any mention of deriving the equation at the whiteboard *before* the formula was named — students who connect their own board work to the formal M_a V_a = M_b V_b are showing that the BTC thin-slice did its job: they own the relationship rather than having copied it.
- Responses that name a groupmate who helped them catch a labeling slip (which number is the acid, which is the base) are signaling that the random-group structure supported their learning — note this as evidence the BTC grouping is working.
- Students who connect the lab color change to the equivalence point on the curve are demonstrating they linked the physical observation (permanent pink drop) to the abstract idea (moles balance) — the central conceptual move of the lesson.

If a student's Exit Ticket shows a correct setup but a wrong final number, the error is in the algebra (isolating the unknown or dividing the wrong direction), not in understanding titration — a brief one-on-one on "solve for the missing letter" at the start of the next lesson should be sufficient. If the setup itself is wrong (acid and base values swapped), the student needs practice labeling M_a, V_a, M_b, V_b before substituting; provide a pre-labeled solving template for the next problem set.
