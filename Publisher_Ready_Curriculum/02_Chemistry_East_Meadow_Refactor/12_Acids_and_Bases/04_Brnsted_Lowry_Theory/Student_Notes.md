## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Systems and System Models:** The Arrhenius model and the Brønsted-Lowry model are two __________ of the same acid-base system. The Brønsted-Lowry model is more __________ because it explains every Arrhenius case *and* new cases (like NH₃) the Arrhenius model __________.

**Two ways to define an acid and a base:**

| | Arrhenius (Lesson 01) | Brønsted-Lowry (today) |
|---|---|---|
| Acid | produces __________ in water | __________ donor |
| Base | produces __________ in water | __________ acceptor |

**Tracking the proton (H⁺):** In any acid-base reaction, one species __________ an H⁺ (the acid) and one species __________ an H⁺ (the base). Follow the proton and you can label everything.

**The ammonia mystery — solved:**

Reaction: NH₃ + H₂O → NH₄⁺ + OH⁻

- NH₃ is the __________ (proton acceptor) → it gains an H⁺ and becomes __________.
- H₂O is the __________ (proton donor) → it loses an H⁺ and becomes __________.
- The OH⁻ that makes the solution basic comes from the __________, not from the ammonia. Ammonia never contained OH⁻; it *caused* OH⁻ by taking a proton away from water.

**Conjugate acid-base pairs:** Two species that differ by exactly one __________ (and one unit of charge). The conjugate **acid** has one __________ H⁺ than its conjugate base.

| Conjugate acid | Conjugate base | They differ by |
|---|---|---|
| NH₄⁺ | NH₃ | one __________ |
| H₂O | OH⁻ | one __________ |
| H₃O⁺ | H₂O | one __________ |

**Charge check:** Adding an H⁺ adds **+1** charge. So the conjugate acid is always one charge unit __________ (more positive / more negative) than its conjugate base.

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** Label the proton donor, the proton acceptor, and both conjugate acid-base pairs in this reaction:

> **HCl + H₂O → Cl⁻ + H₃O⁺**

**Step 1 — Track the proton.** Count H atoms on each species, left to right.

- HCl (1 H) → Cl⁻ (0 H): HCl __________ an H⁺.
- H₂O (2 H) → H₃O⁺ (3 H): H₂O __________ an H⁺.

**Step 2 — Assign roles.**

- Proton donor (acid) = __________
- Proton acceptor (base) = __________

**Step 3 — Find the conjugate pairs.** Pair each species with the partner it became (same backbone, differs by one H⁺).

| Conjugate acid | Conjugate base | Charge change |
|---|---|---|
| HCl | ______ | 0 → −1 |
| H₃O⁺ | ______ | +1 → 0 |

**Step 4 — Check the charge.** Removing one H⁺ removes one __________ charge, so each conjugate base is one unit __________ than its conjugate acid.

**Step 5 — Because / But / So**

In this reaction water is the **base**
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** in the ammonia reaction water was the **acid** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name what water did with the proton here; "but" should note water's opposite role with ammonia; "so" should state that acid/base is a *role*, not a fixed property.

**Reminder:** To label any acid-base reaction, **follow the H⁺**. The species that loses it is the acid (donor); the species that gains it is the base (acceptor). Each one and its before/after partner form a conjugate pair that differs by one H⁺.

**Key reference (from the 2025 NYS Reference Tables):**
Brønsted-Lowry: acid = H⁺ donor, base = H⁺ acceptor.
A conjugate acid has one more H⁺ (and +1 more charge) than its conjugate base.
