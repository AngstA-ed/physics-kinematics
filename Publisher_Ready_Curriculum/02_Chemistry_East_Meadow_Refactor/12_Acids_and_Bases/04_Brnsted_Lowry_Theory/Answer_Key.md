## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. HCl + H₂O → Cl⁻ + H₃O⁺**

- **Proton donor (acid):** HCl (it has 1 H on the left, 0 H as Cl⁻ on the right — it gave the proton away).
- **Proton acceptor (base):** H₂O (it has 2 H on the left, 3 H as H₃O⁺ on the right — it took the proton).
- **Conjugate pair A:** HCl / Cl⁻ (differ by one H⁺; charge 0 → −1).
- **Conjugate pair B:** H₃O⁺ / H₂O (differ by one H⁺; charge +1 → 0).

Step-by-step: track the H⁺ — HCl loses it, water gains it. HCl is the acid; water is the base. The donor and its leftover (HCl/Cl⁻) form one pair; the acceptor and its new form (H₃O⁺/H₂O) form the other. · *Rubric:* 1 point for correctly naming donor = HCl and acceptor = H₂O; 1 point for both correct conjugate pairs (HCl/Cl⁻ and H₃O⁺/H₂O). A student who pairs HCl with H₃O⁺ made the cross-pairing error — note that pairs must share a backbone and differ by exactly one H⁺. NYSSLS: PS1-11.

**2. NH₃ + H₂O → NH₄⁺ + OH⁻**

- **Proton donor (acid):** H₂O (2 H on the left → 1 H as OH⁻ on the right — water gave the proton away).
- **Proton acceptor (base):** NH₃ (3 H on the left → 4 H as NH₄⁺ on the right — ammonia took the proton).
- **Conjugate pair A:** NH₄⁺ / NH₃ (differ by one H⁺; charge +1 → 0).
- **Conjugate pair B:** H₂O / OH⁻ (differ by one H⁺; charge 0 → −1).

Step-by-step: this is the phenomenon reaction. Ammonia is the **base** because it *accepts* a proton (not because it contains OH⁻). Water is the **acid** here because it *donates* a proton, and the leftover OH⁻ is what makes the solution basic. · *Rubric:* 1 point for donor = H₂O and acceptor = NH₃ (the common error is calling NH₃ the acid because it ends up with more H — redirect to who *gave* vs. *gained*); 1 point for both conjugate pairs. NYSSLS: PS1-11.

**3. CH₃COOH + H₂O → CH₃COO⁻ + H₃O⁺**

- **Proton donor (acid):** CH₃COOH (acetic acid; it loses its one acidic H to become CH₃COO⁻).
- **Proton acceptor (base):** H₂O (it gains an H⁺ to become H₃O⁺).
- **Conjugate pair A:** CH₃COOH / CH₃COO⁻ (differ by one H⁺; charge 0 → −1).
- **Conjugate pair B:** H₃O⁺ / H₂O (differ by one H⁺; charge +1 → 0).

Step-by-step: only the one acidic H⁺ on CH₃COOH transfers; the carbon skeleton is the unchanged backbone. Acetic acid is the donor; water is the acceptor. · *Rubric:* 1 point for donor = CH₃COOH and acceptor = H₂O; 1 point for both conjugate pairs. Accept the formula written as HC₂H₃O₂ / C₂H₃O₂⁻ as an equivalent representation of acetic acid / acetate. NYSSLS: PS1-11.

## Exit Ticket Answer

**Reaction:  HF + H₂O → F⁻ + H₃O⁺**

**(a) Acid and base on the reactant side:**

- **Proton donor (acid):** HF (1 H → 0 H as F⁻; it donated the proton).
- **Proton acceptor (base):** H₂O (2 H → 3 H as H₃O⁺; it accepted the proton).

**(b) The two conjugate acid-base pairs:**

| Conjugate acid | Conjugate base | Differ by |
|---|---|---|
| HF | F⁻ | one H⁺ (charge 0 → −1) |
| H₃O⁺ | H₂O | one H⁺ (charge +1 → 0) |

**(c) Why it is a proton transfer, not the production of OH⁻:**

*Full-credit model answer:* No OH⁻ is produced anywhere in this reaction — instead, a single H⁺ moves from HF to water, so the reaction is described as a proton transfer; HF donates the proton (acting as the acid) and water accepts it (acting as the base), and the products differ from the reactants by only the location of that one H⁺.

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that states the H⁺ moved from HF to water (rather than OH⁻ being formed), and identifies the reaction as a transfer/hand-off of a proton, earns full credit. Note this exit-ticket reaction is **not** one of the three worksheet equations, so students must apply the proton-tracking method to a new case rather than recalling an answer.

*Rubric:* (a) 1 point for donor = HF and acceptor = H₂O (½ each). (b) 1 point for both conjugate pairs correct (HF/F⁻ and H₃O⁺/H₂O); a student who pairs HF with H₃O⁺ made the cross-pairing error. (c) 1 point for explaining that an H⁺ transferred from HF to water and no OH⁻ is generated. 3 points total. NYSSLS: PS1-11.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any mention that an old belief ("a base must contain OH⁻") was updated by new evidence (ammonia turning litmus blue with no OH⁻) — students who connect the *model revision* in their own life to the model revision in the lesson are demonstrating the Systems & System Models CCC at the personal level.
- Responses that name a specific person, partner, or moment from the conjugate-pair sort as the thing that changed their thinking — this signals the ACTIVE LEARNING collaborative structure landed.
- Strongest transfer: students who articulate that a more complete idea did not *erase* the old one but *extended* it (Brønsted-Lowry explains everything Arrhenius did, plus more) — this is the precise conceptual move the lesson targets.

If a student's Make-It-Make-Sense work shows correct donor/acceptor roles but cross-paired conjugates (e.g., HCl/H₃O⁺), the error is in the *pairing* step, not the proton-tracking — a brief check at the start of the next lesson on "pair by the same backbone, differ by one H⁺" should be sufficient. If a student labels NH₃ as the acid in equation 2 (because NH₄⁺ ends up with more H), reteach the donor/acceptor direction: the base is the one that *gained* the proton, not the one that ends up holding more.
