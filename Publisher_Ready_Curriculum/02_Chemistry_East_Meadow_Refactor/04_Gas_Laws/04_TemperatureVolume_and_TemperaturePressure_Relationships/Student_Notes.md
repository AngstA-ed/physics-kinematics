## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Scale, Proportion, and Quantity:** For a fixed amount of gas, volume and pressure each have a __________ relationship with temperature — *but only when temperature is measured on the __________ scale.* On that scale, doubling the temperature __________ the volume (constant pressure) or __________ the pressure (constant volume).

**Two relationships:**

| Hold this constant | These vary together | Proportion to use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pressure | temperature ↔ __________ | V₁ / T₁ = V₂ / T₂ | balloon in liquid nitrogen |
| Volume | temperature ↔ __________ | P₁ / T₁ = P₂ / T₂ | tire on a cold morning |

**Converting to Kelvin (the non-negotiable first step):**

> K = °C + ______

- 0 °C = ______ K
- 100 °C = ______ K
- 25 °C (room temperature) = ______ K

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A balloon holds 6.0 L of air at 27 °C. It is warmed to 327 °C at constant pressure. Find the new volume.

**Step 1 — Identify what is constant and choose the proportion:**

Pressure is constant, so use: ____________________

**Step 2 — Convert both temperatures to Kelvin (K = °C + 273):**

T₁ = 27 + 273 = ______ K   T₂ = 327 + 273 = ______ K

**Step 3 — Set up the proportion with the known values:**

6.0 / ______ = V₂ / ______

**Step 4 — Solve for V₂:**

V₂ = 6.0 × (______ / ______) = ______ L

**Step 5 — Check the proportional reasoning:**

The Kelvin temperature went from 300 K to 600 K — it __________ (doubled / halved). So the volume should __________ (double / halve). Does your answer agree? ______

**Step 6 — Because / But / So**

The volume increased when the gas was heated
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name the direct relationship and the Kelvin temperature change; "but" should note that no gas was added; "so" should state the new volume.
