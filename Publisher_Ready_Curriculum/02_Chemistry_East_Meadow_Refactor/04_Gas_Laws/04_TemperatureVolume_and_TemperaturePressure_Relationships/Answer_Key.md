## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. Balloon (T–V): 2.0 L at 27 °C cooled to −123 °C at constant pressure.**

- Convert to Kelvin: T₁ = 27 + 273 = **300 K**; T₂ = −123 + 273 = **150 K**.
- Proportion: 2.0 / 300 = V₂ / 150.
- Solve: V₂ = 2.0 × (150 / 300) = **1.0 L**.

Step-by-step: the Kelvin temperature was halved (300 → 150), so the volume halved (2.0 → 1.0 L). This is the direct relationship: V ÷ T is constant (2.0/300 = 0.0067 = 1.0/150).

*Rubric:* 1 point for converting both temperatures to Kelvin (300 K and 150 K); 1 point for the correct volume (1.0 L) with the proportion shown. A student who uses 27 and −123 directly (getting ≈ 0.66 L, or an undefined/negative result) missed the Kelvin step — provide written feedback: "convert to Kelvin first; a negative temperature in the setup means +273 was skipped." NYSSLS: Gas Laws unit (T–V).

**2. Tire (T–P): 30. psi at 300 K cooled to 270 K at constant volume.**

- Both temperatures already in Kelvin: T₁ = 300 K, T₂ = 270 K.
- Proportion: 30. / 300 = P₂ / 270.
- Solve: P₂ = 30. × (270 / 300) = **27 psi**.

Step-by-step: a 30 K drop on the Kelvin scale (300 → 270) is a 10% drop, so pressure falls 10% (30. → 27 psi). That 3-psi drop is enough to trip a low-pressure warning light — with no air lost.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct proportion setup (P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂ with the matched temperatures); 1 point for the correct pressure (27 psi). Accept 27.0 psi. NYSSLS: Gas Laws unit (T–P).

**3. Warming a gas (T–V): 5.0 L at 200 K heated to 400 K at constant pressure.**

- Both temperatures already in Kelvin: T₁ = 200 K, T₂ = 400 K.
- Proportion: 5.0 / 200 = V₂ / 400.
- Solve: V₂ = 5.0 × (400 / 200) = **10.0 L**.

Step-by-step: the Kelvin temperature doubled (200 → 400), so the volume doubled (5.0 → 10.0 L). This is the cleanest illustration of the direct relationship — when T is in Kelvin, doubling T doubles V exactly.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct proportion; 1 point for the correct volume (10.0 L; accept 10 L). A student who answers 2.5 L inverted the ratio (used 200/400 instead of 400/200) — note that heating must *increase* volume, so the new volume should be larger. NYSSLS: Gas Laws unit (T–V).

## Exit Ticket Answer

**(a) T–V: 4.0 L at 250 K warmed to 500 K at constant pressure.**

- Temperatures (already Kelvin): T₁ = 250 K, T₂ = 500 K.
- Proportion: 4.0 / 250 = V₂ / 500.
- Solve: V₂ = 4.0 × (500 / 250) = **8.0 L**.

The Kelvin temperature doubled (250 → 500), so the volume doubled (4.0 → 8.0 L). These values are different from the worksheet practice (which used 2.0 L / 300 K → 150 K), so a student must apply the method rather than recall an answer.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct proportion setup; 1 point for the correct volume (8.0 L; accept 8 L). A student who answers 2.0 L inverted the ratio — remind them that warming increases volume. NYSSLS: Gas Laws unit (T–V).

**(b) T–P: 200 kPa at 400 K cooled to 200 K at constant volume.**

- Temperatures (already Kelvin): T₁ = 400 K, T₂ = 200 K.
- Proportion: 200 / 400 = P₂ / 200.
- Solve: P₂ = 200 × (200 / 400) = **100 kPa**.

The Kelvin temperature was halved (400 → 200), so the pressure halved (200 → 100 kPa). Different values from the worksheet tire problem (30. psi / 300 → 270 K), so this checks transfer.

*Rubric:* 1 point for the correct proportion setup (P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂); 1 point for the correct pressure (100 kPa). A student who answers 400 kPa inverted the ratio — cooling must *lower* the pressure, so the answer should be smaller than 200 kPa. NYSSLS: Gas Laws unit (T–P).

**(c) Why the tire pressure recovered to 32 psi by afternoon without adding air:**

*Full-credit model answer:* No air ever left the sealed tire — the same number of gas particles stayed inside the whole time. Overnight the cold lowered the air's Kelvin temperature, so its pressure dropped in proportion (a direct relationship); by afternoon the sun warmed the tire back up, raising the Kelvin temperature, so the pressure rose back to 32 psi on its own.

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that (1) states no air was lost/added and (2) attributes the pressure recovery to the temperature rising back up (warming → higher Kelvin temperature → higher pressure, a direct relationship) earns full credit. Responses that say only "it got warmer" without connecting warming to pressure via the direct relationship should receive partial credit.

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for recognizing that no air was gained or lost (the amount of gas was constant), 1 point for connecting the pressure recovery to the rising temperature through the direct relationship. NYSSLS: Gas Laws unit (T–P).

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any mention of the demos (balloon in liquid nitrogen, collapsing can) or the tire phenomenon, connected to the idea that temperature alone changed the gas — students who tie the math back to something they watched are showing the proportion is real, not abstract.
- Responses that name the Kelvin conversion ("I learned you have to add 273 first") signal that the central procedural insight landed. Note these students as having internalized the non-negotiable first step.
- Students who name a partner who caught a missing +273 or an inverted ratio are signaling that the group investigation structure supported their learning — evidence the collaborative format is working.

If a student's Exit Ticket shows a correct proportion but an inverted ratio (e.g., 2.0 L instead of 8.0 L on part a, or 400 kPa instead of 100 kPa on part b), the conceptual model is sound but the directional check is missing — coach the quick sanity check: *heating increases volume; cooling lowers pressure.* If a student left temperatures in Celsius (revealed by a negative or zero value inside a proportion), reteach the K = °C + 273 step before the next lesson builds on it.
