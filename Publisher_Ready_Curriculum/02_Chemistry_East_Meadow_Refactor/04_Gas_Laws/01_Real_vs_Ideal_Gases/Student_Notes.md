## Guided Notes

**The big idea — a model holds under some conditions and fails under others:** The ideal gas is not a real substance. It is a __________ — a simplified picture we invent so gas behavior is easy to predict. The model makes three assumptions about the particles:

1. The particles take up __________ space (they are treated as tiny points).
2. The particles have __________ attractive forces between them.
3. The particles collide perfectly __________ (no energy is lost in collisions).

**How pressure, volume, and temperature trade off (fixed amount of gas):**

| Change | What the particles do | Effect on the gas |
|---|---|---|
| Volume gets smaller (at fixed T) | particles crowded into less space → hit walls more often | pressure goes __________ |
| Temperature gets lower (at fixed P) | particles move __________ → hit walls less often and less hard | volume goes __________ |

*(Note: we describe these trade-offs qualitatively — we do not name individual gas laws.)*

**When does a real gas act most like the ideal model?**

> A real gas behaves most like an ideal gas at __________ pressure and __________ temperature, because the particles are spread far apart and moving fast, so their real volume and their attractions barely matter.

> A real gas **deviates** from ideal behavior at __________ pressure and __________ temperature, because the particles are crowded close and moving slowly, so their real volume and their __________ now matter.

**Systems and System Models (CCC connection):**

Complete the sentence:

> The ideal gas is a __________ that predicts gas behavior very well when the assumptions roughly hold (low pressure, high temperature), but the model __________ when the particles are crowded or slow (high pressure, low temperature).

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** A sealed helium balloon sits in a warm room in the evening, then the room cools overnight. In the morning the balloon is smaller. No helium was added or removed.

**Work through the model:**

1. **What stays the same?** The __________ of gas particles (none added, none removed).

2. **What changes when the gas cools?** The particles move __________, so they hit the balloon walls __________ often and __________ hard.

3. **Effect on the volume:** With less outward push from the particles, the steady outside air pressure presses the balloon to a __________ volume.

4. **Which model are we using, and does it hold here?** We model the helium as an __________ gas. Inside a balloon the pressure is __________ and the temperature is ordinary, so the model ▢ holds well ▢ breaks down.

5. **Because / But / So:** The helium balloon shrinks overnight
   **because** _______________________________________________,
   **but** _______________________________________________,
   **so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name what cooling does to the particles; "but" should note that the amount of gas didn't change (no leak); "so" should connect to the smaller volume.
