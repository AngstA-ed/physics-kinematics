## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

**1. Potassium (K) directly below sodium (Na) in the same column:**

- **Valence electrons:** 1. Potassium is in the same group as sodium (Group 1), so it has the same number of valence electrons — exactly one.
- **How it reacts:** Like sodium — it is a reactive metal that tends to lose its single valence electron. *Reasoning:* same group → same number of valence electrons → similar chemistry. (Potassium is actually *more* reactive than sodium because its valence electron is farther from the nucleus, but "reacts like sodium / loses one electron" is the full-credit answer at this level.) · *Rubric:* 1 point for "1 valence electron"; 1 point for "reacts like sodium / is a reactive metal" with the same-group reasoning. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-1.

**2. A Group 1 element vs. a Group 7 (17) element:**

- **Valence electrons:** Group 1 element = 1 valence electron; Group 17 element = 7 valence electrons.
- **Why they behave differently:** The number of valence electrons sets the chemistry. An element with 1 valence electron tends to *lose* it; an element with 7 valence electrons tends to *gain or share* one to complete its outer shell. Opposite tendencies → very different reactions, even opposite ones (a Group 1 metal and a Group 17 nonmetal often react *with each other* to form a salt). · *Rubric:* 1 point for the correct counts (1 and 7); 1 point for explaining that different valence-electron counts produce different chemistry. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-1 / HS-PS1-2.

**3. An element in Period 4:**

- **What the period number tells you:** Period 4 means the atom's electrons occupy / fill **four** energy levels (shells). The period number = number of occupied shells.
- **Does it tell you the number of outer electrons?** No. The *period* tells you how many shells there are; the *group* (column) tells you how many valence (outer) electrons. Two elements can share Period 4 but have completely different numbers of valence electrons depending on their group. · *Rubric:* 1 point for "4 shells / energy levels"; 1 point for correctly stating that period ≠ valence-electron count and that the group provides that information. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-1.

## Exit Ticket Answer

**(a) Element X, atomic number 12:**

- **Protons:** 12 (atomic number = number of protons).
- **Period:** 3. **Group:** 2.
- This element is magnesium (Mg); its Bohr-model shells are 2, 8, 2 — three shells (Period 3) with 2 valence electrons (Group 2). *Rubric:* 1 point for 12 protons; 1 point for Period 3 **and** Group 2 (both required for the point). Accept the identification "magnesium" as bonus, not required. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-1.

**(b) Beryllium (Be) and magnesium (Mg), both Group 2:**

- **Valence electrons:** Be = 2; Mg = 2. Both are in Group 2, so both have 2 valence electrons.
- **What it tells you:** Because they have the same number of valence electrons, they react in similar ways (both are reactive metals that tend to lose 2 electrons and form similar compounds, e.g., BeO and MgO, or BeCl₂ and MgCl₂). *Rubric:* 1 point for "2 and 2"; 1 point for connecting equal valence-electron count to similar chemical behavior. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-1.

**(c) Why same-group elements behave alike despite different sizes:**

*Full-credit model answer:* Elements in the same group have the same number of valence electrons in their outermost shell, and it is the valence electrons that determine how an atom bonds and reacts — so even though one atom is larger (more inner shells), both react the same way because their *outer*-electron arrangement is identical.

*Acceptable variations:* Any response that identifies the **same number of valence electrons** as the reason for similar behavior, and notes that the size difference (extra inner shells / different period) does not change the outer-electron count, earns full credit. A response that says only "they're in the same column" without naming valence electrons earns partial credit.

*Rubric:* 2 points total — 1 point for naming same number of valence electrons as the cause of similar chemistry; 1 point for explaining that the size/period difference does not change the valence-electron count. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-1.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

The closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any genuine, specific response. Look for:

- Any mention of the card sort or the moment the missing element was revealed — students who connect their *own* prediction to Mendeleev's real prediction are showing that "position predicts properties" landed as a real idea, not a memorized rule.
- Responses that reference the Bohr-model figure ("I didn't realize Li and Na have the same outer electron") show the structural reason for grouping has transferred.
- Students who name a partner who helped them see the column pattern are signaling that the group card-sort structure supported their learning — note this as evidence the ACTIVE LEARNING structure is working.

If a student can name "period" and "group" but cannot connect the group number to the number of valence electrons, that is the key gap to close — a brief one-on-one check (pick a group number, ask for the valence-electron count) at the start of the next lesson should be sufficient before the unit builds on this with bonding and reactivity trends.
