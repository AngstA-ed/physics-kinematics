## Guided Notes

**The big idea — Patterns:** Every periodic trend traces back to just **two** levers:
1. How strongly the __________ pulls (more protons = stronger pull).
2. How __________ the valence electrons sit from the nucleus (more shells = farther, weaker pull).

**The two atomic-radius trends — and their causes:**

| Direction of travel | Radius | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Across a period (left → right) | __________ | no new shell is added; protons keep increasing, so the nucleus pulls the same outer shell __________ |
| Down a group (top → bottom) | __________ | each row adds a whole new __________, so the valence electrons sit farther out |

## Worked Example

**Scenario:** Compare potassium (K) and bromine (Br). K is in Group 1, Period 4. Br is in Group 17, Period 4. (Both are in the same period.)

**Step 1 — Compare atomic radius (same period, so use the across-period trend):**

Going across Period 4 from K toward Br, the radius __________ (because the nuclear charge increases while electrons fill the same shell). So the **larger** atom is ___________.

**Step 2 — Compare ionization energy:**

The smaller atom holds its valence electron more tightly, so it has the **higher** ionization energy. The higher-ionization-energy element is ___________.

**Step 3 — Compare electronegativity:**

Electronegativity increases across a period, so the **more electronegative** element is ___________.

**Step 4 — Compare metallic character:**

Metallic character decreases across a period, so the **more metallic** element is ___________.

**Step 5 — Appositive sentence:**

The ionization energy of bromine — _________________________ — is higher than potassium's because _________________________.

**Step 6 — Because / But / So**

Potassium gives up its valence electron more easily than bromine
**because** _______________________________________________,
**but** _______________________________________________,
**so** _______________________________________________.

*Check your reasoning:* "because" should name the size/distance difference; "but" should name what bromine's stronger nuclear pull does; "so" should state which element is more reactive as a metal.

**Reminder:** Every trend comes from two causes — nuclear pull (protons) and distance of the valence electron (shells). Atomic radius runs *opposite* to ionization energy and electronegativity; metallic character runs *with* atomic radius and *opposite* to ionization energy.
