Lesson 02 · Unit: Kinematics

Distance vs. displacement — same trip, two answers.

A walk around the block accumulates distance with every step but ends up only as far as the straight-line gap from start to end. One quantity is a scalar, one is a vector. Today you'll feel the difference.

HS-PS2-1 thread

01 Phenomenon

A classmate walks from their seat to the door — but takes the long way around (behind chairs, around tables). We measure two things: the length of the path, and the straight-line gap from start to end. Are those the same number?

02 Driving Question

When you describe how far something moved, which number is "right" — the path length or the straight-line gap?

03 Notice & Wonder

Share with your partner: which number is "the answer" — the path length or the straight-line gap? Why?

04 Initial Model

Sketch the path on a grid. Label its length. Then draw a straight arrow from start to end and label its length too.

05 Investigate — path builder

Add steps with the buttons. Toggle distance and displacement to compare.

06 Make it make sense

  1. A jogger runs around a 400 m oval track exactly once. Distance? Displacement?
  2. Build a path with distance = 12 and displacement = 4. Then one with distance = 8 and displacement = 0.
  3. Can displacement ever be larger than distance? Why or why not?

07 Key vocabulary

distance
Total length of the path traveled. A scalar — always ≥ 0.
displacement
Straight-line change in position from start to end, with direction. A vector.
scalar
A quantity with magnitude only (no direction).

08 Revise your model

Re-sketch the classroom walk. Label distance and displacement using today's vocabulary, with an arrow for the displacement.

09 Return to the phenomenon

If we re-do the walk in a perfectly straight line, would distance and displacement still differ?

10 Exit ticket + closing reflection

  1. A student walks 4 m east, then 3 m north, then 4 m west. (a) Distance? (b) Displacement (magnitude and direction)? (c) One sentence on why your two answers differ.
  2. Reflection: What surprised you? Who helped you make sense?
