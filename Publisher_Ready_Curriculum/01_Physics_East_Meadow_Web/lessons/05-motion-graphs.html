Lesson 05 · Unit: Kinematics

Motion graphs — three views, one story.

Same motion can be drawn three different ways: position-time, velocity-time, acceleration-time. Read the slope, not the height. By the end, you'll narrate any motion just by looking at one graph.

Active Learning · gallery walk HS-PS2-1
Download teacher guide (DOCX)

Active Learning Gallery walk · 15 min

Distribute one large grid sheet to each student. Each student draws their own x-t graph for a unique short motion (e.g., "I walked forward 5 s, stopped 3 s, walked forward faster for 4 s"). Tape all graphs along one wall.

Round 1 (8 min): Students walk the gallery in pairs. At each graph, they have 30 seconds to write on a sticky note: "I think this person ___" — one sentence describing the motion. They stick the note to the graph and move on. No talking during the walk.

Round 2 (4 min): Students return to their own graph and read the sticky notes. Did the gallery readers describe their motion correctly?

Round 3 (3 min): Whole-class debrief — pick three graphs that produced especially varied or wrong interpretations. Surface that the slope (not height) carries the velocity information.

01 Phenomenon

A classmate walks a choreography: forward 3 s, stop 2 s, backward 4 s. A second classmate narrates her position aloud each second. We capture the same trip in three different graphs — and they all describe the same motion.

02 Driving Question

How can the same motion produce three different-looking graphs that are all "right"?

03 Notice & Wonder

When the classmate stopped, what happened to the position-time graph? What happened to velocity-time? Same number?

04 Initial Model

Sketch your best guess for the x-t graph of the choreography. Focus on shape, not exact numbers.

05 Investigate — scenario explorer

Pick a scenario, press Play. Watch the dot, then watch the x-t and v-t graphs draw alongside.

When the dot stopped, what was the slope of x-t? What was the value on v-t? Same number?

06 Make it make sense

  1. Pick "Constant velocity 5 m/s." Sketch its x-t graph and v-t graph.
  2. Run "Speed up." A v-t graph rises from 0 to 10 m/s in 5 s. What is the acceleration? Total displacement?
  3. If x-t is a horizontal line, what is the velocity? Sketch the v-t graph.

07 Key vocabulary

position-time graph (x-t)
Position vs. time; slope = velocity.
velocity-time graph (v-t)
Velocity vs. time; slope = acceleration.
area-under-curve
On v-t, the area between the curve and the t-axis equals displacement.

08 Revise your model

Update your initial x-t sketch. Add a v-t graph below it. Label each region (forward / stopped / backward) on both.

09 Return to the phenomenon

Re-narrate the classmate's choreography using all three quantities. Where does each one match up to the graphs?

10 Exit ticket + closing reflection

  1. v-t graph: 0–4 s rises 0→8 m/s; 4–7 s stays at 8; 7–10 s falls 8→0. (a) Accelerations? (b) Sketch x-t. (c) Total displacement (area under v-t).
  2. Reflection: What surprised you? Who helped?
