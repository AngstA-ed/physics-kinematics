Lesson 01 · Unit: Kinematics

Vectors — direction matters as much as size.

Quantities like force and acceleration aren't just numbers — they have a direction. Adding them isn't ordinary arithmetic. Today you'll build, drag, and combine vectors on a grid until the head-to-tail rule feels obvious.

Restorative Circle Active Learning HS-PS2-1

01 Phenomenon

Open the Physics Classroom vector animations. Two arrows are added head-to-tail. The order changes which arrow you draw first, but the resultant — the dashed arrow from the start of the first to the tip of the last — is identical either way.

02 Driving Question

How do we add quantities that point in different directions?

03 Notice & Wonder

List two things you notice and two things you wonder.

Share with your partner: one thing you noticed about the arrows, and one thing you wondered. Pick a wondering you'd like the class to figure out today.

04 Initial Model

Sketch in your notebook: arrow A points 4 squares right, arrow B points 3 squares up. Where does the tip of B end up — and how would you describe that ending point in words?

05 Investigate — vector playground

Click Add vector to drop a vector onto the canvas. Drag its tip to set magnitude and direction. Vectors chain head-to-tail; the dashed black arrow is the resultant from origin to the tip of the last vector. Add up to six.

06 Make it make sense

  1. Set the playground to two vectors, A = 3 right and B = 4 up. What is the magnitude and direction of the resultant?
  2. What happens when A and B point in the same direction? Opposite directions?
  3. If a vector has magnitude 10 and points 30° above the x-axis, what are its x- and y-components? (Toggle Show components to check.)
Stuck? Sentence frame

"The resultant of A + B has magnitude ___ because ___."

07 Key vocabulary

vector
A quantity with both magnitude and direction.
magnitude
The size or length of a vector.
direction
The way a vector points, often given as an angle.

08 Revise your model

Update your initial sketch from §04. Use the words magnitude and direction in one sentence to describe where the tip of B ends up.

09 Return to the phenomenon

Open the Physics Classroom vector animation again. Predict the resultant of the two arrows shown using today's vocabulary. Was your initial guess close? What changed?

10 Exit ticket + closing reflection

  1. A boat sails 5 km east, then 12 km north. Sketch the resultant displacement on a grid and find its magnitude. Show one sentence of reasoning.
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
