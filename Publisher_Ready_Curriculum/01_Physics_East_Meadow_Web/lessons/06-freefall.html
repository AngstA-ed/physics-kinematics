Lesson 06 · Unit: Kinematics

Freefall — gravity's signature.

In a vacuum, every object accelerates at exactly g ≈ 9.8 m/s² — a feather and a bowling ball alike. The reason a feather falls slower in your classroom is air resistance, not gravity. Today you'll race them.

Hochman · because/but/so HS-PS2-1 thread

01 Phenomenon

Watch "Jumping from Space!" A skydiver leaps from the edge of space and falls for several minutes — first in near-vacuum, later in air. How does the same body fall differently in those two regimes?

02 Driving Question

If gravity pulls everything the same way, why does a feather fall slower than a rock?

03 Notice & Wonder

Predict: a heavy textbook and a flat sheet of paper, dropped together — which lands first? What if you crumple the paper into a tight ball?

04 Initial Model

Sketch v-t graphs for a falling rock and a falling feather (both released from rest, in air). Are they the same shape?

05 Investigate — race a rock and a feather

Toggle air resistance on/off and drop both objects. With air resistance off (vacuum), they hit at the same time. With air resistance on, watch the feather reach a low terminal velocity.

06 Make it make sense

  1. Drop both with air resistance ON, then OFF. Compare the feather's "landed at" times.
  2. Use because/but/so to refute "heavier objects always fall faster."
  3. A small rock and a large rock dropped from 1 m. Which hits first?
Hochman because/but/so scaffold
  1. Heavier objects fall faster than lighter ones, because ___.
  2. … but ___ (counter-evidence from the simulator).
  3. … so ___ (corrected conclusion).

07 Key vocabulary

freefall
Motion under gravity alone — air resistance negligible.
gravity (g)
Earth-surface acceleration ≈ 9.8 m/s² downward.
terminal velocity
Maximum steady speed reached when air resistance equals gravity.

08 Revise your model

Update your initial v-t sketches. Use different colors for rock and feather. Mark where the curves diverge.

09 Return to the phenomenon

In the Jumping from Space video, what was the skydiver's motion in near-vacuum vs. in air? Use freefall and terminal velocity.

10 Exit ticket + closing reflection

  1. A pebble dropped from a 45 m cliff (no air resistance, g = 9.8). (a) Velocity after 1 s? After 2 s? (b) How long to hit the ground? (c) Use because/but/so to explain why a feather dropped from the same cliff in air takes longer.
  2. Reflection: What surprised you? Who helped?
