Lesson 09 · Unit: Kinematics

Aim, fire, hit the target.

You've separated motion into x and y. You know freefall. Now combine them: pick a launch speed and angle, and try to land your projectile in the target box. Find the optimal angle. Beat the random launch.

Active Learning Catapult design challenge · 15 min

The challenge. "Design a paper catapult that lands a marshmallow in the target circle at the front of the room (center is at distance D = 2.0 m from your launch table). Document your launch angle, launch speed (estimate), and prediction. You get exactly 3 launches."

  1. Phase 1 (4 min): Build. Random groups of 3. Each group gets: 1 popsicle stick, 1 cup, 2 rubber bands. Build constrained; calibration is the lesson.
  2. Phase 2 (6 min): Calibrate. Two calibration launches at any angle. Measure actual range, back out launch speed using v₀² = R·g / sin(2θ). Use the simulator to predict the angle for the final launch.
  3. Phase 3 (3 min): Final launch. One launch per group. Mark where the marshmallow lands.
  4. Phase 4 (2 min): Debrief. Which groups landed in the target? What was their angle? Did all successful groups choose the same angle? (They did not — multiple angles can hit the same range, by symmetry. 30° and 60° at the same v₀ have the same range.)

Why this works: students engage with the optimization claim before they verbally state it. Teams that try 90° fail (zero range). Teams that try 45° succeed broadly. Teams that try 30° and 60° both succeed — exposing the symmetry. The experience makes the equation memorable.

01 Phenomenon

Watch Jamaal Murray catches his own hail mary. He tosses the ball, sprints around the defender, and catches the same ball. He has internalized — without equations — the launch angle and air-time that bring the ball back to him.

02 Driving Question

What launch angle produces the maximum horizontal range — and why?

03 Notice & Wonder

If you had to throw a softball as far as possible (same speed every time), what angle would you choose? Why?

04 Initial Model

Sketch trajectories at the same v₀ for three angles: 20°, 45°, and 70°. Predict which lands farthest.

06 Make it make sense

  1. Set v₀ = 12 m/s, θ = 30°. Range? Time-of-flight?
  2. At v₀ = 12 m/s, set θ = 60°. Compare range and time-of-flight to the 30° case.
  3. What launch angle maximizes range? What is the maximum range at v₀ = 12 m/s?

07 Key vocabulary

launch angle (θ)
Angle above horizontal at which the projectile is launched.
range equation
R = v₀² sin(2θ) / g, valid for level ground and no air resistance.
optimal angle
45° on level ground in vacuum — the angle that produces maximum range.

08 Revise your model

Update your trajectory sketches. Add range and time-of-flight values for each angle. Highlight the symmetric pairs (15°/75°, 30°/60°).

09 Return to the phenomenon

Estimate Murray's hail mary: ~2.5 s air-time, ~25 m horizontal. What's the launch angle and approximate launch speed?

10 Exit ticket + closing reflection

  1. Soccer kick at 14 m/s and 35° above horizontal. (a) v_x and v_y? (b) Time-of-flight? (c) Range? (d) 55° at the same speed — closer or farther? Why?
  2. Reflection: What surprised you? Who helped?
