Lesson 07 · Unit: Kinematics

Throw it straight up. The trip is symmetric.

Toss a ball vertically. The time to rise equals the time to fall. The speed at any height on the way up equals the speed at the same height on the way down — only the direction flips. Gravity never pauses.

HS-PS2-1 thread

01 Phenomenon

Toss a volleyball straight up so it rises about 2 m. A classmate times its air-time. The ball spent some of that time going up, the rest coming down. Were those equal? At the very top, was the ball ever not moving?

02 Driving Question

What does the velocity of a vertically-thrown object look like at launch, peak, and landing?

03 Notice & Wonder

At the very peak, what is the ball's velocity? What is its acceleration? Are they the same number?

04 Initial Model

Sketch a v-t graph for the entire trip from launch to landing. Mark the peak.

05 Investigate — toss the ball

Slide the launch speed, press Throw. Watch the ball go up, peak (v = 0!), and come back down. The v-t graph alongside is a single straight line through zero — slope = constant −g.

06 Make it make sense

  1. Set v₀ = 10 m/s. What is the velocity at the peak? At what time does the ball reach the peak?
  2. Why is the v-t graph a straight line, even though the ball is sometimes going up and sometimes going down?
  3. A ball thrown up at 8 m/s — how fast is it moving when it returns to the launch height?

07 Key vocabulary

peak
Highest point of the throw; v = 0 momentarily.
time-of-flight
Total time from launch to landing.
symmetry
Time-up = time-down; same speed at the same height up and down.

08 Revise your model

Update your v-t sketch. Add: peak time, launch and landing velocities, slope (it never changes — but draw the temptation, then refute it).

09 Return to the phenomenon

If your earlier toss had ~1.0 s air-time, what was your launch speed?

10 Exit ticket + closing reflection

  1. Volleyball thrown straight up at v₀ = 12 m/s (g = 9.8). (a) Time to peak? (b) Max height above launch? (c) Velocity at return to launch? (d) Total time-of-flight?
  2. Reflection: What surprised you? Who helped?
