Lesson 08 · Unit: Kinematics

Horizontal projectiles — gravity doesn't care which way you're moving sideways.

Roll a marble off the table while another simply drops. Counterintuitive moment: they hit the floor at the same time. The horizontal velocity carries the rolled marble across the room, but it doesn't slow the fall.

Building Thinking Classrooms Hochman · paragraph topic

BTC Thin-slicing puzzles · 10 min

Random groups of 3 (visibly random, by playing-card suit). At each chart-paper station, post four progressively harder puzzles:

  1. Level 1: "A ball is dropped from a 5 m table. How long until it hits the floor?" (≈1.01 s)
  2. Level 2: "Same ball, but rolled off the table at 2 m/s horizontally. Now how long?" (Same: 1.01 s)
  3. Level 3: "How far horizontally does the rolled ball travel before hitting the floor?" (Range = v_x · t ≈ 2.02 m)
  4. Level 4: "If we double the table height to 10 m, what happens to the fall time and the range?" (Both grow by √2, not 2.)

3-minute rotations. Students argue at chart paper. Teacher walks but does not solve.

Hochman Paragraph topic sentence · 10 min

Students write a 3-sentence paragraph explaining why horizontal velocity does not affect fall time. Provide the topic-sentence frame:

"When a projectile is launched horizontally, its fall time depends only on the launch height — not on the horizontal speed."

Students then write two supporting sentences referencing evidence from the BTC stations and the MythBusters phenomenon: an evidence sentence (numbers from Levels 1 and 2), and a causal-mechanism sentence (gravity acts only vertically). Read three student paragraphs aloud; discuss which topic sentence most clearly previews what its supports will show.

01 Phenomenon

Watch MythBusters — Bullet Fired vs. Bullet Dropped. Two bullets are released at exactly the same instant from the same height: one is fired horizontally, the other simply dropped. Predict — which one hits the ground first?

02 Driving Question

Why does horizontal velocity not change how long a projectile stays in the air?

03 Notice & Wonder

Most students predict the dropped bullet lands first. Why is that intuition appealing — and why is it wrong?

04 Initial Model

On a y-vs-x grid, sketch the trajectory of (1) a marble dropped from the table edge, and (2) a marble rolled off at 2 m/s. Mark the moment each one hits the floor.

05 Investigate — twin marbles cliff sandbox

Vary the height and horizontal launch speed. Press Release both. Watch a rolled marble and a simply-dropped marble fall together. Both hit the floor at the same time, every time.

06 Make it make sense

  1. Set h = 1.25 m. Roll one marble at 3 m/s; drop the other simultaneously. Which lands first?
  2. What is the horizontal velocity of the rolled marble at the moment of impact?
  3. Double the table height. What happens to the fall time? Does the range double?
Hochman paragraph organizer
  1. Topic: "When a projectile is launched horizontally, fall time depends only on launch height — not horizontal speed."
  2. Evidence: Use a number from the simulator that supports the topic sentence.
  3. Mechanism: Why? (Hint: gravity acts only ___; horizontal velocity has no force changing it.)

07 Key vocabulary

horizontal velocity (v_x)
Constant horizontal speed of a projectile. No horizontal force, so v_x is unchanged during flight.
vertical velocity (v_y)
Downward speed, growing under gravity.
range
Horizontal distance from launch to landing.

08 Revise your model

Update your y-vs-x sketch. Mark the *fall time* on each trajectory. Are they the same number?

09 Return to the phenomenon

Re-watch the MythBusters bullet experiment. Use today's vocabulary in a one-sentence explanation.

10 Exit ticket + closing reflection

  1. A marble rolls off a 0.80 m table at 1.5 m/s. (a) Fall time? (b) Distance from edge it lands? (c) One Hochman-style topic sentence on why faster horizontal speed → longer range, same fall time.
  2. Reflection: What surprised you? Who helped?
