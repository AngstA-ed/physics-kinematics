Unit assessment · Kinematics

Regents-style cluster — 15 multiple choice + 1 constructed response.

Aligned to HS-PS2-1. Format mirrors the new NYS Physics Regents convention: 60% multiple choice, 40% constructed response. One hour suggested. Hide the answer key while students work.

Stimulus

A student records the motion of a remote-control car (mass = 0.50 kg) on a long, straight, level track using a stopwatch and meter markers. The car starts at rest. The student plots the velocity-time graph below from her data.

Read the graph as: 0–3 s, v rises linearly from 0 to 6 m/s; 3–7 s, v stays at 6 m/s; 7–9 s, v falls linearly from 6 m/s to 0.

MC Multiple choice (15 items, 1 pt each)

  1. During the first phase (0–3 s), the car's acceleration is closest to: (A) 0 m/s² · (B) 1.0 m/s² · (C) 2.0 m/s² · (D) 6.0 m/s²
  2. During the constant-velocity phase (3–7 s), the car's acceleration is closest to: (A) 0 m/s² · (B) 1.5 m/s² · (C) 6.0 m/s² · (D) cannot be determined
  3. During the slow-down phase (7–9 s), the car's acceleration is closest to: (A) +3.0 m/s² · (B) −3.0 m/s² · (C) +1.5 m/s² · (D) −6.0 m/s²
  4. According to F_net = m·a, the magnitude of the net force on the car during the first phase is closest to: (A) 0 N · (B) 0.5 N · (C) 1.0 N · (D) 3.0 N
  5. Magnitude of net force during the constant-velocity phase: (A) 0 N · (B) 1.0 N · (C) 3.0 N · (D) 6.0 N
  6. During the slow-down phase, the direction of the net force on the car relative to its motion is: (A) the same as motion · (B) opposite to motion · (C) perpendicular · (D) zero
  7. Total distance traveled between t = 0 and t = 3 s: (A) 3 m · (B) 6 m · (C) 9 m · (D) 18 m
  8. Total distance traveled between t = 3 s and t = 7 s: (A) 6 m · (B) 12 m · (C) 24 m · (D) 30 m
  9. Total distance across the entire 9 seconds: (A) 24 m · (B) 30 m · (C) 39 m · (D) 54 m
  10. Best description of displacement vs. distance over the 9 seconds: (A) Displacement < distance because the car reverses · (B) Displacement = distance because motion is unidirectional · (C) Displacement > distance because of acceleration · (D) Displacement = 0
  11. If a second car of mass 1.0 kg has the same v-t profile, the net force on it during phase 1 vs. the original car is: (A) the same · (B) half as large · (C) twice as large · (D) four times as large
  12. If the original 0.50 kg car instead reached 6 m/s in 1 s, the average net force during startup is: (A) 1 N · (B) 3 N · (C) 6 N · (D) 12 N
  13. The position-time graph during the constant-velocity phase looks like: (A) horizontal line · (B) straight line, positive slope · (C) negative slope · (D) parabola
  14. The position-time graph during phase 1 (0–3 s) looks like: (A) horizontal · (B) constant positive slope · (C) parabola opening upward · (D) parabola opening downward
  15. Which CCC best describes the relationship between unbalanced force and acceleration shown here? (A) Patterns · (B) Cause and Effect · (C) Stability and Change · (D) Energy and Matter

CR Constructed-response cluster (~10 pts)

Use the v-t graph above. Show your work; partial credit is given for clear reasoning.

(a) (2 pts) Calculate the total displacement from t = 0 to t = 9 s. Show how you used the graph.

(b) (3 pts) Sketch the corresponding position-time graph. Label the slope of each segment qualitatively. State the position at t = 9 s.

(c) (3 pts) The student claims: "The car has zero acceleration during 3–7 s, but the net force is not zero because the wheels are still pushing." Use Newton's Second Law to support or refute. Use evidence from the graph.

(d) (2 pts) Explain in 2–3 sentences how the direction of the net force changes across the three phases. Use the words acceleration and net force.

Key Answer key (teacher use)

Reveal answer key

Multiple choice: 1.C · 2.A · 3.B · 4.C · 5.A · 6.B · 7.C · 8.C · 9.C · 10.B · 11.C · 12.B · 13.B · 14.C · 15.B.

(a) Total area under v-t = 9 + 24 + 6 = 39 m.

(b) 0–3 s: parabola opening up (slope grows). 3–7 s: line of slope +6 m/s. 7–9 s: concave-down curve. Position at t = 9 s = 39 m.

(c) Refute. F_net = m·a. During 3–7 s, a = 0 (slope of v-t = 0), so F_net = 0. The wheels may push forward, but friction/air resistance must balance that push exactly. The student conflates "active push" with "net force."

(d) Phase 1: a > 0, F_net forward. Phase 2: a = 0, F_net = 0. Phase 3: a < 0, F_net opposite to motion (backward).
