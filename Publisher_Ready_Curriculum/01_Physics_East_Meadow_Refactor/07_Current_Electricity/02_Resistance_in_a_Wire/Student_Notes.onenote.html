Guided Notes

What sets a wire’s resistance? Four things:

  1. Length L — a longer wire has __________ resistance. R is __________ to length (R ∝ L).
  2. Cross-sectional area A — a thicker wire has __________ resistance. R is __________ to area (R ∝ 1/A).
  3. Material (resistivity ρ) — a higher-ρ material like nichrome has __________ resistance than a low-ρ material like __________.
  4. Temperature — for most metals, heating the wire __________ its resistance.

The two key patterns:

When length doubles, resistance __________.

When area doubles, resistance __________.

Connection to Ohm’s Law. Resistance is the R in V = I·R. If a wire’s resistance is high, then for the same voltage the current is __________.

Worked Example

Problem. Wire A and Wire B are the same copper, same thickness, but Wire B is three times as long as Wire A. Wire A has resistance 2 Ω. (a) Find Wire B’s resistance. (b) If both are connected to the same 12 V battery, which carries more current?

Solution. 1. Resistance is proportional to length, so tripling the length __________ the resistance: R_B = 3 × 2 Ω = __________ Ω. 2. Using I = V ÷ R: Wire A carries 12 ÷ 2 = __________ A; Wire B carries 12 ÷ 6 = __________ A. 3. Wire A carries more current because it has the __________ resistance.
