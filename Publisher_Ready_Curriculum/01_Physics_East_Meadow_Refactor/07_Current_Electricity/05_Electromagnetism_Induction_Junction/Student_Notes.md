## Guided Notes

**The two-way relationship (the "induction junction"):**

1. **Current → field.** A current in a coil of wire creates a __________ field. The coil acts like a bar magnet — this is an __________. More coil turns or more current makes it __________.

![Diagram of current in a coil creating a magnetic field with north and south poles.](figures/coil_field.png)

2. **Changing field → current.** A *changing* magnetic field through a coil __________ a current. This is **electromagnetic induction**.
   - A magnet held __________ inside a coil induces __________ current (the field isn't changing).
   - Moving the magnet __________ induces a larger current (faster change → more current).

![Diagram of induction: a moving magnet's changing field through a coil induces a current that lights an LED.](figures/induction_diagram.png)

**Generators and motors (energy connection — HS-PS3-5).**

- A **generator** spins a magnet near a coil; the changing field induces a current. It converts __________ energy into __________ energy.
- A **motor** runs the idea backward: current in → __________ out.

## Worked Example

**Problem.** A student moves a magnet into a coil connected to an LED. (a) Why does the LED light only while the magnet moves? (b) She moves it twice as fast — what happens to the LED, and why? (c) Where does the LED's energy come from?

**Solution.**
1. The LED lights only while moving because only a __________ magnetic field induces a current.
2. Moving twice as fast makes the field change __________, so the induced current is __________ and the LED is brighter.
3. The energy comes from the __________ she does moving the magnet — motion energy becomes electrical energy.
