## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. A **magnetic field** forms around the coil; the coil is now acting like a magnet — an **electromagnet**. · *Rubric:* names magnetic field AND electromagnet. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5
2. **No induced current.** A current is induced only by a *changing* magnetic field; a still magnet's field is steady (not changing), so nothing is induced. · *Rubric:* "no" AND explains via unchanging field. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5
3. The energy comes from the **motion** (the muscular work of cranking): cranking spins a magnet near a coil, the changing field induces a current by electromagnetic induction, and that current lights the bulb. · *Rubric:* energy from motion AND links via induction. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) It creates a **magnetic field** around the coil — the coil becomes an **electromagnet**.
(b) **No** — the magnetic field is not changing, and only a *changing* magnetic field induces a current.
(c) **A generator** (or dynamo / wind turbine / hand-crank flashlight). The energy comes from the **motion** that keeps the magnetic field changing through the coil — mechanical energy is converted to electrical energy.

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) magnetic field / electromagnet; (b) "no" with the unchanging-field reason; (c) any induction generator named AND energy traced to motion. 1 point each. NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5.
