## Curated Resources (from East Meadow Scope & Sequence)

### NYSSLS Standards

This lesson is a **NYS Required Investigation** on electromagnetism. It supports **HS-PS3-5**: *"Develop and use a model of two objects interacting through electric or magnetic fields to illustrate the forces between objects and the changes in energy of the objects due to the interaction."* Students model how a current creates a magnetic field, and how a *changing* magnetic field transfers energy into a circuit as an induced current — connecting the current-electricity unit to fields and energy. (It builds on, but does not directly assess, the Ohm's-Law standard HS-PS3-6 from Lessons 01–04.)

### Phenomenon

- A coil of wire + a magnet lighting an LED only while the magnet *moves* through the coil (shake-flashlight / hand-crank generator): <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=magnet+coil+led+induction+demonstration>
- A battery-powered coil (electromagnet) picking up paperclips, then dropping them when the current is cut: <https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=electromagnet+paperclips+demonstration>

| | |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | 42 minutes (1 period) · NYS Required Investigation |
| **NYSSLS link** | HS-PS3-5 (core — electric/magnetic field interaction and energy transfer) |
| **CCC focus** | Energy and Matter — energy is transferred between a moving magnet and a circuit through the magnetic field. |
| **Strategy chips** | ACTIVE LEARNING (predict-test-explain with a real coil/magnet) |
| **Materials** | Coil of magnetized wire, strong magnet, LED or galvanometer (or shake-flashlight); battery + iron nail + insulated wire for the electromagnet; paperclips; devices for Javalab/PhET. |

**Lesson objectives — students can:**

- Explain that a moving charge / electric **current** produces a **magnetic field** (an electromagnet).
- Describe **electromagnetic induction**: a *changing* magnetic field through a coil induces a current.
- Use a field model to explain how generators and motors transfer energy between motion and electric current.

## Phase 1 · Engage *(0 – 10 min)*

### 0–2 min · Opening Connection (SEL)

One-sentence whip-around: *"Name something you charge or power by *moving* it — a shake flashlight, a hand-crank radio, a bike light."* Hold the idea that motion can make electricity.

### 2–7 min · Phenomenon hook — coil, magnet, and LED

**Teacher actions.** Hold a magnet still inside the coil — the LED stays dark. *Move* the magnet in and out — the LED flashes. Then connect the iron-nail coil to a battery and pick up paperclips; disconnect, and they drop.

**Sample teacher language:**

> "Holding the magnet still — nothing. But the moment I *move* it through the coil, the LED lights. And over here, the coil only grabs paperclips while the battery is connected. What links moving magnets and electric current?"

**Anticipated student responses:**

- "You have to keep moving it." — capture; it's the *change* that matters.
- "The coil becomes a magnet when the battery's on." — yes; current makes a magnetic field.
- "Faster shaking makes it brighter." — flag; faster change → more induced current.

### 7–10 min · Notice & Wonder + Turn-and-Talk #1

> "Turn to your partner: what do the LED demo and the paperclip demo have in common? Use the word *magnetic field*."

Target: current ↔ magnetic field — current makes a field; a changing field makes current.

### Bridge to Phase 2

Each student writes a one-sentence **initial model**: "The LED only lights while the magnet moves because ______."

---

## Phase 2 · Explore *(10 – 30 min)*

### 10–14 min · Initial Model (silent / individual)

Prompt on the board:

> *"Draw the coil with the magnet inside it and the LED in the loop. Mark when the LED is ON. Predict: does it matter how *fast* you move the magnet? Does it matter if you stop?"*

### 14–30 min · Investigation — coil/magnet + Javalab/PhET (NYS Investigation)

Hands-on station plus the simulation.

1. **Electromagnet.** Connect the nail-coil to the battery; count paperclips lifted. Add more coil turns or a second battery — does it lift more?
2. **Induction.** Move the magnet through the coil slowly, then quickly. When is the LED brightest? What happens when the magnet sits still?
3. **Reverse it.** Pull the magnet *out* — does the current reverse (LED on opposite lead / galvanometer needle flips)?

**Teacher facilitation language:**

> "When the magnet sits still inside the coil, is the field changing? Is there any current?"
> "Faster motion — what happens to the brightness? What does that tell you about 'rate of change'?"
> "More coil turns or more current — does the electromagnet get stronger?"

Use the two diagrams to consolidate the current→field and field-change→current ideas:

![Diagram of current flowing through a wire coil: the loops of the coil create a magnetic field B running along the coil's axis, with a north pole at one end and a south pole at the other, like a bar magnet.](figures/coil_field.png)

![Diagram of electromagnetic induction: a bar magnet with north and south poles moves toward a wire coil connected to an LED; the changing magnetic field through the coil induces a current that lights the LED.](figures/induction_diagram.png)

### Teacher facilitation during Explore

- **What to look for** — groups stating "current only while the magnet *moves*" and "faster motion → brighter LED."
- **What to resist** — supplying "Faraday's law" equations; keep it at the model level (changing field → induced current).
- **What to redirect** — students who expect a steady LED from a still magnet: point them back to "is the field *changing*?"

## Phase 3 · Explain *(30 – 36 min)*

### 30–33 min · Turn-and-Talk #2 + class consensus

> "Across both demos, what is the two-way relationship between current and magnetic fields?"

Target consensus:

> "An electric current creates a magnetic field (that's an electromagnet). And a *changing* magnetic field through a coil creates — induces — a current. Faster change means more induced current."

### 33–36 min · Vocabulary introduction (≤ 3 terms)

**Sample teacher language:**

> "The region around a magnet or a current where magnetic forces act is the **magnetic field**. A coil carrying current that acts like a magnet is an **electromagnet**. And when a *changing* magnetic field through a coil drives a current, that's **electromagnetic induction** — the principle behind every generator. A generator turns motion into current; a motor runs it backward, turning current into motion."

Connection to energy (HS-PS3-5): the moving magnet transfers energy through the field into the circuit as electrical energy.

### Discussion prompts to deploy here

- "Why doesn't a magnet just resting inside a coil light the LED?"
  - *Sample response:* "The field isn't changing, so no current is induced."
- "How does a bike's hand-crank/dynamo light work?"
  - *Sample response:* "Spinning moves a magnet near a coil, the changing field induces a current that lights the lamp."

---

## Phase 4 · Elaborate *(36 – 40 min)*

### 36–38 min · Apply — generators and motors

> *"A wind turbine spins a magnet inside a coil. Explain, using *electromagnetic induction*, how the wind ends up powering a light bulb."*

Target: the spinning magnet keeps changing the field through the coil, which induces a current that carries energy to the bulb — motion energy → electrical energy.

### 38–40 min · Return to the phenomenon

> "In one sentence using *electromagnetic induction*, explain why the shake-flashlight only makes light while you shake it."

Target: "Shaking keeps moving the magnet through the coil, so the changing field induces a current — stop shaking and the field stops changing, so the current (and light) stops."

---

## Phase 5 · Evaluate *(40 – 42 min)*

### 40–42 min · Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

> *"(a) A current flows through a coil of wire. What does it create around the coil?
> (b) A magnet is held perfectly still inside a coil connected to an LED. Is there an induced current? Why or why not?
> (c) Name one device that uses electromagnetic induction to generate electricity, and explain in one sentence where the energy comes from."*

Expected: (a) a magnetic field (it becomes an electromagnet); (b) no — the field isn't changing, and only a *changing* field induces current; (c) a generator/dynamo/turbine — the energy comes from the motion that keeps the field changing. See `Answer_Key.docx`.

**Closing Reflection (SEL, 30 seconds):**

> "Where in your own life do you now realize electromagnetic induction is quietly working?"

## Common Misconceptions

- **Misconception:** "A magnet sitting in a coil produces a current." → **Correction:** Only a *changing* magnetic field induces a current; a stationary magnet's steady field induces nothing.
- **Misconception:** "An electromagnet is permanently magnetic." → **Correction:** It's magnetic only while current flows; cut the current and the field collapses.
- **Misconception:** "Generators create energy." → **Correction:** Generators *convert* energy — the motion that spins the magnet supplies the energy that becomes electrical energy.

## Access & Differentiation

- **ELL/ENL supports:** Sentence frames: *"A current makes a ___ field."* / *"A changing field induces a ___."* Word box: {magnetic, current, moving, changing, induction, electromagnet}. Pair *induction* with the in-and-out magnet gesture.
- **IEP/SPED supports:** Provide the coil/magnet diagrams pre-labeled; students circle "ON" vs. "OFF" for each scenario (magnet still / moving / stopped). Offer the Javalab sim as a low-stakes alternative to the hands-on station.
- **Extensions:** Have students explain why a faster spin or more coil turns increases the induced voltage (Faraday's law qualitatively) and sketch how a motor reverses a generator (current in → motion out), tying back to energy transfer (HS-PS3-5).

## Strategy Spotlight

**ACTIVE LEARNING (NYS Required Investigation).** Students physically move the magnet and watch the LED rather than being told the rule. Build a predict → test → explain loop: predict whether the LED lights for (magnet still / slow / fast / pulled out), test each, then reconcile. The "still magnet = dark LED" result is the conceptual hinge — it isolates *change* as the cause. Capture each group's observation table; the class consensus on "current ↔ changing field" should come from their data, not the slide.

## Key Vocabulary (max 3)

- **magnetic field** — the region around a magnet or a current-carrying wire where magnetic forces act, represented by field lines from north to south
- **electromagnet** — a coil of wire that becomes magnetic only while an electric current flows through it; more turns or more current makes it stronger
- **electromagnetic induction** — the production of a current in a coil by a *changing* magnetic field; the basis of generators (motion → electricity)
