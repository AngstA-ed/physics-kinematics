## Guided Notes

**A series circuit has a single __________ for charge.** Because there is only one path:

- The **current** is the __________ at every point in the loop.
- The total (equivalent) resistance is the __________ of the resistors:
  > **R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + …**
- The voltage drops across the resistors __________ up to the source voltage.
- One break anywhere stops the current __________.

![Schematic of a series circuit: one battery drives a single current through three resistors in a row; the current is the same everywhere.](figures/series_circuit.png)

**Finding the current.** Once you have R_total, use Ohm's Law:
> I = V ÷ R_total     (this same I flows through every resistor)

**Voltage drops add to the source:**

![Bar chart of voltage drops in a series circuit summing to the source voltage.](figures/series_voltage_drops.png)

Reference: **V = I·R** · series **R_total = R₁ + R₂ + …** · power **P = V·I = I²·R**

## Worked Example

**Problem.** A 20 V battery is in series with R₁ = 2 Ω, R₂ = 3 Ω, and R₃ = 5 Ω. (a) Find R_total. (b) Find the current. (c) Find the voltage drop across R₃.

**Solution.**
1. R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ = 2 + 3 + 5 = __________ Ω.
2. I = V ÷ R_total = 20 ÷ 10 = __________ A (this current flows through every resistor).
3. Voltage across R₃: V₃ = I·R₃ = 2 × 5 = __________ V.
   *(Check: drops are 4 V + 6 V + 10 V = __________ V = the source.)*
