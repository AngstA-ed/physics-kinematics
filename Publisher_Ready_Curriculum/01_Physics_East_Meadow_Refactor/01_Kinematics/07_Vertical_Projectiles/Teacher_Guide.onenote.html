CCC Focus

Patterns — the upward and downward halves of a vertical-throw trajectory are symmetric: time to rise = time to fall, and the speed at the same height on the way up equals the speed on the way down (only the direction differs).

At-a-Glance

  • Duration: 42 minutes (1 period)
  • Materials: A volleyball or tennis ball; a phone or stopwatch; access to the interactive on devices.
  • Safety: None for a soft ball thrown straight up at low height. Avoid throwing toward windows or fixtures.
  • Prior knowledge: Lesson 06 (freefall, g, terminal velocity).

Lesson Objectives

I can… - Describe the velocity of a vertically thrown object at three key moments: launch, peak, and landing. - Explain why the time to rise equals the time to fall (when launch and landing heights are the same). - Predict the peak height and total time-of-flight given an initial velocity.

Discussion Prompts

  • “What is the velocity of the ball at the very peak of its flight? Is it zero, positive, or negative?”
  • Sample student response: “Velocity is zero at the peak — but acceleration is still −g.”
  • “If a ball is launched at 10 m/s, how fast is it moving when it returns to the launch height?”
  • Probe: Surface the symmetry: same speed, opposite direction (so velocity is −10 m/s at landing).

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: “At the peak, both velocity and acceleration are zero.” → Correction: Velocity is zero, but acceleration is still −g. Gravity does not pause; that’s why the ball begins to fall again immediately.
  • Misconception: “It takes longer to fall than to rise (because the ball is slowing down on the way up).” → Correction: Both halves take exactly the same time when launch and landing heights match — that’s the symmetry.
  • Misconception: “Throwing harder makes the ball stay up longer in proportion.” → Correction: Time to peak = v₀/g, so doubling v₀ doubles the air-time. Linear, not magic.

Access & Differentiation

  • ELL/ENL supports: Sentence frames: *“At the peak, the ball’s velocity is ___ but its acceleration is ___.”* Word-choice box: {peak, launch, landing, symmetry, time-of-flight, gravity, zero}.
  • IEP/SPED supports: Provide a pre-printed v-t template with the peak time and zero crossing marked; students fill in the remaining values. Use color (purple for upward, blue for downward) to highlight the sign change.
  • Extensions: Predict the speed (magnitude) of the ball at half its peak height. Or: a ball thrown up from a balcony lands on the ground below — how does the asymmetry between rise and fall times change?

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

A volleyball is thrown straight up with an initial velocity of 12 m/s. Use g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. How long until it reaches its peak?
  2. What is the maximum height above the launch point?
  3. What is the velocity when it returns to the launch height?
  4. Total time-of-flight?

Closing reflection (30 sec): What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • peak — the highest point of a vertical-throw trajectory; v = 0 momentarily
  • time-of-flight — total time from launch to landing
  • symmetry — for a vertical throw with equal launch and landing heights, time-up = time-down, and speed at any height on the way up equals the speed at the same height on the way down
