Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

  1. Set v₀ = 10 m/s. What is the velocity at the peak? At what time does the ball reach the peak? Expected: Velocity at peak = 0 m/s (instantaneously). Time to peak = v₀/g = 10/9.8 ≈ 1.02 s. Rubric: Full credit if both answers correct with units. Half credit for one. Tolerance: ±0.05 s. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1.

  2. Why is the v-t graph a straight line, even though the ball is sometimes going up and sometimes going down? Expected: Because acceleration is constant (a = −g) throughout the entire trip — gravity never pauses. The slope of the v-t graph equals the constant acceleration, so the v-t graph is a single straight line crossing zero at the peak. Rubric: Full credit for “constant acceleration → constant slope → straight line” reasoning. Half credit for “slope = acceleration” without the “constant” connection. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1.

  3. A ball is thrown straight up at 8 m/s. How fast is it moving when it returns to the launch height? Expected: 8 m/s, but moving downward. The magnitudes are identical because of the symmetry; only the direction (sign) flips. Rubric: Full credit for “8 m/s downward” or equivalent. Half credit for “8 m/s” without specifying direction. No credit for any answer that says “less than 8 m/s” — that response shows the asymmetry misconception. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1.

Exit Ticket Answer

Ball thrown straight up with v₀ = 12 m/s, g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. Time to peak: t_peak = v₀/g = 12/9.8 ≈ 1.22 s.
  2. Maximum height above launch: h = v₀² / (2g) = 144 / 19.6 ≈ 7.35 m.
  3. Velocity at the launch height on the way down: 12 m/s downward (same magnitude as launch, opposite sign — symmetry).
  4. Total time-of-flight = 2 × t_peak ≈ 2.45 s.

Rubric: (a) 1 pt; (b) 1 pt; (c) 1 pt (must include direction); (d) 1 pt. Tolerance: ±0.05 s, ±0.1 m.

NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1.

Closing Reflection (rubric)

Closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any honest response. Look for:

  • Surprise / interest signal in answer 1 (e.g., “I didn’t expect the speed coming down to equal the launch speed exactly”)
  • Naming a peer or self-acknowledgment in answer 2
