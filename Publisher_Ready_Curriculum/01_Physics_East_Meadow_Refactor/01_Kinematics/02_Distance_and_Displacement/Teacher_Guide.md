| | |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | 42 minutes (1 period) |
| **NYSSLS link** | HS-PS2-1 (thread continues) |
| **CCC focus** | Patterns — students notice the pattern that distance always grows along a path, while displacement only depends on the start and end points. |

| **Safety** | None. |
| **Prior knowledge** | Lesson 01 (vectors, magnitude, direction); coordinate grids. |

**Lesson objectives — students can:**

- Distinguish **distance** (a scalar — total path length) from **displacement** (a vector — straight-line change in position with direction).
- Calculate displacement from a path drawn on a coordinate grid.
- Identify a real situation where distance and displacement are different, and one where they are equal.

## Phase 1 · Engage *(0 – 12 min)*

### 0–3 min · Opening Connection (SEL)

Pick one quick check-in: a 1-question warmup ("On a scale of 1–5, how's your week going?"), a "name something you noticed since yesterday's lesson," or a quick gratitude / effort acknowledgment. 60–90 seconds total.

### 3–8 min · Phenomenon hook — the long-way walk

**Teacher actions.** Ask a willing student to walk from their seat to the door — but to take the long way (behind chairs, around tables). Time them. Have a second student measure two things, written on the board:

1. The **path length** (count the floor tiles or use a tape measure).
2. The **straight-line gap** from their seat to the door.

**Sample teacher language:**

> "Maya is going to walk from her desk to the door — the long way. The rest of you have one job: tell me how far she traveled. We're going to write down two numbers on the board, and then we'll figure out which one is right."

**Anticipated student responses:**

- Some students give the path length (distance). Some give the straight-line gap (displacement). Some give both.
- "Wait, those are different numbers" — perfect, that's the point.
- "Which one is the right answer?" — *do not answer yet.* Reflect: "Great question. We'll figure that out today."

### 8–12 min · Notice & Wonder + Turn-and-Talk #1

Capture two columns. Then:

> "Turn to your partner. Which number is 'the answer' — the path length or the straight-line gap? Why?"

After 90 seconds, take 2–3 responses. Most students will defend one side or the other. The desirable insight is: *both are real, but they answer different questions.* If no one says that, leave it as an open question for now.

### Bridge to Phase 2

By end of Phase 1, every student should have a written **initial model**: their own walk path on grid paper, with both the path length and the straight-line gap labeled.

---

## Phase 2 · Explore *(12 – 32 min)*

### 12–17 min · Initial Model (silent / individual)

Distribute graph paper. Specific prompt on the board:

> *"On the grid, sketch a walk: 4 squares east, then 3 squares north. Mark the start with a dot and the end with a dot. Label two numbers: the path length, and the straight-line gap from start to end."*

This surfaces the same number-pair as Maya's walk, in students' own hands.

### 17–32 min · Investigation — interactive path builder

Distribute devices. Students open the [interactive path builder](https://angsta-ed.github.io/physics-kinematics/lessons/02-distance-displacement.html) (or the Physics Classroom Vector Walk if devices aren't available). The interactive lets them step a walker N/E/S/W on a grid and toggle "show distance" vs. "show displacement" independently.

**Two specific challenges (write on the board):**

1. *"Build a path with distance = 12 and displacement = 4."*
2. *"Build a path with distance = 8 and displacement = 0."*

**Teacher facilitation language during the investigation:**

> "What did you do to make the displacement zero?"
> "Could you have done it differently? Show me a second way."
> "If I told you the distance is 10 and the displacement is 6, can you imagine the shape of that path?"

### Teacher facilitation during Explore

- **What to look for** — students who deliberately reverse direction to make displacement smaller than distance. The "aha" moment: realizing displacement only depends on start and end.
- **What to resist** — don't define the words yet. Let the experience surface the distinction.
- **What to redirect** — students who think "displacement < distance" is impossible should be redirected to challenge #2 (a round trip — distance > 0, displacement = 0).

---

## Phase 3 · Explain *(32 – 37 min)*

### 32–35 min · Turn-and-Talk #2 + class consensus

Bring the class back. Show a sketched round-trip path on the board (out 5, back 5). Ask:

> "How far did this person travel? How far did they end up from where they started?"

Take volunteer answers. Surface that both answers are correct — they answer different questions. The target consensus statement:

> "*Distance* tells you how much path you walked. *Displacement* tells you the straight-line gap from start to end, with direction. Both are real. They answer different questions."

### 35–37 min · Vocabulary introduction (≤ 3 terms)

**Sample teacher language:**

> "When a quantity has only a *size* — no direction — we call it a **scalar**. Distance is a scalar; it's always positive. **Displacement** is a *vector*: it has size AND direction. Distance is the path; displacement is the gap. From now on, when you read a problem, ask yourself: is it asking me for the path or the gap?"

### Discussion prompts to deploy here

- "If a runner finishes a 400 m lap exactly back at the starting line, what is her distance? What is her displacement?"
  - *Sample student response:* "Distance = 400 m, displacement = 0 because she ends where she started."
- "Can displacement ever be larger than distance? Why or why not?"
  - *Sample student response:* "No. The straight line between two points is always the shortest route, so a curved path can only be longer. Displacement ≤ distance, with equality only in a straight-line trip."

---

## Phase 4 · Elaborate *(37 – 40 min)*

### 37–39 min · Revise the model

Students reopen their initial sketch from Phase 2. Specific prompt:

> *"Update your sketch. Label one number 'distance' and the other 'displacement,' and add an arrow showing the direction of the displacement."*

### 39–40 min · Return to the phenomenon

Re-pose Maya's walk:

> "If we re-do Maya's walk, but she walks in a perfectly straight line from her seat to the door, do distance and displacement still differ? Predict in one sentence."

Target: "No — for a straight-line walk in one direction, distance and displacement are equal." Surface a student response, then move to evaluate.

---

## Phase 5 · Evaluate *(40 – 42 min)*

### 40–42 min · Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

Distribute exit slips. Transfer prompt:

> *"A student walks 4 m east, then 3 m north, then 4 m west.
> (a) What is the total distance traveled?
> (b) What is the displacement (magnitude and direction)?
> (c) In one sentence, explain why your answers to (a) and (b) are different."*

Expected: (a) 11 m. (b) 3 m, north. (c) "The student changed direction; distance accumulates path length while displacement only depends on start vs. end positions." See `Answer_Key.docx` for the rubric.

**Closing Reflection (SEL, 30 seconds, on the same exit slip):**

> "What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?"

## Common Misconceptions

- **Misconception:** "Distance and displacement are the same thing." → **Correction:** They agree only when motion is in a single straight line in one direction. Any turn or reversal makes them differ.
- **Misconception:** "Displacement is always positive." → **Correction:** Displacement is a vector; in 1-D it has sign (left/right or +/−). It can be zero (round trip) or negative.
- **Misconception:** "If you go and come back, you traveled zero." → **Correction:** Your *displacement* is zero, but your *distance* (total path) is not.

---

## Access & Differentiation

- **ELL/ENL supports:** Sentence frame: *"The distance is ___ but the displacement is ___ because ___."* Word-choice box: {path, straight-line, scalar, vector, zero, sign}. Visual-first — emphasize arrow vs. length labels.
- **IEP/SPED supports:** Provide a printed grid with dots already placed at start and end; students need only count squares. Pre-label one example as "distance" and "displacement" so the format is concrete.
- **Extensions:** Have students invent a path where distance equals exactly twice displacement; or where displacement is zero but distance is nonzero in *3* different ways.

## Key Vocabulary (max 3)

- **distance** — the total length of the path traveled (a scalar; always ≥ 0)
- **displacement** — the straight-line change in position from start to end, with direction (a vector; can be zero or negative in 1-D)
- **scalar** — a quantity with magnitude only (no direction); contrasts with *vector*
