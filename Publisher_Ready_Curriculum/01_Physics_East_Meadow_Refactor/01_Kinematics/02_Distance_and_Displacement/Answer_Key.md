# Distance and Displacement — Answer Key

## Cover

Unit: Kinematics — Lesson 02: Distance and Displacement
East Meadow Schools × Valley Stream Central HSD

## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. **A jogger runs around a 400 m oval track exactly once. What is the distance? What is the displacement?**
   *Expected:* Distance = **400 m** (one full lap of the path). Displacement = **0 m** (the runner ends at the same position they started).
   *Rubric:* Full credit for both with units. Students who answer "400 m" for both reveal the additivity misconception — coach with the Hochman *because/but/so*: "The displacement is 400 m **because** I followed the runner around, **but** displacement only cares about start and end, **so** the displacement is actually 0 m."
   *NYSSLS:* HS-PS2-1 (vector vs. scalar distinction).

2. **Sketch a path on the grid that has distance = 12 units and displacement = 4 units. Then sketch one with distance = 8 units and displacement = 0 units.**
   *Expected:* Many valid solutions. For (a), e.g., 4 right → 4 up → 4 left (distance 12, displacement = 4 up). For (b), any closed loop of total length 8, e.g., 2 right → 2 up → 2 left → 2 down.
   *Rubric:* Full credit if both sketches use grid squares and both calculations are shown. Half credit for one valid sketch.
   *NYSSLS:* HS-PS2-1.

3. **Can displacement ever be larger than distance? Explain.**
   *Expected:* **No.** The straight line between two points is always the shortest path between them; a curved or zig-zag path can only be longer. So displacement ≤ distance, with equality only when the motion is a straight line in a single direction.
   *Rubric:* Full credit for "no" plus a reason that invokes "straight line is shortest." Half credit for "no" without justification.
   *NYSSLS:* HS-PS2-1.

## Exit Ticket Answer

A student walks 4 m east, then 3 m north, then 4 m west.

(a) Total distance = 4 + 3 + 4 = **11 m**.
(b) Displacement: Net east-west = 0 m (4 east cancels 4 west). Net north-south = 3 m north. So displacement = **3 m, north**.
(c) Distance and displacement differ here because the student changed direction; distance accumulates path length while displacement only depends on start vs. end positions.

*Rubric:* Full credit for all three parts. (a) and (b) each worth 1 pt; (c) worth 2 pts (1 for noting "distance vs. start-end," 1 for connecting to the change in direction). Tolerance: ±0.

*NYSSLS:* HS-PS2-1.

## Closing Reflection (rubric)

Closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any honest response. Look for:

- Surprise / interest signal in answer 1 (e.g., "I didn't think 'zero' could be a real answer for motion")
- Naming a peer or self-acknowledgment in answer 2
