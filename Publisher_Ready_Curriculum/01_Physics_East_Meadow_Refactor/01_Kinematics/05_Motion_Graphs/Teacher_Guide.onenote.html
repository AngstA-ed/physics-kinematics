Lesson Objectives

I can… - Read a position-time (x-t) graph and describe the motion in words. - Read a velocity-time (v-t) graph and describe the motion in words. - Connect the slope of an x-t graph to velocity, and the slope of a v-t graph to acceleration.

Discussion Prompts

  • “If two graphs both rise, do they show the same kind of motion? What if one is x-t and the other is v-t?”
  • Sample student response: “Rising x-t means the object is moving forward. Rising v-t means it’s speeding up — those aren’t the same.”
  • “What does a horizontal line on a v-t graph mean? On an x-t graph?”
  • “Where on a v-t graph could you find the displacement?”

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: “An x-t graph and a v-t graph that look the same describe the same motion.” → Correction: They describe the same motion only if you read the slope (not the height) and remember which axis you are looking at.
  • Misconception: “A horizontal x-t line means the object is at rest, but a horizontal v-t line means the object is moving slowly.” → Correction: Horizontal v-t at any non-zero height means constant velocity, not slow.
  • Misconception: “Negative slope = bad / wrong.” → Correction: Negative slope on x-t means moving in the −x direction; negative slope on v-t means decelerating (or accelerating in the −x direction).

Access & Differentiation

  • ELL/ENL supports: Sentence frames: “On the x-t graph, the slope is , which means the object is .” Word-choice box: {position, velocity, slope, rising, falling, horizontal, constant}. Pair with visuals — show graph and motion side by side before introducing words.
  • IEP/SPED supports: Pre-printed templates with x-axis (time) and y-axis (position OR velocity) labeled; tick marks placed. Color-code: purple for x-t, blue for v-t, throughout. One-step prompts.
  • Extensions: Given an a-t graph that is constant non-zero, sketch the v-t and x-t graphs. Or: invent an x-t graph that produces a v-t graph with a triangle wave (motion alternates between forward and backward).

Strategy Spotlight

Active Learning — gallery walk (15 min). Distribute one large grid sheet to each student. Each student draws their own x-t graph for a unique short motion (e.g., “I walked forward 5 s, stopped 3 s, walked forward faster for 4 s”). Tape all graphs along one wall.

Round 1 (8 min): Students walk the gallery in pairs. At each graph, they have 30 seconds to write on a sticky note: “I think this person ___” — one sentence describing the motion. They stick the note to the graph and move on. No talking during the walk.

Round 2 (4 min): Students return to their own graph and read the sticky notes. Did the gallery readers describe their motion correctly?

Round 3 (3 min): Whole-class debrief — pick three graphs that produced especially varied or wrong interpretations and discuss why. The aim: surface that the slope (not height) carries the velocity information, and direction-of-slope-change carries the acceleration information.

Why this works: Students get rapid, low-stakes feedback from peers, exercise reading skills (graph → narration), and confront the gap between their graph and others’ interpretations. Direction matters; slope matters.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

Below is a velocity-time graph for a moving object:

  • 0–4 s: v rises from 0 to 8 m/s (straight line)
  • 4–7 s: v stays at 8 m/s (horizontal)
  • 7–10 s: v falls from 8 m/s to 0 (straight line)
  1. What is the acceleration during 0–4 s? During 4–7 s? During 7–10 s?
  2. Sketch the corresponding x-t graph (qualitative shape — no numbers required).
  3. What is the total displacement over the 10 s? (Hint: area under the v-t graph.)

Closing reflection (30 sec): What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • position-time graph (x-t) — graph of position vs time; slope equals velocity
  • velocity-time graph (v-t) — graph of velocity vs time; slope equals acceleration
  • area-under-curve — on a v-t graph, the area between the curve and the time axis equals the displacement over that interval
