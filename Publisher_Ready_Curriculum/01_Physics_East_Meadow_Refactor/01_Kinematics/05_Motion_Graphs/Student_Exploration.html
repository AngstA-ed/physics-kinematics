Unit: Kinematics — Lesson 05: Motion Graphs

ACTIVE LEARNING

Phenomenon

A classmate is going to walk a short choreography: forward 3 seconds, stop 2 seconds, backward 4 seconds. A second classmate will narrate her position aloud each second. Your job: record the same trip in three different graphs — position, velocity, and acceleration — all telling the same story.

Driving Question

How can the same motion produce three different-looking graphs that are all "right"?

Notice & Wonder

When the classmate stopped, what happened to the position-time graph? What happened to the velocity-time graph? Are they the same?

Initial Model

Sketch your best guess at the x-t graph for the choreography. Don't worry about exact numbers — focus on the shape.

Investigate

Press Play below. A dot moves on the track at the top, and the x-t and v-t graphs draw alongside in real time.

When the dot stopped, what was the slope of the x-t graph? What was the value on the v-t graph? Are they the same number?

Make It Make Sense

  1. Watch the dot move with constant velocity 4 m/s for 5 seconds. Sketch its x-t graph and its v-t graph.
  2. An object's v-t graph rises from 0 at t = 0 to 10 m/s at t = 5 s. What is the acceleration? What is the displacement during this interval?
  3. The x-t graph of an object is a horizontal line. What is the velocity? Sketch the corresponding v-t graph.
Stuck? Sentence frame

"On the x-t graph the slope is ___, which means the object is ___."

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • position-time graph (x-t) — graph of position vs time; slope equals velocity
  • velocity-time graph (v-t) — graph of velocity vs time; slope equals acceleration
  • area-under-curve — on a v-t graph, the area between the curve and the t-axis equals the displacement

Revise Your Model

Update your initial x-t sketch from the choreography. Then add a v-t graph below it. Label each region (forward / stopped / backward) on both graphs.

Return to the Phenomenon

Re-narrate the classmate's choreography using all three quantities (position, velocity, acceleration). Where does each one match up to the graphs you drew?

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. A v-t graph: 0–4 s rises from 0 to 8 m/s; 4–7 s stays at 8 m/s; 7–10 s falls from 8 to 0. (a) Accelerations during each interval? (b) Sketch the corresponding x-t graph (qualitative). (c) Total displacement (use area under v-t).
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
