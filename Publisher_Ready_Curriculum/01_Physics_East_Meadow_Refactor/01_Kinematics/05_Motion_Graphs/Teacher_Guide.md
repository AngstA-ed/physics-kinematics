| | |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | 42 minutes (1 period) |
| **NYSSLS link** | HS-PS2-1 |
| **CCC focus** | Patterns — slope of position-time graph = velocity; slope of velocity-time graph = acceleration. Same motion produces *different but related* graph patterns at each level. |
| **Strategy chips** | ACTIVE LEARNING (gallery walk) |
| **Materials** | Chart paper or large grid sheets (1 per student); markers (mixed colors); sticky notes (1 pad per pair); access to the interactive on devices. |
| **Safety** | None. |
| **Prior knowledge** | Lessons 01–04 (vectors, distance/displacement, velocity, acceleration). |

**Lesson objectives — students can:**

- Read a position-time (x-t) graph and describe the motion in words.
- Read a velocity-time (v-t) graph and describe the motion in words.
- Connect the *slope* of an x-t graph to velocity, and the *slope* of a v-t graph to acceleration.

## Phase 1 · Engage *(0 – 12 min)*

### 0–3 min · Opening Connection (SEL)

Quick check-in. 60–90 seconds.

### 3–8 min · Phenomenon hook — the student-walk choreography

**Teacher actions.** Pick a willing student to walk a choreography: 3 seconds forward, 2 seconds stopped, 4 seconds backward. A second student narrates her position out loud each second ("she's at +1 m, +2 m, +3 m, +3 m, +3 m, +2 m, +1 m, 0 m, −1 m"). Time it with a stopwatch.

**Sample teacher language:**

> "Maya is going to walk a specific routine. Tara is going to call out her position every second. Your job: capture this motion in a graph. We're going to do it three different ways and see which one tells the truest story."

**Anticipated student responses:**

- "I should plot position vs. time" — affirm.
- "I should plot velocity vs. time" — also affirm, this is the key second view.
- "When she stops, the position stays the same, so the line is flat" — yes, this insight unlocks the lesson.
- Confusion about whether velocity goes negative — that's productive; we'll resolve it.

### 8–12 min · Notice & Wonder + Turn-and-Talk #1

Capture two columns. Then:

> "Turn to your partner: when Maya stopped, what did the position-time graph look like? What did the velocity-time graph look like? Were those the same number?"

After 90 seconds, take 2–3 responses. The dispute that drives the lesson: students often think a flat line means the same thing on x-t and v-t. They don't.

### Bridge to Phase 2

By end of Phase 1, every student has sketched their **initial model**: their best guess at the x-t graph for Maya's choreography. Just shape — no exact numbers required.

---

## Phase 2 · Explore *(12 – 32 min)*

### 12–17 min · Initial Model + interactive setup

Students sketch the x-t graph individually. Then they open the [interactive scenario explorer](https://angsta-ed.github.io/physics-kinematics/lessons/05-motion-graphs.html). They run the four pre-baked scenarios — constant velocity, constant acceleration, forward → stop → backward, speed-up-then-slow-down — and record what each scenario looks like on x-t, v-t, and a-t.

### 17–32 min · Investigation — Active Learning gallery walk

Distribute one large grid sheet to each student. **Each student draws their own x-t graph for a *unique* short motion** — a story they invent. Examples to suggest:

- "I walked forward 5 s, stopped 3 s, walked forward faster for 4 s."
- "I jogged for 4 s, then sprinted for 2 s, then stopped suddenly for 3 s."
- "I biked uphill (slowing) for 5 s, then downhill (speeding up) for 5 s."

Tape all graphs along one wall. The gallery walk has three rounds:

**Round 1 (8 min): Silent walk.** Students walk the gallery in pairs. At each graph they have 30 seconds to write on a sticky note: *"I think this person ___"* — one sentence describing the motion. They stick the note on the graph and move on. **No talking during the walk.**

**Round 2 (4 min): Return.** Students return to their own graph and read the sticky notes. Did the gallery readers describe their motion correctly? They flag the most-correct and most-wrong interpretations.

**Round 3 (3 min): Whole-class debrief.** Teacher picks three graphs that produced especially varied or wrong interpretations and discusses why. Surface the lesson's central insight.

**Sample teacher facilitation language during the walk:**

> "What's the slope doing in the middle of this graph? What does that mean about the person?"
> "If two graphs both rise, do they show the same kind of motion?"

### Teacher facilitation during Explore

- **What to look for** — students discovering that *slope* (not height) carries the velocity information. Common surface: students realize they can't tell a story from the height alone.
- **What to resist** — don't write "slope of x-t = velocity" on the board. Phase 3 names it.
- **What to redirect** — graphs that are unphysical (e.g., a vertical line on x-t = infinite velocity) become the most productive teaching moments. Highlight one in Round 3.

---

## Phase 3 · Explain *(32 – 37 min)*

### 32–35 min · Turn-and-Talk #2 + class consensus

Bring the class back. Show one of the most-misinterpreted graphs from the gallery. Ask:

> "Talk to your partner. Why did so many readers misread this graph? What information was hidden by the way it was drawn?"

After 90 seconds, take volunteers. The target consensus statement:

> "**The slope of an x-t graph is the velocity** — not the height. **The slope of a v-t graph is the acceleration**. Same motion can be drawn three different ways (x-t, v-t, a-t), and they're all true — they're just different views of the same story."

### 35–37 min · Vocabulary introduction (≤ 3 terms)

**Sample teacher language:**

> "**Position-time graph (x-t)** — position vs. time. Slope tells you the velocity. **Velocity-time graph (v-t)** — velocity vs. time. Slope tells you the acceleration. And the **area under a v-t curve** equals the displacement during that interval — which is going to matter a lot in Lesson 09."

### Discussion prompts to deploy here

- "If two graphs both rise, do they show the same kind of motion? What if one is x-t and the other is v-t?"
  - *Sample student response:* "Rising x-t means the object is moving forward. Rising v-t means it's speeding up — those aren't the same."
- "What does a horizontal line on a v-t graph mean? On an x-t graph?"
  - *Sample student response:* "Horizontal v-t = constant velocity (any value). Horizontal x-t = at rest (zero velocity)."

---

## Phase 4 · Elaborate *(37 – 40 min)*

### 37–39 min · Revise the model

Students reopen their initial x-t sketch from Phase 2. Specific prompt:

> *"Update your sketch. Below it, draw the corresponding v-t graph. Label each region of both graphs (forward / stopped / backward) with one word."*

### 39–40 min · Return to the phenomenon

Re-narrate Maya's choreography. Ask:

> "Now narrate Maya's motion using all three quantities — position, velocity, acceleration. Where does each one match up to the graphs you drew?"

Take a volunteer's narration. Target: "Position increased linearly for 3 s; velocity was positive constant. Position stayed at 3 m for 2 s; velocity was zero; acceleration was zero (no change). Position decreased for 4 s; velocity was negative constant; acceleration was zero (still no change in velocity)."

---

## Phase 5 · Evaluate *(40 – 42 min)*

### 40–42 min · Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

Distribute exit slips. Transfer prompt:

> *"Below is a velocity-time graph for a moving object:
> 0–4 s: v rises from 0 to 8 m/s (straight line)
> 4–7 s: v stays at 8 m/s (horizontal)
> 7–10 s: v falls from 8 m/s to 0 (straight line)
>
> (a) What is the acceleration during 0–4 s? During 4–7 s? During 7–10 s?
> (b) Sketch the corresponding x-t graph (qualitative shape — no numbers required).
> (c) What is the total displacement over the 10 s? (Hint: area under the v-t graph.)"*

Expected: (a) +2 m/s², 0 m/s², −2.67 m/s². (b) Concave-up curve, then straight positive slope, then concave-down curve, monotonically increasing throughout. (c) 52 m total.

**Closing Reflection (SEL, 30 seconds, on the same exit slip):**

> "What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?"

## Common Misconceptions

- **Misconception:** "An x-t graph and a v-t graph that look the same describe the same motion." → **Correction:** Same shape on different axes describes very different motion. Read the slope (not the height) and remember which axis you're looking at.
- **Misconception:** "A horizontal x-t line means the object is at rest, but a horizontal v-t line means the object is moving slowly." → **Correction:** Horizontal v-t at any non-zero height means *constant velocity*, not slow.
- **Misconception:** "Negative slope = bad / wrong." → **Correction:** Negative slope on x-t means moving in the −x direction; negative slope on v-t means decelerating (or accelerating in the −x direction).

---

## Access & Differentiation

- **ELL/ENL supports:** Sentence frames: *"On the x-t graph, the slope is ___, which means the object is ___."* Word-choice box: {position, velocity, slope, rising, falling, horizontal, constant}. Pair with visuals — show graph and motion side by side before introducing words.
- **IEP/SPED supports:** Pre-printed templates with x-axis (time) and y-axis (position OR velocity) labeled; tick marks placed. Color-code: purple for x-t, blue for v-t, throughout. One-step prompts.
- **Extensions:** Given an a-t graph that is constant non-zero, sketch the v-t and x-t graphs. Or: invent an x-t graph that produces a v-t graph with a triangle wave (motion alternates between forward and backward).

## Strategy Spotlight

**Active Learning — gallery walk (15 min)** — see Phase 2, Investigation. The full process: each student draws their own x-t graph for a unique invented motion. Tape them all to one wall. Three rounds: silent walk with sticky-note interpretations (8 min), return to read your own notes (4 min), whole-class debrief (3 min).

The strategy works because students get rapid, low-stakes feedback from peers and exercise reading skills (graph → narration), and confront the gap between their graph and others' interpretations. Direction matters; slope matters. The misreadings are the most productive teaching moments.

## Key Vocabulary (max 3)

- **position-time graph (x-t)** — graph of position vs time; *slope* equals velocity
- **velocity-time graph (v-t)** — graph of velocity vs time; *slope* equals acceleration
- **area-under-curve** — on a v-t graph, the area between the curve and the time axis equals the displacement over that interval
