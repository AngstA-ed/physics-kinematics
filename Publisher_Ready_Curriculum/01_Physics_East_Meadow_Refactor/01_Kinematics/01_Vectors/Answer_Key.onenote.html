Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

  1. Slide the controls so A = 3 and B = 4. What is the magnitude and direction of the resultant? Expected: Magnitude = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5 units. Direction = arctan(4/3) ≈ 53.1° above the x-axis (counter-clockwise from the +x direction). Rubric: Full credit if both magnitude and direction are shown with units / degrees. Half credit if only magnitude is given. Tolerance: ±0.1 on magnitude, ±1° on angle. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1 (vector quantities underpin Newton’s second law).

  2. Describe what happens to the resultant when A and B point in the same direction. What about opposite directions? Expected: When the two vectors point in the same direction, the resultant magnitude equals the sum of the magnitudes (and points the same way). When they point in opposite directions, the resultant equals the absolute difference of their magnitudes (and points along the larger vector). When the magnitudes are equal and opposite, the resultant is zero. Rubric: Full credit if both same-direction and opposite-direction cases are addressed and the student notes that direction matters (not just the numbers). Half credit if only one case is addressed. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1 (sets up the net force idea — same-direction forces add, opposite cancel).

  3. If a vector has magnitude 10 and points 30° above the x-axis, what are its x- and y-components? Expected: x-component = 10·cos(30°) ≈ 8.66. y-component = 10·sin(30°) = 5.0. Rubric: Full credit if both components are correct to 1 decimal place; tolerance ±0.2. Look for cos↔︎sin swap as the most common error — half credit if components are swapped. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1 (component decomposition is the foundation for 2-D motion in Lessons 05–09).

Exit Ticket Answer

A boat sails 5 km east, then 12 km north.

Expected: The two displacements form a right triangle. Resultant magnitude = √(5² + 12²) = √169 = 13 km. Direction ≈ arctan(12/5) ≈ 67.4° north of east. Sketch should show a right triangle with the 5 km leg horizontal, the 12 km leg vertical, and the resultant as the hypotenuse drawn from origin to end.

Rubric:

  • Full credit: magnitude is 13 km (±0.5), reasoning sentence references the Pythagorean theorem or head-to-tail rule, and a labeled sketch is present.
  • Partial credit: magnitude correct without sketch (-1) or sketch correct without explicit reasoning sentence (-1).
  • No credit if student adds 5 + 12 = 17 km — that response shows the additivity misconception. Annotate with a Hochman because/but/so prompt for the next class: “Adding the numbers gives 17 km because I added the magnitudes, but the legs are perpendicular, so I must use Pythagoras and the answer is 13 km.”

NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1 (vector addition under perpendicular components).

Closing Reflection (rubric)

Closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any honest response. Look for:

  • Surprise / interest signal in answer 1 (e.g., “I was surprised that 3 and 4 give 5, not 7” or “I didn’t think direction mattered that much”)
  • Naming a peer or self-acknowledgment in answer 2 (e.g., “Maya helped me see why we use Pythagoras” or “I figured out the components on my own”)
