Unit: Kinematics — Lesson 04: Acceleration

BTC

Phenomenon

Open the Physics Classroom acceleration animations. Watch any one for 30 seconds. Notice how the spacing between the moving dots changes from frame to frame. Why?

Driving Question

How can we measure how fast an object's velocity is changing?

Notice & Wonder

Some animations show dots that get further apart over time. Some show dots getting closer. What do you think the difference means about the object?

Initial Model

Sketch a velocity-time (v-t) graph of what you think "speeding up" looks like. Then sketch what "constant velocity" looks like. What's the difference?

Investigate

Set the acceleration with the slider, then press Run. Watch the dot move and the velocity grow (or shrink) on the v-t graph in real time.

Storyboard frame 1. a = +2 m/s², v₀ = 0. After 8 s, the dot has reached v = 16 m/s. The v-t graph is a straight line going up — slope = +2. The dot's spacing on the position track grows over time.

Storyboard frame 2. a = −1 m/s², v₀ = 10 m/s. The dot starts moving forward but the v-t line trends downward; at t = 10 s, velocity hits zero. The slope of v-t is the (negative) acceleration.

Storyboard frame 3. a = 0, v₀ = 5 m/s. The dot moves at constant velocity. The v-t graph is horizontal — zero slope = zero acceleration.

If two cars have the same acceleration but different starting velocities, what is the same about their v-t graphs? What is different?

Make It Make Sense

  1. Slide a = +3 m/s² and watch the dot for 4 s. What is the dot's velocity at the end? How far did it travel from rest?
  2. What does the v-t graph slope tell you? Sketch a v-t graph for a car that speeds up at 2 m/s² for 5 s, then holds steady for 3 s.
  3. A car moving west has a negative acceleration (in the eastward-positive convention). Is the car speeding up or slowing down? Explain.
Stuck? Sentence frame

"The acceleration is ___ m/s² because the velocity changed by ___ m/s in ___ seconds."

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • acceleration — change in velocity ÷ time; a vector; units m/s²
  • deceleration — informal name for acceleration whose vector opposes the velocity
  • slope — on a v-t graph, the steepness of the line; equals the acceleration

Revise Your Model

Update your initial v-t sketches. Add slope labels (positive, negative, zero). Use the word acceleration.

Return to the Phenomenon

Open one of the Physics Classroom acceleration animations again. Predict what its v-t graph would look like, then check yourself with the interactive above.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. A sprinter starts from rest and reaches 9 m/s in 3 seconds. (a) Average acceleration? (b) Sketch her v-t graph. (c) After the line she slows from 9 to 0 in 6 seconds. What is her acceleration during the slow-down? Positive or negative? Explain.
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
