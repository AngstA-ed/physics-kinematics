Cause and Effect — a change in velocity (cause: net force) produces acceleration (effect). Speeding up, slowing down, and changing direction all count as acceleration.

Lesson Objectives

I can… - Define acceleration as the change in velocity divided by the time interval. - Identify positive, negative, and zero acceleration from a velocity-time graph or a written description. - Explain why “deceleration” is just acceleration with a sign opposite the motion.

Discussion Prompts

  • “If a car’s velocity changes from 5 m/s east to 15 m/s east in 2 seconds, what’s its acceleration?”
  • Sample student response: “(15 − 5) / 2 = 5 m/s². Positive because it’s speeding up east.”
  • “What does it mean if acceleration is negative? Is the object always slowing down?”
  • Probe: “What if the object is moving west and acceleration is negative? Is it speeding up or slowing down?”

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: “Negative acceleration always means slowing down.” → Correction: Negative acceleration means the acceleration vector points in the negative direction. Whether that speeds up or slows down an object depends on the object’s velocity sign. Negative acceleration on a westbound object speeds it up.
  • Misconception: “Constant velocity means there is acceleration.” → Correction: Constant velocity = zero acceleration. Acceleration is about change in velocity.
  • Misconception: “Acceleration is how fast something is going.” → Correction: Acceleration is how fast the velocity is changing — meters per second per second.

Access & Differentiation

  • ELL/ENL supports: Sentence frame: “The acceleration is ___ m/s² because the velocity changed by ___ m/s in ___ seconds.” Word-choice box: {acceleration, deceleration, slope, change, rate}.
  • IEP/SPED supports: Pre-printed v-t graph templates with axis labels and tick marks; students plot data points only. Pair them with a strong group at the BTC stations.
  • Extensions: Invent a v-t graph where the average velocity is positive but the acceleration is negative. Or: a v-t graph that depicts an object speeding up, then slowing down, then reversing direction.

Strategy Spotlight

Building Thinking Classrooms — random groups + vertical non-permanent surfaces (15 min).

  1. Random groups of 3 (visibly random). Use a deck of cards: shuffle, deal one card per student face-down; same-suit groups (clubs together, diamonds together, etc.). The randomness signals that anyone can think with anyone.
  2. VNPS-lite stations. Set up 4 chart-paper stations around the room at standing height. Each station has a different velocity-time graph and a thin-slicing task card.
  3. Thin-slicing tasks (each station, increasing difficulty):
    • Level 1: “Sketch the v-t graph for a car at constant 10 m/s east.”
    • Level 2: “Sketch the v-t graph for a car starting at 0 and reaching 20 m/s east in 5 seconds at constant acceleration.”
    • Level 3: “Given a v-t graph that goes from 10 to −10 m/s in 4 seconds, what is the average acceleration? Is the car speeding up or slowing down?”
    • Level 4: “Sketch a v-t graph showing the car speed up, then slow down, then reverse. Label where acceleration changes sign.”
  4. 3-minute rotations. Group works at the chart paper; teacher walks but does not solve. Use the discussion prompts to nudge.
  5. Consensus. After 12 min, gather class around one station’s chart paper and ask: “What’s the same in everyone’s answers? What’s different?” Surface that the slope of v-t = acceleration.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

A sprinter starts from rest and reaches 9 m/s in 3 seconds.

  1. What is her average acceleration? (b) Sketch her v-t graph. (c) After crossing the line, she slows from 9 m/s to 0 in 6 seconds. What is her acceleration during the slow-down phase? Is it positive or negative? Explain why.

Closing reflection (30 sec): What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • acceleration — change in velocity ÷ time; a vector; units m/s²
  • deceleration — informal name for acceleration whose vector opposes the velocity vector (slowing down)
  • slope — on a v-t graph, the steepness of the line; equals the acceleration
