## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. **Car A travels 100 m east in 5 s. Car B travels 60 m east, then 40 m west, in 5 s. Find each car's average speed and average velocity.**
   *Expected:*
   - Car A: distance = 100 m, displacement = 100 m east. Average speed = 100/5 = **20 m/s**. Average velocity = 100/5 = **20 m/s east**.
   - Car B: distance = 60 + 40 = 100 m, displacement = 60 − 40 = 20 m east. Average speed = 100/5 = **20 m/s**. Average velocity = 20/5 = **4 m/s east**.
   *Rubric:* Full credit if all four numerical answers correct with units; half credit if speeds correct but velocities not. Tolerance: ±0.
   *NYSSLS:* HS-PS2-1.

2. **Use sentence expansion to describe Car B's motion. Start with the kernel "The car moves." Add three details.**
   *Expected (one valid expansion):* Kernel: "The car moves." → Expansion 1 (add speed): "The car moves at 20 m/s." → Expansion 2 (add direction → velocity): "The car moves east at 4 m/s on average." → Expansion 3 (add time and trip detail): "Car B moves east at an average velocity of 4 m/s over 5 seconds, even though its average speed was 20 m/s because the path included a reversal."
   *Rubric:* Full credit if final sentence (a) names speed AND velocity, (b) includes the time, (c) acknowledges the reversal. Partial credit for any two of the three.
   *NYSSLS:* HS-PS2-1 (precise quantitative description of motion).

3. **Can two trips have equal average speeds but different average velocities? Sketch one example.**
   *Expected:* Yes — any two trips with equal distance and time but different *displacement*. Common example: same 100 m walk in 20 s, but one is straight forward (velocity = 5 m/s forward) and one is a 50 m out-and-50 m-back round trip (velocity = 0).
   *Rubric:* Full credit for "yes" plus a labeled sketch with both numbers shown.
   *NYSSLS:* HS-PS2-1.

## Exit Ticket Answer

A cyclist rides 20 km along a trail and returns to her starting point. The whole trip takes 1 hour.

(a) Average speed = total distance ÷ total time = 40 km ÷ 1 h = **40 km/h**.
(b) Average velocity = displacement ÷ time = 0 km ÷ 1 h = **0 km/h** (she ends at her starting point).
(c) Acceptable expansion: "The cyclist rode" → "The cyclist rode along a 20 km trail" → "The cyclist rode 20 km out and 20 km back along the trail" → "The cyclist rode 20 km out and 20 km back in 1 hour, averaging 40 km/h in speed but 0 km/h in velocity because her displacement was zero."

*Rubric:* (a) and (b) each 1 pt. (c) 2 pts: 1 for ending at a sentence that distinguishes speed from velocity numerically, 1 for naming displacement = 0 (or equivalent reasoning). Tolerance: ±0 on numbers.

*NYSSLS:* HS-PS2-1.
