Unit: Kinematics — Lesson 03: Average Speed and Velocity

HOCHMAN

Phenomenon

Two students are going to walk side-by-side from the back of the room to the door at the same pace. Halfway, one stops. The other continues. After 10 seconds, both started in the same place, and (if it works out) ended in the same place. Did they have the same speed? The same velocity?

Driving Question

How can two trips have the same average speed but different average velocities — or vice versa?

Notice & Wonder

Turn to your partner. Did the two students "move the same"? Defend your answer with a number — even a guess.

Initial Model

Write a kernel sentence: "The car moves." We will expand it together during the investigation.

Investigate

Two cars travel between the same start and end markers, but with different paths. Use the sliders to set their speeds. Press Run and compare their average speed and average velocity.

Storyboard frame 1. Car A drives 100 m east at a steady 10 m/s, taking 10 seconds. Average speed = 10 m/s. Average velocity = 10 m/s east.

Storyboard frame 2. Car B drives 100 m east, then 100 m west, then 100 m east — total path 300 m. If its constant speed is 20 m/s, the trip takes 15 s. Average speed = 300/15 = 20 m/s, but average velocity = (100 m east) / 15 s ≈ 6.7 m/s east.

Storyboard frame 3. Two trips can have equal speeds but different velocities, equal velocities but different speeds, or differ in both. The single number "fast" is not enough — direction and round-trip behavior change the picture.

If both cars start at 0 and end at 100 m, but Car B took longer because it doubled back, why does Car A have a higher average velocity even when their average speeds are similar?

Make It Make Sense

  1. Car A travels 100 m east in 5 s. Car B travels 60 m east, then 40 m west, in 5 s. Find each car's average speed and average velocity.
  2. Use sentence expansion to describe Car B's motion. Start with the kernel "The car moves." Add three details.
  3. Can two trips have equal average speeds but different average velocities? Sketch one example.
Hochman expansion organizer
  1. Kernel: The car moves.
  2. + speed: The car moves at ___ m/s.
  3. + direction (→ velocity): The car moves ___ at ___ m/s.
  4. + time and trip detail: The ___ car moves ___ at an average velocity of ___ m/s for ___ seconds.

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • speed — distance ÷ time; a scalar
  • velocity — displacement ÷ time; a vector
  • average rate — total change ÷ total time elapsed

Revise Your Model

Take the kernel sentence "The car moves" and complete a Hochman expansion that ends with a sentence precise enough to describe Car B's motion.

Return to the Phenomenon

Re-classify the two students from the start of class. Did they have the same speed? Same velocity? Use today's vocabulary in your answer.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. A cyclist rides 20 km along a trail and returns to her starting point. The whole trip takes 1 hour. (a) Average speed? (b) Average velocity? (c) Use a Hochman expansion starting with "The cyclist rode" — add three details.
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
