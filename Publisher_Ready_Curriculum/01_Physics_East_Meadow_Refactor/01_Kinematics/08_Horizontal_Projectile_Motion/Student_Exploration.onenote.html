Unit: Kinematics — Lesson 08: Horizontal Projectile Motion

BTC HOCHMAN

Phenomenon

Watch MythBusters — Bullet Fired vs. Bullet Dropped. Two bullets are released at exactly the same instant from the same height: one is fired horizontally, the other simply dropped. Predict — which one hits the ground first?

Driving Question

Why does the horizontal velocity of a projectile not change how long it stays in the air?

Notice & Wonder

Most students predict the dropped bullet lands first (because the fired bullet is "moving away from the ground"). Why is that intuition appealing — and why is it wrong?

Initial Model

On a y-vs-x grid, sketch the trajectory of (1) a marble dropped from a table edge, and (2) a marble rolled off the same table at 2 m/s. Mark the moment each one hits the floor.

Investigate

Roll a marble off a table at the speed you choose. Watch it alongside a second marble that is simply dropped. Compare their fall times and trajectories.

Storyboard frame 1. A blue marble rolls off the table at 2 m/s; a purple marble is simply dropped at the same instant. They fall the same height h.

Storyboard frame 2. Both marbles strike the floor at the same instant. The blue marble has traveled some horizontal distance (the *range*); the purple marble landed directly below the table edge.

Storyboard frame 3. Doubling the launch height does NOT double the fall time. Time of fall scales as √h, so doubling h multiplies time by √2 ≈ 1.41. Range scales the same way (range = v_x · t), so range also grows by √2.

When the rolled marble lands, what is its horizontal velocity? What is its vertical velocity? Did either change during the flight?

Make It Make Sense

  1. Set h = 1.25 m. Release both marbles with v_x = 3 m/s on the rolled one. Which lands first? What is the fall time?
  2. What is the horizontal velocity of the rolled marble at the moment of impact?
  3. If you double the table height, what happens to the fall time? Does the range double too?
Hochman paragraph organizer
  1. Topic sentence: "When a projectile is launched horizontally, its fall time depends only on the launch height — not on the horizontal speed."
  2. Sentence 2 (evidence): Use a number from the interactive that supports the topic sentence.
  3. Sentence 3 (mechanism): Why does this work? (Hint: gravity acts only ___; the horizontal velocity has no force changing it.)

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • horizontal velocity (v_x) — constant horizontal speed of a projectile (no horizontal force)
  • vertical velocity (v_y) — downward speed of the projectile, growing under gravity
  • range — the horizontal distance from launch point to landing point

Revise Your Model

Update your y-vs-x sketch from the Initial Model. Mark the *fall time* on each trajectory. Are they the same number?

Return to the Phenomenon

Re-watch the MythBusters bullet experiment. Use today's vocabulary to write a one-sentence explanation of why both bullets hit the ground at the same time.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. A marble rolls off a 0.80 m table at 1.5 m/s. g = 9.8 m/s². (a) Fall time? (b) Distance from the edge it lands? (c) Write one Hochman-style topic sentence that captures why a faster horizontal speed produces longer range but the same fall time.
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
