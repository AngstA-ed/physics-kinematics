CCC Focus

Cause and Effect — the cause of the simultaneous landing is that gravity acts only on the y-component of motion. The effect is that horizontal velocity does not affect fall time.

Lesson Objectives

I can… - Explain why the horizontal velocity of a projectile does not change the fall time (in the absence of air resistance). - Calculate the time-to-fall and the horizontal range for a projectile launched horizontally from a height. - Write a 3-sentence paragraph (with a topic sentence) explaining why fall time depends only on the launch height.

Discussion Prompts

  • “If you fire a bullet horizontally and drop another bullet at the same instant from the same height, which hits the ground first?”
  • Sample student response: “They hit at the same time, because gravity is the only thing pulling them down — and the bullets started with the same vertical velocity (zero).”
  • “What is the horizontal velocity at the moment of landing? Did it change during the fall?”
  • Probe: “If horizontal velocity didn’t change, what does that say about the horizontal acceleration?”

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: “A faster horizontal launch keeps the projectile in the air longer.” → Correction: Time-of-fall is determined by the vertical component alone. Horizontal velocity affects range (how far it goes), not time.
  • Misconception: “There is a horizontal force on the projectile.” → Correction: Once released, the only force on a projectile (ignoring air resistance) is gravity, which is purely vertical.
  • Misconception: “Doubling the launch height doubles the fall time.” → Correction: Time goes as √h, so doubling h multiplies time by √2 ≈ 1.41, not 2.

Access & Differentiation

  • ELL/ENL supports: Sentence frames embedded in the Hochman scaffold itself: *“Fall time depends only on , because gravity acts only , so horizontal velocity ___.”* Word-choice box: {horizontal, vertical, gravity, range, fall time, independent, components}.
  • IEP/SPED supports: Pre-printed trajectory templates with the y-axis (height) marked at 1 m, 2 m, 5 m. Provide the formula t = √(2h/g) on a reference card.
  • Extensions: Predict the speed (magnitude of v_x and v_y combined) at the moment of impact. Or: a marble rolls off a table 0.8 m high at 1 m/s — where does it land relative to the table edge?

Strategy Spotlight

Part 1 — BTC thin-slicing (10 min).

Random groups of 3 (visibly random, by playing-card suit). At each chart-paper station, post the four progressively harder puzzles:

  1. Level 1: “A ball is dropped from a 5 m table. How long until it hits the floor? Use g = 9.8 m/s².” (Answer: t = √(2·5/9.8) ≈ 1.01 s.)
  2. Level 2: “Same ball, but rolled off the table at 2 m/s horizontally. Now how long until it hits the floor?” (Same answer: 1.01 s.)
  3. Level 3: “Now how far horizontally does the rolled ball travel before hitting the floor?” (Range = v_x · t = 2 × 1.01 ≈ 2.02 m.)
  4. Level 4: “If we double the table height to 10 m, what happens to the fall time and to the range?” (t becomes √2 ≈ 1.43 s; range becomes 2 × 1.43 ≈ 2.86 m. The fall time grew by √2, not 2.)

3-minute rotations. Students argue at chart paper. Teacher walks but does not solve.

Part 2 — Hochman paragraph topic sentence (10 min).

Students write a 3-sentence paragraph explaining why horizontal velocity does not affect fall time. Provide the topic-sentence frame as a starter:

Topic sentence: “When a projectile is launched horizontally, its fall time depends only on the launch height — not on the horizontal speed.”

Students then write two supporting sentences that reference evidence from the BTC stations and from the MythBusters phenomenon:

  • Sentence 2 (evidence): “At Levels 1 and 2 of our station puzzles, the ball took the same time (~1 s) whether it was dropped or rolled off the table at 2 m/s.”
  • Sentence 3 (causal mechanism): “This makes sense because gravity acts only vertically; the horizontal velocity has no force pulling it sideways, so the projectile keeps its launch speed in x while gravity pulls it down in y.”

Read three student paragraphs aloud (with permission). Discuss which topic sentence most clearly previews what the supporting sentences will show.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

A marble rolls off a 0.80 m high table at 1.5 m/s horizontally. Use g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. How long does the marble take to hit the floor?
  2. How far from the table’s edge does it land?
  3. Write one topic sentence (Hochman style) that captures why a marble rolling faster horizontally would land further but at the same time.

Closing reflection (30 sec): What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • horizontal velocity (v_x) — the constant rightward (or leftward) speed of a projectile (no horizontal force, so v_x is unchanged during flight)
  • vertical velocity (v_y) — the downward speed of the projectile, growing under gravity
  • range — the horizontal distance from launch point to landing point
