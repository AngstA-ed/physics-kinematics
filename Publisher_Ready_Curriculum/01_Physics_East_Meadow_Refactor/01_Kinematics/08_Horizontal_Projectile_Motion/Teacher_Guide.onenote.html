Lesson Overview

Duration 42 minutes (1 period; consider double-period day so the BTC plus Hochman can both land)
NYSSLS link HS-PS2-1 (x-y independence)
CCC focus Cause and Effect — the cause of simultaneous landing is that gravity acts only on the y-component of motion. The effect is that horizontal velocity does not affect fall time.
Strategy chips BTC (thin-slicing) + HOCHMAN (paragraph topic-sentence)
Materials Whiteboards / chart paper at standing height (4 stations); markers; deck of cards (random groups); printed thin-slicing task cards (4 levels per station); printed Hochman paragraph organizer (one per student); access to the interactive on devices.
Safety None.
Prior knowledge Lessons 01 (vectors → components), 06–07 (freefall, vertical projectiles).

Lesson objectives — students can:

  • Explain why the horizontal velocity of a projectile does not change the fall time (in the absence of air resistance).
  • Calculate the time-to-fall and the horizontal range for a projectile launched horizontally from a height.
  • Write a 3-sentence paragraph (with a topic sentence) explaining why fall time depends only on the launch height.

Phase 1 · Engage (0 – 12 min)

0–3 min · Opening Connection (SEL)

Quick check-in. 60–90 seconds.

3–8 min · Phenomenon hook — MythBusters bullet experiment

Teacher actions. Play the MythBusters Bullet Fired vs. Bullet Dropped clip — about 2 minutes. The experiment: two bullets released at exactly the same instant from the same height, one fired horizontally, one simply dropped. They land simultaneously.

Sample teacher language:

“Watch this. Predict before they show the result: which bullet hits the ground first — the one fired horizontally, or the one simply dropped?”

Pause for predictions. Take a quick show-of-hands count: “fired” vs. “dropped” vs. “same time.” Most classes split heavily toward “fired” (because the bullet is moving fast — surely it stays aloft longer?). Reveal the result.

Anticipated student responses:

  • “That’s not possible” — surface; the result IS counterintuitive.
  • “The fired bullet must somehow gain altitude” — productive misconception.
  • “Gravity is the only force in the y-direction” — perfect insight; surface for the class.

8–12 min · Notice & Wonder + Turn-and-Talk #1

Capture two columns. Then:

“Most students predict the dropped bullet lands first. Why is that intuition appealing — and why is it wrong?”

After 90 seconds, take 3–4 responses. The desirable insight to surface: gravity only acts vertically. There’s no horizontal force (ignoring air resistance), so horizontal velocity doesn’t slow the fall.

Bridge to Phase 2

By end of Phase 1, every student has sketched their initial model: on a y-vs-x grid, the trajectories of (1) a marble dropped from a table edge, and (2) a marble rolled off at 2 m/s. They mark the moment each one hits the floor.

Phase 2 · Explore (12 – 32 min)

This phase uses BOTH strategies — first BTC thin-slicing (10 min), then Hochman paragraph topic-sentence (10 min).

12–22 min · Investigation — BTC thin-slicing puzzles

Random groups of 3 (visibly random, by playing-card suit). Direct groups to the four chart-paper stations. Each station has the same four progressively harder puzzles printed on a card:

  • Level 1: “A ball is dropped from a 5 m table. How long until it hits the floor? Use g = 9.8 m/s².” (Answer: t = √(2·5/9.8) ≈ 1.01 s.)
  • Level 2: “Same ball, but rolled off the table at 2 m/s horizontally. Now how long until it hits the floor?” (Same answer: 1.01 s. The “aha.”)
  • Level 3: “Now how far horizontally does the rolled ball travel before hitting the floor?” (Range = v_x · t = 2 × 1.01 ≈ 2.02 m.)
  • Level 4: “If we double the table height to 10 m, what happens to the fall time and to the range?” (t becomes √2 ≈ 1.43 s; range becomes 2 × 1.43 ≈ 2.86 m. The fall time grew by √2, not 2.)

Three-minute rotations. Group works at chart paper; teacher walks but does not solve.

Sample teacher facilitation language:

“What’s the same in Levels 1 and 2? What changed?” “If horizontal velocity didn’t change, what does that say about the horizontal acceleration?” “Why does the fall time grow by √2 when you double the height, not by 2?”

22–32 min · Hochman paragraph topic-sentence

After the BTC stations, gather the class and distribute the printed Hochman paragraph organizer. Students write a 3-sentence paragraph explaining why horizontal velocity does not affect fall time.

Provide the topic-sentence frame as a starter:

“When a projectile is launched horizontally, its fall time depends only on the launch height — not on the horizontal speed.”

Students then write two supporting sentences that reference evidence from the BTC stations and the MythBusters phenomenon:

  • Sentence 2 (evidence): “At Levels 1 and 2 of our station puzzles, the ball took the same time (~1 s) whether it was dropped or rolled off the table at 2 m/s.”
  • Sentence 3 (causal mechanism): “This makes sense because gravity acts only vertically; the horizontal velocity has no force pulling it sideways, so the projectile keeps its launch speed in x while gravity pulls it down in y.”

Read three student paragraphs aloud (with permission). Discuss which topic sentence most clearly previews what the supporting sentences will show.

Teacher facilitation language for the Hochman work:

“Read your topic sentence aloud. Without reading the rest, can a listener predict what evidence will follow?” “Sentence 2 should give a number. Which number from the simulator best supports your topic?” “Sentence 3 says why. Which of these words is doing the heavy lifting: ‘because,’ ‘gravity,’ ‘only,’ ‘no force’?”

Teacher facilitation during Explore

  • What to look for — students discovering that fall time is independent of horizontal speed. Then articulating it precisely in their topic sentence.
  • What to resist — don’t write the formal x-y independence rule on the board until Phase 3. The strategy depends on students saying it themselves.
  • What to redirect — students whose topic sentence is “Bullets fall the same as dropped objects” lack the cause (height-only). Redirect: “What does fall time depend on?” Lead them back to the height variable.

Phase 3 · Explain (32 – 37 min)

32–35 min · Turn-and-Talk #2 + class consensus

Bring the class back. Pick one student paragraph that landed cleanly. Read it. Ask:

“Talk to your partner: what does this paragraph tell us about which variable controls fall time? Which doesn’t?”

After 90 seconds, take volunteers. The target consensus statement:

“The horizontal velocity of a projectile does not affect its fall time. Fall time depends only on the launch height (and gravity). The horizontal velocity affects the range — how far the projectile travels — but not how long it stays in the air. The x and y components of motion are independent because gravity is purely vertical.”

35–37 min · Vocabulary introduction (≤ 3 terms)

Sample teacher language:

“Horizontal velocity (v_x) — the constant rightward (or leftward) speed of a projectile. No horizontal force, so v_x doesn’t change during flight. Vertical velocity (v_y) — the downward speed. Grows under gravity. Range — the horizontal distance from launch point to landing point. Range = v_x × t_of.”

Discussion prompts to deploy here

  • “If you fire a bullet horizontally and drop another bullet at the same instant from the same height, which hits the ground first?”
    • Sample student response: “They hit at the same time, because gravity is the only thing pulling them down — and the bullets started with the same vertical velocity (zero).”
  • “What is the horizontal velocity at the moment of landing?”
    • Probe: “Did it change during the fall? What does that say about the horizontal acceleration?”

Phase 4 · Elaborate (37 – 40 min)

37–39 min · Revise the model

Students reopen their y-vs-x sketches from Phase 2. Specific prompt:

“Update your sketch. Mark the fall time on each trajectory. Are they the same number? Add a one-line caption explaining why.”

39–40 min · Return to the phenomenon

Re-watch a 30-second clip of the MythBusters experiment. Ask:

“Use today’s vocabulary in a one-sentence explanation of why both bullets hit the ground at the same time.”

Target: “Both bullets had the same vertical velocity at launch (zero), and the only force acting was gravity, which is purely vertical — so the horizontal velocity of the fired bullet didn’t change its fall time.”

Phase 5 · Evaluate (40 – 42 min)

40–42 min · Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

Distribute exit slips. Transfer prompt:

“A marble rolls off a 0.80 m high table at 1.5 m/s horizontally. Use g = 9.8 m/s². (a) How long does the marble take to hit the floor? (b) How far from the table’s edge does it land? (c) Write one topic sentence (Hochman style) that captures why a marble rolling faster horizontally would land further but at the same time.”

Expected: (a) 0.404 s. (b) 0.61 m. (c) “The horizontal speed of a projectile changes how far it travels but not how long it falls, because gravity acts only vertically.”

Closing Reflection (SEL, 30 seconds, on the same exit slip):

“What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?”

Common Misconceptions

  • Misconception: “A faster horizontal launch keeps the projectile in the air longer.” → Correction: Time-of-fall is determined by the vertical component alone. Horizontal velocity affects range (how far it goes), not time.
  • Misconception: “There is a horizontal force on the projectile.” → Correction: Once released, the only force on a projectile (ignoring air resistance) is gravity, which is purely vertical.
  • Misconception: “Doubling the launch height doubles the fall time.” → Correction: Time goes as √h, so doubling h multiplies time by √2 ≈ 1.41, not 2.

Access & Differentiation

  • ELL/ENL supports: Sentence frames embedded in the Hochman scaffold itself: *“Fall time depends only on , because gravity acts only , so horizontal velocity ___.”* Word-choice box: {horizontal, vertical, gravity, range, fall time, independent, components}.
  • IEP/SPED supports: Pre-printed trajectory templates with the y-axis (height) marked at 1 m, 2 m, 5 m. Provide the formula t = √(2h/g) on a reference card.
  • Extensions: Predict the speed (magnitude of v_x and v_y combined) at the moment of impact. Or: a marble rolls off a table 0.8 m high at 1 m/s — where does it land relative to the table edge?

Strategy Spotlight

Part 1 — BTC thin-slicing (10 min) — see Phase 2, 12–22 min. Random groups of 3 by playing-card suit; four chart-paper stations with the same four-level puzzle card; three-minute rotations. Teacher walks but does not solve. The lesson’s “aha” lands at Level 2 — the moment a group realizes that adding horizontal velocity changed nothing about the fall time. Don’t preview that — let them discover it.

Part 2 — Hochman paragraph topic sentence (10 min) — see Phase 2, 22–32 min. Topic-sentence frame provided (“When a projectile is launched horizontally, its fall time depends only on the launch height — not on the horizontal speed”). Students write two supporting sentences: an evidence sentence (a number from the simulator) and a causal-mechanism sentence (gravity is purely vertical). Read three paragraphs aloud and discuss which topic sentence most clearly previews what its supports will show.

The two strategies stack: BTC produces the discovery; Hochman makes the discovery defensible in writing.
