Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

  1. Set the table height to 1.25 m. Drop a marble (v₀_x = 0) and roll a second marble at 3 m/s simultaneously. Which lands first? Expected: They land at the same time. Time of fall: t = √(2h/g) = √(2·1.25/9.8) ≈ 0.51 s. Both have v_y(0) = 0; only the rolled marble has v_x. Gravity pulls both down equally. Rubric: Full credit for “same time” with the calculated value. Half credit for “same time” without calculation. No credit for “the rolled one lands later” — the dominant misconception. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1.

  2. What is the horizontal velocity of the rolled marble at the moment of impact? Expected: 3 m/s — unchanged from the launch. There is no horizontal force during the fall (ignoring air resistance), so v_x stays constant. Rubric: Full credit for “3 m/s” with the no-horizontal-force reasoning. Half credit for “3 m/s” without reasoning. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1.

  3. If you double the table height, what happens to the fall time? Does the range double too? Expected: Fall time goes as √h, so doubling h multiplies the time by √2 ≈ 1.41, not 2. Range = v_x · t, so range also grows by √2 (assuming the same horizontal launch speed). Range does not double. Rubric: Full credit for both factors of √2 (time AND range). Half credit for time but not range. No credit for “doubles” answers. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1.

Exit Ticket Answer

Marble rolls off a 0.80 m table at v_x = 1.5 m/s. g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. Fall time: t = √(2h/g) = √(1.6/9.8) = √0.1633 ≈ 0.404 s.
  2. Range = v_x · t = 1.5 × 0.404 ≈ 0.61 m from the edge of the table.
  3. Acceptable topic sentence (one of many): “The horizontal speed of a projectile changes how far it travels but not how long it falls, because gravity acts only vertically.” (Or any sentence that names fall time depends only on launch height and previews the causal claim that gravity is purely vertical.)

Rubric: (a) 1 pt; (b) 1 pt; (c) 2 pts: 1 for a topic sentence that distinguishes fall time from range, 1 for previewing the mechanism (gravity is vertical / no horizontal force / x and y components are independent). Tolerance: ±0.05 s, ±0.05 m.

NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1.

Closing Reflection (rubric)

Closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any honest response. Look for:

  • Surprise / interest signal in answer 1 (e.g., “I didn’t believe both bullets land at the same time until I saw the simulation”)
  • Naming a peer or self-acknowledgment in answer 2
