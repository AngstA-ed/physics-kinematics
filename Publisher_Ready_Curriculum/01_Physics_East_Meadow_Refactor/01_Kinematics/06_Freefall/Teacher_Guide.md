## CCC Focus

**Cause and Effect** — *the cause* of freefall acceleration is gravity. The effect is the same constant acceleration (a = −g) on every object, regardless of mass — *as long as air resistance is negligible*.

## Lesson Objectives

- Explain why all objects in **freefall** experience the same acceleration (g ≈ 9.8 m/s² downward) regardless of mass — *when air resistance is negligible*.

- Distinguish *freefall* (gravity only) from real-world falling (gravity plus air resistance).

## Discussion Prompts

- "If you drop a textbook and a flat sheet of paper at the same time, which hits first? What if you crumple the paper into a ball?"
- *Sample student response:* "The textbook hits first when the paper is flat. When the paper is crumpled, they hit nearly together — air resistance was the difference."
- "If we did this experiment on the moon, what would happen?"
- "Why is freefall an *acceleration* problem, not just a *velocity* problem?"

## Common Misconceptions

- **Misconception:** "Heavier objects fall faster than lighter ones." → **Correction:** This is the dominant freefall misconception, present even after demonstration. Use the Hochman because/but/so to confront it explicitly.
- **Misconception:** "If air resistance is negligible, the velocity is constant during the fall." → **Correction:** Velocity is constantly *increasing* by 9.8 m/s every second; *acceleration* is constant (g), not velocity.
- **Misconception:** "Terminal velocity means the object stops." → **Correction:** At terminal velocity the object reaches a *maximum* steady speed because air resistance equals gravity — it keeps moving, just no longer accelerating.

## Access & Differentiation

- **ELL/ENL supports:** The Hochman *because/but/so* scaffold itself acts as the sentence-frame for this lesson. Word-choice box: {gravity, mass, weight, freefall, air resistance, terminal velocity}. Pair the words *mass* and *weight* with concrete examples to head off the conflation.
- **IEP/SPED supports:** Pre-fill the *because* clause; students complete the *but* and *so*. Provide a printed v-t graph template with the y-axis pre-labeled g·t increments (9.8, 19.6, 29.4, …).
- **Extensions:** Compute how long it would take a feather to reach terminal velocity in air. Or: predict what fraction of the skydiver's freefall is *true* freefall (no air resistance) vs. air-affected motion, and explain reasoning.

## Strategy Spotlight

**Hochman *because/but/so* (15 min).**

The kernel claim is the misconception itself: "Heavier objects fall faster than lighter ones."

Distribute a *because/but/so* organizer with three sentences to complete:

1. **"Heavier objects fall faster than lighter ones, *because* …"**
   Students fill in the surface reason — typically "they have more weight" or "gravity pulls them harder."

2. **"… *but* …"**
   Students supply the contrasting evidence — "in a vacuum, all objects fall at the same rate" or "the textbook and crumpled paper hit at the same time."

3. **"… *so* …"**
   Students reach the corrected conclusion — "the apparent difference is from air resistance, not gravity itself; in true freefall, mass doesn't matter."

The full corrected sentence, read aloud at the end, is:

> "Heavier objects fall faster than lighter ones **because** they have more weight pulling them down, **but** in a vacuum (or with negligible air resistance) the textbook and the crumpled paper land at the same time, **so** the apparent difference is caused by air resistance, not by gravity itself — every object freely falls with the same acceleration g ≈ 9.8 m/s²."

Have three students read different versions aloud. Discuss which version best convinces a peer who has not yet been to physics class.

## Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

A pebble is dropped from rest from a 45 m cliff. Ignore air resistance. Use g = 9.8 m/s².

(a) What is its velocity 1 second after release? 2 seconds after?
(b) How long until it hits the ground?
(c) Use because/but/so to explain why a feather dropped from the same cliff in air takes longer than the pebble.

*Closing reflection (30 sec):* What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?

## Key Vocabulary (max 3)

- **freefall** — motion under the influence of gravity alone (air resistance negligible)
- **gravity (g)** — the acceleration due to Earth's gravity, ≈ 9.8 m/s² downward near the surface
- **terminal velocity** — the maximum steady speed an object reaches when air resistance equals gravity (reached only when air resistance is significant)
