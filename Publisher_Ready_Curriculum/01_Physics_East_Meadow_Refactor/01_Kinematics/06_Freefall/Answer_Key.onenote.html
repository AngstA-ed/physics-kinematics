Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

  1. Press Drop. The ball falls from rest. After 2.0 seconds, what is its velocity? How far has it fallen? Expected: v = g·t = 9.8 × 2 = 19.6 m/s downward. Distance = ½·g·t² = ½ × 9.8 × 4 = 19.6 m. Rubric: Full credit if both correct with units and direction. Half credit for one. Tolerance: ±0.5. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1.

  2. Use because/but/so to refute “heavier objects always fall faster.” Expected (one valid completion):

    • Because: “they have more weight pulling them down.”
    • But: “in a vacuum (or when air resistance is negligible) all objects fall with the same acceleration; the textbook and crumpled paper land together.”
    • So: “the apparent difference is caused by air resistance, not by gravity itself — true freefall depends on g alone, not mass.” Rubric: Full credit if all three clauses are present and the but clause names a controlling factor (vacuum, air resistance, or a same-mass-different-shape comparison). Half credit if any clause is missing. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1 (consistent constant-acceleration claim across all masses).

  3. A small rock and a large rock are dropped together from a low height (1 m). Which hits first? Expected: They hit at the same time (both freefall, air resistance negligible at this drop height). Time to fall 1 m: t = √(2h/g) = √(2/9.8) ≈ 0.45 s for both. Rubric: Full credit for “same time” plus calculated time. Half credit for “same time” without calculation. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1.

Exit Ticket Answer

A pebble dropped from rest from a 45 m cliff. g = 9.8 m/s².

  1. After 1 s: v = g·t = 9.8 m/s downward. After 2 s: v = 19.6 m/s downward.
  2. Time to fall 45 m: 45 = ½·g·t² → t² = 90/9.8 ≈ 9.18 → t ≈ 3.03 s.
  3. Acceptable because/but/so:
  • because the pebble is heavier than the feather, students may think it should fall faster;
  • but in a vacuum both fall at the same g, and even in air, the acceleration due to gravity is the same on both;
  • so the difference is caused by air resistance, which has a much larger effect on the feather (low mass, high surface area) than on the pebble — the feather quickly reaches a low terminal velocity, while the pebble is still accelerating when it hits.

Rubric: (a) 1 pt for both speeds. (b) 1 pt for ~3 s. (c) 2 pts: 1 for naming air resistance, 1 for distinguishing terminal velocity from acceleration. Tolerance: ±0.1 on speeds, ±0.1 s on time.

NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1.

Closing Reflection (rubric)

Closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any honest response. Look for:

  • Surprise / interest signal in answer 1 (e.g., “I always thought heavy things fell faster”)
  • Naming a peer or self-acknowledgment in answer 2
