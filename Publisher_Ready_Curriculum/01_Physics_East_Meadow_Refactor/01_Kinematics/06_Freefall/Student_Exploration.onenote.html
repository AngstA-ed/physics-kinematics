Unit: Kinematics — Lesson 06: Freefall

HOCHMAN

Phenomenon

Watch "Jumping from Space!" A skydiver leaps from the edge of space and falls for several minutes before opening a parachute. He starts in near-vacuum and ends in air. How does the same body fall differently in those two regimes?

Driving Question

If gravity pulls everything the same way, why does a feather fall slower than a rock?

Notice & Wonder

Predict: if your teacher drops a heavy textbook and a flat sheet of paper at the same time, which hits first? What if she crumples the paper into a tight ball? Defend your prediction.

Initial Model

Sketch v-t graphs for a falling rock and a falling feather (both released from rest, in air). Are they the same shape?

Investigate

Press Drop. The ball falls under gravity (g = 9.8 m/s²). Velocity and distance update every 0.5 seconds. The "no air resistance" toggle is on; you can turn it off to see what air resistance does to a low-mass, high-surface-area object.

Storyboard frame 1. t = 0 s — ball released from rest. Velocity = 0, distance fallen = 0.

Storyboard frame 2. t = 1 s — velocity = 9.8 m/s downward; distance fallen = ½ × 9.8 × 1² = 4.9 m.

Storyboard frame 3. t = 2 s — velocity = 19.6 m/s downward; distance fallen = ½ × 9.8 × 4 = 19.6 m. The velocity grew by g for every additional second; the distance grew by *more* than the previous second's distance because the average speed during each second is higher.

Toggle "air resistance" on. The ball quickly stops accelerating and reaches a steady (low) speed. Why? What does that mean for our skydiver?

Make It Make Sense

  1. Press Drop. After 2.0 seconds, what is the ball's velocity? How far has it fallen?
  2. Use because/but/so to refute the claim "heavier objects always fall faster."
  3. A small rock and a large rock are dropped together from a low height (1 m). Which hits first?
Hochman because/but/so scaffold
  1. Heavier objects fall faster than lighter ones, because ___.
  2. … but ___ (counter-evidence).
  3. … so ___ (corrected conclusion).

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • freefall — motion under the influence of gravity alone (air resistance negligible)
  • gravity (g) — Earth-surface acceleration ≈ 9.8 m/s² downward
  • terminal velocity — the maximum steady speed reached when air resistance equals gravity

Revise Your Model

Update your initial v-t sketches. Use a different color for the rock and the feather, and label where (if at all) the curves diverge.

Return to the Phenomenon

In the Jumping from Space video, what was the skydiver's motion in near-vacuum vs. lower in the atmosphere? Use the words freefall and terminal velocity in your answer.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. A pebble dropped from rest from a 45 m cliff (no air resistance, g = 9.8 m/s²). (a) Velocity after 1 s? After 2 s? (b) How long until it hits the ground? (c) Use because/but/so to explain why a feather dropped from the same cliff in air takes longer.
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
