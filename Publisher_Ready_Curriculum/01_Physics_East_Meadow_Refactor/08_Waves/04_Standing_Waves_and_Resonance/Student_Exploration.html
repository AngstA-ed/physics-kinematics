Unit: Waves & Sound — Lesson 04: Standing Waves and Resonance

ACTIVE LEARNING

Phenomenon

An opera singer holds one steady note and a wine glass shatters. On a shaken rope, most hand-speeds make a messy mess — but at one special speed the rope locks into a clean, big pattern that seems to stand still. Only certain frequencies "work."

Driving Question

What is special about the one frequency that makes a clean, standing pattern?

Notice & Wonder

Watch the rope lock into a clean pattern at one frequency. Write two things you notice and two things you wonder.

Share with your partner: on the rope at the special frequency, where is there no motion at all, and where is the motion biggest?

Initial Model

Sketch the rope at the special frequency. Mark with an X every point that does not move, and a star at every point that moves the most. Predict: what happens to the number of loops if you shake faster?

Investigate

Pick a harmonic number. The string is fixed at both ends, so only whole numbers of loops fit. Watch the still nodes (red) and the big-swing antinodes (orange) — and count how the harmonic number sets the number of loops.

Loops: 1 · Nodes (incl. ends): 2 · Antinodes: 1

Switch between harmonics. As n goes up, how do the number of nodes and antinodes change? Tell your partner the rule (nodes = n + 1, antinodes = n).

Make It Make Sense

  1. How does a standing wave form? Use the words reflection and interfere.
  2. The 3rd harmonic on a fixed-end string has how many nodes (including the ends) and how many antinodes?
  3. Why did the wine glass shatter at only one specific pitch? Use the word natural frequency.
Stuck? Sentence frame

"A node is a point that ___; an antinode is a point that ___."

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • standing wave — a wave pattern that appears to stand still, formed when a wave and its reflection interfere so that nodes and antinodes stay in fixed positions
  • node — a point on a standing wave that never moves (zero amplitude); an antinode is a point of maximum swing
  • resonance — the large response that occurs when a driving frequency matches an object's natural frequency

Revise Your Model

Return to your rope sketch. Re-label the X's as nodes and the stars as antinodes. Add a one-sentence definition of resonance using the words natural frequency.

Return to the Phenomenon

Back to the wine glass: in one sentence, explain why it shatters at only one specific pitch and not at others, using resonance and natural frequency.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. A standing wave on a string shows 2 full loops between the fixed ends.
    (a) How many nodes are there in total (including the ends)? How many antinodes? (b) Define resonance in one sentence. (c) A bridge can sway dangerously if wind or marching feet hit a certain frequency. Which idea from today explains this?
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
