## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. **Analog** = a continuous signal that can take any value (smooth wave). **Digital** = a signal stored as discrete sampled values encoded as bits (a stair-step of specific levels). · *Rubric:* continuous vs. discrete/sampled. · NYSSLS: HS-PS4-2
2. A digital photo is stored as **exact numbers (bits)** that are reproduced perfectly each copy; a photocopy is analog and adds a little **noise** every time, which accumulates and degrades the image. · *Rubric:* exact numbers reproduce vs. noise accumulates. · NYSSLS: HS-PS4-2
3. Sampling **discards the detail between the sample points** (and rounds each sample to the nearest level); if you don't sample fast enough or use enough bits, the digital version misses fine variation. · *Rubric:* names lost detail between samples / quantization. · NYSSLS: HS-PS4-2

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) **Analog** = continuous signal (any value); **digital** = discrete sampled values stored as bits.
(b) Any two: **resistant to noise** (can be cleaned back to exact levels); **perfect, identical copies**; **easy to store, compress, and error-check**; easy to transmit over long distances without degrading.
(c) Analog can capture **continuous nuance/detail** (the "warmth"), but the friend overlooks that digital is **far more robust to noise and copies perfectly** — a major advantage for transmitting and storing music.

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) both defined; (b) two valid advantages; (c) one fair point for analog AND one clear digital advantage (robustness/fidelity). 1 point each. NYSSLS: HS-PS4-2.
