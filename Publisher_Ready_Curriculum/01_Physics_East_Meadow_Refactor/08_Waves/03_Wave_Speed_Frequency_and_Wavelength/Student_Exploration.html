Unit: Waves & Sound — Lesson 03: Wave Speed, Frequency, and Wavelength

HOCHMAN

Phenomenon

Your teacher plucks a thick low string and a thin high string the same way — they sound like completely different notes. The wave speed on each string is fixed by the string itself, yet the pitches are not the same. The fast, high note and the slow, low note are both racing along at the same speed.

Driving Question

If two notes travel at the same speed, what must be different about their waves?

Notice & Wonder

Listen to the two notes and watch the strings. Write two things you notice and two things you wonder about frequency and wavelength.

Share with your partner: if two waves on a string travel at the same speed but have different frequencies, what has to be different about their wavelengths?

Initial Model

Predict: if you keep the wave speed the same but double the frequency, what happens to the wavelength? Write your prediction and a reason.

Investigate

The medium fixes the wave speed at v = 6 m/s. Drag the frequency slider — watch the wavelength change automatically so that v = f·λ stays constant. You cannot change f and λ independently at a fixed speed.

Fixed speed v = 6.0 m/s · Wavelength λ = v ÷ f = 2.0 m · Check: v = f·λ = 6.0 m/s

Drag the slider so f doubles. By what factor did the wavelength change? Tell your partner why v = f·λ forces that trade-off.

Make It Make Sense

  1. A wave travels at 340 m/s with a frequency of 170 Hz. Find its wavelength. Show v = f·λ rearranged.
  2. At a fixed wave speed, you triple the frequency. What happens to the wavelength, and by what factor?
  3. Write a Because/But/So sentence explaining why frequency and wavelength are inversely related at a constant wave speed.
Stuck? Sentence frame

"Because the speed is fixed, but the frequency went up, so the wavelength must ___."

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • wave speed — how fast a wave's energy travels through a medium (v); set by the medium, not by amplitude or frequency
  • period — the time for one complete cycle to pass a point (T), the reciprocal of frequency (T = 1/f)
  • hertz — the unit of frequency, equal to one cycle per second (Hz)

Revise Your Model

Return to your prediction. Was it right? Rewrite it as a Because / But / So sentence using v = f·λ and the words wave speed, frequency, and wavelength.

Return to the Phenomenon

Back to the two notes: both travel at the same speed on their strings, yet sound different. In one sentence, explain what is different about the high note's wave versus the low note's wave.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. A sound wave in air travels at 340 m/s.
    (a) A note has a frequency of 256 Hz. Find its wavelength. Show v = f·λ rearranged. (b) A higher note has a frequency of 512 Hz at the same speed. Without a calculator, is its wavelength longer or shorter, and by what factor? (c) What is the period of the 256 Hz note?
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
