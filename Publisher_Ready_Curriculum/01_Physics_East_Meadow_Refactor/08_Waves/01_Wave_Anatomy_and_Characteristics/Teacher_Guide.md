## Curated Resources (from East Meadow Scope & Sequence)

### NYSSLS Standards

**HS-PS4-1.** "Use mathematical representations to support a claim regarding relationships among the frequency, wavelength, and speed of waves traveling in various media. *(Cause and Effect)*"

This opening lesson builds the vocabulary and mental picture students need before they can reason mathematically with **v = f·λ** in Lessons 02–03. Establishing crest, trough, amplitude, wavelength, frequency, and period is the prerequisite for every wave claim that follows.

### Phenomenon

- A stadium "wave" travels around the bowl, but each fan stays in their seat — only their arms go up and down: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=njBJW9pStLA>
- A slinky pulse and a plucked guitar string as live, in-room versions of the same idea.

### Javalab / Labs

- PhET *Wave on a String* — drive a string at the left end and watch a wave travel: <https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/wave-on-a-string>

### Assessments

- PhET *Wave on a String* "Oscillate + Slow Motion" check: students set amplitude and frequency and measure wavelength. Exit ticket below serves as the formative check.

## Lesson Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | 42 minutes (1 period) |
| **NYSSLS link** | HS-PS4-1 (vocabulary + model foundation) |
| **CCC focus** | Cause and Effect — driving the source faster (higher frequency) *causes* the wave's shape to change, but the medium itself does not travel. |
| **Strategy chips** | RESTORATIVE CIRCLE (unit opener) |
| **Materials** | Long spring/slinky; a length of rope; a guitar or ukulele (optional); rulers; whiteboards/markers; device for PhET. |
| **Safety** | Give the slinky room; do not stretch past ~3 m or release under tension. |
| **Prior knowledge** | Energy transfer (Energy unit); the idea of a repeating, periodic motion. |

**Lesson objectives — students can:**

- Label the parts of a wave: crest, trough, amplitude, and wavelength.
- Distinguish frequency (cycles per second) from period (seconds per cycle) and state that they are reciprocals (T = 1/f).
- Explain, with evidence, that a wave transfers energy without transferring matter.

## Phase 1 · Engage *(0 – 12 min)*

### 0–3 min · Opening Connection (SEL) — Restorative Circle (unit opener)

This is the first lesson of the Waves unit, so open with a brief **restorative circle**. Pass a talking piece. Prompt: *"Name a sound or signal that travels to you every day — a voice, a song, a notification — and where it comes from."* One sentence each, no cross-talk. This seeds the central idea: something travels to you, but the air, the wire, or the screen did not come with it.

### 3–8 min · Phenomenon hook — the stadium wave

**Teacher actions.** Play the stadium-wave clip with the sound off for ~30 seconds. Then run a slinky pulse across the front bench so students see the in-room version.

**Sample teacher language:**

> "Watch one single fan, not the whole stadium. Did that person travel around the stadium? Then what *did* go all the way around?"

**Anticipated student responses:**

- "The wave moved but the people stayed." — exactly the target; capture the word *stayed*.
- "Energy went around." — affirm and hold; we will sharpen this.
- "The people passed it on." — good, reframe as "each one moved up and down in place."

### 8–12 min · Notice & Wonder + Turn-and-Talk #1

Capture two columns on the board. Then:

> "Turn to your partner: what actually traveled around the stadium, and what stayed put? Use the word *energy* in your answer."

Take 2–3 responses. Desirable insight: *the disturbance (energy) traveled; the matter (people, slinky coils) only moved in place and returned.*

### Bridge to Phase 2

Every student leaves Phase 1 with a one-sentence **initial model**: "In the stadium wave, ______ traveled but ______ stayed put."

---

## Phase 2 · Explore *(12 – 32 min)*

### 12–17 min · Initial Model (silent / individual)

Prompt on the board:

> *"Draw the slinky at one instant as a wave passes. Mark a high point and a low point. Draw an arrow showing which way the wave moves, and an arrow showing which way a single coil moves. Are they the same direction?"*

This surfaces the matter-vs-energy distinction before any vocabulary.

### 17–32 min · Investigation — PhET *Wave on a String* + slinky stations

Students work in pairs at PhET (with a slinky available to handle):

1. **Amplitude** — set the oscillator higher/lower. What part of the wave changes? (Tie a ribbon to one slinky coil — does the ribbon travel down the slinky, or bob in place?)
2. **Frequency** — speed up and slow down the oscillator. Count cycles in 10 seconds. What happens to the spacing of the crests?
3. **Energy, not matter** — pause the sim and tag one point on the string. Step it forward. Does the tagged point move *along* the string, or up and down?

Use the labeled-wave figure to anchor the vocabulary you will name in Phase 3:

![A sine wave with the crest, trough, rest position, amplitude (rest-to-crest), and wavelength (crest-to-crest) all labeled.](figures/wave_anatomy.png)

### Teacher facilitation during Explore

- **What to look for** — pairs noticing that the ribbon/tagged point bobs in place while crests march along; that more cycles per second means crests are closer together.
- **What to resist** — don't define "frequency" or "wavelength" yet; let the counting and the ribbon do the teaching.
- **What to redirect** — students who say "the string moves to the right" should be asked, "does *that piece* of string end up on the right, or does it come back?"

## Phase 3 · Explain *(32 – 37 min)*

### 32–35 min · Turn-and-Talk #2 + class consensus

> "Across both the slinky and PhET, what moved *along* the medium and what only moved *in place*?"

Target consensus:

> "A wave is a disturbance that carries **energy** from place to place. The matter it travels through only vibrates in place — it does not travel with the wave."

### 35–37 min · Vocabulary introduction (≤ 3 terms)

**Sample teacher language:**

> "The height of the wave from rest to a crest is the **amplitude** — bigger amplitude means more energy. The distance from one crest to the next is the **wavelength**. And how many full cycles pass a point each second is the **frequency**, measured in hertz. The time for one cycle is its *period*, and period and frequency are reciprocals: T = 1/f."

Show the frequency/period figure here:

![Two waves over the same two seconds: a 1 Hz wave with period T = 1 s, and a 3 Hz wave with period T = 0.33 s, showing more cycles per second means a shorter period.](figures/frequency_period.png)

### Discussion prompts to deploy here

- "A wave's amplitude doubles. Does it travel faster?"
  - *Sample student response:* "No — amplitude is about energy/height, not speed. Speed depends on the medium."
- "If a wave's frequency is 4 Hz, what is its period?"
  - *Sample student response:* "T = 1/f = 1/4 = 0.25 s per cycle."

---

## Phase 4 · Elaborate *(37 – 40 min)*

### 37–39 min · Revise the model

> *"Return to your slinky drawing. Label the crest, trough, amplitude, and one wavelength. Re-write your one-sentence explanation using the words energy, amplitude, and wavelength."*

### 39–40 min · Return to the phenomenon

> "Back to the stadium: what is the 'amplitude' of the stadium wave, and what stayed in place? Answer in one sentence."

Target: "The amplitude is how high fans raise their arms; the fans (the matter) stayed in their seats while the wave (energy) traveled around."

---

## Phase 5 · Evaluate *(40 – 42 min)*

### 40–42 min · Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

> *"A buoy bobs up and down 12 times in 6 seconds as ocean waves pass.
> (a) What is the frequency of the waves?
> (b) What is the period?
> (c) Does the water itself travel toward the shore with the wave? Explain in one sentence."*

Expected: (a) f = 12/6 = 2 Hz; (b) T = 1/f = 0.5 s; (c) no — the water (matter) bobs in place; the wave carries energy toward shore. See `Answer_Key.docx`.

**Closing Reflection (SEL, 30 seconds):**

> "What is one everyday 'wave' you now see differently? Who in your group helped you picture it?"

## Common Misconceptions

- **Misconception:** "A wave carries the medium along with it (the water moves to shore)." → **Correction:** The medium vibrates in place; only energy is transported.
- **Misconception:** "Bigger waves (higher amplitude) move faster." → **Correction:** Amplitude is energy/height; wave speed is set by the medium, not the amplitude.
- **Misconception:** "Frequency and period are the same thing." → **Correction:** They are reciprocals — frequency is cycles per second, period is seconds per cycle (T = 1/f).

---

## Access & Differentiation

- **ELL/ENL supports:** Sentence frame: *"A wave carries ______ but not ______; the ______ moves up and down in place."* Word-choice box: {energy, matter, amplitude, wavelength, frequency, crest, trough}. Pair *amplitude* with a hand gesture (high vs. low).
- **IEP/SPED supports:** Provide a pre-printed blank sine curve; students drag/label cut-out tags (crest, trough, amplitude, wavelength) rather than drawing from scratch. Offer the slinky as a hands-on alternative to the simulation.
- **Extensions:** Have students measure a real wavelength in PhET by reading the ruler tool, then predict how the wavelength changes when they double the frequency at the same speed (preview of Lesson 03).

## Strategy Spotlight

**RESTORATIVE CIRCLE (unit opener).** Today's circle launches the Waves unit. Use a talking piece, one-sentence responses, and equity of voice (everyone speaks or passes). The prompt — *a sound or signal that travels to you and where it comes from* — is chosen so you can call it back in Phase 3 ("the song reached your ears, but the singer's air did not travel to you — energy did"). Keep the circle to ~2–3 minutes; the goal is community plus a shared anchor.

## Key Vocabulary (max 3)

- **amplitude** — the maximum distance a wave rises from its rest position; larger amplitude means more energy
- **wavelength** — the distance between two matching points on a wave, such as crest to crest (symbol λ)
- **frequency** — the number of complete wave cycles that pass a point each second, measured in hertz (Hz)
