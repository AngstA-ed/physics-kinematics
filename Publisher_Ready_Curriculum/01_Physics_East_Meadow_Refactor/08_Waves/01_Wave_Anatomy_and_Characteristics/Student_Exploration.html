Unit: Waves & Sound — Lesson 01: Wave Anatomy and Characteristics

RESTORATIVE CIRCLE

Phenomenon

A "wave" travels all the way around a packed stadium — but every single fan stays in their own seat, just raising and lowering their arms. On the front bench, a slinky pulse races from one end to the other, yet each coil only jiggles in place. Something clearly travels around the bowl. The fans do not.

Driving Question

When a wave travels, what actually moves — and what stays put?

Notice & Wonder

Watch the stadium wave and the slinky pulse. Write two things you notice and two things you wonder. Use the word energy somewhere.

Share with your partner: what actually traveled around the stadium, and what stayed put? Use the word energy in your answer.

Initial Model

Draw the slinky at one instant as a wave passes. Mark a high point and a low point. Draw one arrow for the direction the wave moves, and a second arrow for the direction a single coil moves. Are the two arrows the same direction?

Investigate

Drag the sliders to change the wave's amplitude (height) and wavelength (crest-to-crest distance). Watch the labeled crest and trough move. Which slider changes how much energy the wave carries?

Energy carried scales with amplitude²: —

A wave's amplitude doubles. Does it travel faster? Convince your partner using the slider above — change only the amplitude and watch the crest spacing.

Make It Make Sense

  1. On the wave diagram above, which measurement tells you how much energy the wave carries? Explain.
  2. A wave's frequency is 5 Hz. What is its period? Show the formula you used.
  3. Two ocean waves have the same speed, but Wave A has a larger amplitude than Wave B. Which carries more energy? Does it travel faster? Explain.
Stuck? Sentence frame

"A wave carries ___ but not ___; the ___ moves up and down in place."

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • amplitude — the maximum distance a wave rises from its rest position; larger amplitude means more energy
  • wavelength — the distance between two matching points on a wave, such as crest to crest (symbol λ)
  • frequency — the number of complete wave cycles that pass a point each second, measured in hertz (Hz)

Revise Your Model

Return to your slinky drawing. Label the crest, trough, amplitude, and one wavelength. Rewrite your one-sentence explanation using the words energy, amplitude, and wavelength.

Return to the Phenomenon

Back to the stadium: what is the "amplitude" of the stadium wave, and what stayed in place? Answer in one sentence using energy and amplitude.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. A buoy bobs up and down 12 times in 6 seconds as ocean waves pass.
    (a) What is the frequency of the waves? (b) What is the period? (c) Does the water itself travel toward the shore with the wave? Explain in one sentence.
  2. Reflection: What is one everyday "wave" you now see differently? Who in your group helped you picture it?
