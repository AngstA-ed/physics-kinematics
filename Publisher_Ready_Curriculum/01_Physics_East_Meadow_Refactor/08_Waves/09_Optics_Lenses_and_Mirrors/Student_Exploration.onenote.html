Unit: Waves & Sound — Lesson 09: Optics: Lenses and Mirrors

ACTIVE LEARNING

Phenomenon

Hold a magnifying glass close to this page — the print looks bigger and right-side-up. Now hold it far from a window — the image flips upside down and shrinks. Same lens, two totally different images. Eyeglasses, magnifiers, and curved mirrors all do this kind of thing.

Driving Question

What decides whether a lens makes an image big-and-upright or small-and-upside-down?

Notice & Wonder

Try a magnifying glass near and far. Write two things you notice and two things you wonder about when the image flips.

Share with your partner: what decides whether the image is big-and-upright or small-and-upside-down?

Initial Model

Draw a lens with a candle on the left. Sketch where the image forms when the candle is far from the lens, then when it is very close. Label which image is upside-down.

Investigate

A converging lens has focal length f = 60 px (marked F). Drag the object distance slider. The two principal rays (parallel-then-through-F, and through-center) locate the image. The lens equation 1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ updates live. Watch the image flip once the object moves inside F.

Storyboard frame 1. Object beyond the focal point F of a converging lens. Two principal rays — one parallel to the axis (refracts through the far F), one straight through the center — cross on the far side, forming a real, inverted image.

Storyboard frame 2. Object inside the focal point. The refracted rays diverge — they never meet on the far side. Traced backward, they appear to come from a larger, upright image on the same side: a virtual image (a magnifier).

Storyboard frame 3. The lens equation 1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ. With f = 10 cm and dₒ = 30 cm: 1/dᵢ = 1/10 − 1/30 = 1/15, so dᵢ = 15 cm — a real image you can catch on a screen. A converging lens and a concave mirror both bring parallel rays to a focus.

Move the object inside the focal point. Could you catch that image on a screen? Tell your partner why a real image can be projected but a virtual one cannot.

Make It Make Sense

  1. An object is 30 cm from a converging lens with f = 10 cm. Use 1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ to find the image distance.
  2. Why can't you project a magnifying glass's enlarged image onto a wall?
  3. A converging lens and a concave mirror are both called "converging." What do they have in common?
Stuck? Sentence frame

"When the object is beyond F the image is ___ and ___; inside F it is ___ and ___."

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • focal length — the distance f from a lens or mirror to the point where parallel rays converge (the focal point F)
  • converging lens — a lens thicker in the middle that bends parallel rays to a focus, forming real or virtual images depending on object distance
  • image — where rays (or their backward extensions) meet; a real image can be projected on a screen, a virtual image cannot

Revise Your Model

Return to your candle sketch. Re-draw both cases with the principal rays. Label one image real, inverted and one virtual, upright, and mark the focal point F.

Return to the Phenomenon

Back to the magnifying glass: in one sentence, explain why holding it close shows a big upright image but holding it far shows a small upside-down one, using focal length.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. A converging lens has a focal length of 12 cm. An object is placed 36 cm from the lens.
    (a) Use 1/f = 1/dₒ + 1/dᵢ to find the image distance. Show your work. (b) Is the image real or virtual? Upright or inverted? (c) If the object is moved to 6 cm (inside the focal length), can the image be projected on a screen? Why or why not?
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
