Unit: Waves & Sound — Lesson 08: Refraction and Snell's Law

HOCHMAN

Phenomenon

A pencil standing in a glass of water looks broken at the surface. A laser beam fired into a tank of water kinks the instant it hits the water. And a Pyrex stirrer placed in oil of just the right kind seems to vanish completely. The light bends only at the boundary.

Driving Question

What changes for the light right at the surface that makes it bend?

Notice & Wonder

Watch a laser kink at the water's surface. Write two things you notice and two things you wonder about where and why it bends.

Share with your partner: the laser bends only at the boundary, not inside the water. What does that tell you about what causes the bend?

Initial Model

Draw a ray hitting the water at an angle. Draw the normal (perpendicular to the surface). Predict: when the light slows down, does the ray bend toward the normal or away from it?

Investigate

Light travels from air (n₁ = 1.00) into water (n₂ = 1.33). Drag the incidence angle slider. The refracted angle is computed live from n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂. Notice the ray bends toward the normal when it enters the slower medium.

Snell: (1.00)·sin θ₁ = (1.33)·sin θ₂ → θ₂ = —° · the ray bends toward the normal

Set the incidence angle large, then small. How does θ₂ compare to θ₁ each time? Tell your partner why entering a slower medium bends the ray toward the normal.

Make It Make Sense

  1. Light goes from air (n = 1.00) into glass (n = 1.50) at θ₁ = 30°. Use Snell's law to find θ₂. Show your work.
  2. Why does light bend toward the normal when it enters a slower (higher-n) medium? Write a Because/But/So sentence.
  3. Total internal reflection: under what conditions can light not escape a medium, reflecting entirely instead?
Stuck? Sentence frame

"Because the water has a higher index, but the light slowed down at the boundary, so the ray bent ___ the normal."

Key Vocabulary (max 3)

  • refraction — the bending of a wave as it crosses a boundary where its speed changes
  • index of refraction — n = c/v, a number telling how much a medium slows light; larger n means slower light and more bending
  • Snell's law — n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂, relating the incident and refracted angles to the two indices

Revise Your Model

Return to your prediction. Label the incident angle θ₁, the refracted angle θ₂, and the normal. Confirm: into a slower medium, the ray bends toward the normal. Write your Because/But/So sentence.

Return to the Phenomenon

Back to the broken-looking pencil: in one sentence, explain why the part underwater looks shifted, using refraction and index of refraction.

Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

  1. Light travels from air (n = 1.00) into water (n = 1.33).
    (a) The incident angle is 40°. Use Snell's law to find the angle of refraction. Show your work. (b) Does the ray bend toward or away from the normal? Why? (c) The Pyrex stirrer "disappears" in oil of the same index. Explain why, in one sentence.
  2. Reflection: What is one thing that surprised you today? Who helped you make sense of something?
