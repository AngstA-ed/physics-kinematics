Unit Scope (from East Meadow Scope & Sequence)

This is the quantitative-readiness unit that opens the physics course. Before students meet a single force or wave, they build the math toolkit every later unit reuses: rearranging formulas, resolving vectors with trig, reading graphs and slopes, writing numbers in scientific notation, estimating by order of magnitude, and converting units. It carries no NYSSLS performance expectation of its own; instead it develops the science and engineering practice “Using Mathematics and Computational Thinking” that threads through every HS-PS standard. It feeds directly into Kinematics (slopes of motion graphs, vector components) and Forces (Fnet = m·a rearrangements, units of the newton).

# Lesson Core idea
01 Algebra and Trigonometry for Physics Isolate a variable; resolve a slant into components with SOH-CAH-TOA
02 Graphing and Interpreting Data Independent vs. dependent variable; line of best fit; slope as a proportionality constant
03 Scientific Notation and Fermi Estimates Powers of ten; scientific notation; order-of-magnitude estimation
04 Units, Prefixes, and Dimensional Analysis SI units; metric prefixes; factor-label conversion and unit checking

Strategy Rotation

  • HOCHMAN (literacy): Lessons 01, 04 — kernel sentences and Because/But/So on the rearrangement rule (01); appositive definitions and Because/But/So on why units must match (04).
  • ACTIVE LEARNING: Lesson 02 — hands-on lid measurement plus the Slow Reveal Graphs discourse routine; students generate the data that yields π.
  • BTC (Building Thinking Classrooms): Lesson 03 — visibly random groups, vertical non-permanent surfaces, and a thin-sliced scientific-notation sequence building to an open Fermi estimate.

SEL (Opening Connection + Closing Reflection), ELL/SPED supports, and a Curated Resources block appear in every lesson.
