## Phenomenon

A recipe reads "250 g flour, 0.2 L milk, bake at 180 °C" — but your kitchen has measuring cups and an oven in °F. A road sign says "100 km/h" — but your speedometer reads mph. Same physical amounts, different units. Today you become the translator.

![Convert 90 km/h to m/s with factor-label](figures/factor_label.png)

**Driving question:** How do we change a quantity's units without changing the actual amount — and how do the units themselves prove we did it right?

| Prefix | Power of ten | Example conversion |
|---|---|---|
| kilo (k) |  | 3 km = __________ m |
| centi (c) |  | 250 cm = __________ m |
| milli (m) |  | 45 mm = __________ m |
| micro (μ) |  | 1 m = __________ μm |

**Part B — Factor-label conversion.** Convert **90 km/h to m/s.** Write each factor as a ratio equal to 1 and cancel units.

> 90 km/h × ( ______ m / 1 km ) × ( 1 h / ______ s ) = __________ m/s

**Key question:** Which units cancel, and how does that tell you the answer is in the right units?
