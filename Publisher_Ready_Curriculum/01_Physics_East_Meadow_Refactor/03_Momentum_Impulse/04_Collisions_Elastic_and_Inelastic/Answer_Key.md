## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. The missing kinetic energy is **converted to heat, sound, and deformation** (the clay squishing). It is not destroyed — total energy is conserved — but it is no longer kinetic. · *Rubric:* names at least two of heat/sound/deformation AND says energy is converted, not lost. · NYSSLS: HS-PS2-2
2. **Inelastic** (perfectly inelastic, since the bumpers lock and the cars move together). Kinetic energy is lost to bending metal, heat, and sound; momentum is still conserved. · *Rubric:* "inelastic" AND a justification about KE loss / sticking. · NYSSLS: HS-PS2-2
3. The inelastic crumple zone **absorbs kinetic energy and extends the contact time**, so the force on the passengers is smaller (F = Δp/Δt). A perfectly elastic bumper would bounce the car back, giving a *larger* change in momentum and a bigger force. · *Rubric:* links energy absorption AND longer Δt → smaller force. · NYSSLS: HS-PS2-3

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) Σp = (2 kg)(4 m/s) + (2 kg)(0) = 8 kg·m/s = (4 kg)(v) → **v = 2 m/s** (same direction as the first ball).
(b) KE_before = ½(2)(4²) = **16 J**. KE_after = ½(4)(2²) = **8 J**. KE is **not conserved** (16 J → 8 J).
(c) **Inelastic** (perfectly inelastic — they stick). The numbers show momentum conserved but KE halved; the missing **8 J went to heat, sound, and deformation**.

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) v = 2 m/s; (b) 16 J before, 8 J after, "not conserved"; (c) "inelastic/perfectly inelastic" + names where the 8 J went. 1 point each. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-2, HS-PS2-3.
