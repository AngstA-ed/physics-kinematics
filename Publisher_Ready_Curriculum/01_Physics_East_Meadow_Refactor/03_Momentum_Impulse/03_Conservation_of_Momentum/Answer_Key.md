## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. The two skaters get **equal and opposite** momenta. Added as signed vectors, +p and −p sum to **zero**, the same total they started with — so the total is conserved even though both are now moving. · *Rubric:* states equal-and-opposite AND that they sum to the original zero. · NYSSLS: HS-PS2-2
2. **Friction** (or anyone touching/pushing the carts) — any **external net force** on the system would prevent momentum from being conserved. · *Rubric:* names an external force; "friction" or "an outside push" accepted. · NYSSLS: HS-PS2-2
3. The **lighter** skater moves faster. Their momenta have equal magnitude (to keep the total zero), and since p = m·v, the smaller mass needs a larger velocity for the same p. · *Rubric:* "lighter is faster" AND reasons from equal p with p = m·v. · NYSSLS: HS-PS2-2

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) Σp_before = (2 kg)(3 m/s) + (1 kg)(0) = **6 kg·m/s, East**.
(b) Combined mass = 2 + 1 = **3 kg**. Conservation: 6 = (3)(v) → **v = 2 m/s, East**.
(c) Momentum is conserved when the **external net force on the system is zero** (a closed/isolated system).

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) 6 kg·m/s East; (b) 3 kg and 2 m/s East; (c) "external net force = zero" / "isolated system." 1 point each. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-2.
