Phenomenon

In a Newton’s cradle, one ball swings in and one ball swings out — every time. Then your teacher asks: a dump truck and a bicycle are both rolling at the same speed toward you. You can only step out of the way of one.

Driving question: What makes one moving object harder to stop than another?

Notice & Wonder

I notice… I wonder…

Turn & Talk #1: What two things seem to decide how hard a moving object is to stop? Use the words mass and speed.

Initial Model

Rank these from easiest to hardest to stop. Then write one sentence explaining the rule you used.

  • a tennis ball thrown gently
  • a tennis ball thrown hard
  • a bowling ball rolled gently
  • a bowling ball rolled hard

My ranking: ______________________________________ My rule: __________________________________________

Investigation

Open PhET Collision Lab. Complete each trial and record the momentum the simulation shows.

Trial Mass (kg) Velocity (m/s) Momentum (kg·m/s)
1. Baseline 1 2 (right)
2. Double the mass 2 2 (right)
3. Double the velocity 1 4 (right)
4. Send it left 1 2 (left)

Key questions:

  • When you doubled the mass, what happened to the momentum? ______________________
  • When you doubled the velocity, what happened to the momentum? ______________________
  • What did the negative sign in Trial 4 tell you? ______________________

Make It Make Sense

  1. A 1,200 kg car moves East at 20 m/s. Calculate its momentum, with units and direction.
  2. Two carts roll at the same speed, but Cart B has twice the mass of Cart A. How do their momenta compare?
  3. A golf ball and a baseball are thrown so they have the same momentum. The golf ball is lighter. What must be true about its speed? Explain.

Revise Your Model

Return to your tennis-ball / bowling-ball ranking. Compute p = m·v for each (tennis ball = 0.06 kg, bowling ball = 6 kg; gentle = 2 m/s, hard = 8 m/s). Does the math match your gut ranking? Rewrite your rule using p = m·v.

Exit Ticket

A 0.145 kg baseball is pitched West at 40 m/s.

  1. Calculate its momentum, with units and direction.

  2. A 0.045 kg golf ball is hit at 70 m/s. Show which ball has more momentum.

  3. Is momentum a vector or a scalar? Give one reason.
