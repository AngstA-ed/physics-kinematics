Phenomenon

Your teacher drops two identical eggs from the same height. One lands on a hard tray and shatters. The other lands on a pillow and survives. Both eggs hit moving at the same speed and end at rest.

Driving question: If both eggs had the same change in momentum, why did only one break?

Notice & Wonder

I notice… I wonder…

Turn & Talk #1: Both eggs had the same change in momentum. What made the force on each egg so different? Use the word time.

Initial Model

Sketch two force-vs-time graphs for the same egg stopping: one landing on the tray, one on the pillow. Make the total “push” (area under the curve) the same, but the shapes different. Then predict which graph has the taller spike.

Make It Make Sense

  1. An egg’s change in momentum stopping on a pillow is the same as on a tray. What single quantity does the pillow make bigger, and what does that do to the force?
  2. A crumple zone makes a car body more damaged in a crash but the people inside safer. Explain using J = F·Δt.
  3. A 0.5 kg ball moving at 8 m/s is caught and stopped. What impulse is needed to stop it? (Give units.)

Exit Ticket

A 0.5 kg ball moving at 8 m/s is brought to rest.

  1. Find the impulse needed to stop it (give units and direction).

  2. If a glove stops it in 0.4 s, what average force does the glove apply? If a bare hand stops it in 0.05 s, what force?

  3. Use J = F·Δt to explain, in one sentence, why the glove hurts less.
