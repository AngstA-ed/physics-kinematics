3. PE = mgh uses h measured from a reference. The same object has different h (and thus different PE) depending on where we call "zero," because potential energy describes *relative* position, not an absolute property. · *Rubric:* states PE is relative to a chosen reference. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-2

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) PE = mgh = 500 kg × 9.81 m/s² × 30 m = **147,150 J** (≈ 1.47 × 10⁵ J) relative to the ground.
(b) PE = 500 × 9.81 × 15 = **73,575 J** (≈ 7.36 × 10⁴ J) — exactly **half**, because PE is proportional to height.
(c) Measuring from a platform halfway up makes h smaller (15 m instead of 30 m), so the PE would be smaller; PE depends on the **reference height** we choose.

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) shows mgh and ≈ 147,000 J; (b) ≈ 73,600 J AND "half"; (c) explains the reference-height dependence. 1 point each. Accept g = 9.8 (≈ 147,000 J / ≈ 73,500 J). NYSSLS: HS-PS3-2.
