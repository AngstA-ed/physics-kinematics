Phenomenon

A roller-coaster car is slowly hauled up the lift hill. Nothing exciting happens on the way up — but every meter of height stores energy the coaster will spend on the way down. A water tower does the same thing with water.

Driving question: What is being stored as the car climbs, and what does the amount of stored energy depend on?

Notice & Wonder

I notice… I wonder…

Turn & Talk #1: What two things about the coaster car decide how much energy is stored at the top? Use the word height.

Initial Model

Draw the coaster car on the lift hill at three heights.

My prediction — when the height doubles, the stored energy becomes ____________ times bigger, because _______________________________________________

Make It Make Sense

  1. A 2.0 kg book sits on a 1.0 m table. What is its gravitational PE relative to the floor? Use PE = mgh with g ≈ 9.81 m/s².
  2. Two coaster cars sit at the same height. Car A is 400 kg; car B is 800 kg. Which has more PE, and by what factor? Explain.
  3. Why must we always state the reference height (the “zero”) when we report a potential energy?

Exit Ticket

A 500 kg roller-coaster car is hauled to the top of a 30 m lift hill. (Use g ≈ 9.81 m/s².)

  1. Calculate its gravitational potential energy relative to the ground. Show PE = mgh.

  2. If the lift hill were only 15 m, what would its PE be, and how does it compare to part (a)?

  3. Explain why your answer to (a) would change if we measured height from a platform halfway up instead of from the ground.
