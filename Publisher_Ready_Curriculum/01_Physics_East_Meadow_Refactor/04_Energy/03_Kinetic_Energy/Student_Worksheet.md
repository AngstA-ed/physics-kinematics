## Phenomenon

A car at 20 mph and the same car at 40 mph: the speed only doubled, but the distance needed to stop roughly *quadruples*. The energy of motion is not growing the way you'd expect.

**Driving question:** How does the energy of a moving object depend on its speed — and why does a small speed increase make it so much harder to stop?

## Make It Make Sense

1. A 2.0 kg ball moves at 3.0 m/s. Calculate its kinetic energy. Show KE = ½mv².
2. Two identical cars move down the road. Car A goes 15 m/s; car B goes 30 m/s. How many times more kinetic energy does car B have? Explain using v².
3. Your friend says "double the speed, double the energy." Use the formula to correct your friend.

## Exit Ticket

A 1500 kg car travels at 10 m/s.

(a) Calculate its kinetic energy. Show KE = ½mv².

(b) The car speeds up to 20 m/s. What is its new kinetic energy, and by what factor did it change?

(c) In one sentence, explain why doubling the speed makes the car so much harder to stop.
