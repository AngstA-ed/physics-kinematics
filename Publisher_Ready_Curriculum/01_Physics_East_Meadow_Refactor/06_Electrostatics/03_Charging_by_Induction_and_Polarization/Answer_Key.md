## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. The rod's charge makes the paper's own charges **shift (polarize)**: the near side becomes oppositely charged to the rod and the far side the same. Because the near (opposite) side is closer, the net force is attraction, even though the paper stays neutral. · *Rubric:* names polarization AND explains near-side attraction. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5
2. The sphere ends up **positive** — the **opposite** sign of the negative rod. The rod repels electrons to the grounded far side; once they drain to ground and the ground/rod are removed (in that order), the sphere is left with a deficit of electrons. · *Rubric:* "positive/opposite" + grounding mechanism. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5
3. Letting the rod touch the sphere makes it **charging by conduction**, and the sphere ends up **negative** — the **same** sign as the rod. · *Rubric:* "conduction" AND "negative/same sign." · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) The sphere's electrons are **repelled away** from the positive rod is wrong — the rod is positive, so it **attracts** electrons: **negatives gather on the near side, positives on the far side**. This is **polarization**.
(b) The sphere ends up **negative** (opposite the positive rod), charged by **induction**.
(c) **Induction** leaves the **opposite** sign with **no contact**; **conduction** leaves the **same** sign through **direct contact**.

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) correct charge placement (− near the + rod) + "polarization"; (b) "negative" + "induction"; (c) names opposite-vs-same AND contact-vs-no-contact. 1 point each. NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5.
