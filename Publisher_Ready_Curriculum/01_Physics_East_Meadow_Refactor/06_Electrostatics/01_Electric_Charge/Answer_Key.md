## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. They **repel**. Rubbing both balloons on the same material gives them the **same kind** of charge, and like charges repel. · *Rubric:* "repel" AND "same charge" → full credit. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5
2. The charge (electrons) came from the **wool sweater/hair** it was rubbed on; that object is left with the **opposite (positive)** charge. Charge was transferred, not created. · *Rubric:* names the source object AND opposite sign. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5
3. The paper was **not given a charge**. The neutral paper's own charges shift slightly (polarization), so its near side is attracted to the balloon. It remains overall neutral. · *Rubric:* says paper stays neutral AND mentions its charges shifting/polarizing. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) The two spheres carry the **same sign** of charge — like charges repel, so they swing apart.
(b) The third sphere is **oppositely charged** to the first (attraction means unlike charges).
(c) The sock carries **+8 units**. Charge is conserved: the 8 units of negative charge (electrons) the balloon gained came *from* the sock, leaving it positive by the same amount.

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) "same sign / like charges" + repel; (b) "opposite sign"; (c) "+8" AND a conservation justification. 1 point each. NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5.
