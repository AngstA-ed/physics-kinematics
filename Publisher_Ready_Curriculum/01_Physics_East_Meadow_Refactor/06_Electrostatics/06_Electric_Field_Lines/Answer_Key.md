## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. The four rules: (1) lines **start on + charges and end on − charges**; (2) they point the way a **positive test charge** would move; (3) lines **never cross**; (4) **closer lines mean a stronger field** (density shows strength). · *Rubric:* all four = full credit (¾ for three). · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5
2. **Diagram A** (the closely spaced lines) has the stronger field, because **line density represents field strength** — crowded lines = strong field. · *Rubric:* "A" AND density-shows-strength reasoning. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5
3. Parallel plates give a **uniform field** — straight, evenly spaced lines, the same strength everywhere. A single charge's field **spreads out and weakens with distance** (lines diverge and the field falls off as 1/r²). · *Rubric:* "uniform vs. spreading/weakening." · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) Any **two** of: lines start on + and end on −; lines never cross; lines point the way a + test charge moves; closer lines mean a stronger field.
(b) **Diagram A** (close-together lines) has the stronger field. Line **density encodes field strength**, so more crowded lines = stronger field.
(c) Straight lines pointing **inward, toward the negative charge** — field lines **end** on negative charges (and point the way a + test charge would be pulled).

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) any two valid rules = 1 pt; (b) "A" + density reasoning; (c) "toward / inward" AND "lines end on −." 1 point each. NYSSLS: HS-PS3-5.
