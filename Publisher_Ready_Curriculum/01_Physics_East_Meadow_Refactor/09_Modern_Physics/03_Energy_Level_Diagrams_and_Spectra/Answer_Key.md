## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. Electrons can only occupy **fixed energy levels**, so only certain jumps are possible. Each jump emits a photon whose energy equals the gap between levels, so only certain photon energies — only certain **colors** — come out. White light, by contrast, contains all energies/colors, giving a continuous rainbow. · *Rubric:* "fixed/discrete levels → only certain jumps → only certain colors." · NYSSLS: HS-ESS1-2 (connection)
2. The **big drop** emits the higher-frequency (bluer) photon. A bigger energy gap means a higher-energy photon, and since E = h·f, higher energy means higher frequency. · *Rubric:* big gap → high energy → high frequency, references E = h·f. · NYSSLS: HS-ESS1-2 (connection)
3. Astronomers spread the starlight into a spectrum and match its **line spectrum** (its fingerprint) to the known line patterns of elements measured on Earth. Matching lines reveal which elements the star contains. · *Rubric:* "match the star's line spectrum to known element fingerprints." · NYSSLS: HS-ESS1-2 (connection)

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) Electrons occupy only **discrete energy levels**, so only certain jumps — and therefore only certain photon energies/colors — are possible; that gives a few sharp lines instead of a full rainbow. White light contains all colors, so it spreads continuously.
(b) The **n = 4 → n = 1** drop emits the higher-frequency photon. It is the larger energy gap, so the photon carries more energy, and E = h·f means more energy → higher frequency.
(c) By matching the **line spectrum** in the star's light to the known fingerprint spectra of elements measured on Earth.

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) discrete levels → limited colors; (b) "n = 4 → n = 1" + "bigger gap → higher frequency (E = h·f)"; (c) "match line spectrum to known element fingerprints." 1 point each. NYSSLS: HS-ESS1-2 (connection).
