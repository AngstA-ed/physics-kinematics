## Guided Notes

**Big idea:** Electrons in an atom can only have certain fixed energies, called **energy levels** — like steps on a ladder, not a ramp.

![Energy-level diagram: an electron absorbs a photon to jump up and emits a photon E = h·f when it drops down between discrete levels.](figures/energy_level_diagram.png)

- To jump **up** a level, an electron must **absorb** a photon.
- To drop **down** a level, an electron __________ a photon — this is **photon emission**.
- The photon's energy equals the size of the __________: E = h·f.
- A **bigger** drop → bigger energy gap → __________ frequency → bluer color.
- A **smaller** drop → smaller gap → __________ frequency → redder color.

**Why a line spectrum:**

![Top: a continuous rainbow from white light. Bottom: hydrogen's line spectrum — a few bright lines on black.](figures/line_spectrum_strip.png)

- Because levels are fixed, only certain jumps — and only certain photon colors — are __________.
- Each element has its own set of gaps, so each makes its own unique **line spectrum** (a __________).
- Astronomers match a star's line spectrum to known elements to learn what the star is __________ of. *(This is part of the evidence behind the Big Bang explanation.)*

## Worked Example

**Problem.** In an atom, electron drops A (n = 5 → n = 1) and B (n = 2 → n = 1) both emit photons. Which photon has the higher frequency?

**Solution.**
1. Compare the energy gaps: drop A is the __________ jump, so the bigger gap.
2. A bigger gap means a __________-energy photon.
3. Since E = h·f, a higher-energy photon has a __________ frequency (bluer).
4. So **drop ___** emits the higher-frequency photon.
