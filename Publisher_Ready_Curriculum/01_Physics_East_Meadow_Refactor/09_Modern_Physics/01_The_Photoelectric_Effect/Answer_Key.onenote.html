Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

  1. Light delivers its energy in separate packets (photons), not as one continuous flow. A single red photon is too weak to free an electron, and many weak photons (a bright red beam) still can’t — each electron is hit by one photon at a time. A violet photon carries enough energy in one packet. · Rubric: names packets/photons AND states “one photon hits one electron” → full credit. · NYSSLS: HS-PS4-4
  2. Brightness adds more photons, so more electrons are freed, but each electron still gets the energy of just one photon — so their speed/energy doesn’t change. Raising the frequency raises each photon’s energy (E = h·f), so each freed electron leaves with more energy. · Rubric: links brightness → number and frequency → energy-per-electron. · NYSSLS: HS-PS4-4
  3. Raise the frequency (use higher-frequency / more-violet light). Since E = h·f and h is fixed, frequency is the only factor that changes a photon’s energy. · Rubric: identifies frequency via E = h·f. · NYSSLS: HS-PS4-4

Exit Ticket Answer

  1. E = h·f = (6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s)(6.0×10¹⁴ Hz) = 3.98×10⁻¹⁹ J ≈ 4.0×10⁻¹⁹ J.
  2. Yes. 6.0×10¹⁴ Hz is above the threshold frequency (5.0×10¹⁴ Hz), so each photon carries enough energy to eject an electron.
  3. Nothing is ejected. 4.0×10¹⁴ Hz is below the threshold; doubling the brightness only doubles the number of too-weak photons — each still lacks the energy to free an electron.

Tolerance/rubric: (a) E ≈ 4.0×10⁻¹⁹ J (accept 3.9–4.0×10⁻¹⁹ J), must show E = h·f; (b) “yes” + “above threshold”; (c) “no electrons” + “below threshold / brightness only adds photons.” 1 point each. NYSSLS: HS-PS4-4.

Closing Reflection (rubric)

Closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any honest response. Look for: - A genuine shift-in-thinking statement (e.g., “I used to think brighter always meant more energy”). - A correct association between frequency/color and photon energy.
