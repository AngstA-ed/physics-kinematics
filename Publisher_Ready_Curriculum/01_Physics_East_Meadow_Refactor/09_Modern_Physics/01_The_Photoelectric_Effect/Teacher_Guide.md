## Curated Resources (from East Meadow Scope & Sequence)

### NYSSLS Standards

This lesson is the core treatment of **HS-PS4-4**: *"Evaluate the validity and reliability of claims in published materials of the effects that different frequencies of electromagnetic radiation have when absorbed by matter. (Cause and Effect)."* The photoelectric effect is the cleanest case in the curriculum where the *frequency* of light — not its brightness — is the cause that determines an effect (whether electrons are ejected). It also closes the Waves unit's wave model of light by introducing the complementary particle (photon) model.

### Phenomenon

- Different-color lasers (red, green, violet) shining on a glow/photo surface — only some colors eject electrons, no matter how bright: <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kcSYV8bMQzg>
- Why a UV lamp fogs photographic paper but a much brighter red lamp does not (cause-and-effect framing).

### Javalab / Labs

- PhET Photoelectric Effect simulation: <https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/photoelectric>

### Assessments

- Exit ticket below (threshold-frequency reasoning) is the formative check. The PhET simulation includes built-in data tables students can screenshot as evidence.

## Lesson Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Duration** | 42 minutes (1 period) |
| **NYSSLS link** | HS-PS4-4 (core) |
| **CCC focus** | Cause and Effect — the *frequency* of light, not its intensity, causes (or fails to cause) electron ejection. |
| **Strategy chips** | HOCHMAN (Because/But/So + appositive on threshold frequency) |
| **Materials** | Projector for the laser/PhET demo; student devices or a shared screen for PhET; whiteboards/markers; reference table (E = h·f, h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s). |
| **Safety** | If using real lasers, never aim at eyes; the simulation is the safe default. |
| **Prior knowledge** | Waves — frequency and wavelength (violet light is higher frequency than red); energy as a measurable quantity. |

**Lesson objectives — students can:**

- Describe light as a stream of energy packets (**photons**) whose energy is set by frequency, E = h·f.
- Explain why electrons are ejected only above a **threshold frequency**, regardless of intensity.
- Use E = h·f to calculate a photon's energy and compare it to a threshold.

## Phase 1 · Engage *(0 – 10 min)*

### 0–2 min · Opening Connection (SEL)

Quick whip-around (one sentence each, pass allowed): *"Name something where doing it harder doesn't help — only doing it a different way does."* (e.g., a key that's the wrong shape — pushing harder won't open the lock.) Park these; you'll call back to "wrong shape vs. harder push" when intensity fails but frequency succeeds.

### 2–7 min · Phenomenon hook — colored lasers on a photo surface

**Teacher actions.** Project the laser clip (or PhET). Shine a bright red beam on the surface: no electrons leave. Switch to a dim violet beam: electrons fly off immediately.

**Sample teacher language:**

> "I just made the red light *much* brighter and nothing happened. Then I used a dim purple light and electrons jumped off right away. Brightness lost to color. Why would the *color* of the light decide this, when more brightness couldn't?"

**Anticipated student responses:**

- "Purple light is stronger." — reframe: "Stronger how? It's actually dimmer. What's different about purple?"
- "Maybe purple has more energy per beam." — capture *energy*; we'll sharpen "per beam" to "per packet."
- "It's the frequency — purple is higher frequency." — gold; hold it for Phase 3.

### 7–10 min · Notice & Wonder + Turn-and-Talk #1

Two columns on the board. Then:

> "Turn to your partner: what does *brightness* change, and what does *color* change? Predict which one controls whether electrons leave."

Target seed: brightness = how many; color (frequency) = how much energy each carries.

### Bridge to Phase 2

Every student leaves with a one-sentence **initial model**: "Electrons are ejected when ______."

---

## Phase 2 · Explore *(10 – 30 min)*

### 10–14 min · Initial Model (silent / individual)

Prompt on the board:

> *"Draw the light hitting the metal. Show what you think 'brighter' does and what 'higher frequency' does. Predict: which change will start ejecting electrons?"*

### 14–30 min · Investigation — PhET Photoelectric Effect

Students (pairs) run the PhET sim and fill the data table on the worksheet:

1. **Hold frequency low (red), raise intensity to max.** Do electrons eject? (No.)
2. **Hold intensity low, raise frequency** until electrons just begin to leave. Record that frequency — the **threshold**.
3. **Above threshold:** raise frequency higher. What happens to the *speed/energy* of ejected electrons? (Increases.)
4. **Above threshold:** raise intensity. What happens to the *number* of electrons? (Increases, but their energy does not.)

**Teacher facilitation language:**

> "At max brightness with red light — anything? Now creep the frequency up. Watch for the exact moment electrons appear."
> "Once they're flying, what does turning up *brightness* change — their number or their speed?"
> "What does turning up *frequency* change instead?"

Use the diagram below to make "one photon, one electron" visible while students model the ejection event:

![A single photon of energy E equals h times f strikes a metal surface and ejects one electron, which flies away.](figures/photon_ejects_electron.png)

### Teacher facilitation during Explore

- **What to look for** — students noticing the *sharp on/off* at a particular frequency, not a gradual fade.
- **What to resist** — don't name "photon" or "quantized" yet; let the threshold surprise them.
- **What to redirect** — "more brightness = more energy" should be tested: more brightness below threshold still ejects *nothing*.

---

## Phase 3 · Explain *(30 – 36 min)*

### 30–33 min · Turn-and-Talk #2 + class consensus

> "Brightness changed the *number* of electrons but never started them. Frequency had an on/off line. What does that tell you about how light delivers its energy?"

Target consensus:

> "Light arrives in separate packets. Each packet's energy depends on frequency. One packet hits one electron — if that single packet doesn't carry enough energy, no amount of extra packets (brightness) helps."

### 33–36 min · Vocabulary introduction (≤ 3 terms)

**Sample teacher language:**

> "Each energy packet of light is a **photon** — a particle of light. Its energy is E = h·f, so higher frequency means a more energetic photon. The ejecting of electrons by light is the **photoelectric effect**. The lowest frequency that can eject any electron is the **threshold frequency** — below it, brightness is useless; above it, brightness only adds more electrons."

### Discussion prompts to deploy here

- "Why can't a brighter red beam ever eject an electron that violet ejects easily?"
  - *Sample student response:* "Brighter red just sends more low-energy photons; each one is still too weak. Violet photons each clear the threshold."
- "If h is tiny, why does frequency matter so much?"
  - *Sample student response:* "E = h·f — h is fixed, so the only way to raise each photon's energy is to raise f."

---

## Phase 4 · Elaborate *(36 – 39 min)*

### 36–38 min · Hochman Because/But/So + revise the model

Students complete the sentence stem (modeled, then independent):

> "A bright red light cannot eject electrons **because** ______, **but** a dim violet light can **because** ______, **so** the deciding factor is ______ , not ______."

Coach the appositive: *"A photon, a single packet of light energy, carries E = h·f."*

### 38–39 min · Return to the phenomenon

> "Restate the laser demo in one sentence using *photon* and *threshold frequency*."

Target: "Violet photons cleared the threshold frequency and ejected electrons; red photons — even many of them — each fell below it."

---

## Phase 5 · Evaluate *(39 – 42 min)*

### 39–42 min · Exit Ticket + Closing Reflection

> *"A metal has a threshold frequency of 5.0×10¹⁴ Hz.
> (a) Find the energy of one photon of light at 6.0×10¹⁴ Hz. (Use E = h·f, h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s.)
> (b) Will that light eject electrons? Explain.
> (c) Doubling the brightness of light at 4.0×10¹⁴ Hz does what? Why?"*

Expected: (a) E = (6.63×10⁻³⁴)(6.0×10¹⁴) ≈ 4.0×10⁻¹⁹ J; (b) yes — 6.0×10¹⁴ Hz is above threshold; (c) nothing is ejected — below threshold, more photons still each lack the energy. See `Answer_Key.docx`.

**Closing Reflection (SEL, 30 seconds):**

> "What is one idea about light that changed for you today?"

## Common Misconceptions

- **Misconception:** "Brighter light always carries more energy to each electron." → **Correction:** Brighter light = *more* photons, not *more energetic* ones. Each photon's energy is set by frequency (E = h·f).
- **Misconception:** "Light is only a wave." → **Correction:** Light also behaves as particles (photons); the photoelectric effect is the evidence the wave model alone cannot explain.
- **Misconception:** "Any light, given enough time/intensity, eventually ejects electrons." → **Correction:** Below the threshold frequency, no intensity and no waiting will eject an electron — it's an on/off threshold.

---

## Access & Differentiation

- **ELL/ENL supports:** Sentence frame: *"Light below the ___ frequency ejects ___ electrons, but light above it ejects electrons whose energy ___ as frequency rises."* Word box: {threshold, no, increases, photon, intensity}. Pair *photon* with a gesture (one fist = one packet).
- **IEP/SPED supports:** Provide the PhET data table pre-labeled; students circle "ejects / does not eject" rather than writing. Offer a partially worked E = h·f calculation with the substitution already set up.
- **Extensions:** Have students compute the threshold *energy* in joules from the threshold frequency and explain why two metals with different thresholds respond to different colors.

## Strategy Spotlight

**HOCHMAN (literacy).** Today's literacy move is the Because/But/So expansion in Phase 4, which forces students to coordinate cause (frequency) against a distractor (intensity) in a single complex sentence. Model one aloud, then release. The appositive drill — *"A photon, a single packet of light energy, carries E = h·f"* — gives students a compact way to define the new term mid-sentence, a transferable academic-writing structure.

## Key Vocabulary (max 3)

- **photon** — a single packet (particle) of light energy; its energy is E = h·f, where h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s
- **photoelectric effect** — the ejection of electrons from a surface when light of high enough frequency strikes it
- **threshold frequency** — the lowest frequency of light that can eject electrons from a given surface; below it, no electrons leave regardless of intensity
