## Guided Notes

**Big idea:** All matter is built from a small set of **fundamental particles** — particles with no smaller parts inside.

![Standard-Model chart: a grid of quarks and leptons (matter particles) plus bosons (force carriers).](figures/standard_model_chart.png)

- Matter particles come in two families: **quarks** and **__________**.
- **Quarks** join together to build protons and __________.
- **Leptons** include the familiar **__________** (which is already fundamental).
- Forces between matter particles are carried by **bosons** (for example, the __________ carries the electromagnetic force).

**Building a proton (and a neutron):**

![A proton drawn as two up quarks and one down quark (uud); charges +2/3, +2/3, −1/3 add to +1.](figures/proton_quark_diagram.png)

- Proton = up + up + down → charge (+⅔) + (+⅔) + (−⅓) = __________.
- Neutron = up + down + down → charge (+⅔) + (−⅓) + (−⅓) = __________.
- So a proton is **not** fundamental — it is made of __________.

The Standard Model organizes particles the way the __________ table organizes elements.

## Worked Example

**Problem.** Is an electron a fundamental particle? Is a proton? Explain how you know.

**Solution.**
1. An electron is a __________ — it has no smaller parts inside, so it **is** fundamental.
2. A proton is made of three __________ (up, up, down), so it has smaller parts inside.
3. Therefore the proton is **not** __________; only its quarks are.
