Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

  1. This is fission — a heavy nucleus is split into lighter nuclei (here, started by an incoming neutron, which also releases more neutrons + energy). · Rubric: “fission” + “heavy nucleus splits.” · NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8
  2. This is fusion — light hydrogen nuclei combine into a heavier helium nucleus. Fusion is what powers stars (lights them and lets them last billions of years) and forms heavier elements, which later build planets and living things — the connection to star formation. · Rubric: “fusion” + “powers stars / forms elements.” · NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8, HS-ESS1-2 (connection)
  3. In a nuclear change, a tiny amount of mass is converted into energy. By E = mc², and because c (the speed of light) is enormous, even a tiny mass yields a huge amount of energy — far more than the energy stored in chemical bonds (burning). (Scale comparison only; no calculation expected.) · Rubric: “small mass → energy via E = mc², huge because c is large.” · NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8

Exit Ticket Answer

  1. Fission — a heavy nucleus splits into two lighter nuclei.
  2. Fusion — light nuclei combine into a heavier one. Fusion powers and lights stars and forges heavier elements, so it is central to how stars form and how the matter around us was made.
  3. A small amount of mass is converted to energy; because E = mc² and c is enormous, even a tiny mass releases a huge amount of energy — far more than a chemical reaction like burning. (Scale idea only, no calculation.)

Tolerance/rubric: (a) “fission”; (b) “fusion” + star/element formation connection; (c) “mass → energy, E = mc², huge because c is large.” 1 point each. NYSSLS: HS-PS1-8; (b) also HS-ESS1-2 (connection).
