## Guided Notes

**Big idea:** An object moving in a circle at constant speed is still __________, because its velocity __________ keeps changing (velocity is a vector — size *and* direction).

- The acceleration points toward the __________ of the circle. We call it **centripetal acceleration**: ac = v² / __________.
- The net force that causes it also points toward the __________. We call it the **centripetal force**: Fc = m · v² / __________.
- "Centripetal" means __________-seeking.
- The centripetal force is always provided by a **real** force. Match them:
  - Ball on a string → __________
  - Car on a flat curve → __________
  - Moon orbiting Earth → __________
  - Object at the bottom of a swung bucket → __________ force

**The misconception (cross it out in your head):**

- There is **no** "centrifugal force" throwing you outward. That outward *feeling* is just your __________ — your body trying to go __________ (in a straight line / tangent) while something curves you inward.
- If the centripetal force disappears, the object flies off on a __________ (straight line), **not** outward along the radius.

**How the numbers behave (from Fc = m·v²/r):**

- Double the speed v → force becomes about __________ times bigger (v is squared).
- Smaller radius r → __________ force needed (sharper turn is harder).
- More mass m → __________ force needed.

## Worked Example

**Problem.** A 0.50 kg toy car rounds a circular track of radius 2.0 m at a constant speed of 4.0 m/s. Find the centripetal force on the car and state its direction.

**Solution.**
1. Write the equation (2025 Reference Table): Fc = m · v² / r.
2. Substitute: Fc = (0.50 kg) · (4.0 m/s)² / (2.0 m).
3. Compute the top: (4.0)² = 16, so 0.50 · 16 = 8.0; then 8.0 / 2.0 = __________ N.
4. Direction: toward the __________ of the circle.
5. What provides it here? The __________ between the tires and the track.
