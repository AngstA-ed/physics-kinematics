## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. The classmate is **wrong**. Acceleration is any change in *velocity*, and velocity is a vector (size *and* direction). In a circle the direction changes every instant, so the velocity changes and the ball **is** accelerating — toward the center — even though its speed is constant. · *Rubric:* states acceleration = change in velocity AND identifies the changing direction → full credit. · NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1
2. (a) **Friction** between the tires and the road. (b) **Gravity** (Earth's gravitational pull on the Moon). (c) The **normal force** from the bottom of the bucket pushing the water inward (toward the center). · *Rubric:* one real force named correctly per case. · NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1
3. The tension **quadruples** (becomes about 4× as large). In Fc = m·v²/r the speed v is *squared*, so doubling v multiplies the force by 2² = 4. · *Rubric:* "quadruples" AND cites v² → full credit. · NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1

## Exit Ticket Answer

Given: m = 0.20 kg, r = 0.50 m, v = 3.0 m/s.

(a) **Yes, it is accelerating.** Its speed is constant but its velocity *direction* is changing, so its velocity is changing — that is acceleration (directed toward the center).

(b) Fc = m·v²/r = (0.20 kg)(3.0 m/s)² / (0.50 m) = (0.20)(9.0) / (0.50) = 1.8 / 0.50 = **3.6 N**, directed **toward the center** of the circle.

(c) **No, the student is not correct.** There is no real "centrifugal force." The only real force is the inward (centripetal) tension in the string. The outward feeling is the ball's **inertia** — its tendency to travel straight (tangent) while the string curves it inward — not a force.

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) must say "yes" AND justify via changing direction; (b) correct setup with v² and a numeric answer of 3.6 N toward the center (accept minor rounding), units required; (c) "no" AND identifies inertia / absence of a real outward force. 1 point each. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1.
