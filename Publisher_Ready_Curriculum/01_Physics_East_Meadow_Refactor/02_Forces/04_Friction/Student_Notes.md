## Guided Notes

**Big idea:** Friction is a __________ force that opposes the relative motion (or *attempted* motion) of two surfaces touching each other.

- Friction always points __________ the direction the object moves or tries to move.
- **Static friction** acts when the object is __________ sliding. It grows to match your push, up to a __________ value.
- **Kinetic friction** acts when the object __________ sliding. It is roughly __________ (it does not change much with speed).
- It is usually harder to *start* an object sliding than to *keep* it sliding, because the static maximum is __________ than the kinetic friction.

**The equation (2025 Reference Table):**

> **Ff = μ·FN**
>
> - Ff = the __________ force (newtons, N)
> - μ = the __________ of friction (no units) — depends on the two __________
> - FN = the __________ force (the surface's push up on the object, in N)

**So friction gets bigger when:** μ is __________ (rougher pairing) OR FN is __________ (pressed together harder).

## Worked Example

**Problem.** A 50 N wooden block slides across a tabletop. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the block and the table is μ = 0.20. What is the kinetic friction force on the block?

**Solution.**
1. On a flat surface, the normal force equals the weight, so FN = __________ N.
2. Write the equation: Ff = μ·FN = __________ × __________.
3. Ff = __________ N, pointing __________ to the block's motion.

*Check:* If we doubled the block's weight to 100 N, the friction force would __________ to __________ N — because Ff depends on FN.
