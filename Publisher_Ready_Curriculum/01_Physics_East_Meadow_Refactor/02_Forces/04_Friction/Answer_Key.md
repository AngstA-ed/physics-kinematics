## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. **Static friction has a larger maximum than kinetic friction.** Starting the desk means overcoming the bigger static maximum; once it slides, only the smaller, roughly constant kinetic friction opposes you. · *Rubric:* names static > kinetic AND links to start vs. keep moving. · NYSSLS: HS-PS2-1
2. **The heavier box** needs more force. More weight means a larger **normal force (FN)**, and since **Ff = μ·FN**, larger FN means larger friction. · *Rubric:* links weight → normal force → larger Ff. · NYSSLS: HS-PS2-3
3. Sandpaper is **rougher**, so the pairing has a **larger coefficient of friction (μ)**. With the same normal force, a larger μ gives a larger Ff = μ·FN. · *Rubric:* names rougher surface → larger μ. · NYSSLS: HS-PS2-3

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) **Static friction.** The crate is not sliding, so the friction is static — it grows to match your push (up to its maximum), keeping the net force zero so the crate stays at rest.
(b) **Ff = μ·FN = 0.30 × 200 N = 60 N.** (On a flat floor FN equals the 200 N weight.)
(c) **Cause and Effect:** any one of — use a rougher surface (larger μ), add weight / press it down harder (larger FN). Either correctly increases Ff = μ·FN.

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) must say static AND justify "not sliding"; (b) must show Ff = μ·FN with the answer **60 N** (full credit for correct setup + answer; half for setup only); (c) any change that raises μ or FN. 1 point each. NYSSLS: HS-PS2-3 (and HS-PS2-1).
