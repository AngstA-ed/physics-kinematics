## Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

1. **Action:** your foot pushes the ground **backward**; **Reaction:** the ground pushes you **forward**. The two forces act on two **different** objects — you and the ground — and the forward push on you is what moves you. · *Rubric:* names both forces AND identifies the two different objects → full credit. · Supports HS-PS2-1
2. The rocket pushes its exhaust **gas backward**; the **gas pushes the rocket forward** — an action–reaction pair that needs no air to push against. · *Rubric:* states the rocket–gas force pair, not "pushes against air." · Supports HS-PS2-2
3. The two forces are **equal in magnitude**, but the smaller student has **less mass**, so the same force produces a **greater acceleration** (a = F/m). Equal forces, unequal masses → unequal motion. · *Rubric:* equal forces AND links smaller mass → larger acceleration. · Supports HS-PS2-1

## Exit Ticket Answer

(a) **Action:** the swimmer pushes the **wall backward**; **Reaction:** the **wall pushes the swimmer forward**. The two forces act on two different objects — the swimmer and the wall.
(b) The two forces act on **different objects**, so they do not cancel on the swimmer. The swimmer feels only the wall's **forward** push, which is a real net force forward, so the swimmer accelerates.
(c) **False.** Action and reaction forces act on **two different objects**, so they can never cancel on a single object. Forces only cancel when they act on the *same* object.

*Tolerance/rubric:* (a) names both forces and both objects; (b) "different objects → swimmer feels only the forward force"; (c) False + "different objects." 1 point each. Supports HS-PS2-1 / HS-PS2-2.
