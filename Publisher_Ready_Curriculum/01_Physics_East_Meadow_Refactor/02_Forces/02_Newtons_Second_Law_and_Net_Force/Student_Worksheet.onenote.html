Phenomenon

Your teacher pulls the same rubber band back the same distance every time — so the push is the same — and launches three different balls: a ping-pong ball, a golf ball, and a steel ball. The light ball rockets away. The heavy steel ball barely leaves.

Driving question: If the push is the same every time, what decides how much each object speeds up?

Notice & Wonder

I notice… I wonder…

Turn & Talk #1: The push was the same on all three balls, so what made the light ball speed up so much more? Use the word mass in your answer.

Initial Model

Draw the three balls leaving the slingshot. Draw one arrow for the push (same size on each ball) and a second arrow for how fast each one speeds up. Then predict which ball speeds up the most and why.

Make It Make Sense

  1. A 2 kg ball is pushed with a net force of 6 N. What is its acceleration? Show your work using a = F_net / m.
  2. Two carts get the same net force, but Cart B has twice the mass of Cart A. How do their accelerations compare?
  3. A net force of 20 N gives a cart an acceleration of 4 m/s². What is the mass of the cart?

Exit Ticket

A net force of 12 N acts on a 4 kg cart.

  1. Find the cart’s acceleration. Show F_net = m·a (or a = F_net / m).

  2. If the mass were doubled to 8 kg with the same 12 N net force, what is the new acceleration?

  3. In one sentence, explain what happened to the acceleration and why.
