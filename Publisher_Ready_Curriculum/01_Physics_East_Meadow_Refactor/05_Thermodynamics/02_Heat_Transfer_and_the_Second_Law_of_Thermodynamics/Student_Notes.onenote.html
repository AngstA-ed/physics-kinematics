Guided Notes

Big idea: When two objects at different temperatures touch, thermal energy flows from the __________ object to the __________ object. It never flows the other way on its own.

  • The energy moving because of a temperature difference is called __________.
  • The flow continues until both objects reach the __________ temperature — this is __________.
  • At equilibrium there is no __________ flow of energy (particles still move, but energy is spread evenly).

Three ways thermal energy transfers:

  • Conduction — energy passes by __________ (particle to particle), e.g. a spoon handle warming.
  • Convection — a moving __________ (liquid or gas) carries the energy, e.g. warm water rising.
  • Radiation — energy travels as __________ waves and needs no medium, e.g. warmth from the Sun.

Second law of thermodynamics: In a __________ system, thermal energy spontaneously spreads to a more __________ distribution.

Worked Example

Problem. A hot rock at 80 °C is placed in a sealed, insulated box with cool water at 10 °C. Describe what happens over time.

Solution. 1. Thermal energy flows from the __________ rock to the __________ water (hot → cold). 2. The rock’s temperature __________ and the water’s temperature __________. 3. They keep changing until both reach the __________ temperature → thermal equilibrium. 4. The energy that was concentrated in the rock is now spread more __________ across the system — the second law in action.
