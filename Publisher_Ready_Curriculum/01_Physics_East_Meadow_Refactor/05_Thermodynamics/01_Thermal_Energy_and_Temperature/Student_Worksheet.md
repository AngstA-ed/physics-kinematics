## Phenomenon

A lit sparkler burns at over **1,000 °C** — yet a child can hold it inches away and be fine. A warm bath is only about **40 °C**, but if it were ever 1,000 °C it would be deadly. Same idea, very different danger.

![Slow particles versus fast particles](figures/particle_speed.png)

**Driving question:** What is the difference between something being *hot* and something *holding a lot of energy*?

## Exit Ticket

A lit sparkler tip is about 1,000 °C. A warm bath is about 40 °C.

(a) Which has the higher temperature, and what does temperature measure about the particles?

(b) Which one could transfer more thermal energy to your body, and why?

(c) Two buckets of water read exactly 25 °C, but one holds 1 L and the other holds 50 L. Which has more thermal energy? Explain.
