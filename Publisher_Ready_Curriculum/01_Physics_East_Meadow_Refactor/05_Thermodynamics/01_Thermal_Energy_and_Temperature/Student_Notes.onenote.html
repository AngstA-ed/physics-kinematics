Guided Notes

Big idea: Everything is made of particles that are always __________. The energy of that motion is __________ energy.

  • Temperature measures the __________ kinetic energy of the particles → how __________ they move, on average.
  • Heating a substance makes its particles move __________ (not bigger). Cooling makes them move __________.
  • Thermal energy is the __________ kinetic energy of __________ the particles.
  • Thermal energy depends on two things: the __________ (average speed) and the __________ of substance (how many particles).

Same temperature, different thermal energy:

  • A cup and a swimming pool can both read 20 °C → the particles move at the same __________ speed.
  • But the pool has far more __________, so it holds far more __________ energy.

Worked Example

Problem. A 0.25 kg cup of tea and a 500,000 kg swimming pool both read 25 °C. Which holds more thermal energy, and why?

Solution. 1. Same temperature → the particles move at the same __________ speed (same average kinetic energy each). 2. Thermal energy = average KE × the __________ of particles. 3. The pool has about __________ times as many particles → far more __________ kinetic energy. The pool holds vastly more thermal energy, even though it is not hotter.
