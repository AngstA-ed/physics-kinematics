Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

  1. The pool has more thermal energy. Both are at 30 °C, so the particles move at the same average speed (same temperature). But the pool has enormously more particles, so the total kinetic energy — the thermal energy — is far greater. · Rubric: names “same average speed / same temperature” AND “more particles → more total energy” → full credit. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-4 (foundation)
  2. The sparkler’s particles move very fast (high temperature), but there are very few of them, so the total thermal energy it can transfer to your skin is tiny. Bathwater has slower particles but a huge number of them. · Rubric: distinguishes high temperature from low total thermal energy. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-2 / HS-PS3-4
  3. The average particle speed stays the same (temperature is unchanged). The total thermal energy doubles, because there are now twice as many moving particles. · Rubric: average speed unchanged AND total energy doubles. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-2

Exit Ticket Answer

  1. The sparkler has the higher temperature. Temperature measures the average kinetic energy (average speed) of the particles.
  2. The bath could transfer more thermal energy. It holds far more particles, so its total thermal energy is much greater than the sparkler’s, even though each sparkler particle moves faster.
  3. The 50 L bucket has more thermal energy. Both read 25 °C (same average particle speed), but the 50 L bucket has about 50× as many particles, so its total kinetic energy is much larger.

Tolerance/rubric: (a) sparkler AND “average kinetic energy/average speed”; (b) bath AND “more particles → more total thermal energy”; (c) 50 L AND “same temperature, far more particles.” 1 point each. NYSSLS: HS-PS3-4 (foundation), HS-PS3-2.

Closing Reflection (rubric)

Closing reflection is formative, not graded. Award full credit for any honest response. Look for: - A genuine shift-in-thinking statement in answer 1 (e.g., “I used to think hotter always meant more energy”). - Naming a peer or self-acknowledgment in answer 2.
