Make-It-Make-Sense Answers

  1. With equal masses of the same substance, the two samples have the same m and the same c, so a given temperature change costs the same energy on each side. The hot water’s temperature drops by the same amount the cold water’s temperature rises, landing exactly in the middle — the average. · Rubric: links equal m and c to equal-size, opposite ΔT → average. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-4
  2. Below the average. The large cold mass needs a large Q to change temperature, so it barely warms, while the small hot sample cools a lot. The final temperature is pulled toward the larger (cold) mass. · Rubric: states “closer to the larger/cold mass” with a mass-based reason. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-4
  3. The foam cup with a lid is an insulator, keeping the system closed so almost no thermal energy escapes. That makes heat lost ≈ heat gained, which is what we are testing. A metal beaker would conduct energy out to the surroundings. · Rubric: identifies insulation / closed system. · NYSSLS: HS-PS3-4

Revise-Your-Model (unequal mix) answer: Set heat lost = heat gained: (0.10)(c)(80 − T_f) = (0.30)(c)(T_f − 20). The c cancels: 0.10(80 − T_f) = 0.30(T_f − 20) → 8 − 0.10 T_f = 0.30 T_f − 6 → 14 = 0.40 T_f → T_f = 35 °C (below 50 °C, as predicted — the larger cold mass dominates).

Exit Ticket Answer

  1. Equal masses of the same substance → final temperature is the average: T_f = 50 °C.
  2. Q gained by cold water = m·c·ΔT = (0.20 kg)(4,186 J/kg·°C)(50 − 20 °C) = (0.20)(4,186)(30) = 25,116 J ≈ 2.5 × 10⁴ J.
  3. The heat lost by the hot water is Q = (0.20)(4,186)(80 − 50) = 25,116 J — equal in size to the heat gained. This shows conservation of energy in a closed system (heat lost = heat gained), consistent with the second law’s drive toward thermal equilibrium.

Tolerance/rubric: (a) 50 °C; (b) 2.5 × 10⁴ J (accept 25,000–25,200 J); (c) “equal” AND “energy conserved / heat lost = heat gained.” 1 point each. NYSSLS: HS-PS3-4.
