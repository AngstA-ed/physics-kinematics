01 Phenomenon

A glass of ice water sits on a lab bench. The ice cubes float. For almost every substance on Earth, the solid form is denser than the liquid — solid iron sinks in molten iron, solid copper sinks in molten copper. But water is the spectacular exception: ice floats on liquid water. Because of this, lakes freeze from the top down, and the liquid water beneath the ice protects aquatic life through winter.

02 Driving question

Why does ice float on water when most solids sink?

07 Key vocabulary

density
the mass per unit volume of a substance; D = m/V; expressed in g/mL or g/cm³; an intensive property that does not change with sample size; objects with density less than 1.0 g/mL float in water, objects with density greater than 1.0 g/mL sink
mass
the amount of matter in an object, measured in grams (g) on a balance; an extensive property that depends on sample size; not the same as weight (weight depends on gravity; mass does not)
volume
the amount of space an object occupies, measured in milliliters (mL) for liquids and irregular solids by water displacement, or in cubic centimeters (cm³) for regular geometric solids; 1 mL = 1 cm³

10 Explore further

  • Density lab — measure and plot: Students measure the mass (balance) and volume (graduated cylinder / water displacement) of several metal samples (aluminum, copper, etc.), plot mass on the y-axis versus volume on the x-axis, draw a best-fit line through the origin, and read the slope. The slope equals density. Students compare their experimental slope to a density reference table and identify the metal. This ABCs activity precedes the formal definition of density.
  • Layered-liquids demo: Teacher layers several liquids (corn syrup, water with food coloring, vegetable oil) in a graduated cylinder. Students predict and observe that each liquid "finds its level" based on its density relative to the others. This connects directly to the float/sink framework.
  • Floating-egg (salt water) demo: A raw egg sinks in fresh water (density ≈ 1.0 g/mL) and floats when enough salt is dissolved. Students use D = m/V and the concept of changing the liquid's density to explain the phenomenon. Connects to ocean ecosystems and the Dead Sea.
