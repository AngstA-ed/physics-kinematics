01 Phenomenon

A green plant sits on a windowsill, motionless, for weeks. It is not plugged into anything — just air, water, and light. Yet it grows new leaves out of almost nothing. If you dried one of those leaves and set it on fire, it would release heat and light. That heat had to come from somewhere. The plant “charged up” the leaf with energy — and burning it pays the energy back out.

02 Driving question

How does a plant "capture" sunlight as chemical energy you can later eat?

03 Notice & wonder

I notice… I wonder…

  1. The atoms are the same on both sides. So if something is different between the reactants and the products, it cannot be the atoms. What do you think changed?

07 Key vocabulary

chemical potential energy
energy stored in the chemical bonds of a substance, ready to be released when the substance reacts; the products of photosynthesis (sugar + O₂) hold more chemical potential energy than the reactants (CO₂ + H₂O), and that difference is the captured light energy
photosynthesis
the process by which a plant uses light energy to build high-energy sugar and oxygen from low-energy carbon dioxide and water (6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂); in energy terms, it stores the Sun's energy in chemical bonds
conservation of energy
the law that energy is never created or destroyed, only transferred or changed in form; the light energy stored by photosynthesis equals the energy later released when the sugar is burned or respired

09 Exit ticket

(a) In the reaction 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂, which side stores more chemical potential energy — the reactants or the products? Explain how you know in one sentence.

(b) What supplies the energy that photosynthesis stores? Where does it ultimately come from?

(c) A marshmallow roasting over a campfire releases heat. Trace that heat energy backward, step by step, all the way to its original source.

10 Explore further

  • Bond-energy diagram build (core activity): Working at vertical surfaces in random groups, students arrange cards for reactants (6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O) and products (C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂) onto a two-level energy diagram and decide which side sits higher. They justify the placement with the phenomenon: the products are the fuel, so the products must hold more energy. Connect to `figures/bond_energy_photosynthesis.png`.
  • Combustion as the reverse (comparison): Students compare photosynthesis (energy *in*, products higher) to the burning of sugar/wood (energy *out*, products lower). The same equation is read left-to-right for photosynthesis and right-to-left for combustion/respiration. A Javalab "energy of reactions" interactive or a teacher-run candle/wood-splint burn (energy released as heat and light) makes the reverse direction concrete. No external URL is required; a simple controlled burn of a wood splint or a peanut works.
  • Atom-and-energy bookkeeping check: Students count atoms on both sides of 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂ to confirm matter is conserved (the equation balances), then annotate where the energy went (into the bonds of the products).
