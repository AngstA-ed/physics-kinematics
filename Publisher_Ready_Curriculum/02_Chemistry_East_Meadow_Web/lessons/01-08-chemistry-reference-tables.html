01 Phenomenon

Every chemistry student in New York receives the exact same 2025 NYS Chemistry Reference Tables booklet on Regents day. Two students of equal ability sit down — one finds a needed value in under fifteen seconds; the other never opens the booklet and guesses. The booklet is identical. The skill of using it is not.

02 Driving question

How does one shared document support every chemistry concept on the exam?

03 Notice & wonder

I notice… I wonder…

Turn & Talk #1: Flip through the reference tables for 90 seconds without instruction. Where do you predict you would find the density of water? Where would you find the atomic masses of elements? Write one prediction for each:

Density of water: I predict it is in the _______________ section because _______________.

Atomic masses: I predict they are in the _______________ section because _______________.

04 Initial model

Before any instruction, try to find the following three values using only the reference tables booklet. Write the value (with units) AND the name of the section where you found it.

Lookup task Value I found (with units) Section / table name
Density of water at 4 °C
Atomic symbol for gold
Meaning of the prefix kilo- (what multiplier does it represent?)

After the class debrief: did your predictions from Notice & Wonder match where you actually found these values? What surprised you?

07 Key vocabulary

reference table
a standardized document of chemical data provided on the Regents exam; the 2025 NYS Chemistry Reference Tables organize recurring chemical data patterns — periodic properties, physical constants, SI units, solubility rules — into named sections for rapid, accurate lookup; fluency with the tables' structure is a direct Regents assessment target
STP (standard temperature and pressure)
the agreed reference conditions defined in the reference tables as 273 K (0 °C) for temperature and 101.3 kPa for pressure; STP is the standard baseline for comparing the behavior of gases and for gas-law calculations throughout the course; always look up the precise values rather than estimating from memory
density
mass per unit volume (g/mL or g/cm³), revisited from Lesson 05; the density of water at 4 °C is 1.00 g/mL — an anchor value in the Physical Constants table used in multiple Regents calculations; looking it up from the reference tables rather than memorizing it is the fluency move this lesson reinforces

Use each term in a sentence about today’s investigation. Do not copy the definition — describe something you actually did or observed.

10 Explore further

  • "Find the Data" scavenger-hunt stations (main activity): Students work in pairs at four or five stations, each with a specific lookup task: (1) find the density of water; (2) find the atomic symbol and atomic mass of a specified element; (3) find the SI unit symbol and prefix for milli- and kilo-; (4) find the STP conditions (temperature and pressure); (5) locate the solubility guidelines. For each lookup, students record the *value*, the *units*, and the *name of the table* where they found it. One station includes a one-step calculation using a looked-up value (e.g., use the density of water to find the mass of 250 mL of water).
  • Old vs. new edition comparison (if a previous edition is available): Students receive one page from the 2011/2012 edition and compare it to the corresponding section of the 2025 edition. They list one thing that changed and write one sentence explaining why the change might matter for accuracy or usability.
